.. _D.51:

Proposition 51 [να΄ · Prop. LV]
-------------------------------

.. folio:: Menge 92
   :page: 92

.. enunciation::

   If two straight lines have a given ratio to one another, and upon them be described any rectilineal figures given in species, those figures will have a given ratio to one another.

*Functional Signature*: ``Given(Ratio(AB : CD), Species(E), Species(F)) -> Determined(Ratio(Area(E) : Area(F)))`` · *Dependencies*: :just:`[Data 8] <D.8>`, :just:`[Data 49] <D.49>`, :just:`[Data 50] <D.50>`, :just:`[VI.18] <VI.18>`, :just:`[VI.20] <VI.20>`

Let two straight lines :math:`AB` and :math:`CD` have a given ratio :math:`AB : CD`, and on them let *any* two rectilinear figures :math:`E` (on :math:`AB`) and :math:`F` (on :math:`CD`), each given in species, be described. I say that :math:`E` has a given ratio to :math:`F`.

On :math:`AB` describe a rectilinear figure :math:`G` similar and similarly situated to :math:`F` (:just:`[VI.18] <VI.18>`). Because :math:`F` is given in species and :math:`G \sim F`, :math:`G` is also given in species (:just:`[Data Def. 3] <D.def.3>`). Now :math:`E` and :math:`G` are two rectilinear figures given in species described on the *same* straight line :math:`AB`; therefore the ratio :math:`E : G` is given (:just:`[Data 49] <D.49>`). Moreover, :math:`G` and :math:`F` are similar and similarly situated figures described on :math:`AB` and :math:`CD`, which have the given ratio :math:`AB : CD`; therefore the ratio :math:`G : F` is given (:just:`[Data 50] <D.50>`). Combining :math:`E : G` and :math:`G : F` yields :math:`E : F` given (:just:`[Data 8] <D.8>`).
