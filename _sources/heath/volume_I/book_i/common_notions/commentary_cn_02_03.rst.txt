Commentary on Book I Common Notions 2, 3
========================================

Back to :ref:`Book I Common Notions <I.cn>`.

2. Καὶ ἐὰν ἴσοις ἴσα προστεθῇ, τὰ ὅλα ἐστὶν ἴσα.
3. Καὶ ἐὰν ἀπὸ ἴσων ἴσα ἀφαιρεθῇ, τὰ καταλειπόμενά ἐστιν ἴσα.

2. *If equals be added to equals, the wholes are equal.*
3. *If equals be subtracted from equals, the remainders are equal.*

----

These two Common Notions are recognised by :person:`Heron <heron>` and :person:`Proclus <proclus>` as genuine. The latter is the axiom which is so favourite an illustration with :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>`.

Following them in the MSS. and editions there came four others of the same type as 1—3. Three of these are given by :person:`Heiberg <heiberg>` in brackets; the fourth he omits altogether.

The three are:

(*a*) *If equals be added to unequals, the wholes are unequal.*
(*b*) *Things which are double of the same thing are equal to one another.*
(*c*) *Things which are halves of the same thing are equal to one another.*

The fourth, which was placed between (*a*) and (*b*), was:

(*d*) *If equals be subtracted from unequals, the remainders are unequal.*

:person:`Proclus <proclus>`, in observing that axioms ought not to be multiplied, indicates that all should be rejected which follow from the five admitted by him and appearing in the text above (p. 155). He mentions the second of those just quoted (*b*) as one of those to be excluded, since it follows from *Common Notion* 1. :person:`Proclus <proclus>` does not mention (*a*), (*c*) or (*d*); :person:`an-Nairīzī <an-nairizi>` gives (*a*), (*d*), (*b*) and (*c*), in that order, as Euclid's, adding a note of :person:`Simplicius <simplicius>` that "three axioms (sententiae acceptae) only are extant in the ancient manuscripts, but the number was increased in the more recent."

(*a*) stands self-condemned because "unequal" tells us nothing. It is easy to see what is wanted if we refer to I. 17, where the same angle is added to a *greater* and a *less*, and it is inferred that the first sum is greater than the second. So far however as the wording of (*a*) is concerned, the addition of equal to *greater* and *less* might be supposed to produce *less* and *greater* respectively. If therefore such an axiom were given at all, it should be divided into two. :person:`Heiberg <heiberg>` conjectures that this axiom may have been taken from the commentary of :person:`Pappus <pappus>`, who had the axiom about equals added to unequals quoted below (*e*); if so, it can only be an unskilful adaptation of some remark of :person:`Pappus <pappus>`, for his axiom (*e*) has some point, whereas (*a*) is useless.

As regards (*b*), I agree with :person:`Tannery <tannery>` in seeing no sufficient reason why, if
we reject it (as we certainly must), the words in I. 47 "But things which are double of equals are equal to one another" should be condemned as an interpolation. If they were interpolated, we should have expected to find the same interpolation in I. 42, where the axiom is *tacitly* assumed. I think it quite possible that :person:`Euclid <euclid>` may have inserted such words in one case and left them out in another, without necessarily implying either that he was quoting a formal *Common Notion* of his own or that he had *not* included among his Common Notions the particular fact stated as obvious.

The corresponding axiom (*c*) about the *halves* of equals can hardly be genuine if (*b*) is not, and :person:`Proclus <proclus>` does not mention it. :person:`Tannery <tannery>` acutely observes however that, when :person:`Heiberg <heiberg>`, in I. 37, 38, brackets words stating that "the halves of equal things are equal to one another" on the ground that axiom (*c*) was interpolated (although before Theon's time), and explains that :person:`Euclid <euclid>` used *Common Notion* 3 in making his inference, he is clearly mistaken. For, while axiom (*b*) is an obvious inference from *Common Notion* 2, axiom (*c*) is not an inference from *Common Notion* 3. :person:`Tannery <tannery>` says, in a note, that (*c*) would have to be established by *reductio ad absurdum* with the help of axiom (*b*), that is to say, of *Common Notion* 2. But, as the hypothesis in the *reductio ad absurdum* would be that one of the halves is *greater* than the other, and it would therefore be necessary to prove that the one whole is *greater* than the other, while axiom (*b*) or *Common Notion* 2 only refers to *equals*, a little argument would be necessary in addition to the reference to *Common Notion* 2. I think :person:`Euclid <euclid>` would not have gone through this process in order to prove (*c*), but would have assumed it as equally obvious with (*b*).

:person:`Proclus <proclus>` (:cite:`pp. 197, 6—198, 5 <proclus:in-euclid:197.6-198.5>`) definitely rejects two other axioms of the above kind given by :person:`Pappus <pappus>`, observing that, as they follow from the genuine axioms, they are rightly omitted in most copies, although :person:`Pappus <pappus>` said that they were "on record" with the others (συναναγράφεσθαι):

(*e*) *If unequals be added to equals, the difference between the wholes is equal to the difference between the added parts*; and
(*f*) *If equals be added to unequals, the difference between the wholes is equal to the difference between the original unequals.*

:person:`Proclus <proclus>` and :person:`Simplicius <simplicius>` (in :person:`an-Nairīzī <an-nairizi>`) give proofs of both. The proof of the former, as given by :person:`Simplicius <simplicius>`, is as follows:

Let :g:`AB <0238.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`CD <0238.g1:/ C D />` be :eq:`equal <0238.g1:/ A B / = 0238.g1:/ C D />` magnitudes; and let :g:`EB <0238.g1:/ B E />`, :g:`FD <0238.g1:/ D F />` be added to them respectively, :g:`EB <0238.g1:/ B E />` being greater than :g:`FD <0238.g1:/ D F />`.

I say that :g:`AE <0238.g1:/ A E />` exceeds :g:`CF <0238.g1:/ C F />` by the same difference as that by which :g:`BE <0238.g1:/ B E />` exceeds :g:`DF <0238.g1:/ D F />`.

Cut off from :g:`BE <0238.g1:/ B E />` the magnitude :g:`BG <0238.g1:/ B G />` :eq:`equal <0238.g1:/ B G / = 0238.g1:/ D F />` to :g:`DF <0238.g1:/ D F />`.

Then, since :g:`AE <0238.g1:/ A E />` exceeds :g:`AG <0238.g1:/ A G />` by :g:`GE <0238.g1:/ G E />`, and :g:`AG <0238.g1:/ A G />` is :eq:`equal <0238.g1:/ A G / = 0238.g1:/ C F /; 0238.g1:/ B G / = 0238.g1:/ D F />` to :g:`CF <0238.g1:/ C F />` and :g:`BG <0238.g1:/ B G />` to :g:`DF <0238.g1:/ D F />`,

:g:`AE <0238.g1:/ A E />` exceeds :g:`CF <0238.g1:/ C F />` by the same difference as that by which :g:`BE <0238.g1:/ B E />` exceeds :g:`DF <0238.g1:/ D F />`.
