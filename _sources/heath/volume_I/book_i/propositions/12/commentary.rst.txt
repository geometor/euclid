Commentary on Proposition I.12
==============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition I.12 <I.12>`.

.. folio:: 0285
   :page: 271

“This problem,” says :person:`Proclus <proclus>` (:cite:`p. 283, 7—10 <proclus:in-euclid:283.7-10>`), “was first investigated by :person:`Oenopides <oenopides>` [5th cent. B.C.], who thought it useful for astronomy. He however calls the perpendicular, in the archaic manner, (a line drawn)

.. _folio-i-0286:

.. folio:: 0286
   :page: 272

gnomon-wise (κατὰ γνώμονα), because the *gnomon* is also at right angles to the horizon.” In this earlier sense the *gnomon* was a staff placed in a vertical position for the purpose of casting shadows and so serving as a means of measuring time (:person:`Cantor <cantor>`, *Geschichte der Mathematik*, I₃, p. 161). The later meanings of the word as used in Eucl. Book II. and elsewhere will be explained in the note on Book II. Def. 2.

:person:`Proclus <proclus>` says that two kinds of perpendicular were distinguished, the “plane” (ἐπίπεδος) and the “solid” (στερεά), the former being the perpendicular dropped on a line in a plane and the latter the perpendicular dropped on a plane. The term “solid perpendicular” is sufficiently curious, but it may perhaps be compared with the Greek term “solid locus” applied to a conic section, apparently on the ground that it has its origin in the section of a solid, namely a cone.

Attention is called by most editors to the assumption in this proposition that, if only :g:`D <0285.g1:D>` be taken on the side of :g:`AB <0285.g1:[ A B ]>` remote from :g:`C <0285.g1:C>`, the circle described with :g:`CD <0285.g1:/ C D />` as radius must necessarily cut :g:`AB <0285.g1:[ A B ]>` in two points. To satisfy us of this we need, as in I. 1, some postulate of continuity, e.g. something like that suggested by :person:`Killing <killing>` (see note on the Principle of Continuity above, p. 235):
“If a point [here the point describing the circle] moves in a figure which is divided into two parts [by the straight line], and if it belongs at the beginning of the motion to one part and at another stage of the motion to the other part, it must during the motion cut the boundary between the two parts,” and this of course applies to the motion in *two* directions from :g:`D <0285.g1:D>`.

But the editors have not, as a rule, noticed a possible objection to the Euclidean statement of this problem which is much more difficult to dispose of at this stage, i.e. without employing any proposition later than this in Euclid’s order. How do we know, says the supposed critic, that the circle does not cut :g:`AB <0285.g1:[ A B ]>` in *three* or more points, in which case there would be not one perpendicular but *three* or more? :person:`Proclus <proclus>` (:cite:`pp. 286, 12—289, 6 <proclus:in-euclid:286.12-289.6>`) tries to refute this objection, and it is interesting to follow his argument, though it will easily be seen to be inconclusive. He takes in order three possible suppositions.

1. May not the circle meet :g:`AB <0286.g1:[ A B ]>` in a third point :g:`K <0286.g1:K>` between the middle point of :g:`GE <0286.g1:/ G E />` and either extremity of it, taking the form drawn in the figure appended?

.. picture:: 0286.g1.svg
   :alt: Proclus Refutation of Objections to I. 12 Case 1

Suppose this possible. Bisect :g:`GE <0286.g1:/ G E />` in :g:`H <0286.g1:H>`. Join :g:`CH <0286.g1:/ C H />`, and produce it to meet the circle in :g:`L <0286.g1:L>`. Join :g:`CG <0286.g1:/ C G />`, :g:`CK <0286.g1:/ C K />`, :g:`CE <0286.g1:/ C E />`.

Then, since :g:`CG <0286.g1:/ C G />` is equal to :g:`CE <0286.g1:/ C E />`, and :g:`CH <0286.g1:/ C H />` is common, while the base :g:`GH <0286.g1:/ G H />` is equal to the base :g:`HE <0286.g1:/ H E />`, the angles :g:`CHG <0286.g1:< C H G )>`, :g:`CHE <0286.g1:< C H E )>` are equal and, since they are adjacent, they are both right.

Again, since :g:`CG <0286.g1:/ C G />` is equal to :g:`CE <0286.g1:/ C E />`, the angles at :g:`G <0286.g1:G>` and :g:`E <0286.g1:E>` are equal.

Lastly, since :g:`CK <0286.g1:/ C K />` is equal to :g:`CG <0286.g1:/ C G />` and also to :g:`CE <0286.g1:/ C E />`, the angles :g:`CGK <0286.g1:< C G K )>`, :g:`CKG <0286.g1:< C K G )>` are equal, as also are the angles :g:`CKE <0286.g1:< C K E )>`, :g:`CEK <0286.g1:< C E K )>`.

Since the angles :g:`CGK <0286.g1:< C G K )>`, :g:`CEK <0286.g1:< C E K )>` are equal, it follows that the angles :g:`CKG <0286.g1:< C K G )>`, :g:`CKE <0286.g1:< C K E )>` are equal and therefore both right.

Therefore the angle :g:`CKH <0286.g1:< C K H )>` is equal to the angle :g:`CHK <0286.g1:< C H K )>`, and :g:`CH <0286.g1:/ C H />` is equal to :g:`CK <0286.g1:/ C K />`.

.. _folio-i-0287:

.. folio:: 0287
   :page: 273

But :g:`CK <0286.g1:/ C K />` is equal to :g:`CL <0286.g1:/ C L />`, by the definition of the circle; therefore :g:`CH <0286.g1:/ C H />` is equal to :g:`CL <0286.g1:/ C L />`: which is impossible.

Thus :person:`Proclus <proclus>`; but why should not the circle meet :g:`AB <0286.g1:[ A B ]>` in :g:`H <0286.g1:H>` as well as :g:`K <0286.g1:K>`?

2. May not the circle meet :g:`AB <0287.g1:[ A B ]>` in :g:`H <0287.g1:H>` the middle point of :g:`GE <0287.g1:/ G E />` and take the form shown in the second figure?

.. picture:: 0287.g1.svg
   :alt: Proclus Refutation of Objections to I. 12 Case 2

In that case, says :person:`Proclus <proclus>`, join :g:`CG <0287.g1:/ C G />`, :g:`CH <0287.g1:/ C H />`, :g:`CE <0287.g1:/ C E />` as before. Then bisect :g:`HE <0287.g1:/ H E />` at :g:`K <0287.g1:K>`, join :g:`CK <0287.g1:/ C K />` and produce it to meet the circumference at :g:`L <0287.g1:L>`.

Now, since :g:`HK <0287.g1:/ H K />` is equal to :g:`KE <0287.g1:/ K E />`, :g:`CK <0287.g1:/ C K />` is common, and the base :g:`CH <0287.g1:/ C H />` is equal to the base :g:`CE <0287.g1:/ C E />`, the angles at :g:`K <0287.g1:K>` are equal and therefore both right angles.

Therefore the angle :g:`CHK <0287.g1:< C H K )>` is equal to the angle :g:`CKH <0287.g1:< C K H )>`, whence :g:`CK <0287.g1:/ C K />` is equal to :g:`CH <0287.g1:/ C H />` and therefore to :g:`CL <0287.g1:/ C L />`: which is impossible.

So :person:`Proclus <proclus>`; but why should not the circle meet :g:`AB <0287.g1:[ A B ]>` in :g:`K <0287.g1:K>` as well as :g:`H <0287.g1:H>`?

3. May not the circle meet :g:`AB <0287.g2:[ A B ]>` in two points besides :g:`G <0287.g2:G>`, :g:`E <0287.g2:E>` and pass, between those two points, to the side of :g:`AB <0287.g2:[ A B ]>` towards :g:`C <0287.g2:C>`, as in the next figure?

.. picture:: 0287.g2.svg
   :alt: Proclus Refutation of Objections to I. 12 Case 3

Here again, by the same method, :person:`Proclus <proclus>` proves that, :g:`K <0287.g2:K>`, :g:`L <0287.g2:L>` being the other two points in which the circle cuts :g:`AB <0287.g2:[ A B ]>`, :g:`CK <0287.g2:/ C K />` is equal to :g:`CH <0287.g2:/ C H />`, and, since the circle cuts :g:`CH <0287.g2:/ C H />` in :g:`M <0287.g2:M>`, :g:`CM <0287.g2:/ C M />` is equal to :g:`CK <0287.g2:/ C K />` and therefore to :g:`CH <0287.g2:/ C H />`: which is impossible.

But, again, why should the circle not cut :g:`AB <0287.g2:[ A B ]>` in the point :g:`H <0287.g2:H>` as well?

In fact, :person:`Proclus <proclus>`’ cases are not mutually exclusive, and his method of proof only enables us to show that, if the circle meets :g:`AB <0287.g2:[ A B ]>` in one more point besides :g:`G <0287.g2:G>`, :g:`E <0287.g2:E>`, it must meet it in more points still. We can always find a new point of intersection by bisecting the distance separating any two points of intersection, and so, applying the method *ad infinitum*, we should have to conclude ultimately that the circle with radius :g:`CH <0287.g2:/ C H />` (or :g:`CG <0287.g2:/ C G />`) coincides with :g:`AB <0287.g2:[ A B ]>`. It would follow that a circle with centre :g:`C <0287.g2:C>` and radius greater than :g:`CH <0287.g2:/ C H />` would not meet :g:`AB <0287.g2:[ A B ]>` at all. Also, since all straight lines from :g:`C <0287.g2:C>` to points on :g:`AB <0287.g2:[ A B ]>` would be equal in length, there would be an infinite number of perpendiculars from :g:`C <0287.g2:C>` on :g:`AB <0287.g2:[ A B ]>`.

Is this under any circumstances possible? It is not possible in Euclidean space, but it is possible, under the :person:`Riemann <riemann>` hypothesis (where a straight line is a “closed series” and returns on itself), in the case where :g:`C <0287.g2:C>` is the pole of the straight line :g:`AB <0287.g2:[ A B ]>`.

It is natural therefore that, for a proof that in Euclidean space there is only one perpendicular from a point to a straight line, we have to wait until I. 16, the precise proposition which under the :person:`Riemann <riemann>` hypothesis is only valid with a certain restriction and not universally. There is no difficulty involved by waiting until I. 16, since I. 12 is not used before that proposition is reached; and we are only in the same position as when, in order to satisfy ourselves of the number of possible solutions of I. 1, we have to wait till I. 7.

But if we wish, after all, to prove the truth of the assumption *without recourse to any later proposition than* I. 12, we can do so by means of this same invaluable I. 7.

If the circle intersects :g:`AB <0288.g1:[ A B ]>` as before in :g:`G <0288.g1:G>`, :g:`E <0288.g1:E>`, let :g:`H <0288.g1:H>` be the middle point of :g:`GE <0288.g1:/ G E />`, and suppose, if possible, that the circle also intersects :g:`AB <0288.g1:[ A B ]>` in any other point :g:`K <0288.g1:K>` on :g:`AH <0288.g1:/ A H />`.

From :g:`H <0288.g1:H>`, on the side of :g:`AB <0288.g1:[ A B ]>` opposite to :g:`C <0288.g1:C>`, draw :g:`HL <0288.g1:/ H L />` at right angles to :g:`AB <0288.g1:[ A B ]>`, and make :g:`HL <0288.g1:/ H L />` equal to :g:`HC <0288.g1:/ H C />`. Join :g:`CG <0288.g1:/ C G />`, :g:`LG <0288.g1:/ L G />`, :g:`CK <0288.g1:/ C K />`, :g:`LK <0288.g1:/ L K />`.

.. picture:: 0288.g1.svg
   :alt: Heath Reflection Diagram for I. 12 via I. 7

Now, in the triangles :g:`CHG <0288.g1:< C H G >>`, :g:`LHG <0288.g1:< L H G >>`, :g:`CH <0288.g1:/ C H />` is equal to :g:`LH <0288.g1:/ L H />`, and :g:`HG <0288.g1:/ H G />` is common. Also the angles :g:`CHG <0288.g1:< C H G )>`, :g:`LHG <0288.g1:< L H G )>`, being both right, are equal. Therefore the base :g:`CG <0288.g1:/ C G />` is equal to the base :g:`LG <0288.g1:/ L G />`. Similarly we prove that :g:`CK <0288.g1:/ C K />` is equal to :g:`LK <0288.g1:/ L K />`. But, by hypothesis, since :g:`K <0288.g1:K>` is on the circle, :g:`CK <0288.g1:/ C K />` is equal to :g:`CG <0288.g1:/ C G />`. Therefore :g:`CG <0288.g1:/ C G />`, :g:`CK <0288.g1:/ C K />`, :g:`LG <0288.g1:/ L G />`, :g:`LK <0288.g1:/ L K />` are all equal.

Now the next proposition, I. 13, will tell us that :g:`CH <0288.g1:/ C H />`, :g:`HL <0288.g1:/ H L />` are in a straight line; but we will not assume this. Join :g:`CL <0288.g1:/ C L />`. Then on the same base :g:`CL <0288.g1:/ C L />` and on the same side of it we have two pairs of straight lines drawn from :g:`C <0288.g1:C>`, :g:`L <0288.g1:L>` to :g:`G <0288.g1:G>` and :g:`K <0288.g1:K>` such that :g:`CG <0288.g1:/ C G />` is equal to :g:`CK <0288.g1:/ C K />` and :g:`LG <0288.g1:/ L G />` to :g:`LK <0288.g1:/ L K />`. But this is impossible [I. 7]. Therefore the circle cannot cut :g:`BA <0288.g1:/ B A />` or :g:`BA <0288.g1:[ B A ]>` produced in any point other than :g:`G <0288.g1:G>` on that side of :g:`CL <0288.g1:/ C L />` on which :g:`G <0288.g1:G>` is. Similarly it cannot cut :g:`AB <0288.g1:/ A B />` or :g:`AB <0288.g1:[ A B ]>` produced at any point other than :g:`E <0288.g1:E>` on the other side of :g:`CL <0288.g1:/ C L />`.

The only possibility left therefore is that the circle might cut :g:`AB <0288.g1:[ A B ]>` in the same point as that in which :g:`CL <0288.g1:/ C L />` cuts it. But this is shown to be impossible by an adaptation of the proof of I. 7.

For the assumption is that there may be some point :g:`M <0288.g2:M>` on :g:`CL <0288.g2:/ C L />` such that :g:`CM <0288.g2:/ C M />` is equal to :g:`CG <0288.g2:/ C G />` and :g:`LM <0288.g2:/ L M />` to :g:`LG <0288.g2:/ L G />`. If possible, let this be the case, and produce :g:`CG <0288.g2:/ C G />` to :g:`N <0288.g2:N>`.

.. picture:: 0288.g2.svg
   :alt: Heath Diagram for Point M on CL

Then, since :g:`CM <0288.g2:/ C M />` is equal to :g:`CG <0288.g2:/ C G />`, the angle :g:`NGM <0288.g2:< N G M )>` is equal to the angle :g:`GML <0288.g2:< G M L )>` [I. 5, part 2]. Therefore the angle :g:`GML <0288.g2:< G M L )>` is greater than the angle :g:`MGL <0288.g2:< M G L )>`. Again, since :g:`LG <0288.g2:/ L G />` is equal to :g:`LM <0288.g2:/ L M />`, the angle :g:`GML <0288.g2:< G M L )>` is equal to the angle :g:`MGL <0288.g2:< M G L )>`. But it was also greater: which is impossible. Hence the circle in the original figure cannot cut :g:`AB <0288.g1:[ A B ]>` in the point in which :g:`CL <0288.g1:/ C L />` cuts it. Therefore the circle cannot cut :g:`AB <0288.g1:[ A B ]>` in any point whatever except :g:`G <0288.g1:G>` and :g:`E <0288.g1:E>`.

[This proof of course does not prove that :g:`CK <0288.g1:/ C K />` is less than :g:`CG <0288.g1:/ C G />`, but only that it is not equal to it. The proposition that, of the obliques drawn from :g:`C <0288.g1:C>` to :g:`AB <0288.g1:[ A B ]>`, that is less the foot of which is nearer to :g:`H <0288.g1:H>` can only be proved later. The proof by I. 7 also fails, under the :person:`Riemann <riemann>` hypothesis, if :g:`C <0288.g1:C>`, :g:`L <0288.g1:L>` are the poles of the straight line :g:`AB <0288.g1:[ A B ]>`, since the broken lines :g:`CGL <0288.g1:/ C G L />`, :g:`CKL <0288.g1:/ C K L />` etc. become equal straight lines, all perpendicular to :g:`AB <0288.g1:[ A B ]>`.]

:person:`Proclus <proclus>` rightly adds (p. 289, 18 sqq.) that it is not necessary to take :g:`D <0285.g1:D>` on the side of :g:`AB <0285.g1:[ A B ]>` away from :g:`C <0285.g1:C>` if an objector “says that there is no space on
that side.” If it is not desired to trespass on that side of :g:`AB <0285.g1:[ A B ]>`, we can take :g:`D <0285.g1:D>` anywhere on :g:`AB <0285.g1:[ A B ]>` and describe the arc of a circle between :g:`D <0285.g1:D>` and the point where it meets :g:`AB <0285.g1:[ A B ]>` again, drawing the arc on the side of :g:`AB <0285.g1:[ A B ]>` on which :g:`C <0285.g1:C>` is. If it should happen that the selected point :g:`D <0285.g1:D>` is such that the circle only meets :g:`AB <0285.g1:[ A B ]>` in *one* point (:g:`D <0285.g1:D>` itself), we have only to describe the circle with :g:`CD <0285.g1:/ C D />` as radius, then, if :g:`E <0285.g1:E>` be a point on this circle, take :g:`F <0285.g1:F>` a point further from :g:`C <0285.g1:C>` than :g:`E <0285.g1:E>` is, and describe with :g:`CF <0285.g1:/ C F />` as radius the circular arc meeting :g:`AB <0285.g1:[ A B ]>` in two points.
