Commentary on Proposition I.24
==============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition I.24 <I.24>`.

.. folio:: 0311
   :page: 297

:person:`Proclus <proclus>` of course gives the other two cases which arise if we do not first provide that :g:`$DE$ <0310.g2:/ D E />` is not greater than :g:`$DF$ <0310.g2:/ D F />`.

(1) In the first case :g:`$G$ <0311.g1:G>` may fall on :g:`$EF$ <0311.g1:[ E F ]>` produced, and it is then obvious that :g:`$EG$ <0311.g1:/ E G />` is greater than :g:`$EF$ <0311.g1:/ E F />`.

.. picture:: 0311.g1.svg
   :alt: Folio 0311 Figure 1 - Proclus Case 1 for Proposition 24 (G on EF produced)

(2) In the second case :g:`$EG$ <0311.g2:/ E G />` may fall below :g:`$EF$ <0311.g2:/ E F />`.

If so, by I. 21, :g:`$DF$ <0311.g2:/ D F />`, :g:`$FE$ <0311.g2:/ F E />` are together less than :g:`$DG$ <0311.g2:/ D G />`, :g:`$GE$ <0311.g2:/ G E />`. But :g:`$DF$ <0311.g2:/ D F />` is equal to :g:`$DG$ <0311.g2:/ D G />`; therefore :g:`$EF$ <0311.g2:/ E F />` is less than :g:`$EG$ <0311.g2:/ E G />`, i.e. than :g:`$BC$ <0311.g2:/ B C />`.

.. picture:: 0311.g2.svg
   :alt: Folio 0311 Figure 2 - Proclus Case 2 for Proposition 24 (EG below EF)

These two cases are therefore decidedly simpler than the case taken by :person:`Euclid <euclid>` as typical, and could well be left to the ingenuity of the learner.

If however after all we prefer to insert Simson's words and avoid the latter
two cases, the proof is not complete unless we show that, with his assumption, :g:`$F$ <0310.g2:F>` must, in the figure of the proposition, fall below :g:`$EG$ <0310.g2:/ E G />`.

.. _folio-i-0312:

.. folio:: 0312
   :page: 298

:person:`De Morgan <de-morgan>` would make the following proposition precede: *Every straight line drawn from the vertex of a triangle to the base is less than the greater of the two sides, or than either if they are equal*, and he would prove it by means of the proposition relating to perpendicular and obliques given above, p. 291.

But it is easy to prove directly that :g:`$F$ <0312.g1:F>` falls below :g:`$EG$ <0312.g1:/ E G />`, if :g:`$DE$ <0312.g1:/ D E />` is not greater than :g:`$DG$ <0312.g1:/ D G />`, by the method employed by Pfleiderer, Lardner, and :person:`Todhunter <todhunter>`.

.. picture:: 0312.g1.svg
   :alt: Folio 0312 Figure 1 - Proof that F falls below EG (Pfleiderer, Lardner, Todhunter)

Let :g:`$DF$ <0312.g1:[ D F ]>`, produced if necessary, meet :g:`$EG$ <0312.g1:/ E G />` in :g:`$H$ <0312.g1:H>`. Then the angle :g:`$DHG$ <0312.g1:< D H G )>` is greater than the angle :g:`$DEG$ <0312.g1:< D E G )>`; [I. 16] and the angle :g:`$DEG$ <0312.g1:< D E G )>` is not less than the angle :g:`$DGE$ <0312.g1:< D G E )>`; [I. 18] therefore the angle :g:`$DHG$ <0312.g1:< D H G )>` is greater than the angle :g:`$DGH$ <0312.g1:< D G H )>`. Hence :g:`$DH$ <0312.g1:/ D H />` is less than :g:`$DG$ <0312.g1:/ D G />`, [I. 19] and therefore :g:`$DH$ <0312.g1:/ D H />` is less than :g:`$DF$ <0312.g1:/ D F />`.

**Alternative proof.**

Lastly, the modern alternative proof is worth giving.

.. picture:: 0312.g2.svg
   :alt: Folio 0312 Figure 2 - Modern Alternative Proof of Proposition 24

Let :g:`$DH$ <0312.g2:/ D H />` bisect the angle :g:`$FDG$ <0312.g2:< F D G )>` (after the triangle :g:`$DEG$ <0312.g2:< D E G >>` has been made equal in all respects to the triangle :g:`$ABC$ <0312.g2:< A B C >>`, as in the proposition), and let :g:`$DH$ <0312.g2:/ D H />` meet :g:`$EG$ <0312.g2:/ E G />` in :g:`$H$ <0312.g2:H>`. Join :g:`$HF$ <0312.g2:/ H F />`.

Then, in the triangles :g:`$FDH$ <0312.g2:< F D H >>`, :g:`$GDH$ <0312.g2:< G D H >>`, the two sides :g:`$FD$ <0312.g2:/ F D />`, :g:`$DH$ <0312.g2:/ D H />` are equal to the two sides :g:`$GD$ <0312.g2:/ G D />`, :g:`$DH$ <0312.g2:/ D H />`, and the included angles :g:`$FDH$ <0312.g2:< F D H )>`, :g:`$GDH$ <0312.g2:< G D H )>` are equal; therefore the base :g:`$HF$ <0312.g2:/ H F />` is equal to the base :g:`$HG$ <0312.g2:/ H G />`. Accordingly :g:`$EG$ <0312.g2:/ E G />` is equal to the sum of :g:`$EH$ <0312.g2:/ E H />`, :g:`$HF$ <0312.g2:/ H F />`; and :g:`$EH$ <0312.g2:/ E H />`, :g:`$HF$ <0312.g2:/ H F />` are together greater than :g:`$EF$ <0312.g2:/ E F />`; [I. 20] therefore :g:`$EG$ <0312.g2:/ E G />`, or :g:`$BC$ <0312.g2:/ B C />`, is greater than :g:`$EF$ <0312.g2:/ E F />`.

:person:`Proclus <proclus>` (:cite:`p. 339, 11 <proclus:in-euclid:339.11>` sqq.) answers by anticipation the possible question that might occur to any one on this proposition, viz. why does :person:`Euclid <euclid>` not compare the areas of the triangles as he does in I. 4? He observes that inequality of the areas does not follow from the inequality of the angles contained by the equal sides, and that :person:`Euclid <euclid>` leaves out all reference to the question both for this reason and because the areas cannot be compared without the help of the theory of parallels. “But if,” says :person:`Proclus <proclus>`, “we must anticipate what is to come and make our comparison of the areas at once, we assert that (1) *if the angles* :g:`$A$ <0310.g2:< B A C )>`, :g:`$D$ <0310.g2:< E D F )>`—*supposing that our argument proceeds with reference to the figure in the proposition—are* (together) *equal to two right angles, the triangles*
*are proved equal, (2) if greater than two right angles, that triangle which has the greater angle is less, and (3) if they are less, greater.”* :person:`Proclus <proclus>` then gives the proof, but without any reference to the source from which he quoted the proposition. Now :person:`an-Nairīzī <an-nairizi>` adds a similar proposition to I. 38, but definitely attributes it to :person:`Heron <heron>`. I shall accordingly give it in the place where :person:`Heron <heron>` put it
