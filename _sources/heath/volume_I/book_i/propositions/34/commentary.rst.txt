Commentary on Proposition I.34
==============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition I.34 <I.34>`.

.. _folio-i-0339:

.. folio:: 0339
   :page: 325

After specifying the particular kinds of parallelograms (squares and rhombi) in which the diagonals bisect the angles which they join, as well as the areas, and those (rectangles and rhomboids) in which the diagonals do not bisect the angles, :person:`Proclus <proclus>` proceeds (pp. 390 sqq.) to analyse this proposition with reference to the distinction in Aristotle’s *Anal. Post.* (I. 4, 5, 73 a 21–74 b 4) between attributes which are only predicable of every individual thing (κατὰ παντός) in a class and those which are true of it *primarily* (τούτου πρώτου) and *generally* (καθόλου). We are *apt*, says :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>`, to mistake a proof κατὰ παντός for a proof τούτου πρώτου καθόλου because it is either impossible to find a higher generality to comprehend all the particulars of which the predicate is true, or to find a name for it. (Part of this passage of :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>` has been quoted above in the note on I. 32, pp. 319–320.)

Now, says :person:`Proclus <proclus>`, adapting Aristotle’s distinction to *theorems*, the present proposition exhibits the distinction between theorems which are *general* and theorems which are *not general*. According to :person:`Proclus <proclus>`, the first part of the proposition stating that the opposite sides and angles of a parallelogram are equal is *general* because the property is only true of parallelograms; but the second part which asserts that the diameter bisects the area is *not general* because it does not include all the figures of which this property is true, e.g. circles and ellipses. Indeed, says :person:`Proclus <proclus>`, the first attempts upon problems seem usually to have been of this partial character (μερικώτεραι), and generality was only attained by degrees. Thus “the ancients, after investigating the fact that the diameter bisects an ellipse, a circle, and a parallelogram respectively, proceeded to investigate what was common to these cases,” though “it is difficult to show what is common to an ellipse, a circle and a parallelogram.”

I doubt whether the supposed distinction between the two parts of the proposition, in point of “generality,” can be sustained. :person:`Proclus <proclus>` himself admits that it is presupposed that the subject of the proposition is a *quadrilateral*, because there are other figures (e.g. regular polygons of an even number of sides) besides parallelograms which have their opposite sides and angles equal; therefore the second part of the theorem is, in this respect, no more *general* than the other, and, if we are entitled to the tacit limitation of the theorem to quadrilaterals in one part, we are equally entitled to it in the other.

It would almost appear as though :person:`Proclus <proclus>` had drawn the distinction for the mere purpose of alluding to investigations by Greek geometers on the general subject of *diameters* of all sorts of figures; and it may have been these which brought the subject to the point at which :person:`Apollonius <apollonius>` could say in the first definitions at the beginning of his *Conics* that “In *any bent line*, such as is in one plane, I give the name *diameter* to any straight line which, being drawn from the bent line, bisects all the straight lines (chords) drawn in the line parallel to any straight line.” The term *bent line* (καμπύλη :term:`γραμμή <line>`) includes, e.g. in :person:`Archimedes <archimedes>`, not only curves, but any composite line made
up of straight lines and curves joined together in any manner. It is of course clear that either diagonal of a parallelogram bisects all lines drawn within the parallelogram parallel to the other diagonal.

An-Nairīzī gives after I. 31 a neat construction for dividing a straight line into any number of equal parts (ed. :person:`Curtze <curtze>`, p. 74, ed. Besthorn-Heiberg, pp. 141–3) which requires only one measurement repeated, together with the properties of parallel lines including I. 33, 34. As I. 33, 34 are assumed, I place the problem here. The particular case taken is the problem of dividing a straight line into *three* equal parts.

.. picture:: 0340.g1.svg
   :alt: Folio 0340 Figure 1 - An-Nairīzī's Construction for Dividing a Straight Line into Equal Parts

Let :g:`$AB$ <0340.g1:/ A B />` be the given straight line. Draw :g:`$AC$ <0340.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$BD$ <0340.g1:/ B D />` at right angles to it on opposite sides.

An-Nairīzī takes :g:`$AC$ <0340.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$BD$ <0340.g1:/ B D />` of the :eq:`same length <0340.g1:/ A C / = 0340.g1:/ B D />` and then :eq:`bisects <0340.g1:/ A E / = 0340.g1:/ E C /; 0340.g1:/ B F / = 0340.g1:/ F D />` :g:`$AC$ <0340.g1:/ A C />` at :g:`$E$ <0340.g1:E>` and :g:`$BD$ <0340.g1:/ B D />` at :g:`$F$ <0340.g1:F>`. But of course it is even simpler to measure :g:`$AE$ <0340.g1:/ A E />`, :g:`$EC$ <0340.g1:/ E C />` along one perpendicular :eq:`equal <0340.g1:/ A E / = 0340.g1:/ E C />` and of any length, and :g:`$BF$ <0340.g1:/ B F />`, :g:`$FD$ <0340.g1:/ F D />` along the other also :eq:`equal <0340.g1:/ B F / = 0340.g1:/ F D / = 0340.g1:/ A E / = 0340.g1:/ E C />` and of the same length.

Join :g:`$ED$ <0340.g1:/ E D />`, :g:`$CF$ <0340.g1:/ C F />` meeting :g:`$AB$ <0340.g1:/ A B />` in :g:`$G$ <0340.g1:G>`, :g:`$H$ <0340.g1:H>` respectively.

Then shall :g:`$AG$ <0340.g1:/ A G />`, :g:`$GH$ <0340.g1:/ G H />`, :g:`$HB$ <0340.g1:/ H B />` all be :eq:`equal <0340.g1:/ A G / = 0340.g1:/ G H / = 0340.g1:/ H B />`.

Draw :g:`$HK$ <0340.g1:/ H K />` parallel to :g:`$AC$ <0340.g1:/ A C />`, or at right angles to :g:`$AB$ <0340.g1:/ A B />`.

Since now :g:`$EC$ <0340.g1:/ E C />`, :g:`$FD$ <0340.g1:/ F D />` are :eq:`equal <0340.g1:/ E C / = 0340.g1:/ F D />` and parallel, :g:`$ED$ <0340.g1:/ E D />`, :g:`$CF$ <0340.g1:/ C F />` are :eq:`equal <0340.g1:/ E D / = 0340.g1:/ C F />` and parallel. [I. 33] And :g:`$HK$ <0340.g1:/ H K />` was drawn parallel to :g:`$AC$ <0340.g1:/ A C />`. Therefore :g:`$ECHK$ <0340.g1:< E C H K >>` is a parallelogram; whence :g:`$KH$ <0340.g1:/ K H />` is :eq:`equal <0340.g1:/ K H / = 0340.g1:/ E C / = 0340.g1:/ E A />` as well as parallel to :g:`$EC$ <0340.g1:/ E C />`, and therefore to :g:`$EA$ <0340.g1:/ E A />`.

The triangles :g:`$EAG$ <0340.g1:< E A G >>`, :g:`$KHG$ <0340.g1:< K H G >>` have now two angles respectively equal and the sides :g:`$AE$ <0340.g1:/ A E />`, :g:`$HK$ <0340.g1:/ H K />` :eq:`equal <0340.g1:/ A E / = 0340.g1:/ H K />`. Thus the triangles are :eq:`equal <0340.g1:< E A G > = 0340.g1:< K H G >>` in all respects, and :g:`$AG$ <0340.g1:/ A G />` is :eq:`equal <0340.g1:/ A G / = 0340.g1:/ G H />` to :g:`$GH$ <0340.g1:/ G H />`.

Similarly the triangles :g:`$KHG$ <0340.g1:< K H G >>`, :g:`$FBH$ <0340.g1:< F B H >>` are :eq:`equal <0340.g1:< K H G > = 0340.g1:< F B H >>` in all respects, and :g:`$GH$ <0340.g1:/ G H />` is :eq:`equal <0340.g1:/ G H / = 0340.g1:/ H B />` to :g:`$HB$ <0340.g1:/ H B />`.

If now we wish to extend the problem to the case where :g:`$AB$ <0340.g1:/ A B />` is to be divided into $n$ parts, we have only to measure ($n - 1$) successive equal lengths along :g:`$AC$ <0340.g1:/ A C />` and ($n - 1$) successive lengths, each equal to the others, along :g:`$BD$ <0340.g1:/ B D />`. Then join the first point arrived at on :g:`$AC$ <0340.g1:/ A C />` to the last point on :g:`$BD$ <0340.g1:/ B D />`, the second on :g:`$AC$ <0340.g1:/ A C />` to the last but one on :g:`$BD$ <0340.g1:/ B D />`, and so on; and the joining lines cut :g:`$AB$ <0340.g1:/ A B />` in points dividing it into $n$ equal parts.
