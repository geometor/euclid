Commentary on Proposition II.6
==============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition II.6 <II.6>`.

.. folio:: 0400
   :page: 386

In this case the rectangle :g:`$AD$ <0400.g1:/ A D />`, :g:`$DB$ <0400.g1:/ D B />` is "a rectangle applied to a given straight line (:g:`$AB$ <0400.g1:/ A B />`) but *exceeding* by a square (the side of which is equal to :g:`$BD$ <0400.g1:/ B D />`)"; and the problem suggested by II. 6 is to find a rectangle of this description equal to a given area, which we will, for convenience, suppose to be a square; i.e., in the language of geometry, *to apply to a given straight line a rectangle which shall be equal to a given square and shall exceed by a square figure*.

We suppose that in Euclid's figure $AB = a$, $BD = x$; then, if the given square be $b^2$, the problem is to solve geometrically the equation
\[
ax + x^2 = b^2.
\]

The solution of a problem theoretically equivalent to the solution of a quadratic equation of this kind is presupposed in the fragment of Hippocrates' *Quadrature of lunes* preserved in a quotation by :person:`Simplicius <simplicius>` (*Comment. in*
*Aristot. Phys.* pp. 61—68, ed. Diels) from Eudemus' *History of Geometry*. In this fragment :person:`Hippocrates <hippocrates-of-chios>` (5th cent. B.C.) assumes the following construction.

.. _folio-i-0401:

.. folio:: 0401
   :page: 387

:g:`$AB$ <0401.g1:/ A B />` being the diameter and :g:`$O$ <0401.g1:O>` the centre of a semicircle, and :g:`$C$ <0401.g1:C>` being the middle point of :g:`$OB$ <0401.g1:/ O B />` and :g:`$CD$ <0401.g1:/ C D />` at right angles to :g:`$AB$ <0401.g1:/ A B />`, a straight line of length such that its square is $1\frac{1}{2}$ times the square on the radius (i.e. of length $a\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}$, where $a$ is the radius) is to be so placed, as :g:`$EF$ <0401.g1:/ E F />`, between :g:`$CD$ <0401.g1:/ C D />` and the circumference :g:`$AD$ <0401.g1:( O A )>` that it "verges towards :g:`$B$ <0401.g1:B>`," that is, :g:`$EF$ <0401.g1:/ E F />` when produced passes through :g:`$B$ <0401.g1:B>`.

.. picture:: 0401.g1.svg
   :alt: Diagram

Now the right-angled triangles :g:`$BFC$ <0401.g1:< B F C >>`, :g:`$BAE$ <0401.g1:< B A E >>` are similar, so that

$$BF : BC = BA : BE,$$

and therefore the rectangle $BE, BF = \text{rect. } BA, BC$

$$= \text{sq. on } BO.$$

In other words, :g:`$EF$ <0401.g1:/ E F />` ($= a\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}$) being given in length, :g:`$BF$ <0401.g1:/ B F />` ($= x$, say) has to be found such that

$$\left(\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}a + x\right)x = a^2;$$

or the quadratic equation

$$\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}ax + x^2 = a^2$$

has to be solved.

A straight line of length $a\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}$ would easily be constructed, for, in the figure, $CD^2 = AC \cdot CB = \frac{3}{4}a^2$, or $CD = \frac{1}{2}a\sqrt{3}$, and $a\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}$ is the diagonal of a square of which :g:`$CD$ <0401.g1:/ C D />` is the side.

There is no doubt that :person:`Hippocrates <hippocrates-of-chios>` could have solved the equation by the geometrical construction given below, but he may have contemplated, on this occasion, the merely *mechanical* process of *placing* the straight line of the length required between :g:`$CD$ <0401.g1:/ C D />` and the circumference :g:`$AD$ <0401.g1:( O A )>` and *moving* it until :g:`$E$ <0401.g1:E>`, :g:`$F$ <0401.g1:F>`, :g:`$B$ <0401.g1:B>` were in a straight line. :person:`Zeuthen <zeuthen>` (:cite:`*Die Lehre von den Kegelschnitten im Altertum* <zeuthen:kegelschnitte>`, pp. 270, 271) thinks this probable because, curiously enough, the fragment speaks immediately afterwards of "joining :g:`$B$ <0401.g1:B>` to :g:`$F$ <0401.g1:F>`."

To solve the equation

$$ax + x^2 = b^2$$

we have to find the rectangle :g:`$AH$ <0401.g2:< A H >>`, or the gnomon :g:`$NOP$ <0401.g2:< N O P >>`, which is equal in area to $b^2$ and has one of the sides containing the inner right angle equal to :g:`$CB$ <0401.g2:/ C B />` or $\frac{1}{2}a$. Thus we know $\left(\frac{1}{2}a\right)^2$ and $b^2$, and we have to find, by I. 47, a square equal to the sum of two given squares.

.. picture:: 0401.g2.svg
   :alt: Diagram

To do this :person:`Simson <simson>` draws :g:`$BQ$ <0401.g2:/ B Q />` at right angles to :g:`$AB$ <0401.g2:/ A B />` and equal to $b$, joins :g:`$CQ$ <0401.g2:/ C Q />` and, with centre :g:`$C$ <0401.g2:C>` and radius :g:`$CQ$ <0401.g2:/ C Q />`, describes a circle cutting :g:`$AB$ <0401.g2:/ A B />` produced in :g:`$D$ <0401.g2:D>`. Thus :g:`$BD$ <0401.g2:/ B D />`, or $x$, is found.

Now the rectangle :g:`$AD$ <0401.g2:/ A D />`, :g:`$DB$ <0401.g2:/ D B />` together with the square on :g:`$CB$ <0401.g2:/ C B />`
is equal to the square on :g:`$CD$ <0401.g2:/ C D />`,
i.e. to the square on :g:`$CQ$ <0401.g2:/ C Q />`,
i.e. to the squares on :g:`$CB$ <0401.g2:/ C B />`, :g:`$BQ$ <0401.g2:/ B Q />`.

.. _folio-i-0402:

.. folio:: 0402
   :page: 388

Therefore the rectangle :g:`$AD$ <0401.g2:/ A D />`, :g:`$DB$ <0401.g2:/ D B />` is equal to the square on :g:`$BQ$ <0401.g2:/ B Q />`, that is,
\[
ax + x^2 = b^2.
\]
From Euclid's point of view there would only be one solution in this case.

This proposition enables us also to solve the equation
\[
x^2 - ax = b^2
\]
in a similar manner.

We have only to suppose that $AB = a$, and :g:`$AD$ <0401.g2:/ A D />` (instead of :g:`$BD$ <0401.g2:/ B D />`) $= x$; then
\[
x^2 - ax = \text{the gnomon.}
\]
To find the gnomon we have its area ($b^2$) and the area, $CB^2$ or $\left(\frac{1}{2}a\right)^2$, by which the gnomon differs from $CD^2$. Thus we can find :g:`$D$ <0401.g2:D>` (and therefore :g:`$AD$ <0401.g2:/ A D />` or $x$) by the same construction as that just given.

Converse propositions to II. 5, 6 are given by :person:`Pappus <pappus>` (VII. pp. 948—950) among his lemmas to the *Conics* of :person:`Apollonius <apollonius>` to the effect that,

1. if :g:`$D$ <0400.g1:D>` be a point dividing :g:`$AB$ <0400.g1:/ A B />` unequally, and :g:`$C$ <0400.g1:C>` another point on :g:`$AB$ <0400.g1:/ A B />` such that the rectangle :g:`$AD$ <0400.g1:/ A D />`, :g:`$DB$ <0400.g1:/ D B />` together with the square on :g:`$CD$ <0400.g1:/ C D />` is equal to the square on :g:`$AC$ <0400.g1:/ A C />`, then

\[
AC \text{ is equal to } CB;
\]

2. if :g:`$D$ <0400.g1:D>` be a point on :g:`$AB$ <0400.g1:/ A B />` produced, and :g:`$C$ <0400.g1:C>` a point on :g:`$AB$ <0400.g1:/ A B />` such that the rectangle :g:`$AD$ <0400.g1:/ A D />`, :g:`$DB$ <0400.g1:/ D B />` together with the square on :g:`$CB$ <0400.g1:/ C B />` is equal to the square on :g:`$CD$ <0400.g1:/ C D />`, then

\[
AC \text{ is equal to } CB.
\]
