# Chapter VII Summary and Outline: Euclid in Arabia

## Executive Summary

Sir Thomas L. Heath provides a comprehensive examination of the Arabic transmission of Euclid's *Elements*, detailing the historic translation movement under the Abbasid Caliphate, the surviving Arabic and Hebrew manuscripts, the text-critical debate between Martin Klamroth and J. L. Heiberg regarding the authority of the Arabic tradition, and an exhaustive biographical-bibliographical catalogue of 31 Arabian mathematicians and commentators who cultivated and expanded Euclidean geometry.

### 1. The Translation Movements and Recensions
The assimilation of Euclid into Arabic occurred in successive waves:
1. **The al-Ḥajjāj Versions**:
   - **al-Ḥajjāj b. Yūsuf b. Maṭar** made the earliest Arabic translations from Greek manuscripts obtained from Byzantium by the Caliphs al-Manṣūr (754–775), Hārūn ar-Rashīd (786–809), and al-Ma'mūn (813–833).
   - First version: *al-Hārūnī* (composed for the vizier Yaḥyā b. Khālid b. Barmak; lost).
   - Second version: *al-Ma'mūnī* (composed for al-Ma'mūn; preserved in Books I–VI in **Codex Leidensis 399, 1**, ed. Besthorn and Heiberg). Characterized by didactic simplification, stylistic expansion, repetitions of constructions for separate cases, and numerical illustrations.
2. **The Isḥāq-Thābit Version**:
   - **Isḥāq b. Ḥunain** (d. 910), son of the legendary translator Ḥunain b. Isḥāq, produced a highly literal translation direct from the Greek.
   - **Thābit b. Qurra** (836–901) collated and improved Isḥāq's translation against superior Greek manuscripts, creating the standard critical recension.
   - Surviving witnesses: **Bodleian MS. 279** (**O**, dated 1238), **Bodleian MS. 280** (dated 1260–1), and **Copenhagen MS. LXXXI** (**K**, 13th c.). Characterized by extreme precision, technical consistency (e.g. distinguishing between ἐφαρμόζειν and ἐφαρμόζεσθαι), and preservation of Euclid's formal structure. Non-Euclidean Books XIV and XV were translated by **Qusṭā b. Lūqā**.
3. **The Recension of Naṣīraddīn aṭ-Ṭūsī (1201–1274)**:
   - Composed at the Marāġa observatory; represents a rewritten mathematical paraphrase (*Taḥrīr*) rather than a translation.
   - Issued in two forms: larger edition (printed in Rome in Arabic, 1594) and smaller edition (printed at Constantinople in 1801 and Calcutta in 1824).
4. **Western Transmission**:
   - The Arabic text was the fountainhead of medieval European geometry. The Latin versions of **Athelhard of Bath** (c. 1120) and **Campanus of Novara** (13th c., editio princeps by Erhard Ratdolt at Venice, 1482) descend directly from Arabic sources, retaining Arabic technical vocabulary and idiosyncratic structural variations (such as 34 propositions in Book V).

### 2. The Text-Critical Debate: Klamroth vs. Heiberg
- **Martin Klamroth's Thesis (1881)**:
  - Noted that the Arabic tradition (9th c.) is historically older than the earliest extant Byzantine manuscripts (**B** from 888 A.D., **P** from 10th c.).
  - Because the Arabic manuscripts omit numerous definitions, porisms, lemmas, and alternative proofs found in the Greek MSS., Klamroth argued that the Arabic text reflects a purer, ante-Theonine archetype free from Greek corruptions.
- **J. L. Heiberg's Refutation (1884)**:
  - Heiberg demonstrated that the omissions in the Arabic versions were the result of conscious pedagogical condensation and editorial tampering by Arabic translators and teachers, not an uncorrupted Greek prototype.
  - Ancient Greek external testimonies (**Pappus**, **Proclus**, **Simplicius**, **Eutocius**) and early fragments (the 7th–8th c. British Museum palimpsest **L**) prove that the disputed definitions, lemmas, and propositions existed in Greek centuries before the Arabic translations were undertaken.
  - Furthermore, Arabic copyists frequently shortened proofs, dropped redundant cases, omitted difficult stereo-metric material (Books XI–XIII), and replaced abstract proofs with numerical examples.

### 3. Biographical Catalogue of 31 Arabian Commentators
Heath details the scholarly output of 31 Arabian mathematicians and astronomers cited in Ibn an-Nadīm's *Kitāb al-Fihrist*, Ibn al-Qifṭī, Ibn abī Uṣaibi'a, Ḥāji Khalfa, and Heinrich Suter's bio-bibliographical collections, including major monographs on the Parallel Postulate and the foundations of Book I:
- **an-Nairīzī** (fl. 900): Commentary preserving Heron and Simplicius (ed. Besthorn-Heiberg; Latin tr. Gherard of Cremona ed. Curtze).
- **al-Māhānī** (d. c. 880): Treatises on Book V (proportion) and Book X (irrationals).
- **Abū 'l Wafā al-Būzjānī** (940–997): Geometric commentary, manual on geometric constructions (*A'māl handasiyya*), and algebra.
- **al-Kindī** ("Philosopher of the Arabs", d. c. 873): Treatises on Euclid's postulates, optics, and geometry.
- **Thābit b. Qurra** (836–901): Foundational tracts on the Parallel Postulate (*On the fact that two lines produced at angles less than two right angles meet*), division of figures, and composition of ratios.
- **Ibn al-Haitham (Alhazen, c. 965–1039)**: 10 major works on Euclid, including commentary on the definitions/postulates, solution of difficulties in Book I, analysis of propositions, and an independent treatise attempting to demonstrate the Parallel Postulate through kinematic considerations of equidistant lines.
- **Ibn Sīnā (Avicenna, 980–1037)**: Compendium of Euclid in the mathematical section of the *Kitāb ash-Shifā'*.
- **Naṣīraddīn aṭ-Ṭūsī** (1201–1274): Comprehensive edition of the *Elements* and influential treatise on the Parallel Postulate.

## Detailed Outline

### § 1. Early Caliphate Translations and Primary Versions (pp. 75–78)
- Missions of Caliphs al-Manṣūr and al-Ma'mūn to Byzantium for Greek mathematical codices.
- **al-Ḥajjāj b. Yūsuf b. Maṭar**:
  - The *Hārūnī* (first edition) under Hārūn ar-Rashīd and Yaḥyā b. Khālid b. Barmak.
  - The *Ma'mūnī* (second edition) in Leiden MS. 399, 1.
- **Isḥāq b. Ḥunain and Thābit b. Qurra**:
  - Direct translation from Greek by Isḥāq; systematic collation and revision by Thābit.
  - Testimonies from the Hebrew tradition (Moses b. Tibbon, Jakob b. Machir).
  - Surviving manuscripts: Bodleian 279 (O), Bodleian 280, Copenhagen LXXXI (K).
  - Translation of Books XIV–XV by Qusṭā b. Lūqā al-Ba'labakkī.
- **Naṣīraddīn aṭ-Ṭūsī (1201–1274)**:
  - The Marāġa recension; larger Rome edition (1594) and smaller editions.
  - Comparisons with Campanus of Novara (1482) and Athelhard of Bath (c. 1120).

### § 2. Comparative Analysis of Proposition Counts (pp. 79–80)
- Proposition concordance table across the Arabic tradition (Isḥāq, aṭ-Ṭūsī, Campanus) and Greek editions (Gregory, August, Heiberg).
- Discrepancies in Books III, V, VI, VII, VIII, IX, X, XI, XII, and XIII.
- Examination of Books XIV and XV proposition counts.

### § 3. Textual Criticisms: The Klamroth-Heiberg Controversy (pp. 80–85)
- **1. Propositions**: Order and omissions (I. 45, VI. 12, VIII. 24, 25, IX. 30, 31, X. 27, 28, XI. 38, XIII. 6).
- **2. Definitions**: Omission of Definitions 4, 7, 8 in Book I; Klamroth's theory vs. Heiberg's proofs from Heron, Proclus, and Simplicius.
- **3. Lemmas and Porisms**: Klamroth's claim of Arabic authenticity refuted by Heiberg; ancient external citations in Pappus, Eutocius, and British Museum palimpsest L.
- **4. Alternative Proofs**: Condensation of proofs by Arabic translators; comparison with the Bologna MS. **b**.
- **Stylistic Contrasts**:
  - al-Ḥajjāj: Expansive, repetitive, pedagogical, numerical demonstrations, distinct diagrams for obtuse/acute cases.
  - Thābit-Isḥāq: Severe, precise, literal fidelity to the Greek idiom.

### § 4. Catalogue of Arabian Commentators (pp. 85–90)
- Notices on 31 commentators from the *Fihrist*, Ibn al-Qifṭī, Ibn abī Uṣaibi'a, and Suter:
  - 1. **an-Nairīzī** (fl. 922): Leiden MS. 399, 1 and Gherard of Cremona Latin translation.
  - 2. **al-Karābīsī** (9th–10th c.): Distinguished geometer and arithmetician.
  - 3. **al-Jauharī** (fl. 830): Ma'mūn astronomer; additions to Book I.
  - 4. **al-Māhānī** (d. c. 880): On proportion (Book V) and irrationals (Book X).
  - 5. **al-Khāzin** (d. 961–971): Commentary on Book X; rearranged postulates.
  - 6. **Abū 'l Wafā al-Būzjānī** (940–997): Geometric constructions and Book X.
  - 7. **Ibn Rāhawaihi**: Commentary on Book X.
  - 8. **al-Anṭākī** (d. 987): Commentary on the *Elements*.
  - 9. **Sind b. 'Alī** (fl. 830): Commentary on Euclid and parallel postulate.
  - 10. **ar-Rāzī**: Solution of difficulties in Euclid.
  - 11. **al-Kindī** (d. c. 873): 10 geometrical tracts on Euclid's axioms, optics, and elements.
  - 12. **Naẓīf b. Yumn** (d. 990): Physician and translator of Book X.
  - 13. **Yūḥannā b. Yūsuf** (d. 943): On the rational and irrational.
  - 14. **al-Ḥasan b. 'Ubaidallāh** (d. 987): Commentary on the difficult parts.
  - 15. **Qusṭā b. Lūqā** (d. 912): Translator of Books XIV and XV.
  - 16. **Thābit b. Qurra** (836–901): On parallels, division of figures, proportion.
  - 17. **Sinān b. Thābit** (d. 943): Revision of geometrical elements.
  - 18. **al-Kūhī** (fl. 988): Additions to Euclid and Archimedes.
  - 19. **al-Fārābī** (870–950): Commentary on premises to Books I and V.
  - 20. **Ibn al-Haitham (Alhazen)** (c. 965–1039): Exhaustive 10-item bibliography on Euclid.
  - 21. **Ibn Sīnā (Avicenna)** (980–1037): *Kitāb ash-Shifā'*.
  - 22. **al-Ahwāzī**: Commentary on Book X.
  - 23. **Naṣīraddīn aṭ-Ṭūsī** (1201–1274): *Taḥrīr* of the *Elements*.
  - 24. **as-Samarqandī** (fl. 1276): *Ashkāl at-Ta'sīs* (Fundamental Theorems).
  - 25. **Qāḍīzāde ar-Rūmī** (fl. 1436): Commentary on as-Samarqandī.
  - 26. **Sulaimān b. 'Uqba**: Commentary on Book X.
  - 27. **Sa'īd b. Mas'ūd**: Glossator on the *Elements*.
  - 28. **al-Baġdādī al-Faraḍī** (d. 950): On irrational lines.
  - 29. **Ibn al-Lubūdī** (1211–1267): Compendium and commentary.
  - 30. **al-Jayyānī** (11th c.): Commentary on Book V.
  - 31. **Manṣūr b. 'Alī b. 'Irāq**: Teacher of al-Bīrūnī; additions to Book XIII.
