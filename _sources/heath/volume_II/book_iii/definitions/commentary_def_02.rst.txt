Commentary on Book III Definition 2
===================================

Back to :ref:`Book III Definitions <III.def>`.

Εὐθεῖα :term:`κύκλου <circle>` :term:`ἐφάπτεσθαι <ephaptesthai>` λέγεται, ἥτις ἁπτομένη τοῦ :term:`κύκλου <circle>` καὶ ἐκβαλλομένη οὐ τέμνει τὸν κύκλον.

Euclid's phraseology here shows the regular distinction between ἅπτεσθαι and its compound :term:`ἐφάπτεσθαι <ephaptesthai>`, the former meaning "to *meet*" and the latter "to *touch*." The distinction was generally observed by Greek geometers from Euclid onwards. There are however exceptions so far as ἅπτεσθαι is concerned; thus it means "to *touch*" in Eucl. IV. Def. 5 and sometimes in :person:`Archimedes <archimedes>`. On the other hand, :term:`ἐφάπτεσθαι <ephaptesthai>` is used by :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>` in certain
cases where the orthodox geometrical term would be ἅπτεσθαι. Thus in :cite:`*Meteorologica* III. 5 (376 b 9) <aristotle:meteorologica:iii-5-376-b-9>` he says a certain circle *will pass through all the angles* (ἁπασῶν ἐφάψεται τῶν γωνιῶν), and (376 a 6) :math:`\text{M}` *will lie on a given (circular) circumference* (δεδομένης :term:`περιφερείας <periphereia>` ἐφάψεται τὸ Μ). We shall find ἅπτεσθαι used in these senses in Book IV. Deff. 2, 6 and Deff. 1, 3 respectively. The latter of the two expressions quoted from :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>` means that *the locus of* :math:`\text{M}` *is a given circle*, just as in :person:`Pappus <pappus>` ἅψεται τὸ σημεῖον θέσει δεδομένης εὐθείας means that *the locus of* the point is a straight line given in position.
