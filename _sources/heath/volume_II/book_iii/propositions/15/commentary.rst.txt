Commentary on Proposition III.15
================================

Back to :ref:`Proposition III.15 <III.15>`.

.. folio:: 0043
   :page: 37

It will be observed that Euclid's proof differs from that given in our text-books (which is :person:`Simson <simson>`'s) in that Euclid introduces another line :g:`MN <0042.g1:/ M N />`, which is drawn so as to be equal to :g:`BC <0042.g1:/ B C />` but at right angles to :g:`EK <0042.g1:/ E K />` and therefore parallel to :g:`FG <0042.g1:/ F G />`. :person:`Simson <simson>` dispenses with :g:`MN <0042.g1:/ M N />` and bases his proof on a similar proof by Theodosius ($Sphaerica$ I. 6). He proves that the sum of the squares on :g:`EH <0042.g1:/ E H />`, :g:`HB <0042.g1:/ H B />` is equal to the sum of the squares on :g:`EK <0042.g1:/ E K />`, :g:`KF <0042.g1:/ K F />`; whence he infers that, since the square on :g:`EH <0042.g1:/ E H />` is less than the square on :g:`EK <0042.g1:/ E K />`, the square on :g:`BH <0042.g1:/ B H />` is greater than the square on :g:`FK <0042.g1:/ F K />`. It may be that Euclid would have regarded this as too complicated an inference to make without explanation or without an increase in the number of his axioms. But, on the other hand, Euclid himself assumes that the angle subtended at the centre by :g:`MN <0042.g1:/ M N />` is greater than the angle subtended by :g:`FG <0042.g1:/ F G />`, or, in other words, that :g:`M <0042.g1:M>`, :g:`N <0042.g1:N>` both fall outside the triangle :g:`FEG <0042.g1:< F E G >>`. This is a similar assumption to that made in III. 7, 8, as already noticed; and its truth is obvious because :g:`EM <0042.g1:/ E M />`, :g:`EN <0042.g1:/ E N />`, being radii of the circle, are greater than the distances from :g:`E <0042.g1:E>` to the points in which :g:`MN <0042.g1:/ M N />` cuts :g:`EF <0042.g1:/ E F />`, :g:`EG <0042.g1:/ E G />`, and therefore the latter points are nearer than :g:`M <0042.g1:M>`, :g:`N <0042.g1:N>` are to :g:`L <0042.g1:L>`, the foot of the perpendicular from :g:`E <0042.g1:E>` to :g:`MN <0042.g1:/ M N />`.

:person:`Simson <simson>` adds the converse of the proposition, proving it in the same way as he proves the proposition itself.
