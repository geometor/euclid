Commentary on Proposition III.21
================================

Back to :ref:`Proposition III.21 <III.21>`.

.. folio:: 0055
   :page: 49

Under the restriction that the "angle at the centre" used in III. 20 must be less than two right angles, Euclid's proof of this proposition only applies to the case of a segment greater than a semicircle, and the case of a segment equal to or less than a semicircle has to be considered separately. The simplest proof, of many, seems to be that of :person:`Simson <simson>`.

.. _folio-ii-0056:

.. folio:: 0056
   :page: 50

“But, if the segment :g:`BAED <0056.g1:< B A E D )>` be not greater than a semicircle, let :g:`BAD <0056.g1:< B A D )>`, :g:`BED <0056.g1:< B E D )>` be angles in it: these also are equal to one another.

Draw :g:`AF <0056.g1:/ A F />` to the centre, and produce it to :g:`C <0056.g1:C>`, and join :g:`CE <0056.g1:/ C E />`.

.. picture:: 0056.g1.svg
   :alt: Diagram for alternative proof of Proposition 21

Therefore the segment :g:`BADC <0056.g1:< B A D C )>` is greater than a semicircle, and the angles in it :g:`BAC <0056.g1:< B A C )>`, :g:`BEC <0056.g1:< B E C )>` are equal, by the first case.

For the same reason, because :g:`CBED <0056.g1:< C B E D )>` is greater than a semicircle,

.. assertion::

   the angles :g:`CAD <0056.g1:< C A D )>`, :g:`CED <0056.g1:< C E D )>` are equal.

Therefore the whole angle :g:`BAD <0056.g1:< B A D )>` is equal to the whole angle :g:`BED <0056.g1:< B E D )>`.”

We can prove, by means of :term:`*reductio ad absurdum* <reductio-ad-absurdum>`, the important converse of this proposition, namely that, *if there be any two triangles on the same base and on the same side of it, and with equal vertical angles, the circle passing through the extremities of the base and the vertex of one triangle will pass through the vertex of the other triangle also*. That a circle can be thus described about a triangle is clear from Euclid's construction in III. 9, which shows how to draw a circle passing through any three points, though it is in IV. 5 only that we have the problem stated. Now, suppose a circle :g:`BAC <0056.g2:( B A C )>` drawn through the angular points of a triangle :g:`BAC <0056.g2:< B A C >>`, and let :g:`BDC <0056.g2:< B D C >>` be another triangle with the same base :g:`BC <0056.g2:/ B C />` and on the same side of it, and having its vertical angle :g:`D <0056.g2:< B D C )>` equal to the angle :g:`A <0056.g2:< B A C )>`. Then shall the circle pass through :g:`D <0056.g2:D>`.

.. picture:: 0056.g2.svg
   :alt: Diagram for converse of Proposition 21

For, if it does not, it must pass through some point :g:`E <0056.g2:E>` on :g:`BD <0056.g2:/ B D />` or on :g:`BD <0056.g2:/ B D />` produced. If then :g:`EC <0056.g2:/ E C />` be joined, the angle :g:`BEC <0056.g2:< B E C )>` is equal to the angle :g:`BAC <0056.g2:< B A C )>`, by III. 21, and therefore equal to the angle :g:`BDC <0056.g2:< B D C )>`. Therefore an exterior angle of a triangle is equal to the interior and opposite angle: which is impossible, by I. 16.
Therefore :g:`D <0056.g2:D>` lies on the circle :g:`BAC <0056.g2:( B A C )>`.

Similarly for any other triangle on the base :g:`BC <0056.g2:/ B C />` and with vertical angle equal to :g:`A <0056.g2:< B A C )>`. Thus, *if any number of triangles be constructed on the same base and on the same side of it, with equal vertical angles, the vertices will all lie on the circumference of a segment of a circle*.

A useful theorem derivable from III. 21 is given by :person:`Serenus <serenus>` (:cite:`*De sectione coni* <serenus:de-sectione-coni>`, Props. 52, 53).

If :g:`ADB <0056.g3:( A D B )>` be any segment of a circle, and :g:`C <0056.g3:C>` be such a point on the circumference that :g:`AC <0056.g3:/ A C />` is equal to :g:`CB <0056.g3:/ C B />`, and if there be described with :g:`C <0056.g3:C>` as centre and radius :g:`CA <0056.g3:/ C A />` or :g:`CB <0056.g3:/ C B />` the circle :g:`AHB <0056.g3:( A H B )>`, then, :g:`ADB <0056.g3:< A D B )>` being any other angle in the segment :g:`ACB <0056.g3:( A C B )>`, and :g:`BD <0056.g3:/ B D />` being produced to meet the outer segment in :g:`E <0056.g3:E>`, the sum of :g:`AD <0056.g3:/ A D />`, :g:`DB <0056.g3:/ D B />` is equal to :g:`BE <0056.g3:/ B E />`.

.. picture:: 0056.g3.svg
   :alt: Diagram for Serenus's theorem on Proposition 21

If :g:`BC <0056.g3:/ B C />` be produced to meet the outer segment in :g:`F <0056.g3:F>`, and :g:`FA <0056.g3:/ F A />` be joined,

.. assertion::

   :g:`CA <0056.g3:/ C A />`, :g:`CB <0056.g3:/ C B />`, :g:`CF <0056.g3:/ C F />` are by hypothesis equal.

Therefore the angle :g:`FAC <0056.g3:< F A C )>` is equal to the angle :g:`AFC <0056.g3:< A F C )>`.
Also, by III. 21, the angles :g:`ACB <0056.g3:< A C B )>`, :g:`ADB <0056.g3:< A D B )>` are equal;

.. _folio-ii-0057:

.. folio:: 0057
   :page: 51

therefore their supplements, the angles :g:`ACF <0056.g3:< A C F )>`, :g:`ADE <0056.g3:< A D E )>`, are equal.
Further, by III. 21, the angles :g:`AEB <0056.g3:< A E B )>`, :g:`AFB <0056.g3:< A F B )>` are equal.
Hence in the triangles :g:`ACF <0056.g3:< A C F >>`, :g:`ADE <0056.g3:< A D E >>` two angles are respectively equal;
therefore the third angles :g:`EAD <0056.g3:< E A D )>`, :g:`FAC <0056.g3:< F A C )>` are equal.
But the angle :g:`FAC <0056.g3:< F A C )>` is equal to the angle :g:`AFC <0056.g3:< A F C )>`, and therefore equal to the angle :g:`AED <0056.g3:< A E D )>`.
Therefore the angles :g:`AED <0056.g3:< A E D )>`, :g:`EAD <0056.g3:< E A D )>` are equal, or the triangle :g:`DEA <0056.g3:< D E A >>` is isosceles,

.. assertion::

   and :g:`AD <0056.g3:/ A D />` is equal to :g:`DE <0056.g3:/ D E />`.

Adding :g:`BD <0056.g3:/ B D />` to both, we see that

.. assertion::

   :g:`BE <0056.g3:/ B E />` is equal to the sum of :g:`AD <0056.g3:/ A D />` and :g:`DB <0056.g3:/ D B />`.

Now, :g:`BF <0056.g3:/ B F />` being a diameter of the circle of which the outer segment is a part,

.. assertion::

   :g:`BF <0056.g3:/ B F />` is greater than :g:`BE <0056.g3:/ B E />`;

therefore :g:`AC <0056.g3:/ A C />`, :g:`CB <0056.g3:/ C B />` are together greater than :g:`AD <0056.g3:/ A D />`, :g:`DB <0056.g3:/ D B />`.

And, generally, *of all triangles on the same base and on the same side of it which have equal vertical angles, the isosceles triangle is that which has the greatest perimeter, and of the others that has the lesser perimeter which is further from being isosceles*.

The theorem of :person:`Serenus <serenus>` gives us the means of solving the following problem given in :person:`Todhunter <todhunter>`'s Euclid, p. 324.

*To find a point in the circumference of a given segment of a circle such that the straight lines which join the point to the extremities of the straight line on which the segment stands may be together equal to a given straight line* (the length of which is of course subject to limits).

Let :g:`ACB <0056.g3:( A C B )>` in the above figure be the given segment. Find, by bisecting :g:`AB <0056.g3:/ A B />` at right angles, a point :g:`C <0056.g3:C>` on it such that :g:`AC <0056.g3:/ A C />` is equal to :g:`CB <0056.g3:/ C B />`.
Then with centre :g:`C <0056.g3:C>` and radius :g:`CA <0056.g3:/ C A />` or :g:`CB <0056.g3:/ C B />` describe the segment of a circle :g:`AHB <0056.g3:( A H B )>` on the same side of :g:`AB <0056.g3:/ A B />`.
Lastly, with :g:`A <0056.g3:A>` or :g:`B <0056.g3:B>` as centre and radius equal to the given straight line describe a circle. This circle will, if the given straight line be greater than :g:`AB <0056.g3:/ A B />` and less than twice :g:`AC <0056.g3:/ A C />`, meet the outer segment in two points, and if we join those points to the centre of the circle last drawn (whether :g:`A <0056.g3:A>` or :g:`B <0056.g3:B>`), the joining straight lines will cut the inner segment in points satisfying the given condition. If the given straight line be equal to twice :g:`AC <0056.g3:/ A C />`, :g:`C <0056.g3:C>` is of course the required point. If the given straight line be greater than twice :g:`AC <0056.g3:/ A C />`, there is no possible solution.
