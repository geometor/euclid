PROPOSITION 4.
==============

.. enunciation::

   In a given triangle to inscribe a circle.

Let :g:`$ABC$ <0091.g1:< A B C >>` be the given triangle;
thus it is required to inscribe a circle in the triangle :g:`$ABC$ <0091.g1:< A B C >>`.

Let the angles :g:`$ABC$ <0091.g1:< A B C )>`, :g:`$ACB$ <0091.g1:< A C B )>`
be bisected by the straight lines
:g:`$BD$ <0091.g1:/ B D />`, :g:`$CD$ <0091.g1:/ C D />` :just:`[I. 9] <I.9>`, and let these meet
one another at the point :g:`$D$ <0091.g1:D>`;
from :g:`$D$ <0091.g1:D>` let :g:`$DE$ <0091.g1:/ D E />`, :g:`$DF$ <0091.g1:/ D F />`, :g:`$DG$ <0091.g1:/ D G />` be
drawn perpendicular to the straight
lines :g:`$AB$ <0091.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$BC$ <0091.g1:/ B C />`, :g:`$CA$ <0091.g1:/ C A />`.

.. picture:: 0091.g1.svg
   :alt: Proposition 4 diagram

Now, since the angle :g:`$ABD$ <0091.g1:< A B D )>`
is equal to the angle :g:`$CBD$ <0091.g1:< C B D )>`,
and the right angle :g:`$BED$ <0091.g1:< B E D )>` is also equal to the right angle
:g:`$BFD$ <0091.g1:< B F D )>`,
:g:`$EBD$ <0091.g1:< E B D >>`, :g:`$FBD$ <0091.g1:< F B D >>` are two triangles having two angles equal to two
angles and one side equal to one side, namely that subtending
one of the equal angles, which is :g:`$BD$ <0091.g1:/ B D />` common to the
triangles;
therefore they will also have the remaining sides equal to
the remaining sides; :just:`[I. 26] <I.26>`

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$DE$ <0091.g1:/ D E />` is equal to :g:`$DF$ <0091.g1:/ D F />`.

For the same reason

.. assertion::

   :g:`$DG$ <0091.g1:/ D G />` is also equal to :g:`$DF$ <0091.g1:/ D F />`.

Therefore the three straight lines :g:`$DE$ <0091.g1:/ D E />`, :g:`$DF$ <0091.g1:/ D F />`, :g:`$DG$ <0091.g1:/ D G />` are equal
to one another;
therefore the circle described with centre :g:`$D$ <0091.g1:D>` and distance
one of the straight lines :g:`$DE$ <0091.g1:/ D E />`, :g:`$DF$ <0091.g1:/ D F />`, :g:`$DG$ <0091.g1:/ D G />`\ [#f1]_ will pass also
through the remaining points, and will touch the straight
lines :g:`$AB$ <0091.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$BC$ <0091.g1:/ B C />`, :g:`$CA$ <0091.g1:/ C A />`, because the angles at the points :g:`$E$ <0091.g1:E>`, :g:`$F$ <0091.g1:F>`, :g:`$G$ <0091.g1:G>`
are right.

For, if it cuts them, the straight line drawn at right angles
to the diameter of the circle from its extremity will be found
to fall within the circle: which was proved absurd; :just:`[III. 16] <III.16>`
therefore the circle described with centre :g:`$D$ <0091.g1:D>` and distance
one of the straight lines :g:`$DE$ <0091.g1:/ D E />`, :g:`$DF$ <0091.g1:/ D F />`, :g:`$DG$ <0091.g1:/ D G />`\ [#f1]_ will not cut the
straight lines :g:`$AB$ <0091.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$BC$ <0091.g1:/ B C />`, :g:`$CA$ <0091.g1:/ C A />`;
therefore it will touch them, and will be the circle inscribed
in the triangle :g:`$ABC$ <0091.g1:< A B C >>`. :just:`[IV. Def. 5] <IV.def.5>`

.. _folio-ii-0092:

.. folio:: 0092
   :page: 86

Let it be inscribed, as :g:`$FGE$ <0091.g1:( F G E )>`.

Therefore in the given triangle :g:`$ABC$ <0091.g1:< A B C >>` the circle :g:`$EFG$ <0091.g1:( E F G )>` has
been inscribed.

.. qed::

   :just:`Q. E. F.`

.. [#f1] **and distance one of the (straight lines D)E, (D)F, (D** :g:`$G$ <0091.g1:G>` **)**. The words and letters here shown in brackets are put in to fill out the rather careless language of the Greek. Here and in several other places in Book IV. Euclid says literally "and with distance one of the (points) $E$, $F$, $G$" ($\kappa\alpha\grave{\iota}\ \delta\iota\alpha\sigma\tau\acute{\eta}\mu\alpha\tau\iota\ \dot{\varepsilon}\nu\grave{\iota}\ \tau\tilde{\omega}\nu\ \text{E, Z, H}$) and the like. In one case (IV. 13) he actually has "with distance one of the *points* $G$, $H$, $K$, $L$, $M$" ($\delta\iota\alpha\sigma\tau\acute{\eta}\mu\alpha\tau\iota\ \dot{\varepsilon}\nu\grave{\iota}\ \tau\tilde{\omega}\nu\ \text{H, }\Theta\text{, K, }\Lambda\text{, M }\sigma\eta\mu\varepsilon\acute{\iota}\omega\nu$). :person:`Heiberg <heiberg>` notes "Graecam locutionem satis miram et negligentem," but, in view of its frequent occurrence in good MSS., does not venture to correct it.
