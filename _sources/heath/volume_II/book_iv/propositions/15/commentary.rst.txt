Commentary on Proposition IV.15
===============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition IV.15 <IV.15>`.

.. folio:: 0115
   :page: 109

:person:`Heiberg <heiberg>`, I think with good reason, considers the Porism to this proposition
to be referred to in the instance which :person:`Proclus <proclus>` (:cite:`p. 304, 2 <proclus:in-euclid:304-2>`) gives of a porism
following a *problem*. As the text of :person:`Proclus <proclus>` stands, "the (porism) found
in the second Book (τὸ δὲ ἐν τῷ δευτέρῳ βιβλίῳ κείμενον) is a porism to a
problem"; but this is not true of the only porism that we find in the second
Book, namely the porism to II. 4. Hence :person:`Heiberg <heiberg>` thinks that for τῷ
δευτέρῳ βιβλίῳ should be read τῷ δʹ βιβλίῳ, i.e. the fourth Book. Moreover
:person:`Proclus <proclus>` speaks of *the* porism in the particular Book, from which we gather
that there was only *one* porism in Book IV. as he knew it, and therefore
that he did not regard as a *porism* the addition to IV. 5. Cf. note on that
proposition.

It appears that Theon substituted for the first words of the Porism to

IV. 15 "And in like manner as in the case of the pentagon" (ὁμοίως δὲ

τοῖς ἐπὶ τοῦ πενταγώνου) the simple word "and" or "also" (καί), apparently
thinking that the words had the same meaning as the similar words lower
down. This is however not the case, the meaning being that "if, as in the
case of the pentagon, we draw tangents, we can prove, also as was done in
the case of the pentagon, that the figure so formed is a circumscribed regular
hexagon."
