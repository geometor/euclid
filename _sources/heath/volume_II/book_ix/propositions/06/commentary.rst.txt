Commentary on Proposition IX.6
==============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition IX.6 <IX.6>`.

.. folio:: 0395
   :page: 389

If :math:`a^2` is a cube number, :math:`a` is also a cube.

For

.. math::

   1 : a = a : a^2 = a^2 : a^3.

Now :math:`a^2`, :math:`a^3` are both cubes, and therefore "similar solids"; therefore there are two mean proportionals between them. :just:`[VIII. 19] <VIII.19>`

Therefore there are two mean proportionals between :math:`a`, :math:`a^2`. :just:`[VIII. 8] <VIII.8>`

And :math:`a^2` is a cube: therefore :math:`a` is also a cube number. :just:`[VIII. 23] <VIII.23>`

It will be noticed that the last step is not an exact quotation of the result of VIII. 23, because it is there the *first* of four terms which is known to be a cube, and the *last* which is proved to be a cube; here the case is reversed. But there is no difficulty. Without inverting the proportions, we have only to refer to VIII. 21 which proves that :math:`a`, :math:`a^2`, having two mean proportionals between them, are two similar solid numbers; whence, since :math:`a^2` is a cube, :math:`a` is also a cube.
