PROPOSITION 19.
===============

.. enunciation::

   Given three numbers, to investigate when it is possible to find a fourth proportional to them.

Let :g:`$A$ <0415.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0415.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0415.g1:C>` be the given three numbers, and let it be required to investigate when it is possible to find a fourth proportional to them.

.. picture:: 0415.g1.svg
   :alt: Proposition 19

Now either they are not in continued proportion, and the extremes of them are prime to one another ; or they are in continued proportion, and the extremes of them are not prime to one another ; or they are not in continued proportion, nor are the extremes of them prime to one another ; or they are in continued proportion, and the extremes of them are prime to one another.

.. subclause::

   If then :g:`$A$ <0415.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0415.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0415.g1:C>` are in continued proportion, and the extremes of them :g:`$A$ <0415.g1:A>`, :g:`$C$ <0415.g1:C>` are prime to one another,

.. assertion::

   it has been proved that it is impossible to find a fourth proportional number to them. :just:`[IX. 17] <IX.17>`

†Next, let :g:`$A$ <0415.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0415.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0415.g1:C>` not be in continued proportion, the extremes being again prime to one another ;

I say that in this case also it is impossible to find a fourth proportional to them.

For, if possible, let :g:`$D$ <0415.g1:D>` have been found, so that, as :g:`$A$ <0415.g1:A>` is to :g:`$B$ <0415.g1:B>`, so is :g:`$C$ <0415.g1:C>` to :g:`$D$ <0415.g1:D>`, and let it be contrived that, as :g:`$B$ <0415.g1:B>` is to :g:`$C$ <0415.g1:C>`, so is :g:`$D$ <0415.g1:D>` to :g:`$E$ <0415.g1:E>`.

.. subclause::

   Now, since, as :g:`$A$ <0415.g1:A>` is to :g:`$B$ <0415.g1:B>`, so is :g:`$C$ <0415.g1:C>` to :g:`$D$ <0415.g1:D>`, and, as :g:`$B$ <0415.g1:B>` is to :g:`$C$ <0415.g1:C>`, so is :g:`$D$ <0415.g1:D>` to :g:`$E$ <0415.g1:E>`,

.. assertion:: [VII. 14] <VII.14>

   therefore, :term:`*ex aequali* <ex-aequali>`, as :g:`$A$ <0415.g1:A>` is to :g:`$C$ <0415.g1:C>`, so is :g:`$C$ <0415.g1:C>` to :g:`$E$ <0415.g1:E>`.

.. subclause::

   But :g:`$A$ <0415.g1:A>`, :g:`$C$ <0415.g1:C>` are prime, primes are also least, :just:`[VII. 21] <VII.21>` and the least numbers measure those which have the same ratio, the antecedent the antecedent and the consequent the consequent. :just:`[VII. 20] <VII.20>`

.. assertion::

   Therefore :g:`$A$ <0415.g1:A>` measures :g:`$C$ <0415.g1:C>` as antecedent antecedent.

.. _folio-ii-0416:

.. folio:: 0416
   :page: 410

.. subclause::

   But it also measures itself ;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$A$ <0415.g1:A>` measures :g:`$A$ <0415.g1:A>`, :g:`$C$ <0415.g1:C>` which are prime to one another : which is impossible.

.. assertion::

   Therefore it is not possible to find a fourth proportional to :g:`$A$ <0415.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0415.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0415.g1:C>`.†

Next, let :g:`$A$ <0415.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0415.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0415.g1:C>` be again in continued proportion, but let :g:`$A$ <0415.g1:A>`, :g:`$C$ <0415.g1:C>` not be prime to one another.

I say that it is possible to find a fourth proportional to them.

For let :g:`$B$ <0415.g1:B>` by multiplying :g:`$C$ <0415.g1:C>` make :g:`$D$ <0415.g1:D>` ;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$A$ <0415.g1:A>` either measures :g:`$D$ <0415.g1:D>` or does not measure it.

First, let it measure it according to :g:`$E$ <0415.g1:E>` ;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$A$ <0415.g1:A>` by multiplying :g:`$E$ <0415.g1:E>` has made :g:`$D$ <0415.g1:D>`.

.. subclause::

   But, further, :g:`$B$ <0415.g1:B>` has also by multiplying :g:`$C$ <0415.g1:C>` made :g:`$D$ <0415.g1:D>` ;

.. assertion::

   therefore the product of :g:`$A$ <0415.g1:A>`, :g:`$E$ <0415.g1:E>` is equal to the product of :g:`$B$ <0415.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0415.g1:C>` ; therefore, proportionally, as :g:`$A$ <0415.g1:A>` is to :g:`$B$ <0415.g1:B>`, so is :g:`$C$ <0415.g1:C>` to :g:`$E$ <0415.g1:E>` ; :just:`[VII. 19] <VII.19>` therefore :g:`$E$ <0415.g1:E>` has been found a fourth proportional to :g:`$A$ <0415.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0415.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0415.g1:C>`.

Next, let :g:`$A$ <0415.g1:A>` not measure :g:`$D$ <0415.g1:D>` ;

I say that it is impossible to find a fourth proportional number to :g:`$A$ <0415.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0415.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0415.g1:C>`.

For, if possible, let :g:`$E$ <0415.g1:E>` have been found ;

.. assertion::

   therefore the product of :g:`$A$ <0415.g1:A>`, :g:`$E$ <0415.g1:E>` is equal to the product of :g:`$B$ <0415.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0415.g1:C>`. :just:`[VII. 19] <VII.19>`

.. subclause::

   But the product of :g:`$B$ <0415.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0415.g1:C>` is :g:`$D$ <0415.g1:D>` ;

.. assertion::

   therefore the product of :g:`$A$ <0415.g1:A>`, :g:`$E$ <0415.g1:E>` is also equal to :g:`$D$ <0415.g1:D>`.

.. assertion::

   Therefore :g:`$A$ <0415.g1:A>` by multiplying :g:`$E$ <0415.g1:E>` has made :g:`$D$ <0415.g1:D>` ; therefore :g:`$A$ <0415.g1:A>` measures :g:`$D$ <0415.g1:D>` according to :g:`$E$ <0415.g1:E>`, so that :g:`$A$ <0415.g1:A>` measures :g:`$D$ <0415.g1:D>`.

.. subclause::

   But it also does not measure it :

.. assertion::

   which is absurd.

.. assertion::

   Therefore it is not possible to find a fourth proportional number to :g:`$A$ <0415.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0415.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0415.g1:C>` when :g:`$A$ <0415.g1:A>` does not measure :g:`$D$ <0415.g1:D>`.

Next, let :g:`$A$ <0415.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0415.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0415.g1:C>` not be in continued proportion, nor the extremes prime to one another.

And let :g:`$B$ <0415.g1:B>` by multiplying :g:`$C$ <0415.g1:C>` make :g:`$D$ <0415.g1:D>`.

.. assertion::

   Similarly then it can be proved that, if :g:`$A$ <0415.g1:A>` measures :g:`$D$ <0415.g1:D>`, it is possible to find a fourth proportional to them, but, if it does not measure it, impossible.

.. qed::

   :just:`Q. E. D.`
