Commentary on Proposition V.8
=============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition V.8 <V.8>`.

.. folio:: 0158
   :page: 152

The two separate cases found in the Greek text of the demonstration can practically be compressed into one. Also the expositor of the two cases makes them differ more than they need. It is necessary in each case to select the *smaller* of the two segments :g:`$AE$ <0157.g1:/ A E />`, :g:`$EB$ <0157.g1:/ E B />` of :g:`$AB$ <0157.g1:/ A B />` with a view to taking a multiple of it which is greater than :g:`$D$ <0157.g1:D>`; in the first case therefore :g:`$AE$ <0157.g1:/ A E />` is taken, in the second :g:`$EB$ <0157.g1:/ E B />`. But, while in the first case successive multiples of :g:`$D$ <0157.g1:D>` are taken in order to find the first multiple that is greater than :g:`$GH$ <0157.g1:/ G H />` (or :g:`$K$ <0157.g1:K>`), in the second case the multiple is taken which is the first that is greater than :g:`$FG$ <0157.g1:/ F G />`. This difference is not necessary; the first multiple of :g:`$D$ <0157.g1:D>` that is greater than :g:`$GH$ <0157.g1:/ G H />` would equally serve in the second case. Lastly, the use of the magnitude :g:`$K$ <0157.g1:K>` might have been dispensed with in both cases; it is of no practical use and only lengthens the proofs. For these reasons :person:`Simson <simson>` considers that Theon, or some other unskilful editor, has vitiated the proposition. This however seems an unsafe assumption; for, while it was not the habit of the great Greek geometers to discuss separately a number of different cases (e.g. in I. 7 and I. 35 Euclid proves one case and leaves the others to the reader), there are many exceptions to prove the rule, e.g. Eucl. III. 25 and 33; and we know that many fundamental propositions, afterwards proved generally, were first discovered in relation to particular cases and then generalised, so that Book V., presenting a comparatively new theory, might fairly be expected to exhibit more instances than the earlier books do of unnecessary subdivision. The use of the :g:`$K$ <0157.g1:K>` is no more conclusive against the genuineness of the proofs.

Nevertheless :person:`Simson <simson>`'s version of the proof is certainly shorter, and moreover it takes account of the case in which :g:`$AE$ <0157.g1:/ A E />` is :eq:`*equal* <0157.g1:/ A E / = 0157.g1:/ E B />` to :g:`$EB$ <0157.g1:/ E B />`, and of the case in which :g:`$AE$ <0157.g1:/ A E />`, :g:`$EB$ <0157.g1:/ E B />` are both greater than :g:`$D$ <0157.g1:D>` (though these cases are scarcely worth separate mention).

"If the magnitude which is not the greater of the two :g:`$AE$ <0157.g1:/ A E />`, :g:`$EB$ <0157.g1:/ E B />` be (1) not less than :g:`$D$ <0157.g1:D>`, take :g:`$FG$ <0157.g1:/ F G />`, :g:`$GH$ <0157.g1:/ G H />` the doubles of :g:`$AE$ <0157.g1:/ A E />`, :g:`$EB$ <0157.g1:/ E B />`.

But if that which is not the greater of the two :g:`$AE$ <0157.g1:/ A E />`, :g:`$EB$ <0157.g1:/ E B />` be (2) less than :g:`$D$ <0157.g1:D>`, this magnitude can be multiplied so as to become greater than :g:`$D$ <0157.g1:D>` whether it be :g:`$AE$ <0157.g1:/ A E />` or :g:`$EB$ <0157.g1:/ E B />`.

Let it be multiplied until it becomes greater than :g:`$D$ <0157.g1:D>`, and let the other be multiplied as often; let :g:`$FG$ <0157.g1:/ F G />` be the multiple thus taken of :g:`$AE$ <0157.g1:/ A E />` and :g:`$GH$ <0157.g1:/ G H />` the same multiple of :g:`$EB$ <0157.g1:/ E B />`,

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$FG$ <0157.g1:/ F G />` and :g:`$GH$ <0157.g1:/ G H />` are each of them greater than :g:`$D$ <0157.g1:D>`.

And, in every one of the cases, take :g:`$L$ <0157.g1:L>` the double of :g:`$D$ <0157.g1:D>`, :g:`$M$ <0157.g1:M>` its triple and so on, till the multiple of :g:`$D$ <0157.g1:D>` be that which first becomes greater than :g:`$GH$ <0157.g1:/ G H />`.

Let :g:`$N$ <0157.g1:N>` be that multiple of :g:`$D$ <0157.g1:D>` which is first greater than :g:`$GH$ <0157.g1:/ G H />`, and :g:`$M$ <0157.g1:M>` the multiple of :g:`$D$ <0157.g1:D>` which is next less than :g:`$N$ <0157.g1:N>`.

.. folio:: 0159
   :page: 153

Then, because :g:`$N$ <0157.g1:N>` is the multiple of :g:`$D$ <0157.g1:D>` which is the first that becomes greater than :g:`$GH$ <0157.g1:/ G H />`,
the next preceding multiple is not greater than :g:`$GH$ <0157.g1:/ G H />` ;

.. assertion::

   that is, :g:`$GH$ <0157.g1:/ G H />` is not less than :g:`$M$ <0157.g1:M>`.

And, since :g:`$FG$ <0157.g1:/ F G />` is the same multiple of :g:`$AE$ <0157.g1:/ A E />` that :g:`$GH$ <0157.g1:/ G H />` is of :g:`$EB$ <0157.g1:/ E B />`,
:g:`$GH$ <0157.g1:/ G H />` is the same multiple of :g:`$EB$ <0157.g1:/ E B />` that :g:`$FH$ <0157.g1:/ F H />` is of :g:`$AB$ <0157.g1:/ A B />` ; :just:`[V. 1] <V.1>`
wherefore :g:`$FH$ <0157.g1:/ F H />`, :g:`$GH$ <0157.g1:/ G H />` are equimultiples of :g:`$AB$ <0157.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$EB$ <0157.g1:/ E B />`.

And it was shown that :g:`$GH$ <0157.g1:/ G H />` was not less than :g:`$M$ <0157.g1:M>` ;

.. subclause::

   and, by the construction, :g:`$FG$ <0157.g1:/ F G />` is greater than :g:`$D$ <0157.g1:D>` ;

.. assertion::

   therefore the whole :g:`$FH$ <0157.g1:/ F H />` is greater than :g:`$M$ <0157.g1:M>`, :g:`$D$ <0157.g1:D>` together.

But :g:`$M$ <0157.g1:M>`, :g:`$D$ <0157.g1:D>` together are equal to :g:`$N$ <0157.g1:N>` ;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$FH$ <0157.g1:/ F H />` is greater than :g:`$N$ <0157.g1:N>`.

But :g:`$GH$ <0157.g1:/ G H />` is not greater than :g:`$N$ <0157.g1:N>` ;
and :g:`$FH$ <0157.g1:/ F H />`, :g:`$GH$ <0157.g1:/ G H />` are equimultiples of :g:`$AB$ <0157.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$BE$ <0157.g1:/ E B />`,

.. subclause::

   and :g:`$N$ <0157.g1:N>` is a multiple of :g:`$D$ <0157.g1:D>` ;

.. assertion:: [V. Def. 7] <V.def.7>

   therefore :g:`$AB$ <0157.g1:/ A B />` has to :g:`$D$ <0157.g1:D>` a greater ratio than :g:`$BE$ <0157.g1:/ E B />` (or :g:`$C$ <0157.g1:C>`) has to :g:`$D$ <0157.g1:D>`.

Also :g:`$D$ <0157.g1:D>` has to :g:`$BE$ <0157.g1:/ E B />` a greater ratio than it has to :g:`$AB$ <0157.g1:/ A B />`.

For, having made the same construction, it may be shown, in like manner,
that :g:`$N$ <0157.g1:N>` is greater than :g:`$GH$ <0157.g1:/ G H />` but that it is not greater than :g:`$FH$ <0157.g1:/ F H />` ;
and :g:`$N$ <0157.g1:N>` is a multiple of :g:`$D$ <0157.g1:D>`,
and :g:`$GH$ <0157.g1:/ G H />`, :g:`$FH$ <0157.g1:/ F H />` are equimultiples of :g:`$EB$ <0157.g1:/ E B />`, :g:`$AB$ <0157.g1:/ A B />` ;

.. assertion:: [V. Def. 7] <V.def.7>

   Therefore :g:`$D$ <0157.g1:D>` has to :g:`$EB$ <0157.g1:/ E B />` a greater ratio than it has to :g:`$AB$ <0157.g1:/ A B />`."

The proof may perhaps be more readily grasped in the more symbolical form thus.

Take the $m$th equimultiples of :g:`$C$ <0157.g1:C>`, and of the excess of :g:`$AB$ <0157.g1:/ A B />` over :g:`$C$ <0157.g1:C>` (that is, of :g:`$AE$ <0157.g1:/ A E />`), such that each is greater than :g:`$D$ <0157.g1:D>` ;
and, of the multiples of :g:`$D$ <0157.g1:D>`, let $pD$ be the first that is greater than $mC$, and $nD$ the next less multiple of :g:`$D$ <0157.g1:D>`.

Then, since $mC$ is not less than $nD$,
and, by the construction, $m(AE)$ is greater than :g:`$D$ <0157.g1:D>`,
the sum of $mC$ and $m(AE)$ is greater than the sum of $nD$ and :g:`$D$ <0157.g1:D>`.

.. assertion::

   That is, m(:g:`$AB$ <0157.g1:/ A B />`) is greater than $pD$.

And, by the construction, $mC$ is less than $pD$.

.. assertion::

   Therefore :just:`[V. Def. 7] <V.def.7>` $AB$ has to :g:`$D$ <0157.g1:D>` a greater ratio than :g:`$C$ <0157.g1:C>` has to :g:`$D$ <0157.g1:D>`.

Again, since $pD$ is less than $m(AB)$,
and $pD$ is greater than $mC$,

.. assertion::

   :g:`$D$ <0157.g1:D>` has to :g:`$C$ <0157.g1:C>` a greater ratio than :g:`$D$ <0157.g1:D>` has to :g:`$AB$ <0157.g1:/ A B />`.
