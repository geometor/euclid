Commentary on Proposition VI.4
==============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition VI.4 <VI.4>`.

.. folio:: 0208
   :page: 202

:person:`Todhunter <todhunter>` remarks that "the manner in which the two triangles are to be placed is very imperfectly described; their bases are to be in the same straight line and contiguous, their vertices are to be on the same side of the base, and each of the two angles which have a common vertex is to be equal to the remote angle of the other triangle." But surely Euclid's description is sufficient, except for not saying that :g:`$B$ <0207.g1:B>` and :g:`$D$ <0207.g1:D>` must be on the same side of :g:`$BCE$ <0207.g1:/ B C E />`.

VI. 4 can be immediately deduced from VI. 2 if we superpose one triangle on the other three times in succession, so that each angle successively coincides with its equal, the triangles being similarly situated, e.g. if (:g:`$A$ <0207.g1:< A )>`, :g:`$B$ <0207.g1:< B )>`, :g:`$C$ <0207.g1:< C )>` and :g:`$D$ <0209.g2:< D )>`, :g:`$E$ <0209.g2:< E )>`, :g:`$F$ <0209.g2:< F )>` being the :eq:`equal <0207.g1:< A ) = 0209.g2:< D ); 0207.g1:< B ) = 0209.g2:< E ); 0207.g1:< C ) = 0209.g2:< F )>` angles respectively) we apply the angle :g:`$DEF$ <0209.g2:< D E F )>` to the angle :g:`$ABC$ <0207.g1:< A B C )>` so that :g:`$D$ <0209.g2:D>` lies on :g:`$AB$ <0207.g1:/ A B />` (produced if necessary) and :g:`$F$ <0209.g2:F>` on :g:`$BC$ <0207.g1:/ B C />` (produced if necessary). :person:`De Morgan <de-morgan>` prefers this method. "Abandon," he says, "the peculiar mode of construction by which Euclid proves two cases at once; make an angle coincide with its equal, and suppose this process repeated three times, one for each angle."
