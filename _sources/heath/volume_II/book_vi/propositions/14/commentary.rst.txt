Commentary on Proposition VI.14
===============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition VI.14 <VI.14>`.

.. folio:: 0223
   :page: 217

:person:`De Morgan <de-morgan>` says upon this proposition: “Owing to the disjointed manner in which Euclid treats compound ratio, this proposition is strangely out of place. It is a particular case of VI. 23, being that in which the ratio of the sides, compounded, gives a ratio of equality. The proper definition of four magnitudes being reciprocally proportional is that the ratio compounded of their ratios is that of equality.”

.. _folio-ii-0224:

.. folio:: 0224
   :page: 218

It is true that VI. 14 is a particular case of VI. 23, but, if either is out of place, it is rather the latter that should be placed before VI. 14, since most of the propositions between VI. 15 and VI. 23 depend upon VI. 14 and 15. But is perfectly consistent with Euclid's manner to give a particular case first and its extension later, and such an arrangement often has great advantages in that it enables the more difficult parts of a subject to be led up to more easily and gradually. Now, if :person:`De Morgan <de-morgan>`'s view were here followed, we should, as it seems to me, be committing the mistake of explaining what is relatively easy to understand, viz. two ratios of which one is the inverse of the other, by a more complicated conception, that of compound ratio. In other words, it is easier for a learner to realise the relation indicated by the statement that the sides of equal and equiangular parallelograms are “reciprocally proportional” than to form a conception of parallelograms such that “the ratio compounded of the ratio of their sides is one of equality.” For this reason I would adhere to Euclid's arrangement.

The conclusion that, since :g:`DB <0223.g1:/ D B />`, :g:`BE <0223.g1:/ B E />` are placed in a straight line, :g:`FB <0223.g1:/ F B />`, :g:`BG <0223.g1:/ B G />` are also in a straight line is referred to I. 14. The deduction is made clearer by the following steps.

.. subclause::

   The angle :g:`DBF <0223.g1:< D B F )>` is :eq:`equal <0223.g1:< D B F ) = 0223.g1:< G B E )>` to the angle :g:`GBE <0223.g1:< G B E )>`; add to each the angle :g:`FBE <0223.g1:< F B E )>`;

.. assertion::

   therefore the angles :g:`DBF <0223.g1:< D B F )>`, :g:`FBE <0223.g1:< F B E )>` are together equal to the angles :g:`GBE <0223.g1:< G B E )>`, :g:`FBE <0223.g1:< F B E )>`. :just:`[C. N. 2] <I.cn.2>`

.. subclause:: [I. 13] <I.13>

   But the angles :g:`DBF <0223.g1:< D B F )>`, :g:`FBE <0223.g1:< F B E )>` are together equal to two right angles,

.. assertion::

   therefore the angles :g:`GBE <0223.g1:< G B E )>`, :g:`FBE <0223.g1:< F B E )>` are together equal to two right angles, :just:`[C. N. 1] <I.cn.1>`

.. assertion:: [I. 14] <I.14>

   and hence :g:`FB <0223.g1:/ F B />`, :g:`BG <0223.g1:/ B G />` are in one straight line.

The result is also obvious from the converse of I. 15 given by :person:`Proclus <proclus>` (see note on I. 15).

The proposition VI. 14 contains a theorem and one partial converse of it; so also does VI. 15. To each proposition may be added the other partial converse, which may be enunciated as follows, the words in square brackets applying to the case of triangles (VI. 15).

.. picture:: 0224.g1.svg
   :alt: Converse of Propositions 14 and 15

.. enunciation::

   Equal parallelograms [triangles] which have the sides about one angle in each reciprocally proportional are equiangular [have the angles included by those sides either equal or supplementary].

Let :g:`AB <0224.g1:< A B >>`, :g:`BC <0224.g1:< B C >>` be :eq:`equal <0224.g1:< A B > = 0224.g1:< B C >>` parallelograms, or let :g:`FBD <0224.g1:< F B D >>`, :g:`EBG <0224.g1:< E B G >>` be :eq:`equal <0224.g1:< F B D > = 0224.g1:< E B G >>`
triangles, such that the sides about the angles at :g:`B <0224.g1:B>` are reciprocally proportional, i.e. such that

.. _folio-ii-0225:

.. folio:: 0225
   :page: 219

.. assertion::

   :g:`DB <0224.g1:/ D B />` : :g:`BE <0224.g1:/ B E />` = :g:`GB <0224.g1:/ G B />` : :g:`BF <0224.g1:/ B F />`.

We shall prove that the angles :g:`FBD <0224.g1:< F B D )>`, :g:`EBG <0224.g1:< E B G )>` are either :eq:`equal <0224.g1:< F B D ) = 0224.g1:< E B G )>` or supplementary.

Place the figures so that :g:`DB <0224.g1:/ D B />`, :g:`BE <0224.g1:/ B E />` are in one straight line. Then :g:`FB <0224.g1:/ F B />`, :g:`BG <0224.g1:/ B G />` are either in a straight line, or not in a straight line.

(1) If :g:`FB <0224.g1:/ F B />`, :g:`BG <0224.g1:/ B G />` are in a straight line, the figure of the proposition (with the diagonals :g:`FD <0224.g1:/ F D />`, :g:`EG <0224.g1:/ E G />` drawn) represents the facts, and

.. assertion:: [I. 15] <I.15>

   the angle :g:`FBD <0224.g1:< F B D )>` is :eq:`equal <0224.g1:< F B D ) = 0224.g1:< E B G )>` to the angle :g:`EBG <0224.g1:< E B G )>`.

(2) If :g:`FB <0224.g1:/ F B />`, :g:`BG <0224.g1:/ B G />` are not in a straight line, produce :g:`FB <0224.g1:/ F B />` to :g:`H <0224.g1:H>` so that :g:`BH <0224.g1:/ B H />` may be :eq:`equal <0224.g1:/ B H / = 0224.g1:/ B G />` to :g:`BG <0224.g1:/ B G />`. Join :g:`EH <0224.g1:/ E H />`, and complete the parallelogram :g:`EBHK <0224.g1:< E B H K >>`.

Now, since

.. assertion::

   :g:`DB <0224.g1:/ D B />` : :g:`BE <0224.g1:/ B E />` = :g:`GB <0224.g1:/ G B />` : :g:`BF <0224.g1:/ B F />` and :g:`GB <0224.g1:/ G B />` = :g:`HB <0224.g1:/ H B />`, :g:`DB <0224.g1:/ D B />` : :g:`BE <0224.g1:/ B E />` = :g:`HB <0224.g1:/ H B />` : :g:`BF <0224.g1:/ B F />`,

and therefore, by VI. 14 or 15, the parallelograms :g:`AB <0224.g1:< A B >>`, :g:`BK <0224.g1:< B K >>` are :eq:`equal <0224.g1:< A B > = 0224.g1:< B K >>`, or the triangles :g:`FBD <0224.g1:< F B D >>`, :g:`EBH <0224.g1:< E B H >>` are :eq:`equal <0224.g1:< F B D > = 0224.g1:< E B H >>`.

But the parallelograms :g:`AB <0224.g1:< A B >>`, :g:`BC <0224.g1:< B C >>` are :eq:`equal <0224.g1:< A B > = 0224.g1:< B C >>`, and the triangles :g:`FBD <0224.g1:< F B D >>`, :g:`EBG <0224.g1:< E B G >>` are :eq:`equal <0224.g1:< F B D > = 0224.g1:< E B G >>`; therefore the parallelograms :g:`BC <0224.g1:< B C >>`, :g:`BK <0224.g1:< B K >>` are :eq:`equal <0224.g1:< B C > = 0224.g1:< B K >>`, and the triangles :g:`EBH <0224.g1:< E B H >>`, :g:`EBG <0224.g1:< E B G >>` are :eq:`equal <0224.g1:< E B H > = 0224.g1:< E B G >>`.

Therefore these parallelograms or triangles are within the same parallels: that is, :g:`G <0224.g1:G>`, :g:`C <0224.g1:C>`, :g:`H <0224.g1:H>`, :g:`K <0224.g1:K>` are in a straight line which is parallel to :g:`DE <0224.g1:/ D E />`. :just:`[I. 39] <I.39>`

Now, since :g:`BG <0224.g1:/ B G />`, :g:`BH <0224.g1:/ B H />` are :eq:`equal <0224.g1:/ B G / = 0224.g1:/ B H />`, the angles :g:`BGH <0224.g1:< B G H )>`, :g:`BHG <0224.g1:< B H G )>` are :eq:`equal <0224.g1:< B G H ) = 0224.g1:< B H G )>`.

By parallels, it follows that

.. assertion::

   the angle :g:`EBG <0224.g1:< E B G )>` is :eq:`equal <0224.g1:< E B G ) = 0224.g1:< D B H )>` to the angle :g:`DBH <0224.g1:< D B H )>`,

whence the angle :g:`EBG <0224.g1:< E B G )>` is supplementary to the angle :g:`FBD <0224.g1:< F B D )>`.
