Commentary on Proposition VI.16
===============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition VI.16 <VI.16>`.

.. folio:: 0228
   :page: 222

This proposition is a particular case of VI. 14, but one which is on all accounts worth separate statement. It may also be enunciated in the following form:

*Rectangles which have their bases reciprocally proportional to their heights are equal in area; and equal rectangles have their bases reciprocally proportional to their heights.*

Since any parallelogram is equal to a rectangle of the same height and on the same base, and any triangle with the same height and on the same base is equal to half the parallelogram or rectangle, it follows that *Equal parallelograms or triangles have their bases reciprocally proportional to their heights and vice versa.*

The present place is suitable for giving certain important propositions, including those which :person:`Simson <simson>` adds to Book VI. as Props. B, C and D, which are proved directly by means of VI. 16.

1. Proposition B is a particular case of the following theorem.

*If a circle be circumscribed about a triangle :g:`$ABC$ <0229.g1:< A B C >>` and there be drawn through :g:`$A$ <0229.g1:A>` any two straight lines either both within or both without the angle :g:`$BAC$ <0229.g1:< B A C )>`, viz.*

.. _folio-ii-0229:

.. folio:: 0229
   :page: 223

.. enunciation::

   :g:`$AD$ <0229.g1:/ A D />` meeting :g:`$BC$ <0229.g1:/ B C />` (*produced if necessary*) in :g:`$D$ <0229.g1:D>` and :g:`$AE$ <0229.g1:/ A E />` meeting the circle again in :g:`$E$ <0229.g1:E>`, such that the angles :g:`$DAB$ <0229.g1:< D A B )>`, :g:`$EAC$ <0229.g1:< E A C )>` are :eq:`equal <0229.g1:< D A B ) = 0229.g1:< E A C )>`, then the rectangle :g:`$AD$ <0229.g1:/ A D />`, :g:`$AE$ <0229.g1:/ A E />` is equal to the rectangle :g:`$BA$ <0229.g1:/ B A />`, :g:`$AC$ <0229.g1:/ A C />`.

.. picture:: 0229.g1.svg
   :alt: Proposition B general theorem (internal)

.. picture:: 0229.g2.svg
   :alt: Proposition B general theorem (external)

Join :g:`$CE$ <0229.g1:/ C E />`.

The angles :g:`$BAD$ <0229.g1:< B A D )>`, :g:`$EAC$ <0229.g1:< E A C )>` are :eq:`equal <0229.g1:< B A D ) = 0229.g1:< E A C )>`, by hypothesis; and the angles :g:`$ABD$ <0229.g1:< A B D )>`, :g:`$AEC$ <0229.g1:< A E C )>` are :eq:`equal <0229.g1:< A B D ) = 0229.g1:< A E C )>`. :just:`[III. 21, 22]`

.. assertion::

   Therefore the triangles :g:`$ABD$ <0229.g1:< A B D >>`, :g:`$AEC$ <0229.g1:< A E C >>` are :eq:`equiangular <0229.g1:< B A D ) = 0229.g1:< E A C ); 0229.g1:< A B D ) = 0229.g1:< A E C )>`.

Hence

.. assertion::

   :g:`$BA$ <0229.g1:/ B A />` is to :g:`$AD$ <0229.g1:/ A D />` as :g:`$EA$ <0229.g1:/ E A />` is to :g:`$AC$ <0229.g1:/ A C />`,

.. assertion::

   and therefore the rectangle :g:`$BA$ <0229.g1:/ B A />`, :g:`$AC$ <0229.g1:/ A C />` is equal to the rectangle :g:`$AD$ <0229.g1:/ A D />`, :g:`$AE$ <0229.g1:/ A E />`. :just:`[VI. 16] <VI.16>`

There are now two particular cases to be considered.

(*a*) Suppose that :g:`$AD$ <0229.g3:/ A D />`, :g:`$AE$ <0229.g3:/ A E />` coincide; :g:`$ADE$ <0229.g3:/ A D E />` will then bisect the angle :g:`$BAC$ <0229.g3:< B A C )>`.

(*b*) Suppose that :g:`$AD$ <0229.g3:/ A D />`, :g:`$AE$ <0229.g3:/ A E />` are in one straight line but that :g:`$D$ <0229.g3:D>`, :g:`$E$ <0229.g3:E>` are on opposite sides of :g:`$A$ <0229.g3:A>`; :g:`$AD$ <0229.g3:/ A D />` will then bisect the external angle at :g:`$A$ <0229.g3:A>`.

.. picture:: 0229.g3.svg
   :alt: Proposition B particular cases (a) and (b)

In the first case (*a*) we have

.. assertion::

   the rectangle :g:`$BA$ <0229.g3:/ B A />`, :g:`$AC$ <0229.g3:/ A C />` equal to the rectangle :g:`$EA$ <0229.g3:/ E A />`, :g:`$AD$ <0229.g3:/ A D />`;

.. subclause::

   and the rectangle :g:`$EA$ <0229.g3:/ E A />`, :g:`$AD$ <0229.g3:/ A D />` is equal to the rectangle :g:`$ED$ <0229.g3:/ E D />`, :g:`$DA$ <0229.g3:/ D A />` together with the square on :g:`$AD$ <0229.g3:/ A D />`, :just:`[II. 3] <II.3>` i.e. to the rectangle :g:`$BD$ <0229.g3:/ B D />`, :g:`$DC$ <0229.g3:/ D C />` together with the square on :g:`$AD$ <0229.g3:/ A D />`. :just:`[III. 35] <III.35>`

.. assertion::

   Therefore the rectangle :g:`$BA$ <0229.g3:/ B A />`, :g:`$AC$ <0229.g3:/ A C />` is equal to the rectangle :g:`$BD$ <0229.g3:/ B D />`, :g:`$DC$ <0229.g3:/ D C />` together with the square on :g:`$AD$ <0229.g3:/ A D />`. :just:`[This is Simson's Prop. B]`

.. subclause::

   In case (*b*) the rectangle :g:`$EA$ <0229.g3:/ E A />`, :g:`$AD$ <0229.g3:/ A D />` is equal to the excess of the rectangle :g:`$ED$ <0229.g3:/ E D />`, :g:`$DA$ <0229.g3:/ D A />` over the square on :g:`$AD$ <0229.g3:/ A D />`;

.. assertion::

   therefore the rectangle :g:`$BA$ <0229.g3:/ B A />`, :g:`$AC$ <0229.g3:/ A C />` is equal to the excess of the rectangle :g:`$BD$ <0229.g3:/ B D />`, :g:`$DC$ <0229.g3:/ D C />` over the square on :g:`$AD$ <0229.g3:/ A D />`.

.. _folio-ii-0230:

.. folio:: 0230
   :page: 224

The following converse of :person:`Simson <simson>`'s Prop. B may be given: *If a straight line :g:`$AD$ <0230.g1:/ A D />` be drawn from the vertex :g:`$A$ <0230.g1:A>` of a triangle to meet the base, so that the square on :g:`$AD$ <0230.g1:/ A D />` together with the rectangle :g:`$BD$ <0230.g1:/ B D />`, :g:`$DC$ <0230.g1:/ D C />` is equal to the rectangle :g:`$BA$ <0230.g1:/ B A />`, :g:`$AC$ <0230.g1:/ A C />`, the line :g:`$AD$ <0230.g1:/ A D />` will bisect the angle :g:`$BAC$ <0230.g1:< B A C )>` except when the sides :g:`$AB$ <0230.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$AC$ <0230.g1:/ A C />` are :eq:`equal <0230.g1:/ A B / = 0230.g1:/ A C />`, in which case every line drawn to the base will have the property mentioned.*

Let the circumscribed circle be drawn, and let :g:`$AD$ <0230.g1:/ A D />` produced meet it in :g:`$E$ <0230.g1:E>`; join :g:`$CE$ <0230.g1:/ C E />`.

.. assertion:: [III. 35] <III.35>

   The rectangle :g:`$BD$ <0230.g1:/ B D />`, :g:`$DC$ <0230.g1:/ D C />` is equal to the rectangle :g:`$ED$ <0230.g1:/ E D />`, :g:`$DA$ <0230.g1:/ D A />`.

Add to each the square on :g:`$AD$ <0230.g1:/ A D />`;

.. assertion::

   therefore the rectangle :g:`$BA$ <0230.g1:/ B A />`, :g:`$AC$ <0230.g1:/ A C />` is equal to the rectangle :g:`$EA$ <0230.g1:/ E A />`, :g:`$AD$ <0230.g1:/ A D />`. :just:`[hyp. and II. 3]`

Hence

.. assertion:: [VI. 16] <VI.16>

   :g:`$AB$ <0230.g1:/ A B />` is to :g:`$AD$ <0230.g1:/ A D />` as :g:`$AE$ <0230.g1:/ A E />` to :g:`$AC$ <0230.g1:/ A C />`.

.. subclause:: [III. 21] <III.21>

   But the angle :g:`$ABD$ <0230.g1:< A B D )>` is :eq:`equal <0230.g1:< A B D ) = 0230.g1:< A E C )>` to the angle :g:`$AEC$ <0230.g1:< A E C )>`.

.. assertion::

   Therefore the angles :g:`$BDA$ <0230.g1:< B D A )>`, :g:`$ECA$ <0230.g1:< E C A )>` are either :eq:`equal <0230.g1:< B D A ) = 0230.g1:< E C A )>` or supplementary. :just:`[VI. 7 and note]`

(*a*) If they are :eq:`equal <0230.g1:< B D A ) = 0230.g1:< E C A )>`, the angles :g:`$BAD$ <0230.g1:< B A D )>`, :g:`$EAC$ <0230.g1:< E A C )>` are also :eq:`equal <0230.g1:< B A D ) = 0230.g1:< E A C )>`, and :g:`$AD$ <0230.g1:/ A D />` bisects the angle :g:`$BAC$ <0230.g1:< B A C )>`.

(*b*) If they are supplementary, the angle :g:`$ADC$ <0230.g1:< A D C )>` must be :eq:`equal <0230.g1:< A D C ) = 0230.g1:< A C E )>` to the angle :g:`$ACE$ <0230.g1:< A C E )>`.

Therefore the angles :g:`$BAD$ <0230.g1:< B A D )>`, :g:`$ABD$ <0230.g1:< A B D )>` are together equal to the angles :g:`$ACB$ <0230.g1:< A C B )>`, :g:`$BCE$ <0230.g1:< B C E )>`, i.e. to the angles :g:`$ACD$ <0230.g1:< A C D )>`, :g:`$BAD$ <0230.g1:< B A D )>`.

Take away the common angle :g:`$BAD$ <0230.g1:< B A D )>`, and

.. assertion::

   the angles :g:`$ABD$ <0230.g1:< A B D )>`, :g:`$ACD$ <0230.g1:< A C D )>` are :eq:`equal <0230.g1:< A B D ) = 0230.g1:< A C D )>`, or :g:`$AB$ <0230.g1:/ A B />` is :eq:`equal <0230.g1:/ A B / = 0230.g1:/ A C />` to :g:`$AC$ <0230.g1:/ A C />`.

.. picture:: 0230.g1.svg
   :alt: Converse of Simson's Prop. B

Euclid himself assumes, in Prop. 67 of the $Data$, the result of so much of this proposition as relates to the case where :g:`$BA$ <0230.g1:/ B A />` = :g:`$AC$ <0230.g1:/ A C />`. He assumes namely, without proof, that, if :g:`$BA$ <0230.g1:/ B A />` = :g:`$AC$ <0230.g1:/ A C />`, and if :g:`$D$ <0230.g1:D>` be any point on :g:`$BC$ <0230.g1:/ B C />`, the rectangle :g:`$BD$ <0230.g1:/ B D />`, :g:`$DC$ <0230.g1:/ D C />` together with the square on :g:`$AD$ <0230.g1:/ A D />` is equal to the square on :g:`$AB$ <0230.g1:/ A B />`.

PROPOSITION C.
--------------

.. enunciation::

   If from any angle of a triangle a straight line be drawn perpendicular to the opposite side, the rectangle contained by the other two sides of the triangle is equal to the rectangle contained by the perpendicular and the diameter of the circle circumscribed about the triangle.

Let :g:`$ABC$ <0230.g3:< A B C >>` be a triangle and :g:`$AD$ <0230.g3:/ A D />` the perpendicular on :g:`$BC$ <0230.g3:/ B C />`. Draw the diameter :g:`$AE$ <0230.g3:/ A E />` of the circle circumscribed about the triangle :g:`$ABC$ <0230.g3:< A B C >>`.

.. picture:: 0230.g3.svg
   :alt: Proposition C (internal altitude)

.. picture:: 0230.g2.svg
   :alt: Proposition C (external altitude)

.. assertion::

   Then shall the rectangle :g:`$BA$ <0230.g3:/ B A />`, :g:`$AC$ <0230.g3:/ A C />` be equal to the rectangle :g:`$EA$ <0230.g3:/ E A />`, :g:`$AD$ <0230.g3:/ A D />`.

Join :g:`$EC$ <0230.g3:/ E C />`.

.. _folio-ii-0231:

.. folio:: 0231
   :page: 225

Since the right angle :g:`$BDA$ <0230.g3:< B D A )>` is :eq:`equal <0230.g3:< B D A ) = 0230.g3:< E C A )>` to the right angle :g:`$ECA$ <0230.g3:< E C A )>` in a semicircle, :just:`[III. 31] <III.31>`

and the angles :g:`$ABD$ <0230.g3:< A B D )>`, :g:`$AEC$ <0230.g3:< A E C )>` in the same segment are :eq:`equal <0230.g3:< A B D ) = 0230.g3:< A E C )>`, :just:`[III. 21] <III.21>`

.. assertion::

   the triangles :g:`$ABD$ <0230.g3:< A B D >>`, :g:`$AEC$ <0230.g3:< A E C >>` are :eq:`equiangular <0230.g3:< B D A ) = 0230.g3:< E C A ); 0230.g3:< A B D ) = 0230.g3:< A E C )>`.

Therefore,

.. assertion:: [VI. 4] <VI.4>

   as :g:`$BA$ <0230.g3:/ B A />` is to :g:`$AD$ <0230.g3:/ A D />`, so is :g:`$EA$ <0230.g3:/ E A />` to :g:`$AC$ <0230.g3:/ A C />`,

.. assertion:: [VI. 16] <VI.16>

   whence the rectangle :g:`$BA$ <0230.g3:/ B A />`, :g:`$AC$ <0230.g3:/ A C />` is equal to the rectangle :g:`$EA$ <0230.g3:/ E A />`, :g:`$AD$ <0230.g3:/ A D />`.

This result corresponds to the trigonometrical formula for $R$, the radius of the circumscribed circle,

$$R = \frac{abc}{4\Delta}.$$

PROPOSITION D.
--------------

This is the highly important lemma given by :person:`Ptolemy <ptolemy>` (ed. :person:`Heiberg <heiberg>`, Vol. I, pp. 36—7) which is the basis of his calculation of the table of chords in the section of Book I. of the μεγάλη σύνταξις entitled "concerning the size of the straight lines [i.e. chords] in the circle" (περὶ τῆς πηλικότητος τῶν ἐν τῷ κύκλῳ εὐθειῶν).

The theorem may be enunciated thus.

.. enunciation::

   The rectangle contained by the diagonals of any quadrilateral inscribed in a circle is equal to the sum of the rectangles contained by the pairs of opposite sides.

I shall give the proof in :person:`Ptolemy <ptolemy>`'s words, with the addition only, in brackets, of two words applying to a second figure not given by :person:`Ptolemy <ptolemy>`.

"Let there be a circle with any quadrilateral :g:`$ABCD$ <0231.g2:< A B C D >>` inscribed in it, and let :g:`$AC$ <0231.g2:/ A C />`, :g:`$BD$ <0231.g2:/ B D />` be joined.

It is to be proved that the rectangle contained by :g:`$AC$ <0231.g2:/ A C />` and :g:`$BD$ <0231.g2:/ B D />` is equal to the sum of the rectangles :g:`$AB$ <0231.g2:/ A B />`, :g:`$DC$ <0231.g2:/ D C />` and :g:`$AD$ <0231.g2:/ A D />`, :g:`$BC$ <0231.g2:/ B C />`.

For let the angle :g:`$ABE$ <0231.g2:< A B E )>` be made :eq:`equal <0231.g2:< A B E ) = 0231.g2:< D B C )>` to the angle contained by :g:`$DB$ <0231.g2:/ D B />`, :g:`$BC$ <0231.g2:/ B C />`.

.. picture:: 0231.g2.svg
   :alt: Proposition D (Ptolemy's figure)

.. picture:: 0231.g1.svg
   :alt: Proposition D (supplementary figure)

If then we add [or subtract] the angle :g:`$EBD$ <0231.g2:< E B D )>`,

.. assertion::

   the angle :g:`$ABD$ <0231.g2:< A B D )>` will also be :eq:`equal <0231.g2:< A B D ) = 0231.g2:< E B C )>` to the angle :g:`$EBC$ <0231.g2:< E B C )>`.

.. subclause::

   But the angle :g:`$BDA$ <0231.g2:< B D A )>` is also :eq:`equal <0231.g2:< B D A ) = 0231.g2:< B C E )>` to the angle :g:`$BCE$ <0231.g2:< B C E )>`, :just:`[III. 21] <III.21>` for they subtend the same segment;

.. assertion::

   therefore the triangle :g:`$ABD$ <0231.g2:< A B D >>` is :eq:`equiangular <0231.g2:< A B D ) = 0231.g2:< E B C ); 0231.g2:< B D A ) = 0231.g2:< B C E )>` with the triangle :g:`$EBC$ <0231.g2:< E B C >>`.

Hence, proportionally,

.. assertion:: [VI. 4] <VI.4>

   as :g:`$BC$ <0231.g2:/ B C />` is to :g:`$CE$ <0231.g2:/ C E />`, so is :g:`$BD$ <0231.g2:/ B D />` to :g:`$DA$ <0231.g2:/ D A />`.

.. assertion::

   Therefore the rectangle :g:`$BC$ <0231.g2:/ B C />`, :g:`$AD$ <0231.g2:/ A D />` is equal to the rectangle :g:`$BD$ <0231.g2:/ B D />`, :g:`$CE$ <0231.g2:/ C E />`. :just:`[VI. 16] <VI.16>`

Again, since the angle :g:`$ABE$ <0231.g2:< A B E )>` is :eq:`equal <0231.g2:< A B E ) = 0231.g2:< D B C )>` to the angle :g:`$DBC$ <0231.g2:< D B C )>`, and the angle :g:`$BAE$ <0231.g2:< B A E )>` is also :eq:`equal <0231.g2:< B A E ) = 0231.g2:< B D C )>` to the angle :g:`$BDC$ <0231.g2:< B D C )>`, :just:`[III. 21] <III.21>`

.. assertion::

   the triangle :g:`$ABE$ <0231.g2:< A B E >>` is :eq:`equiangular <0231.g2:< A B E ) = 0231.g2:< D B C ); 0231.g2:< B A E ) = 0231.g2:< B D C )>` with the triangle :g:`$DBC$ <0231.g2:< D B C >>`.

.. _folio-ii-0232:

.. folio:: 0232
   :page: 226

Therefore, proportionally,

.. assertion:: [VI. 4] <VI.4>

   as :g:`$BA$ <0231.g1:/ B A />` is to :g:`$AE$ <0231.g1:/ A E />`, so is :g:`$BD$ <0231.g1:/ B D />` to :g:`$DC$ <0231.g1:/ D C />`;

.. assertion:: [VI. 16] <VI.16>

   therefore the rectangle :g:`$BA$ <0231.g1:/ B A />`, :g:`$DC$ <0231.g1:/ D C />` is equal to the rectangle :g:`$BD$ <0231.g1:/ B D />`, :g:`$AE$ <0231.g1:/ A E />`.

But it was also proved that

.. assertion::

   the rectangle :g:`$BC$ <0231.g1:/ B C />`, :g:`$AD$ <0231.g1:/ A D />` is equal to the rectangle :g:`$BD$ <0231.g1:/ B D />`, :g:`$CE$ <0231.g1:/ C E />`;

.. assertion::

   therefore the rectangle :g:`$AC$ <0231.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$BD$ <0231.g1:/ B D />` as a whole is equal to the sum of the rectangles :g:`$AB$ <0231.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$DC$ <0231.g1:/ D C />` and :g:`$AD$ <0231.g1:/ A D />`, :g:`$BC$ <0231.g1:/ B C />`:

.. qed::

   (being) what it was required to prove."

Another proof of this proposition, and of its converse, is indicated by Dr Lachlan (*Elements of Euclid*, pp. 273—4). It depends on two preliminary propositions.

(1) *If two circles be divided, by a chord in each, into segments which are similar respectively, the chords are proportional to the corresponding diameters.*

The proof is instantaneous if we join the ends of each chord to the centre of the circle which it divides, when we obtain two similar triangles.

(2) *If :g:`$D$ <0232.g2:D>` be any point on the circle circumscribed about a triangle :g:`$ABC$ <0232.g2:< A B C >>`, and :g:`$DX$ <0232.g2:/ D X />`, :g:`$DY$ <0232.g2:/ D Y />`, :g:`$DZ$ <0232.g2:/ D Z />` be perpendicular to the sides :g:`$BC$ <0232.g2:/ B C />`, :g:`$CA$ <0232.g2:/ C A />`, :g:`$AB$ <0232.g2:/ A B />` of the triangle respectively, then :g:`$X$ <0232.g2:X>`, :g:`$Y$ <0232.g2:Y>`, :g:`$Z$ <0232.g2:Z>` lie in one straight line; and, conversely, if the feet of the perpendiculars from any point :g:`$D$ <0232.g2:D>` on the sides of a triangle lie in one straight line, :g:`$D$ <0232.g2:D>` lies on the circle circumscribed about the triangle.*

The proof depending on III. 21, 22 is well known.

Now suppose that :g:`$D$ <0232.g1:D>` is any point in the plane of a triangle :g:`$ABC$ <0232.g1:< A B C >>`, and that :g:`$DX$ <0232.g1:/ D X />`, :g:`$DY$ <0232.g1:/ D Y />`, :g:`$DZ$ <0232.g1:/ D Z />` are perpendicular to the sides :g:`$BC$ <0232.g1:/ B C />`, :g:`$CA$ <0232.g1:/ C A />`, :g:`$AB$ <0232.g1:/ A B />` respectively.

Join :g:`$YZ$ <0232.g1:/ Y Z />`, :g:`$DA$ <0232.g1:/ D A />`.

Then, since the angles at :g:`$Y$ <0232.g1:Y>`, :g:`$Z$ <0232.g1:Z>` are right, :g:`$A$ <0232.g1:A>`, :g:`$Y$ <0232.g1:Y>`, :g:`$D$ <0232.g1:D>`, :g:`$Z$ <0232.g1:Z>` lie on a circle of which :g:`$DA$ <0232.g1:/ D A />` is the diameter.

And :g:`$YZ$ <0232.g1:/ Y Z />` divides this circle into segments which are similar respectively to the segments into which :g:`$BC$ <0232.g1:/ B C />` divides the circle circumscribing :g:`$ABC$ <0232.g1:< A B C >>`, since the angles :g:`$ZAY$ <0232.g1:< Z A Y )>`, :g:`$BAC$ <0232.g1:< B A C )>` coincide, and their supplements are equal.

Therefore, if *d* be the diameter of the circle circumscribing :g:`$ABC$ <0232.g1:< A B C >>`,

.. assertion::

   :g:`$BC$ <0232.g1:/ B C />` is to *d* as :g:`$YZ$ <0232.g1:/ Y Z />` is to :g:`$DA$ <0232.g1:/ D A />`;

.. assertion::

   and therefore the rectangle :g:`$AD$ <0232.g1:/ A D />`, :g:`$BC$ <0232.g1:/ B C />` is equal to the rectangle *d*, :g:`$YZ$ <0232.g1:/ Y Z />`.

.. assertion::

   Similarly the rectangle :g:`$BD$ <0232.g1:/ B D />`, :g:`$CA$ <0232.g1:/ C A />` is equal to the rectangle *d*, :g:`$ZX$ <0232.g1:/ Z X />`, and the rectangle :g:`$CD$ <0232.g1:/ C D />`, :g:`$AB$ <0232.g1:/ A B />` is equal to the rectangle *d*, :g:`$XY$ <0232.g1:/ X Y />`.

Hence, in a quadrilateral in general, the rectangle contained by the diagonals is less than the sum of the rectangles contained by the pairs of opposite sides.

.. picture:: 0232.g1.svg
   :alt: Dr. Lachlan's first figure

Next, suppose that :g:`$D$ <0232.g2:D>` lies on the circle circumscribed about :g:`$ABC$ <0232.g2:< A B C >>`, but so that :g:`$A$ <0232.g2:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0232.g2:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0232.g2:C>`, :g:`$D$ <0232.g2:D>` follow each other on the circle in this order, as in the figure annexed.

Let :g:`$DX$ <0232.g2:/ D X />`, :g:`$DY$ <0232.g2:/ D Y />`, :g:`$DZ$ <0232.g2:/ D Z />` be perpendicular to :g:`$BC$ <0232.g2:/ B C />`, :g:`$CA$ <0232.g2:/ C A />`, :g:`$AB$ <0232.g2:/ A B />` respectively, so that :g:`$X$ <0232.g2:X>`, :g:`$Y$ <0232.g2:Y>`, :g:`$Z$ <0232.g2:Z>` are in a straight line.

.. picture:: 0232.g2.svg
   :alt: Dr. Lachlan's second figure

Then, since the rectangles :g:`$AD$ <0232.g2:/ A D />`, :g:`$BC$ <0232.g2:/ B C />`; :g:`$BD$ <0232.g2:/ B D />`, :g:`$CA$ <0232.g2:/ C A />`; :g:`$CD$ <0232.g2:/ C D />`, :g:`$AB$ <0232.g2:/ A B />` are equal to the rectangles *d*, :g:`$YZ$ <0232.g2:/ Y Z />`; *d*, :g:`$ZX$ <0232.g2:/ Z X />`; *d*, :g:`$XY$ <0232.g2:/ X Y />` respectively, and :g:`$XZ$ <0232.g2:/ X Z />` is equal to the sum of
:g:`$XY$ <0232.g2:/ X Y />`, :g:`$YZ$ <0232.g2:/ Y Z />`, so that the rectangle *d*, :g:`$XZ$ <0232.g2:/ X Z />` is equal to the sum of the rectangles *d*, :g:`$XY$ <0232.g2:/ X Y />` and *d*, :g:`$YZ$ <0232.g2:/ Y Z />`, it follows that

.. _folio-ii-0233:

.. folio:: 0233
   :page: 227

.. assertion::

   the rectangle :g:`$AC$ <0232.g2:/ A C />`, :g:`$BD$ <0232.g2:/ B D />` is equal to the sum of the rectangles :g:`$AD$ <0232.g2:/ A D />`, :g:`$BC$ <0232.g2:/ B C />` and :g:`$AB$ <0232.g2:/ A B />`, :g:`$CD$ <0232.g2:/ C D />`.

$Conversely$, if the latter statement is true, while we are supposed to know nothing about the position of :g:`$D$ <0232.g2:D>`, it follows that

.. assertion::

   :g:`$XZ$ <0232.g2:/ X Z />` must be equal to the sum of :g:`$XY$ <0232.g2:/ X Y />`, :g:`$YZ$ <0232.g2:/ Y Z />`,

so that :g:`$X$ <0232.g2:X>`, :g:`$Y$ <0232.g2:Y>`, :g:`$Z$ <0232.g2:Z>` must be in a straight line.

Hence, from the theorem (2) above, it follows that :g:`$D$ <0232.g2:D>` must lie on the circle circumscribed about :g:`$ABC$ <0232.g2:< A B C >>`, *i.e.* that :g:`$ABCD$ <0232.g2:< A B C D >>` is a quadrilateral about which a circle can be described.

All the above propositions can be proved on the basis of Book III. and without using Book VI., since it is possible by the aid of III. 21 and 35 alone to prove that *in equiangular triangles the rectangles contained by the non-corresponding sides about equal angles are equal to one another* (a result arrived at by combining VI. 4 and VI. 16). This is the method adopted by Casey, H. M. Taylor, and Lachlan; but I fail to see any particular advantage in it.

PROPOSITION E.
--------------

Lastly, the following proposition may be given which :person:`Playfair <playfair>` added as VI. E. It appears in the $Data$ of Euclid, Prop. 93, and may be thus enunciated.

.. enunciation::

   If the angle :g:`$BAC$ <0233.g1:< B A C )>` of a triangle :g:`$ABC$ <0233.g1:< A B C >>` be bisected by the straight line :g:`$AD$ <0233.g1:/ A D />` meeting the circle circumscribed about the triangle in :g:`$D$ <0233.g1:D>`, and if :g:`$BD$ <0233.g1:/ B D />` be joined, then the sum of :g:`$BA$ <0233.g1:/ B A />`, :g:`$AC$ <0233.g1:/ A C />` is to :g:`$AD$ <0233.g1:/ A D />` as :g:`$BC$ <0233.g1:/ B C />` is to :g:`$BD$ <0233.g1:/ B D />`.

Join :g:`$CD$ <0233.g1:/ C D />`. Then, since :g:`$AD$ <0233.g1:/ A D />` bisects the angle :g:`$BAC$ <0233.g1:< B A C )>`, the subtended arcs :g:`$BD$ <0233.g1:( O B )< B O D >>`, :g:`$DC$ <0233.g1:( O D )< D O C >>`, and therefore the chords :g:`$BD$ <0233.g1:/ B D />`, :g:`$DC$ <0233.g1:/ D C />`, are :eq:`equal <0233.g1:( O B )< B O D > = 0233.g1:( O D )< D O C >; 0233.g1:/ B D / = 0233.g1:/ D C />`.

.. picture:: 0233.g1.svg
   :alt: Proposition E

(1) The result can now be easily deduced from :person:`Ptolemy <ptolemy>`'s theorem.

For the rectangle :g:`$AD$ <0233.g1:/ A D />`, :g:`$BC$ <0233.g1:/ B C />` is equal to the sum of the rectangles :g:`$AB$ <0233.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$DC$ <0233.g1:/ D C />` and :g:`$AC$ <0233.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$BD$ <0233.g1:/ B D />`, *i.e.* (since :g:`$BD$ <0233.g1:/ B D />`, :g:`$CD$ <0233.g1:/ C D />` are :eq:`equal <0233.g1:/ B D / = 0233.g1:/ C D />`) to the rectangle contained by :g:`$BA$ <0233.g1:/ B A />` + :g:`$AC$ <0233.g1:/ A C />` and :g:`$BD$ <0233.g1:/ B D />`.

.. assertion::

   Therefore the sum of :g:`$BA$ <0233.g1:/ B A />`, :g:`$AC$ <0233.g1:/ A C />` is to :g:`$AD$ <0233.g1:/ A D />` as :g:`$BC$ <0233.g1:/ B C />` is to :g:`$BD$ <0233.g1:/ B D />`. :just:`[VI. 16] <VI.16>`

(2) Euclid proves it differently in $Data$, Prop. 93.

Let :g:`$AD$ <0233.g1:/ A D />` meet :g:`$BC$ <0233.g1:/ B C />` in :g:`$E$ <0233.g1:E>`.

Then, since :g:`$AE$ <0233.g1:/ A E />` bisects the angle :g:`$BAC$ <0233.g1:< B A C )>`,

.. assertion:: [VI. 3] <VI.3>

   :g:`$BA$ <0233.g1:/ B A />` is to :g:`$AC$ <0233.g1:/ A C />` as :g:`$BE$ <0233.g1:/ B E />` to :g:`$EC$ <0233.g1:/ E C />`,

or, alternately,

.. assertion:: [V. 16] <V.16>

   :g:`$AB$ <0233.g1:/ A B />` is to :g:`$BE$ <0233.g1:/ B E />` as :g:`$AC$ <0233.g1:/ A C />` to :g:`$CE$ <0233.g1:/ C E />`.

Therefore also

.. assertion:: [V. 12] <V.12>

   :g:`$BA$ <0233.g1:/ B A />` + :g:`$AC$ <0233.g1:/ A C />` is to :g:`$BC$ <0233.g1:/ B C />` as :g:`$AC$ <0233.g1:/ A C />` to :g:`$CE$ <0233.g1:/ C E />`.

Again, since the angles :g:`$BAD$ <0233.g1:< B A D )>`, :g:`$EAC$ <0233.g1:< E A C )>` are :eq:`equal <0233.g1:< B A D ) = 0233.g1:< E A C )>`, and the angles :g:`$ADB$ <0233.g1:< A D B )>`, :g:`$ACE$ <0233.g1:< A C E )>` are also :eq:`equal <0233.g1:< A D B ) = 0233.g1:< A C E )>`, :just:`[III. 21] <III.21>`

.. assertion::

   the triangles :g:`$ABD$ <0233.g1:< A B D >>`, :g:`$AEC$ <0233.g1:< A E C >>` are :eq:`equiangular <0233.g1:< B A D ) = 0233.g1:< E A C ); 0233.g1:< A D B ) = 0233.g1:< A C E )>`.

Therefore

.. assertion:: [VI. 4] <VI.4>

   :g:`$AC$ <0233.g1:/ A C />` is to :g:`$CE$ <0233.g1:/ C E />` as :g:`$AD$ <0233.g1:/ A D />` to :g:`$BD$ <0233.g1:/ B D />`.

.. _folio-ii-0234:

.. folio:: 0234
   :page: 228

Hence

.. assertion:: [V. 11] <V.11>

   :g:`$BA$ <0233.g1:/ B A />` + :g:`$AC$ <0233.g1:/ A C />` is to :g:`$BC$ <0233.g1:/ B C />` as :g:`$AD$ <0233.g1:/ A D />` to :g:`$BD$ <0233.g1:/ B D />`,

and, alternately,

.. assertion:: [V. 16] <V.16>

   :g:`$BA$ <0233.g1:/ B A />` + :g:`$AC$ <0233.g1:/ A C />` is to :g:`$AD$ <0233.g1:/ A D />` as :g:`$BC$ <0233.g1:/ B C />` to :g:`$BD$ <0233.g1:/ B D />`.

Euclid concludes that, if the circle :g:`$ABC$ <0233.g1:( A B C )>` is given in magnitude, and the chord :g:`$BC$ <0233.g1:/ B C />` cuts off a segment of it containing a given angle (so that, by $Data$ Prop. 87, :g:`$BC$ <0233.g1:/ B C />` and also :g:`$BD$ <0233.g1:/ B D />` are given in magnitude),

.. assertion::

   the ratio of :g:`$BA$ <0233.g1:/ B A />` + :g:`$AC$ <0233.g1:/ A C />` to :g:`$AD$ <0233.g1:/ A D />` is given,

and further that (since, by similar triangles, :g:`$BD$ <0233.g1:/ B D />` is to :g:`$DE$ <0233.g1:/ D E />` as :g:`$AC$ <0233.g1:/ A C />` is to :g:`$CE$ <0233.g1:/ C E />`, while :g:`$BA$ <0233.g1:/ B A />` + :g:`$AC$ <0233.g1:/ A C />` is to :g:`$BC$ <0233.g1:/ B C />` as :g:`$AC$ <0233.g1:/ A C />` is to :g:`$CE$ <0233.g1:/ C E />`),

.. assertion::

   the rectangle (:g:`$BA$ <0233.g1:/ B A />` + :g:`$AC$ <0233.g1:/ A C />`), :g:`$DE$ <0233.g1:/ D E />`, being equal to the rectangle :g:`$BC$ <0233.g1:/ B C />`, :g:`$BD$ <0233.g1:/ B D />`, is also given.
