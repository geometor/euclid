PROPOSITION 3.
==============

.. enunciation::

   Given three numbers not prime to one another, to find their greatest common measure.

Let :g:`$A$ <0306.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0306.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0306.g1:C>` be the three given numbers not prime to one another ;

thus it is required to find the greatest common measure of :g:`$A$ <0306.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0306.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0306.g1:C>`.

For let the greatest common measure, :g:`$D$ <0306.g1:D>`, of the two numbers :g:`$A$ <0306.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0306.g1:B>` be taken ; :just:`[VII. 2] <VII.2>`

then :g:`$D$ <0306.g1:D>` either measures, or does not measure, :g:`$C$ <0306.g1:C>`.

.. picture:: 0306.g1.svg
   :alt: Proposition 3 Diagram

First, let it measure it.

.. subclause::

   But it measures :g:`$A$ <0306.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0306.g1:B>` also ;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$D$ <0306.g1:D>` measures :g:`$A$ <0306.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0306.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0306.g1:C>` ;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$D$ <0306.g1:D>` is a common measure of :g:`$A$ <0306.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0306.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0306.g1:C>`.

I say that it is also the greatest.

.. _folio-ii-0307:

.. folio:: 0307
   :page: 301

.. subclause::

   For, if :g:`$D$ <0306.g1:D>` is not the greatest common measure of :g:`$A$ <0306.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0306.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0306.g1:C>`, some number which is greater than :g:`$D$ <0306.g1:D>` will measure the numbers :g:`$A$ <0306.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0306.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0306.g1:C>`.

Let such a number measure them, and let it be :g:`$E$ <0306.g1:E>`.

.. subclause::

   Since then :g:`$E$ <0306.g1:E>` measures :g:`$A$ <0306.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0306.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0306.g1:C>`, it will also measure :g:`$A$ <0306.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0306.g1:B>` ;

.. assertion::

   therefore it will also measure the greatest common measure of :g:`$A$ <0306.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0306.g1:B>`. :just:`[VII. 2, Por.]`

.. subclause::

   But the greatest common measure of :g:`$A$ <0306.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0306.g1:B>` is :g:`$D$ <0306.g1:D>` ;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$E$ <0306.g1:E>` measures :g:`$D$ <0306.g1:D>`, the greater the less : which is impossible.

.. assertion::

   Therefore no number which is greater than :g:`$D$ <0306.g1:D>` will measure the numbers :g:`$A$ <0306.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0306.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0306.g1:C>` ;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$D$ <0306.g1:D>` is the greatest common measure of :g:`$A$ <0306.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0306.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0306.g1:C>`.

Next, let :g:`$D$ <0306.g1:D>` not measure :g:`$C$ <0306.g1:C>` ;

I say first that :g:`$C$ <0306.g1:C>`, :g:`$D$ <0306.g1:D>` are not prime to one another.

.. subclause::

   For, since :g:`$A$ <0306.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0306.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0306.g1:C>` are not prime to one another, some number will measure them.

.. subclause::

   Now that which measures :g:`$A$ <0306.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0306.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0306.g1:C>` will also measure :g:`$A$ <0306.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0306.g1:B>`, and will measure :g:`$D$ <0306.g1:D>`, the greatest common measure of :g:`$A$ <0306.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0306.g1:B>`. :just:`[VII. 2, Por.]`

.. subclause::

   But it measures :g:`$C$ <0306.g1:C>` also ;

.. assertion::

   therefore some number will measure the numbers :g:`$D$ <0306.g1:D>`, :g:`$C$ <0306.g1:C>` ;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$D$ <0306.g1:D>`, :g:`$C$ <0306.g1:C>` are not prime to one another.

Let then their greatest common measure :g:`$E$ <0306.g1:E>` be taken. :just:`[VII. 2] <VII.2>`

.. subclause::

   Then, since :g:`$E$ <0306.g1:E>` measures :g:`$D$ <0306.g1:D>`, and :g:`$D$ <0306.g1:D>` measures :g:`$A$ <0306.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0306.g1:B>`,

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$E$ <0306.g1:E>` also measures :g:`$A$ <0306.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0306.g1:B>`.

.. subclause::

   But it measures :g:`$C$ <0306.g1:C>` also ;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$E$ <0306.g1:E>` measures :g:`$A$ <0306.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0306.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0306.g1:C>` ;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$E$ <0306.g1:E>` is a common measure of :g:`$A$ <0306.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0306.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0306.g1:C>`.

I say next that it is also the greatest.

.. subclause::

   For, if :g:`$E$ <0306.g1:E>` is not the greatest common measure of :g:`$A$ <0306.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0306.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0306.g1:C>`, some number which is greater than :g:`$E$ <0306.g1:E>` will measure the numbers :g:`$A$ <0306.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0306.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0306.g1:C>`.

Let such a number measure them, and let it be :g:`$F$ <0306.g1:F>`.

.. _folio-ii-0308:

.. folio:: 0308
   :page: 302

.. subclause::

   Now, since :g:`$F$ <0306.g1:F>` measures :g:`$A$ <0306.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0306.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0306.g1:C>`, it also measures :g:`$A$ <0306.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0306.g1:B>` ;

.. assertion::

   therefore it will also measure the greatest common measure of :g:`$A$ <0306.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0306.g1:B>`. :just:`[VII. 2, Por.]`

.. subclause::

   But the greatest common measure of :g:`$A$ <0306.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0306.g1:B>` is :g:`$D$ <0306.g1:D>` ;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$F$ <0306.g1:F>` measures :g:`$D$ <0306.g1:D>`.

.. subclause::

   And it measures :g:`$C$ <0306.g1:C>` also ;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$F$ <0306.g1:F>` measures :g:`$D$ <0306.g1:D>`, :g:`$C$ <0306.g1:C>` ;

.. assertion::

   therefore it will also measure the greatest common measure of :g:`$D$ <0306.g1:D>`, :g:`$C$ <0306.g1:C>`. :just:`[VII. 2, Por.]`

.. subclause::

   But the greatest common measure of :g:`$D$ <0306.g1:D>`, :g:`$C$ <0306.g1:C>` is :g:`$E$ <0306.g1:E>` ;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$F$ <0306.g1:F>` measures :g:`$E$ <0306.g1:E>`, the greater the less : which is impossible.

.. assertion::

   Therefore no number which is greater than :g:`$E$ <0306.g1:E>` will measure the numbers :g:`$A$ <0306.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0306.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0306.g1:C>` ;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$E$ <0306.g1:E>` is the greatest common measure of :g:`$A$ <0306.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0306.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0306.g1:C>`.

.. qed::

   :just:`Q. E. D.`
