Commentary on Proposition VII.19
================================

Back to :ref:`Proposition VII.19 <VII.19>`.

.. folio:: 0325
   :page: 319

If

$$a : b = c : d,$$

then

$$ad = bc; \text{ and conversely.}$$

The proof is equivalent to the following.

(1)

$$\begin{aligned}
ac : ad &= c : d & \text{[VII. 17]} \\
&= a : b. \\
\text{But}\qquad a : b &= ac : bc. & \text{[VII. 18]} \\
\text{Therefore}\qquad ac : ad &= ac : bc, \\
\text{or}\qquad ad &= bc.
\end{aligned}$$

.. _folio-ii-0326:

.. folio:: 0326
   :page: 320

(2) Since

$$ad = bc,$$

$$ac : ad = ac : bc.$$

$$\begin{aligned}
\text{But}\qquad ac : ad &= c : d, & \text{[VII. 17]} \\
\text{and}\qquad ac : bc &= a : b. & \text{[VII. 18]} \\
\text{Therefore}\qquad a : b &= c : d.
\end{aligned}$$

As indicated in the note on VII. 14 above, :person:`Heiberg <heiberg>` regards Euclid as basing the inferences contained in the last step of part (1) of this proof and in the first step of part (2) on the propositions v. 9 and v. 7 respectively, since he has not proved those propositions separately for numbers in this Book. I prefer to suppose that he regarded the inferences as obvious and not needing proof, in view of the definition of numbers which are in proportion. E.g., if $ac$ is the same fraction ("part" or "parts") of $ad$ that $ac$ is of $bc$, it is obvious that $ad$ must be equal to $bc$.

:person:`Heiberg <heiberg>` omits from his text here, and relegates to an Appendix, a proposition appearing in the manuscripts V, p, $\phi$ to the effect that, *if three numbers be proportional, the product of the extremes is equal to the square of the mean, and conversely*. It does not appear in P in the first hand, B has it in the margin only, and :person:`Campanus <campanus>` omits it, remarking that Euclid does not give the proposition about *three* proportionals as he does in VI. 17, since it is easily proved by the proposition just given. Moreover :person:`an-Nairīzī <an-nairizi>` quotes the proposition about three proportionals as an observation on VII. 19 probably due to :person:`Heron <heron>` (who is mentioned by name in the preceding paragraph).
