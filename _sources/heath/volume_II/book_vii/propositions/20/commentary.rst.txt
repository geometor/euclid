Commentary on Proposition VII.20
================================

Back to :ref:`Proposition VII.20 <VII.20>`.

.. folio:: 0327
   :page: 321

If :math:`a`, :math:`b` are the least numbers among those which have the same ratio (i.e. if :math:`a/b` is a fraction in its lowest terms), and :math:`c`, :math:`d` are any others in the same ratio, i.e. if

.. math::

   a : b = c : d,

then :math:`a = \frac{1}{n} c` and :math:`b = \frac{1}{n} d`, where :math:`n` is some integer.

The proof is by :term:`*reductio ad absurdum* <reductio-ad-absurdum>`, thus.

[Since :math:`a < c`, :math:`a` is some proper fraction ("part" or "parts") of :math:`c`, by VII. 4.]

Now :math:`a` cannot be equal to :math:`\frac{m}{n} c`, where :math:`m` is an integer less than :math:`n` but greater than 1.

For, if :math:`a = \frac{m}{n} c`, :math:`b = \frac{m}{n} d` also. :just:`[VII. 13 and Def. 20] <VII.13, VII.def.20>`

Take each of the :math:`m` parts of :math:`a` with each of the :math:`m` parts of :math:`b`, two and two; the ratio of the members of all pairs is the same ratio :math:`\frac{1}{m} a : \frac{1}{m} b`.

Therefore

.. math::

   \frac{1}{m} a : \frac{1}{m} b = a : b. \qquad \text{[VII. 12]}

But :math:`\frac{1}{m} a` and :math:`\frac{1}{m} b` are respectively less than :math:`a`, :math:`b` and they are in the same ratio : which contradicts the hypothesis.

.. _folio-ii-0328:

.. folio:: 0328
   :page: 322

Hence :math:`a` can only be "a part" of :math:`c`, or

.. math::

   a \text{ is of the form } \frac{1}{n} c,

and therefore

.. math::

   b \text{ is of the form } \frac{1}{n} d.

Here also :person:`Heiberg <heiberg>` omits a proposition which was no doubt interpolated by Theon (B, V, p, :math:`\phi` have it as VII. 22, but P only has it in the margin and in a later hand; :person:`Campanus <campanus>` also omits it) proving for numbers the *ex aequali* proposition when "the proportion is perturbed," i.e. (cf. enunciation of V. 22) if

.. math::

   \begin{aligned}
   a : b &= e : f, \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(1) \\
   \text{and}\qquad b : c &= d : e, \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(2) \\
   \text{then}\qquad a : c &= d : f.
   \end{aligned}

The proof (see :person:`Heiberg <heiberg>`'s Appendix) depends on VII. 19. From (1) we have

.. math::

   af = be,

and from (2)

.. math::

   be = cd. \qquad \text{[VII. 19]}

Therefore

.. math::

   af = cd,

and accordingly

.. math::

   a : c = d : f. \qquad \text{[VII. 19]}
