Commentary on Book X Definition 3
=================================

Back to :ref:`Book X Definitions <X.def>`.

.. container:: center

   Τούτων ὑποκειμένων δείκνυται, ὅτι τῇ προτεθείσῃ εὐθείᾳ ὑπάρχουσιν εὐθεῖαι πλήθει ἄπειροι σύμμετροί τε καὶ ἀσύμμετροι αἱ μὲν :term:`μήκει <in-length>` μόνον, αἱ δὲ καὶ :term:`δυνάμει <in-square>`. καλείσθω οὖν ἡ μὲν προτεθεῖσα :term:`εὐθεῖα <straight-line>` ῥητή, καὶ αἱ ταύτῃ σύμμετροι εἴτε :term:`μήκει <in-length>` καὶ :term:`δυνάμει <in-square>` εἴτε :term:`δυνάμει <in-square>` μόνον ῥηταί, αἱ δὲ ταύτῃ ἀσύμμετροι ἄλογοι καλείσθωσαν.

The first sentence of the definition is decidedly elliptical. It should, strictly speaking, assert that "with a given straight line there are an infinite number of straight lines which are (1) commensurable either (:math:`a`) in square only or (:math:`b`) in square and in length also, and (2) incommensurable, either (:math:`a`) in length only or (:math:`b`) in length and in square also."

The relativity of the terms *rational* and *irrational* is well brought out in this definition. We may set out *any straight line* and call it rational, and it is then with reference to this assumed rational straight line that others are called *rational* or *irrational*.

We should carefully note that the signification of *rational* in Euclid is wider than in our terminology. With him, not only is a straight line commensurable in *length* with a rational straight line rational, but a straight line is rational which is commensurable with a rational straight line *in square only*. That is, if :math:`\rho` is a rational straight line, not only is :math:`\frac{m}{n}\rho` rational, where :math:`m, n` are integers and
:math:`m/n` in its lowest terms is not square, but :math:`\sqrt{\frac{m}{n}} . \rho` is *rational* also. We should in this case call :math:`\sqrt{\frac{m}{n}} . \rho` irrational. It would appear that Euclid's terminology here differed as much from that of his predecessors as it does from ours. We are familiar with the phrase :term:`ἄρρητος διάμετρος τῆς πεμπάδος <irrational-diameter-of-five>` by which :person:`Plato <plato>` (evidently after the Pythagoreans) describes the diagonal of a square on a straight line containing 5 units of length. This "inexpressible diameter of five (squared)" means :math:`\sqrt{50}`, in contrast to the ῥητὴ :term:`διάμετρος <diameter>`, the "expressible diameter" of the same square, by which is meant the approximation :math:`\sqrt{50 - 1}`, or :math:`7`. Thus for Euclid's predecessors :math:`\sqrt{\frac{m}{n}} . \rho` would apparently not have been rational but :term:`ἄρρητος <irrational>`, "inexpressible," i.e. irrational.

I shall throughout my notes on this Book denote a *rational straight line* in Euclid's sense by :math:`\rho`, and by :math:`\rho` and :math:`\sigma` when two different rational straight lines are required. Wherever then I use :math:`\rho` or :math:`\sigma`, it must be remembered that :math:`\rho, \sigma` may have either of the forms :math:`a, \sqrt{k} . a`, where :math:`a` represents :math:`a` units of length, :math:`a` being either an integer or of the form :math:`m/n`, where :math:`m, n` are both integers, and :math:`k` is an integer or of the form :math:`m/n` (where both :math:`m, n` are integers) but not square. In other words, :math:`\rho, \sigma` may have either of the forms :math:`a` or :math:`\sqrt{A}`, where :math:`A` represents :math:`A` units of *area* and :math:`A` is integral or of the form :math:`m/n`, where :math:`m, n` are both integers. It has been the habit of writers to give :math:`a` and :math:`\sqrt{a}` as the alternative forms of :math:`\rho`, but I shall always use :math:`\sqrt{A}` for the second in order to keep the dimensions right, because it must be borne in mind throughout that :math:`\rho` is an *irrational straight line*.

As Euclid extends the signification of *rational* (:term:`ῥητός <rational>`, literally *expressible*), so he limits the scope of the term :term:`ἄλογος <irrational>` (literally *having no ratio*) as applied to straight lines. That this limitation was started by himself may perhaps be inferred from the form of words "*let straight lines incommensurable with it be called irrational*." Irrational straight lines then are with Euclid straight lines commensurable *neither in length nor in square* with the assumed rational straight line. :math:`\sqrt{k} . a` where :math:`k` is not square is not irrational; :math:`\sqrt[4]{k} . a` is irrational, and so (as we shall see later on) is :math:`(\sqrt{k} \pm \sqrt{\lambda}) a`.
