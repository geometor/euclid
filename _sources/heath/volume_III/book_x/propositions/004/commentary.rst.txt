Commentary on Proposition X.4
=============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition X.4 <X.4>`.

.. _folio-iii-0028:

.. folio:: 0028
   :page: 24

This proposition again corresponds exactly to VII. 3 for numbers. As there Euclid thinks it necessary to prove that, :math:`a, b, c` not being prime to one another, :math:`d` and :math:`c` are also not prime to one another, so here he thinks it necessary to prove that :math:`d, c` are commensurable, as they must be since any common measure of :math:`a, b` must be a measure of their greatest common measure :math:`d` (X. 3, Por.).

The argument in the proof that :math:`e`, the greatest common measure of :math:`d, c`, is the greatest common measure of :math:`a, b, c`, is the same as that in VII. 3 and X. 3.

The Porism contains the extension of the process to the case of four or more magnitudes, corresponding to :person:`Heron <heron>`'s remark with regard to the similar extension of VII. 3 to the case of four or more *numbers*.
