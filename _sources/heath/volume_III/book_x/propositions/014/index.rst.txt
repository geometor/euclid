PROPOSITION 14.
===============

.. enunciation::

   If four straight lines be proportional, and the square on the first be greater than the square on the second by the square on a straight line commensurable with the first,\ [#f1]_ the square on the third will also be greater than the square on the fourth by the square on a straight line commensurable with the third.

.. enunciation::

   And, if the square on the first be greater than the square on the second by the square on a straight line incommensurable with the first, the square on the third will also be greater than the square on the fourth by the square on a straight line incommensurable with the third.

Let :g:`$A$ <0041.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0041.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0041.g1:C>`, :g:`$D$ <0041.g1:D>` be four straight lines in proportion, so that, as :g:`$A$ <0041.g1:A>` is to :g:`$B$ <0041.g1:B>`, so is :g:`$C$ <0041.g1:C>` to :g:`$D$ <0041.g1:D>`; and let the square on :g:`$A$ <0041.g1:A>` be greater than the square on :g:`$B$ <0041.g1:B>` by the square on :g:`$E$ <0041.g1:E>`, and let the square on :g:`$C$ <0041.g1:C>` be greater than the square on :g:`$D$ <0041.g1:D>` by the square on :g:`$F$ <0041.g1:F>`; I say that, if :g:`$A$ <0041.g1:A>` is commensurable with :g:`$E$ <0041.g1:E>`, :g:`$C$ <0041.g1:C>` is also commensurable with :g:`$F$ <0041.g1:F>`, and, if :g:`$A$ <0041.g1:A>` is incommensurable with :g:`$E$ <0041.g1:E>`, :g:`$C$ <0041.g1:C>` is also incommensurable with :g:`$F$ <0041.g1:F>`.

.. picture:: 0041.g1.svg
   :alt: Proposition 14 Diagram

.. subclause::

   For since, as :g:`$A$ <0041.g1:A>` is to :g:`$B$ <0041.g1:B>`, so is :g:`$C$ <0041.g1:C>` to :g:`$D$ <0041.g1:D>`,

.. assertion::

   therefore also, as the square on :g:`$A$ <0041.g1:A>` is to the square on :g:`$B$ <0041.g1:B>`, so is the square on :g:`$C$ <0041.g1:C>` to the square on :g:`$D$ <0041.g1:D>`. :just:`[VI. 22] <VI.22>`

.. subclause::

   But the squares on :g:`$E$ <0041.g1:E>`, :g:`$B$ <0041.g1:B>` are equal to the square on :g:`$A$ <0041.g1:A>`, and the squares on :g:`$D$ <0041.g1:D>`, :g:`$F$ <0041.g1:F>` are equal to the square on :g:`$C$ <0041.g1:C>`.

.. _folio-iii-0042:

.. folio:: 0042
   :page: 38

.. assertion::

   Therefore, as the squares on :g:`$E$ <0041.g1:E>`, :g:`$B$ <0041.g1:B>` are to the square on :g:`$B$ <0041.g1:B>`, so are the squares on :g:`$D$ <0041.g1:D>`, :g:`$F$ <0041.g1:F>` to the square on :g:`$D$ <0041.g1:D>`;

.. assertion::

   therefore, :term:`*separando* <separando>`, as the square on :g:`$E$ <0041.g1:E>` is to the square on :g:`$B$ <0041.g1:B>`, so is the square on :g:`$F$ <0041.g1:F>` to the square on :g:`$D$ <0041.g1:D>`; :just:`[V. 17] <V.17>`

.. assertion::

   therefore also, as :g:`$E$ <0041.g1:E>` is to :g:`$B$ <0041.g1:B>`, so is :g:`$F$ <0041.g1:F>` to :g:`$D$ <0041.g1:D>`; :just:`[VI. 22] <VI.22>` therefore, inversely, as :g:`$B$ <0041.g1:B>` is to :g:`$E$ <0041.g1:E>`, so is :g:`$D$ <0041.g1:D>` to :g:`$F$ <0041.g1:F>`.

.. subclause::

   But, as :g:`$A$ <0041.g1:A>` is to :g:`$B$ <0041.g1:B>`, so also is :g:`$C$ <0041.g1:C>` to :g:`$D$ <0041.g1:D>`;

.. assertion:: [V. 22] <V.22>

   therefore, :term:`*ex aequali* <ex-aequali>`, as :g:`$A$ <0041.g1:A>` is to :g:`$E$ <0041.g1:E>`, so is :g:`$C$ <0041.g1:C>` to :g:`$F$ <0041.g1:F>`.

.. assertion::

   Therefore, if :g:`$A$ <0041.g1:A>` is commensurable with :g:`$E$ <0041.g1:E>`, :g:`$C$ <0041.g1:C>` is also commensurable with :g:`$F$ <0041.g1:F>`, and, if :g:`$A$ <0041.g1:A>` is incommensurable with :g:`$E$ <0041.g1:E>`, :g:`$C$ <0041.g1:C>` is also incommensurable with :g:`$F$ <0041.g1:F>`. :just:`[X. 11] <X.11>`

.. qed::

   Therefore etc.

.. [#f1] **with the first.** Euclid speaks of the square on the first (third) being greater than the square on the second (fourth) by the square on a straight line commensurable (incommensurable) "with *itself* (ἑαυτῇ)," and similarly in all like phrases throughout the Book. For clearness' sake I substitute "the first," "the third," or whatever it may be, for "*itself*" in these cases.
