Commentary on Proposition X.17
==============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition X.17 <X.17>`.

.. folio:: 0047
   :page: 43

This proposition gives the condition that the roots of the equation in :math:`x`,

.. math::

   ax - x^2 = \beta \left(= \frac{b^2}{4}\text{, say}\right),

are commensurable with :math:`a`, or that :math:`x` is expressible in terms of :math:`a` and integral numbers, i.e. is of the form :math:`\frac{m}{n}a`. No better proof can be found for the fact that Euclid and the Greeks used their solutions of quadratic equations for *numerical* problems. On no other assumption could an elaborate discussion of the conditions of incommensurability of the roots with given lengths or
with a given number of units of length be explained. In a purely *geometrical* solution the distinction between commensurable and incommensurable roots has no point, because each can equally easily be represented by straight lines. On the other hand, on the assumption that the *numerical* solution of quadratic equations was an important part of the system of the Greek geometers, the distinction between the cases where the roots are commensurable and incommensurable respectively with a given length or unit becomes of great importance. Since the Greeks had no means of *expressing* what we call an irrational number, the case of an equation with incommensurable roots could *only* be represented by them geometrically; and the geometrical representations had to serve instead of what we can express by formulae involving surds.

.. _folio-iii-0048:

.. folio:: 0048

Euclid proves in this proposition and the next that, :math:`x` being determined from the equation

.. math::

   x(a - x) = \frac{b^2}{4} \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots (1),

:math:`x`, :math:`(a - x)` are commensurable in length when :math:`\sqrt{a^2 - b^2}`, :math:`a` are so, and incommensurable in length when :math:`\sqrt{a^2 - b^2}`, :math:`a` are incommensurable; and conversely.

Observe the similarity of his proof to our algebraical method of solving the equation. :math:`a` being represented in the figure by :g:`BC <0046.g1:/ B C />`, and :math:`x` by :g:`CD <0046.g1:/ D C />`,

.. math::

   EF = ED = \frac{a}{2} - x

and

.. math::

   x(a - x) + \left(\frac{a}{2} - x\right)^2 = \frac{a^2}{4}, \qquad \text{by Eucl. II. 5.}

If we multiply throughout by 4,

.. math::

   4x(a - x) + 4\left(\frac{a}{2} - x\right)^2 = a^2,

whence, by (1),

.. math::

   b^2 + (a - 2x)^2 = a^2,

or

.. math::

   a^2 - b^2 = (a - 2x)^2,

and

.. math::

   \sqrt{a^2 - b^2} = a - 2x.

We have to prove in this proposition

(1) that, if :math:`x`, :math:`(a - x)` are commensurable in length, so are :math:`a`, :math:`\sqrt{a^2 - b^2}`,

(2) that, if :math:`a`, :math:`\sqrt{a^2 - b^2}` are commensurable in length, so are :math:`x`, :math:`(a - x)`.

(1) To prove that :math:`a`, :math:`a - 2x` are commensurable in length Euclid employs several successive steps, thus.

.. list-table::

   * -
     -
     -
   * - Since :math:`(a - x) \mathrel{⌢} x,`
     - :math:`a \mathrel{⌢} x.`
     - :just:`[X. 15] <X.15>`
   * - But
     - :math:`x \mathrel{⌢} 2x.`
     - :just:`[X. 6] <X.6>`
   * - Therefore
     - :math:`a \mathrel{⌢} 2x`
     - :just:`[X. 12] <X.12>`
   * -
     - :math:`\mathrel{⌢} (a - 2x).`
     - :just:`[X. 15] <X.15>`
   * - That is,
     - :math:`a \mathrel{⌢} \sqrt{a^2 - b^2}.`
     -
   * - (2) Since :math:`a \mathrel{⌢} \sqrt{a^2 - b^2},`
     - :math:`a \mathrel{⌢} a - 2x,`
     -
   * - whence
     - :math:`a \mathrel{⌢} 2x.`
     - :just:`[X. 15] <X.15>`
   * - But
     - :math:`2x \mathrel{⌢} x;`
     - :just:`[X. 6] <X.6>`
   * - therefore
     - :math:`a \mathrel{⌢} x,`
     - :just:`[X. 12] <X.12>`
   * - and hence
     - :math:`(a - x) \mathrel{⌢} x.`
     - :just:`[X. 15] <X.15>`

.. folio:: 0049

It is often more convenient to use the symmetrical form of equation in this and similar cases, viz.

.. math::

   \left.\begin{aligned} xy &= \frac{b^2}{4} \\ x + y &= a \end{aligned}\right\}

The result with this mode of expression is that

(1) if :math:`x \mathrel{⌢} y`, then :math:`a \mathrel{⌢} \sqrt{a^2 - b^2}`; and

(2) if :math:`a \mathrel{⌢} \sqrt{a^2 - b^2}`, then :math:`x \mathrel{⌢} y`. The truth of the proposition is even easier to see in this case, since :math:`(x - y)^2 = (a^2 - b^2)`.
