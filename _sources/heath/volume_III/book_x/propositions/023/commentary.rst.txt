Commentary on Proposition X.23
==============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition X.23 <X.23>`.

.. folio:: 0058
   :page: 54

As explained in the bracketed passage following this proposition, a straight line commensurable with a medial straight line in square only, as well as a straight line commensurable with it in length, is medial.

Algebraical notation shows this easily.

If :math:`k^{\frac{1}{4}} \rho` be the given straight line, :math:`\lambda k^{\frac{1}{4}} \rho` is a straight line commensurable in length with it and :math:`\sqrt{\lambda} \cdot k^{\frac{1}{4}} \rho` a straight line commensurable with it in square only.

But :math:`\lambda \rho` and :math:`\sqrt{\lambda} \cdot \rho` are both rational [X. Def. 3] and therefore can be expressed by :math:`\rho'`, and we thus arrive at :math:`k^{\frac{1}{4}} \rho'`, which is clearly medial.

Euclid's proof amounts to the following.

Apply both the areas :math:`\sqrt{k} \cdot \rho^2` and :math:`\lambda^2 \sqrt{k} \cdot \rho^2` (or :math:`\lambda \sqrt{k} \cdot \rho^2`) to a rational straight line :math:`\sigma`.

The breadths :math:`\sqrt{k} \cdot \frac{\rho^2}{\sigma}` and :math:`\lambda^2 \sqrt{k} \cdot \frac{\rho^2}{\sigma}` (or :math:`\lambda \sqrt{k} \cdot \frac{\rho^2}{\sigma}`) are in the ratio of the areas :math:`\sqrt{k} \cdot \rho^2` and :math:`\lambda^2 \sqrt{k} \cdot \rho^2` (or :math:`\lambda \sqrt{k} \cdot \rho^2`) themselves and are therefore commensurable.

Now [X. 22] :math:`\sqrt{k} \cdot \frac{\rho^2}{\sigma}` is rational but incommensurable with :math:`\sigma`.

Therefore :math:`\lambda^2 \sqrt{k} \cdot \frac{\rho^2}{\sigma}` (or :math:`\lambda \sqrt{k} \cdot \frac{\rho^2}{\sigma}`) is so also;

whence the area :math:`\lambda^2 \sqrt{k} \cdot \rho^2` (or :math:`\lambda \sqrt{k} \cdot \rho^2`) is contained by two rational straight lines commensurable in square only, so that :math:`\lambda k^{\frac{1}{4}} \rho` (or :math:`\sqrt{\lambda} \cdot k^{\frac{1}{4}} \rho`) is a medial straight line.

It is in the Porism that we have the first mention of a *medial area*. It is the area which is equal to the square on a medial straight line, an area, therefore, of the form :math:`k^{\frac{1}{2}} \rho^2`, which is, as a matter of fact, arrived at, though not named, before the *medial straight line* itself (X. 21).

The Porism states that :math:`\lambda k^{\frac{1}{2}} \rho^2` is a medial area, which is indeed obvious.
