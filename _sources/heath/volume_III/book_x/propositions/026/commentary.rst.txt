Commentary on Proposition X.26
==============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition X.26 <X.26>`.

.. folio:: 0063
   :page: 59

"Apply" the two given medial areas to one and the same rational straight line :math:`\rho`. They can then be written in the form :math:`\rho \,.\, k^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho`, :math:`\rho \,.\, \lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho`.

The difference is then :math:`(\sqrt{k} - \sqrt{\lambda})\rho^2`; and the proposition asserts that this cannot be rational, i.e. :math:`(\sqrt{k} - \sqrt{\lambda})` cannot be equal to :math:`k'`. Cf. the proposition corresponding to this in algebraical text-books.

To make Euclid's proof clear we will put :math:`x` for :math:`k^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho` and :math:`y` for :math:`\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho`.
Suppose

.. math::

   \rho(x - y) = \rho z,

and, if possible, let :math:`\rho z` be rational, so that :math:`z` must be rational and :math:`\frown \rho \text{ ...(1).}`

Since :math:`\rho x`, :math:`\rho y` are medial,

.. math::

   x \text{ and } y \text{ are respectively rational and } \smile \rho \text{ .................(2).}

From (1) and (2),

.. math::

   y \smile z.

Now

.. math::

   y : z = y^2 : yz,

so that

.. math::

   y^2 \smile yz.

.. _folio-iii-0064:

.. folio:: 0064
   :page: 60

But

.. math::

   y^2 + z^2 \frown y^2,

and

.. math::

   2yz \frown yz.

Therefore

.. math::

   y^2 + z^2 \smile 2yz,

whence

.. math::

   (y + z)^2 \smile (y^2 + z^2),

or

.. math::

   x^2 \smile (y^2 + z^2).

And :math:`(y^2 + z^2)` is rational; therefore :math:`x^2`, and consequently :math:`x`, is irrational.

But, by (2), :math:`x` is rational: which is impossible.

Therefore :math:`\rho z` is *not rational*.
