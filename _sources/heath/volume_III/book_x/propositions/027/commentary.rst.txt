Commentary on Proposition X.27
==============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition X.27 <X.27>`.

.. folio:: 0065
   :page: 61

Euclid takes two rational straight lines commensurable in square only, say :math:`\rho`, :math:`k^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho`. Find the mean proportional, i.e. :math:`k^{\frac{1}{4}}\rho`. Take :math:`x` such that

.. math::

   \rho : k^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho = k^{\frac{1}{4}}\rho : x \ \text{.........................................(1).}

This gives :math:`x = k^{\frac{3}{4}}\rho`, and the lines required are :math:`k^{\frac{1}{4}}\rho`, :math:`k^{\frac{3}{4}}\rho`. For (:math:`\alpha`) :math:`k^{\frac{1}{4}}\rho` is medial. And (:math:`\beta`), by (1), since :math:`\rho \sim k^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho`,

.. math::

   k^{\frac{1}{4}}\rho \sim k^{\frac{3}{4}}\rho,

whence [addition to X. 23], since :math:`k^{\frac{1}{4}}\rho` is medial,

.. math::

   k^{\frac{3}{4}}\rho \text{ is also medial.}

The medial straight lines thus found may take either of the forms

.. math::

   (1) \quad \sqrt{a\sqrt{B}}, \quad \sqrt{\frac{B\sqrt{B}}{a}} \quad \text{or} \quad (2) \quad \sqrt[4]{AB}, \quad \sqrt{B\sqrt{\frac{B}{A}}}.
