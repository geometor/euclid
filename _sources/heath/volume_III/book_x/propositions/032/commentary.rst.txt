Commentary on Proposition X.32
==============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition X.32 <X.32>`.

.. folio:: 0077
   :page: 73

I. Euclid takes three straight lines of the form $\rho$, $\rho\sqrt{\lambda}$, $\rho\sqrt{1-k^2}$, takes the mean proportional $\rho\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}}$ between the first two ....................(1), and then finds $x$ such that

$$\rho\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}} : \rho\sqrt{\lambda} = \rho\sqrt{1-k^2} : x \text{ .........................................(2),}$$

whence $x = \rho\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}}\sqrt{1-k^2}$, and the straight lines $\rho\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}}$, $\rho\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}}\sqrt{1-k^2}$ satisfy the given conditions.

Now ($\alpha$) $\rho\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}}$ is medial.

($\beta$) We have, from (1) and (2),

$$\rho : \rho\sqrt{1-k^2} = \rho\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}} : x \text{ .........................................(3),}$$

whence $x \mathbin{\sim} \rho\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}}$ ; and $x$ is therefore medial and $\mathbin{\sim} \rho\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}}$.

($\gamma$) $x\ .\ \rho\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}} = \rho\sqrt{\lambda}\ .\ \rho\sqrt{1-k^2}$. But the latter is medial ; [x. 21] therefore $x\ .\ \rho\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}}$, or $\rho\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}}\ .\ \rho\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}}\sqrt{1-k^2}$, is medial.

Lastly ($\delta$) $\rho$, $\rho\sqrt{1-k^2}$ have the remaining property in the enunciation ; therefore $\rho\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}}$, $\rho\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}}\sqrt{1-k^2}$ have it also. [x. 14]

(Euclid has not the assistance of symbols to prove the proportion (3) above. He therefore uses the lemmas $ab : bc = a : c$ and $d^2 : de = d : e$ to deduce from the relations

$$\left.\begin{array}{r} ab = d^2 \\ d : b = c : e \end{array}\right\}$$

and that $a : c = d : e$.)

The straight lines $\rho\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}}$, $\rho\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}}\sqrt{1-k^2}$ may take any of the following forms according as the straight lines first taken are (1) $a$, $\sqrt{B}$, $\sqrt{a^2-c^2}$, (2) $\sqrt{A}$, $\sqrt{B}$, $\sqrt{A-k^2A}$, (3) $\sqrt{A}$, $b$, $\sqrt{A-k^2A}$.

$$\begin{array}{lcc}
(1) & \sqrt{a\sqrt{B}}, & \dfrac{\sqrt{B(a^2-c^2)}}{\sqrt{a\sqrt{B}}} ; \\
(2) & \sqrt[4]{AB}, & \dfrac{\sqrt{B(A-k^2A)}}{\sqrt[4]{AB}} ; \\
(3) & \sqrt{b\sqrt{A}}, & \dfrac{b\sqrt{A-k^2A}}{\sqrt{b\sqrt{A}}}.
\end{array}$$

II. If the other conditions are the same, but the square on the first medial straight line is to exceed the square on the second by the square on a straight line *incommensurable* with the first, we begin with the three straight lines $\rho$, $\rho\sqrt{\lambda}$, $\dfrac{\rho}{\sqrt{1+k^2}}$, and the medial straight lines are

$$\rho\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}},\quad \dfrac{\rho\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}}}{\sqrt{1+k^2}}.$$

The possible forms are even more various in this case owing to the more various forms that the original lines may take, e.g.

$$\begin{array}{llll}
(1) & a, & \sqrt{B}, & \sqrt{a^2-C} ; \\
(2) & \sqrt{A}, & b, & \sqrt{A-c^2} ; \\
(3) & \sqrt{A}, & b, & \sqrt{A-C} ; \\
(4) & \sqrt{A}, & \sqrt{B}, & \sqrt{A-c^2} ; \\
(5) & \sqrt{A}, & \sqrt{B}, & \sqrt{A-C} ;
\end{array}$$

the medial straight lines corresponding to these being

$$\begin{array}{lcc}
(1) & \sqrt{a\sqrt{B}}, & \dfrac{\sqrt{B(a^2-C)}}{\sqrt{a\sqrt{B}}} ; \\
(2) & \sqrt{b\sqrt{A}}, & \dfrac{b\sqrt{A-c^2}}{\sqrt{b\sqrt{A}}} ; \\
(3) & \sqrt{b\sqrt{A}}, & \dfrac{b\sqrt{A-C}}{\sqrt{b\sqrt{A}}} ; \\
(4) & \sqrt[4]{AB}, & \dfrac{\sqrt{B(A-c^2)}}{\sqrt[4]{AB}} ; \\
(5) & \sqrt[4]{AB}, & \dfrac{\sqrt{B(A-C)}}{\sqrt[4]{AB}}.
\end{array}$$

LEMMA.
~~~~~~

Let :g:`ABC <0078.g1:< A B C >>` be a right-angled triangle having the angle :g:`A <0078.g1:< B A C )>` right, and let the :eq:`perpendicular <0078.g1:< A D B ) = 0078.g1:< A D C )>` :g:`AD <0078.g1:/ A D />` be drawn ;

I say that the rectangle :g:`CB <0078.g1:/ C B />`, :g:`BD <0078.g1:/ B D />` is equal to the square on :g:`BA <0078.g1:/ B A />`, the rectangle :g:`BC <0078.g1:/ B C />`, :g:`CD <0078.g1:/ C D />` equal to the square on :g:`CA <0078.g1:/ C A />`, the rectangle :g:`BD <0078.g1:/ B D />`, :g:`DC <0078.g1:/ D C />` equal to the square on :g:`AD <0078.g1:/ A D />`, and, further, the rectangle :g:`BC <0078.g1:/ B C />`, :g:`AD <0078.g1:/ A D />` equal to the rectangle :g:`BA <0078.g1:/ B A />`, :g:`AC <0078.g1:/ A C />`.

   :alt: Lemma diagram

And first that the rectangle :g:`CB <0078.g1:/ C B />`, :g:`BD <0078.g1:/ B D />` is equal to the square on :g:`BA <0078.g1:/ B A />`.

.. subclause::

   For, since in a right-angled triangle :g:`AD <0078.g1:/ A D />` has been drawn from the right angle :eq:`perpendicular <0078.g1:< A D B ) = 0078.g1:< A D C )>` to the base,

.. assertion::

   therefore the triangles :g:`ABD <0078.g1:< A B D >>`, :g:`ADC <0078.g1:< A D C >>` are similar both to the whole :g:`ABC <0078.g1:< A B C >>` and to one another. :just:`[VI. 8] <VI.8>`

.. subclause::

   And since the triangle :g:`ABC <0078.g1:< A B C >>` is similar to the triangle :g:`ABD <0078.g1:< A B D >>`,

.. assertion::

   therefore, as :g:`CB <0078.g1:/ C B />` is to :g:`BA <0078.g1:/ B A />`, so is :g:`BA <0078.g1:/ B A />` to :g:`BD <0078.g1:/ B D />` ; :just:`[VI. 4] <VI.4>` therefore the rectangle :g:`CB <0078.g1:/ C B />`, :g:`BD <0078.g1:/ B D />` is equal to the square on :g:`AB <0078.g1:/ A B />`. :just:`[VI. 17] <VI.17>`

.. subclause::

   For the same reason the rectangle :g:`BC <0078.g1:/ B C />`, :g:`CD <0078.g1:/ C D />` is also equal to the square on :g:`AC <0078.g1:/ A C />`.

.. subclause::

   And since, if in a right-angled triangle a perpendicular be drawn from the right angle to the base, the perpendicular so drawn is a mean proportional between the segments of the base, :just:`[VI. 8, Por.]`

.. assertion::

   therefore, as :g:`BD <0078.g1:/ B D />` is to :g:`DA <0078.g1:/ D A />`, so is :g:`AD <0078.g1:/ A D />` to :g:`DC <0078.g1:/ D C />` ; therefore the rectangle :g:`BD <0078.g1:/ B D />`, :g:`DC <0078.g1:/ D C />` is equal to the square on :g:`AD <0078.g1:/ A D />`. :just:`[VI. 17] <VI.17>`

I say that the rectangle :g:`BC <0078.g1:/ B C />`, :g:`AD <0078.g1:/ A D />` is also equal to the rectangle :g:`BA <0078.g1:/ B A />`, :g:`AC <0078.g1:/ A C />`.

.. subclause::

   For since, as we said, :g:`ABC <0078.g1:< A B C >>` is similar to :g:`ABD <0078.g1:< A B D >>`,

.. assertion:: [VI. 4] <VI.4>

   therefore, as :g:`BC <0078.g1:/ B C />` is to :g:`CA <0078.g1:/ C A />`, so is :g:`BA <0078.g1:/ B A />` to :g:`AD <0078.g1:/ A D />`.

.. assertion::

   Therefore the rectangle :g:`BC <0078.g1:/ B C />`, :g:`AD <0078.g1:/ A D />` is equal to the rectangle :g:`BA <0078.g1:/ B A />`, :g:`AC <0078.g1:/ A C />`. :just:`[VI. 16] <VI.16>`
