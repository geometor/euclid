PROPOSITION 44.
===============

.. enunciation::

   A second bimedial straight line is divided at one point only.

Let :g:`$AB$ <0099.g1:/ A B />` be a second bimedial straight line divided at :g:`$C$ <0099.g1:C>`, so that :g:`$AC$ <0099.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$CB$ <0099.g1:/ C B />` are medial straight lines commensurable in square only and containing a medial rectangle; :just:`[X. 38] <X.38>` it is then manifest that :g:`$C$ <0099.g1:C>` is not at the point of bisection, because the segments are not commensurable in length.

I say that :g:`$AB$ <0099.g1:/ A B />` is not so divided at another point.

.. picture:: 0099.g1.svg
   :alt: Proposition 44 diagram

For, if possible, let it be divided at :g:`$D$ <0099.g1:D>` also, so that :g:`$AC$ <0099.g1:/ A C />` is not the same with :g:`$DB$ <0099.g1:/ D B />`, but :g:`$AC$ <0099.g1:/ A C />` is supposed greater; it is then clear that the squares on :g:`$AD$ <0099.g1:/ A D />`, :g:`$DB$ <0099.g1:/ D B />` are also, as we proved above :just:`[Lemma]`, less than the squares on :g:`$AC$ <0099.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$CB$ <0099.g1:/ C B />`; and suppose that :g:`$AD$ <0099.g1:/ A D />`, :g:`$DB$ <0099.g1:/ D B />` are medial straight lines commensurable in square only and containing a medial rectangle.

Now let a rational straight line :g:`$EF$ <0099.g1:/ E F />` be set out, let there be applied to :g:`$EF$ <0099.g1:/ E F />` the rectangular parallelogram :g:`$EK$ <0099.g1:< E K >>` equal to the square on :g:`$AB$ <0099.g1:/ A B />`, and let :g:`$EG$ <0099.g1:< E G >>` equal to the squares on :g:`$AC$ <0099.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$CB$ <0099.g1:/ C B />` be subtracted;

.. assertion::

   therefore the remainder :g:`$HK$ <0099.g1:< H K >>` is equal to twice the rectangle :g:`$AC$ <0099.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$CB$ <0099.g1:/ C B />`. :just:`[II. 4] <II.4>`

Again, let there be subtracted :g:`$EL$ <0099.g1:< E L >>`, equal to the squares on :g:`$AD$ <0099.g1:/ A D />`, :g:`$DB$ <0099.g1:/ D B />`, which were proved less than the squares on :g:`$AC$ <0099.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$CB$ <0099.g1:/ C B />` :just:`[Lemma]`;

.. _folio-iii-0100:

.. folio:: 0100
   :page: 96

.. assertion::

   therefore the remainder :g:`$MK$ <0099.g1:< M K >>` is also equal to twice the rectangle :g:`$AD$ <0099.g1:/ A D />`, :g:`$DB$ <0099.g1:/ D B />`.

.. subclause::

   Now, since the squares on :g:`$AC$ <0099.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$CB$ <0099.g1:/ C B />` are medial,

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$EG$ <0099.g1:< E G >>` is medial.

.. subclause::

   And it is applied to the rational straight line :g:`$EF$ <0099.g1:/ E F />`;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$EH$ <0099.g1:/ E H />` is rational and incommensurable in length with :g:`$EF$ <0099.g1:/ E F />`. :just:`[X. 22] <X.22>`

.. subclause::

   For the same reason

.. assertion::

   :g:`$HN$ <0099.g1:/ H N />` is also rational and incommensurable in length with :g:`$EF$ <0099.g1:/ E F />`.

.. subclause::

   And, since :g:`$AC$ <0099.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$CB$ <0099.g1:/ C B />` are medial straight lines commensurable in square only,

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$AC$ <0099.g1:/ A C />` is incommensurable in length with :g:`$CB$ <0099.g1:/ C B />`.

.. subclause::

   But, as :g:`$AC$ <0099.g1:/ A C />` is to :g:`$CB$ <0099.g1:/ C B />`, so is the square on :g:`$AC$ <0099.g1:/ A C />` to the rectangle :g:`$AC$ <0099.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$CB$ <0099.g1:/ C B />`;

.. assertion::

   therefore the square on :g:`$AC$ <0099.g1:/ A C />` is incommensurable with the rectangle :g:`$AC$ <0099.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$CB$ <0099.g1:/ C B />`. :just:`[X. 11] <X.11>`

.. subclause::

   But the squares on :g:`$AC$ <0099.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$CB$ <0099.g1:/ C B />` are commensurable with the square on :g:`$AC$ <0099.g1:/ A C />`; for :g:`$AC$ <0099.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$CB$ <0099.g1:/ C B />` are commensurable in square. :just:`[X. 15] <X.15>`

.. subclause::

   And twice the rectangle :g:`$AC$ <0099.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$CB$ <0099.g1:/ C B />` is commensurable with the rectangle :g:`$AC$ <0099.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$CB$ <0099.g1:/ C B />`. :just:`[X. 6] <X.6>`

.. assertion::

   Therefore the squares on :g:`$AC$ <0099.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$CB$ <0099.g1:/ C B />` are also incommensurable with twice the rectangle :g:`$AC$ <0099.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$CB$ <0099.g1:/ C B />`. :just:`[X. 13] <X.13>`

.. subclause::

   But :g:`$EG$ <0099.g1:< E G >>` is equal to the squares on :g:`$AC$ <0099.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$CB$ <0099.g1:/ C B />`, and :g:`$HK$ <0099.g1:< H K >>` is equal to twice the rectangle :g:`$AC$ <0099.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$CB$ <0099.g1:/ C B />`;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$EG$ <0099.g1:< E G >>` is incommensurable with :g:`$HK$ <0099.g1:< H K >>`, so that :g:`$EH$ <0099.g1:/ E H />` is also incommensurable in length with :g:`$HN$ <0099.g1:/ H N />`. :just:`[VI. 1, X. 11] <VI.1, X.11>`

.. subclause::

   And they are rational ;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$EH$ <0099.g1:/ E H />`, :g:`$HN$ <0099.g1:/ H N />` are rational straight lines commensurable in square only.

.. subclause::

   But, if two rational straight lines commensurable in square only be added together, the whole is the irrational which is called binomial. :just:`[X. 36] <X.36>`

.. assertion::

   Therefore :g:`$EN$ <0099.g1:/ E N />` is a binomial straight line divided at :g:`$H$ <0099.g1:H>`.

.. assertion::

   In the same way :g:`$EM$ <0099.g1:/ E M />`, :g:`$MN$ <0099.g1:/ M N />` will also be proved to be rational straight lines commensurable in square only; and :g:`$EN$ <0099.g1:/ E N />` will be a binomial straight line divided at different points, :g:`$H$ <0099.g1:H>` and :g:`$M$ <0099.g1:M>`.

.. _folio-iii-0101:

.. folio:: 0101
   :page: 97

.. subclause::

   And :g:`$EH$ <0099.g1:/ E H />` is not the same with :g:`$MN$ <0099.g1:/ M N />`.

.. subclause::

   For the squares on :g:`$AC$ <0099.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$CB$ <0099.g1:/ C B />` are greater than the squares on :g:`$AD$ <0099.g1:/ A D />`, :g:`$DB$ <0099.g1:/ D B />`.

.. subclause::

   But the squares on :g:`$AD$ <0099.g1:/ A D />`, :g:`$DB$ <0099.g1:/ D B />` are greater than twice the rectangle :g:`$AD$ <0099.g1:/ A D />`, :g:`$DB$ <0099.g1:/ D B />`;

.. assertion::

   therefore also the squares on :g:`$AC$ <0099.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$CB$ <0099.g1:/ C B />`, that is, :g:`$EG$ <0099.g1:< E G >>`, are much greater than twice the rectangle :g:`$AD$ <0099.g1:/ A D />`, :g:`$DB$ <0099.g1:/ D B />`, that is, :g:`$MK$ <0099.g1:< M K >>`, so that :g:`$EH$ <0099.g1:/ E H />` is also greater than :g:`$MN$ <0099.g1:/ M N />`.

.. qed::

   Therefore :g:`$EH$ <0099.g1:/ E H />` is not the same with :g:`$MN$ <0099.g1:/ M N />`. :just:`Q. E. D.`
