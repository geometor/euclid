Commentary on Proposition X.58
==============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition X.58 <X.58>`.

.. folio:: 0133
   :page: 129

We have here to find the square root of the expression [cf. X. 52]

.. math::

   \rho(k\rho\sqrt{1+\lambda} + k\rho).

As usual, we put

.. math::

   \left.\begin{aligned} u+v &= k\rho\sqrt{1+\lambda} \\ uv &= \frac{1}{4}k^2\rho^2 \end{aligned}\right\}\quad\quad\quad\quad\dots\dots(1).

Then, :math:`u`, :math:`v` being found, we take

.. math::

   \left.\begin{aligned} x^2 &= \rho u \\ y^2 &= \rho v \end{aligned}\right\}\quad\quad\quad\quad\dots\dots(2),

and :math:`(x+y)`, so found, is our required square root.

Euclid's proof of the class of :math:`(x+y)` is as follows:
By X. 18, :math:`u \smile v`;
therefore :math:`\rho u \smile \rho v`,
so that :math:`x^2 \smile y^2`,
and :math:`x \bumpeq y \quad\quad\quad\quad\dots\dots(3).`

Next :math:`u+v \smile k\rho`
:math:`\phantom{\text{Next }u+v}\smile \rho,`
whence :math:`\rho(u+v)`, or :math:`(x^2+y^2)`, is a medial area :math:`\dots\dots(4).`

Lastly, :math:`xy = \frac{1}{2}k\rho^2`, which is a rational area :math:`\dots\dots(5).`

Hence [(3), (4), (5)] :math:`(x+y)` is the *side of a rational plus a medial area*. :just:`[X. 40] <X.40>`

If we solve algebraically, we obtain

.. math::

   \begin{aligned} u &= \frac{k\rho}{2}(\sqrt{1+\lambda} + \sqrt{\lambda}), \\ v &= \frac{k\rho}{2}(\sqrt{1+\lambda} - \sqrt{\lambda}), \end{aligned}

and

.. math::

   x+y = \rho\sqrt{\frac{k}{2}(\sqrt{1+\lambda}+\sqrt{\lambda})} + \rho\sqrt{\frac{k}{2}(\sqrt{1+\lambda}-\sqrt{\lambda})}.

The corresponding "side" found in X. 95 is a straight line which produces with a rational area a medial whole, being of the form :math:`(x-y)`, where :math:`x`, :math:`y` have the same values as above.

The two square roots are (cf. note on X. 52) the roots of the biquadratic equation

.. math::

   x^4 - 2k\rho^2\sqrt{1+\lambda}\cdot x^2 + \lambda k^2\rho^4 = 0.
