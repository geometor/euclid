Commentary on Proposition X.60
==============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition X.60 <X.60>`.

.. folio:: 0138
   :page: 134

In the hexad of propositions beginning with this we have the solution of the converse problem to that of X. 54—59. We find the *squares* of the irrational straight lines of X. 36—41 and prove that they are respectively equal to the rectangles contained by a rational straight line and the *first*, *second*, *third*, *fourth*, *fifth* and *sixth binomials*.

In X. 60 we prove that, :math:`\rho + \sqrt{k} \,.\, \rho` being a *binomial* straight line [X. 36],

.. math::

   \frac{(\rho + \sqrt{k} \,.\, \rho)^2}{\sigma}

is a :term:`*first binomial* <first-binomial>` straight line, and we find it geometrically.

The procedure may be represented thus. Take :math:`x, y, z` such that

.. math::

   \begin{aligned}
   \sigma x &= \rho^2,\\
   \sigma y &= k\rho^2,\\
   \sigma \cdot 2z &= 2\sqrt{k} \cdot \rho^2,
   \end{aligned}

:math:`\rho^2, k\rho^2` being of course the squares on the terms of the original binomial, and :math:`2\sqrt{k} \cdot \rho^2` twice the rectangle contained by them.

Then

.. math::

   (x + y) + 2z = \frac{(\rho + \sqrt{k} \cdot \rho)^2}{\sigma},

and we have to prove that :math:`(x + y) + 2z` is a :term:`*first binomial* <first-binomial>` straight line of which :math:`(x + y), 2z` are the terms and :math:`(x + y)` the greater.

Euclid divides the proof into two parts, showing first that :math:`(x + y) + 2z` is *some* binomial, and secondly that it is the :term:`*first binomial* <first-binomial>`.

.. _folio-iii-0139:

.. folio:: 0139
   :page: 135

(:math:`\alpha`) :math:`\rho \sim \sqrt{k} \cdot \rho`, so that :math:`\rho^2, k\rho^2` are rational and commensurable; therefore :math:`\rho^2 + k\rho^2`, or :math:`\sigma (x + y)`, is a rational area, whence

.. math::

   (x + y)\text{ is rational and } \sim \sigma \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(1).

Next, :math:`2\rho \cdot \sqrt{k} \cdot \rho` is a medial area, so that :math:`\sigma \cdot 2z` is a medial area, whence

.. math::

   2z\text{ is rational but } \smile \sigma \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(2).

Hence [(1), (2)] :math:`(x + y), 2z` are rational and commensurable in square only :math:`\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(3);` thus :math:`(x + y) + 2z` is a *binomial* straight line. [X. 36]

(:math:`\beta`)

.. math::

   \rho^2 : \sqrt{k} \cdot \rho^2 = \sqrt{k} \cdot \rho^2 : k\rho^2,

so that

.. math::

   \sigma x : \sigma z = \sigma z : \sigma y,

and

.. math::

   x : z = z : y,

or

.. math::

   xy = z^2 = \frac{1}{4}(2z)^2 \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(4).

Now :math:`\rho^2, k\rho^2` are commensurable, so that :math:`\sigma x, \sigma y` are commensurable, and therefore

.. math::

   x \sim y \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(5).

And, since [Lemma] :math:`\rho^2 + k\rho^2 > 2\sqrt{k} \cdot \rho^2,`

.. math::

   x + y > 2z.

But :math:`(x + y)` is given, being equal to :math:`\frac{\rho^2 + k\rho^2}{\sigma} \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(6).`

Therefore [(4), (5), (6), and X. 17] :math:`\sqrt{(x + y)^2 - (2z)^2} \sim (x + y).` And :math:`(x + y), 2z` are rational and :math:`\smile` [(3)], while :math:`(x + y) \sim \sigma` [(1)].

Hence :math:`(x + y) + 2z` is a :term:`*first binomial* <first-binomial>`.

The actual value of :math:`(x + y) + 2z` is, of course,

.. math::

   \frac{\rho^2}{\sigma}(1 + k + 2\sqrt{k}).
