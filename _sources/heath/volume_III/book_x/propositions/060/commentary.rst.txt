Commentary on Proposition X.60
==============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition X.60 <X.60>`.

.. folio:: 0138
   :page: 134

In the hexad of propositions beginning with this we have the solution of the converse problem to that of X. 54—59. We find the *squares* of the irrational straight lines of X. 36—41 and prove that they are respectively equal to the rectangles contained by a rational straight line and the *first*, *second*, *third*, *fourth*, *fifth* and *sixth binomials*.

In X. 60 we prove that, $\rho + \sqrt{k} \,.\, \rho$ being a *binomial* straight line [X. 36],

$$\frac{(\rho + \sqrt{k} \,.\, \rho)^2}{\sigma}$$

is a :term:`*first binomial* <first-binomial>` straight line, and we find it geometrically.

The procedure may be represented thus. Take $x, y, z$ such that

$$\begin{aligned}
\sigma x &= \rho^2,\\
\sigma y &= k\rho^2,\\
\sigma \cdot 2z &= 2\sqrt{k} \cdot \rho^2,
\end{aligned}$$

$\rho^2, k\rho^2$ being of course the squares on the terms of the original binomial, and $2\sqrt{k} \cdot \rho^2$ twice the rectangle contained by them.

Then

$$(x + y) + 2z = \frac{(\rho + \sqrt{k} \cdot \rho)^2}{\sigma},$$

and we have to prove that $(x + y) + 2z$ is a :term:`*first binomial* <first-binomial>` straight line of which $(x + y), 2z$ are the terms and $(x + y)$ the greater.

Euclid divides the proof into two parts, showing first that $(x + y) + 2z$ is *some* binomial, and secondly that it is the :term:`*first binomial* <first-binomial>`.

.. _folio-iii-0139:

.. folio:: 0139
   :page: 135

($\alpha$) $\rho \sim \sqrt{k} \cdot \rho$, so that $\rho^2, k\rho^2$ are rational and commensurable; therefore $\rho^2 + k\rho^2$, or $\sigma (x + y)$, is a rational area, whence

$$(x + y)\text{ is rational and } \sim \sigma \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(1).$$

Next, $2\rho \cdot \sqrt{k} \cdot \rho$ is a medial area, so that $\sigma \cdot 2z$ is a medial area, whence

$$2z\text{ is rational but } \smile \sigma \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(2).$$

Hence [(1), (2)] $(x + y), 2z$ are rational and commensurable in square only $\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(3);$ thus $(x + y) + 2z$ is a *binomial* straight line. [X. 36]

($\beta$)

$$\rho^2 : \sqrt{k} \cdot \rho^2 = \sqrt{k} \cdot \rho^2 : k\rho^2,$$

so that

$$\sigma x : \sigma z = \sigma z : \sigma y,$$

and

$$x : z = z : y,$$

or

$$xy = z^2 = \frac{1}{4}(2z)^2 \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(4).$$

Now $\rho^2, k\rho^2$ are commensurable, so that $\sigma x, \sigma y$ are commensurable, and therefore

$$x \sim y \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(5).$$

And, since [Lemma] $\rho^2 + k\rho^2 > 2\sqrt{k} \cdot \rho^2,$

$$x + y > 2z.$$

But $(x + y)$ is given, being equal to $\frac{\rho^2 + k\rho^2}{\sigma} \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(6).$

Therefore [(4), (5), (6), and X. 17] $\sqrt{(x + y)^2 - (2z)^2} \sim (x + y).$ And $(x + y), 2z$ are rational and $\smile$ [(3)], while $(x + y) \sim \sigma$ [(1)].

Hence $(x + y) + 2z$ is a :term:`*first binomial* <first-binomial>`.

The actual value of $(x + y) + 2z$ is, of course,

$$\frac{\rho^2}{\sigma}(1 + k + 2\sqrt{k}).$$
