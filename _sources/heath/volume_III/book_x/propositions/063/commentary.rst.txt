Commentary on Proposition X.63
==============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition X.63 <X.63>`.

.. folio:: 0145
   :page: 141

We have to prove that [cf. x. 39]

.. math::

   \frac{1}{\sigma} \left\{ \frac{\rho}{\sqrt{2}} \sqrt{1 + \frac{k}{\sqrt{1 + k^2}}} + \frac{\rho}{\sqrt{2}} \sqrt{1 - \frac{k}{\sqrt{1 + k^2}}} \right\}^2

is a :term:`*fourth binomial* <fourth-binomial>` straight line.
For brevity we must call this expression

.. math::

   \frac{1}{\sigma} (u + v)^2.

Take :math:`x`, :math:`y`, :math:`z` such that

.. math::

   \left.
   \begin{aligned}
   \sigma x &= u^2, \\
   \sigma y &= v^2, \\
   \sigma \cdot 2z &= 2uv
   \end{aligned}
   \right\},

wherein it has to be remembered [x. 39] that :math:`u`, :math:`v` are incommensurable in square, :math:`(u^2 + v^2)` is rational, and :math:`uv` is medial.
(:math:`\alpha`) :math:`(u^2 + v^2)`, and therefore :math:`\sigma(x + y)`, is rational ;
therefore :math:`(x + y)` is rational and :math:`\sim \sigma` .......................................(1).
:math:`2uv`, and therefore :math:`\sigma \cdot 2z`, is medial ;
therefore :math:`2z` is rational and :math:`\smile \sigma` ..........................................(2).
Thus :math:`(x + y)`, :math:`2z` are rational and :math:`\sim` ...................................(3),
so that :math:`(x + y) + 2z` is a binomial straight line.
(:math:`\beta`) As before, :math:`x + y > 2z`,
and :math:`xy = z^2`.
Now, since :math:`u^2 \smile v^2`,
:math:`\sigma x \smile \sigma y`, or :math:`x \smile y`.
Hence [x. 18] :math:`\sqrt{(x + y)^2 - (2z)^2} \smile (x + y)` ........................(4).
And :math:`(x + y) \sim \sigma`, by (1).
Therefore [(3), (4)] :math:`(x + y) + 2z` is a :term:`*fourth binomial* <fourth-binomial>` straight line.
It is of course

.. math::

   \frac{\rho^2}{\sigma} \left\{ 1 + \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + k^2}} \right\}.
