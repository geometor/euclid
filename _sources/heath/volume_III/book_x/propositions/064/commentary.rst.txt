Commentary on Proposition X.64
==============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition X.64 <X.64>`.

.. folio:: 0147
   :page: 143

To prove that [cf. x. 40]

.. math::

   \frac{1}{\sigma} \left\{ \frac{\rho}{\sqrt{2(1 + k^2)}} \sqrt{\sqrt{1 + k^2} + k} + \frac{\rho}{\sqrt{2(1 + k^2)}} \sqrt{\sqrt{1 + k^2} - k} \right\}^2

is a :term:`*fifth binomial* <fifth-binomial>` straight line.
For brevity denote it by :math:`\frac{1}{\sigma} (u + v)^2`, and put

.. math::

   \left.
   \begin{aligned}
   \sigma x &= u^2, \\
   \sigma y &= v^2, \\
   \sigma \cdot 2z &= 2uv.
   \end{aligned}
   \right\}

Remembering that [x. 40] :math:`u^2 \smile v^2`, :math:`(u^2 + v^2)` is medial, and :math:`2uv` is rational,
we proceed thus.
(:math:`\alpha`) :math:`\sigma(x + y)` is medial ;
therefore :math:`(x + y)` is rational and :math:`\smile \sigma` .................................(1).
Next, :math:`\sigma \cdot 2z` is rational ;
therefore :math:`2z` is rational and :math:`\sim \sigma` .......................................(2).
Thus :math:`(x + y)`, :math:`2z` are rational and :math:`\sim` ................................(3),
so that :math:`(x + y) + 2z` is a binomial straight line.
(:math:`\beta`) As before,

.. math::

   \begin{aligned}
   x + y &> 2z, \\
   xy &= z^2,
   \end{aligned}

and

.. math::

   x \smile y.

Therefore [x. 18] :math:`\sqrt{(x + y)^2 - (2z)^2} \smile (x + y)` .......................(4).
Hence [(2), (3), (4)] :math:`(x + y) + 2z` is a :term:`*fifth binomial* <fifth-binomial>` straight line.
It is of course

.. math::

   \frac{\rho^2}{\sigma} \left\{ \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + k^2}} + \frac{1}{1 + k^2} \right\}.
