Commentary on Proposition X.71
==============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition X.71 <X.71>`.

.. folio:: 0158
   :page: 154

A rational area being of the form $k\rho^2$, and a medial area of the form $\sqrt\lambda . \rho^2$, the problem is to classify

$$\sqrt{k\rho^2 + \sqrt\lambda . \rho^2}$$

according to the different possible relations between $k, \lambda$.

Put

$$\begin{aligned}
\sigma u &= k\rho^2, \\
\sigma v &= \sqrt\lambda . \rho^2.
\end{aligned}$$

Then, since the former rectangle is rational, the latter medial, $u$ is rational and $\frown \sigma$, $v$ is rational and $\smile \sigma$.

Also the rectangles are incommensurable; so that

$$u \smile v.$$

Hence $u, v$ are rational and $\sim$; whence $(u + v)$ is a binomial straight line.

.. _folio-iii-0159:

.. folio:: 0159
   :page: 155

The possibilities now are as follows :

I. $u > v$. Then either

(1) $\sqrt{u^2 - v^2} \frown u$, or (2) $\sqrt{u^2 - v^2} \smile u$, while in both cases $u \frown \sigma$.

In case (1) $(u + v)$ is a :term:`*first binomial* <first-binomial>` straight line, and in case (2) $(u + v)$ is a :term:`*fourth binomial* <fourth-binomial>` straight line.

Thus $\sqrt{\sigma (u + v)}$ is either (1) a *binomial* straight line :just:`[X. 54] <X.54>` or (2) a *major* irrational straight line :just:`[X. 57] <X.57>`.

II. $v > u$. Then either

(1) $\sqrt{v^2 - u^2} \frown v$, or (2) $\sqrt{v^2 - u^2} \smile v$, while in both cases $v \smile \sigma$, but $u \frown \sigma$.

Hence, in case (1), $(v + u)$ is a :term:`*second binomial* <second-binomial>` straight line, and, in case (2), $(v + u)$ is a :term:`*fifth binomial* <fifth-binomial>` straight line.

Thus $\sqrt{\sigma (v + u)}$ is either (1) a :term:`*first bimedial* <first-bimedial>` straight line :just:`[X. 55] <X.55>`, or (2) a *side of a rational plus a medial area* :just:`[X. 58] <X.58>`.
