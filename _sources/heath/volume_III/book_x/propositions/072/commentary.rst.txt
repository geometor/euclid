Commentary on Proposition X.72
==============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition X.72 <X.72>`.

.. folio:: 0161
   :page: 157

We have to classify, according to the different possible relations between $k, \lambda$, the straight line

$$\sqrt{\sqrt k.\rho^2 + \sqrt\lambda.\rho^2},$$

where $\sqrt k.\rho^2$ and $\sqrt\lambda.\rho^2$ are incommensurable.

Suppose that

$$\begin{aligned}
\sigma u &= \sqrt k.\rho^2, \\
\sigma v &= \sqrt\lambda.\rho^2.
\end{aligned}$$

It is immaterial whether $\sqrt k.\rho^2$ or $\sqrt\lambda.\rho^2$ is the greater. Suppose, e.g., that the former is.

Now, $\sqrt k.\rho^2, \sqrt\lambda.\rho^2$ being both *medial* areas, and $\sigma$ rational, $u, v$ are both rational and $\smile \sigma \dotfill (1).$

Again, by hypothesis,

$$\sigma u \smile \sigma v,$$

$$\text{or } u \smile v \dotfill (2).$$

Hence [(1), (2)] $(u + v)$ is a binomial straight line.

Next, $\sqrt{u^2 - v^2}$ is either commensurable or incommensurable in length with $u$.

($\alpha$) Suppose $\sqrt{u^2 - v^2} \frown u$. In this case $(u + v)$ is a :term:`*third binomial* <third-binomial>` straight line, and therefore :just:`[X. 56] <X.56>`

$$\sqrt{\sigma(u + v)} \text{ is a second bimedial straight line.}$$

($\beta$) If $\sqrt{u^2 - v^2} \smile u$, $(u + v)$ is a :term:`*sixth binomial* <sixth-binomial>` straight line, and therefore :just:`[X. 59] <X.59>`

$$\sqrt{\sigma(u + v)} \text{ is a side of the sum of two medial areas.}$$

----

.. enunciation::

   The binomial straight line and the irrational straight lines after it are neither the same with the medial nor with one another.

.. subclause::

   For the square on a medial, if applied to a rational straight line, produces as breadth a straight line rational and incommensurable in length with that to which it is applied. :just:`[X. 22] <X.22>`

.. subclause::

   But the square on the binomial, if applied to a rational straight line, produces as breadth the first binomial. :just:`[X. 60] <X.60>`

.. subclause::

   The square on the first bimedial, if applied to a rational straight line, produces as breadth the second binomial. :just:`[X. 61] <X.61>`

.. _folio-iii-0162:

.. folio:: 0162
   :page: 158

.. subclause::

   The square on the second bimedial, if applied to a rational straight line, produces as breadth the third binomial. :just:`[X. 62] <X.62>`

.. subclause::

   The square on the major, if applied to a rational straight line, produces as breadth the fourth binomial. :just:`[X. 63] <X.63>`

.. subclause::

   The square on the side of a rational plus a medial area, if applied to a rational straight line, produces as breadth the fifth binomial. :just:`[X. 64] <X.64>`

.. subclause::

   The square on the side of the sum of two medial areas, if applied to a rational straight line, produces as breadth the sixth binomial. :just:`[X. 65] <X.65>`

.. assertion::

   And the said breadths differ both from the first and from one another : from the first because it is rational, and from one another because they are not the same in order ; so that the irrational straight lines themselves also differ from one another.

----

The explanation after X. 72 is for the purpose of showing that all the irrational straight lines treated hitherto are different from one another, viz. the medial, the six irrational straight lines beginning with the binomial, and the six consisting of the first, second, third, fourth, fifth and sixth binomials.
