Commentary on Proposition X.86
==============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition X.86 <X.86>`.

.. folio:: 0185
   :page: 181

Take, as before, :math:`k\rho` commensurable in length with :math:`\rho`.
Let :math:`m^2, n^2` be again square numbers, but :math:`(m^2 - n^2)` not square.
Take :math:`x` such that

.. math::

   (m^2 - n^2) : m^2 = k^2\rho^2 : x^2 \tag{1},

whence

.. math::

   x = k\rho \frac{m}{\sqrt{m^2 - n^2}}

.. math::

   = \frac{k\rho}{\sqrt{1 - \lambda^2}}, \text{ say.}

Thus :math:`x` is greater than :math:`k\rho`.
Then :math:`x - k\rho`, or :math:`\frac{k\rho}{\sqrt{1 - \lambda^2}} - k\rho`, is a :term:`*second apotome* <second-apotome>`.

For (:math:`\alpha`), as before, :math:`x` is rational and :math:`\sim k\rho`.
(:math:`\beta`) If :math:`x^2 - k^2\rho^2 = y^2`, we have, from (1),

.. math::

   m^2 : n^2 = x^2 : y^2.

Thus :math:`y`, or :math:`\sqrt{x^2 - k^2\rho^2}`, is commensurable in length with :math:`x`.
And :math:`k\rho \frown \rho`.
Therefore :math:`x - k\rho` is a :term:`*second apotome* <second-apotome>`.

As explained in the note on x. 49, the second apotome

.. math::

   \frac{k\rho}{\sqrt{1 - \lambda^2}} - k\rho

is the lesser root of the equation

.. math::

   x^2 - \frac{2k\rho}{\sqrt{1 - \lambda^2}} \cdot x + \frac{\lambda^2}{1 - \lambda^2} k^2\rho^2 = 0.
