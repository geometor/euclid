Commentary on Proposition X.88
==============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition X.88 <X.88>`.

.. folio:: 0189
   :page: 185

Beginning with $\rho$, $k\rho$, as in x. 85, 86, we take numbers $m$, $n$ such that $(m+n)$ has not to either of the numbers $m$, $n$ the ratio of a square number to a square number.

Take $x$ such that

$$(m+n) : n = k^2\rho^2 : x^2 \text{ ..............................(1),}$$

whence

$$x = k\rho \sqrt{\frac{n}{m+n}}$$

$$= \frac{k\rho}{\sqrt{1+\lambda}}\text{, say.}$$

Then shall $(k\rho - x)$, or $\left(k\rho - \frac{k\rho}{\sqrt{1+\lambda}}\right)$, be a :term:`*fourth apotome* <fourth-apotome>`.

.. _folio-iii-0190:

.. folio:: 0190
   :page: 186

For, by (1), $x$ is rational and $\sim k\rho$.
Also $\sqrt{k^2\rho^2 - x^2}$ is incommensurable with $k\rho$, since

$$(m+n) : m = k^2\rho^2 : (k^2\rho^2 - x^2)\text{,}$$

and the ratio $(m+n) : m$ is not that of a square number to a square number.
And $k\rho \sim \rho$.
As explained in the note on x. 51, the :term:`*fourth apotome* <fourth-apotome>`

$$k\rho - \frac{k\rho}{\sqrt{1+\lambda}}$$

is the lesser root of the quadratic equation

$$x^2 - 2k\rho \cdot x + \frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda} k^2\rho^2 = 0.$$
