PROPOSITION 5.
==============

.. enunciation::

   If a straight line be set up at right angles to three straight lines which meet one another, at their common point of section, the three straight lines are in one plane.

For let a straight line :g:`$AB$ <0285.g1:/ A B />` be set up at right angles to the three straight lines :g:`$BC$ <0285.g1:/ B C />`, :g:`$BD$ <0285.g1:/ B D />`, :g:`$BE$ <0285.g1:/ B E />`, at their point of meeting at :g:`$B$ <0285.g1:B>`; I say that :g:`$BC$ <0285.g1:/ B C />`, :g:`$BD$ <0285.g1:/ B D />`, :g:`$BE$ <0285.g1:/ B E />` are in one plane.

.. picture:: 0285.g1.svg
   :alt: Book XI, Proposition 5

.. subclause::

   For suppose they are not, but, if possible, let :g:`$BD$ <0285.g1:/ B D />`, :g:`$BE$ <0285.g1:/ B E />` be in the plane of reference and :g:`$BC$ <0285.g1:/ B C />` in one more elevated; let the plane through :g:`$AB$ <0285.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$BC$ <0285.g1:/ B C />` be produced; it will thus make, as common section in the plane of reference, a straight line. :just:`[XI. 3] <XI.3>`

Let it make :g:`$BF$ <0285.g1:/ B F />`.

.. assertion::

   Therefore the three straight lines :g:`$AB$ <0285.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$BC$ <0285.g1:/ B C />`, :g:`$BF$ <0285.g1:/ B F />` are in one plane, namely that drawn through :g:`$AB$ <0285.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$BC$ <0285.g1:/ B C />`.

.. subclause::

   Now, since :g:`$AB$ <0285.g1:/ A B />` is at right angles to each of the straight lines :g:`$BD$ <0285.g1:/ B D />`, :g:`$BE$ <0285.g1:/ B E />`,

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$AB$ <0285.g1:/ A B />` is also at right angles to the plane through :g:`$BD$ <0285.g1:/ B D />`, :g:`$BE$ <0285.g1:/ B E />`. :just:`[XI. 4] <XI.4>`

.. _folio-iii-0286:

.. folio:: 0286
   :page: 282

.. subclause::

   But the plane through :g:`$BD$ <0285.g1:/ B D />`, :g:`$BE$ <0285.g1:/ B E />` is the plane of reference;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$AB$ <0285.g1:/ A B />` is at right angles to the plane of reference.

.. assertion::

   Thus :g:`$AB$ <0285.g1:/ A B />` will also make right angles with all the straight lines which meet it and are in the plane of reference. :just:`[XI. Def. 3] <XI.def.3>`

.. subclause::

   But :g:`$BF$ <0285.g1:/ B F />` which is in the plane of reference meets it;

.. assertion::

   therefore the angle :g:`$ABF$ <0285.g1:< A B F )>` is right.

.. subclause::

   But, by hypothesis, the angle :g:`$ABC$ <0285.g1:< A B C )>` is also right;

.. assertion::

   therefore the angle :g:`$ABF$ <0285.g1:< A B F )>` is :eq:`equal <0285.g1:< A B F ) = 0285.g1:< A B C )>` to the angle :g:`$ABC$ <0285.g1:< A B C )>`.

.. subclause::

   And they are in one plane:

.. assertion::

   which is impossible.

.. assertion::

   Therefore the straight line :g:`$BC$ <0285.g1:/ B C />` is not in a more elevated plane; therefore the three straight lines :g:`$BC$ <0285.g1:/ B C />`, :g:`$BD$ <0285.g1:/ B D />`, :g:`$BE$ <0285.g1:/ B E />` are in one plane.

.. qed::

   Therefore, if a straight line be set up at right angles to three straight lines, at their point of meeting, the three straight lines are in one plane. :just:`Q. E. D.`
