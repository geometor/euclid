Commentary on Proposition XI.15
===============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition XI.15 <XI.15>`.

.. folio:: 0302
   :page: 298

This result is arrived at in the American text-books already quoted by starting from the relation between a plane and a straight line parallel to it. The series of propositions is worth giving. A straight line and a plane being parallel if they do not meet however far they may be produced, we have the following propositions.

1. *Any plane containing one, and only one, of two parallel straight lines is parallel to the other.*

For suppose :g:`AB <0302.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`CD <0302.g1:/ C D />` to be parallel and :g:`CD <0302.g1:/ C D />` to lie in the plane :g:`MN <0302.g1:< M N >>`. Then :g:`AB <0302.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`CD <0302.g1:/ C D />` determine a plane intersecting :g:`MN <0302.g1:< M N >>` in the straight line :g:`CD <0302.g1:/ C D />` Thus, if :g:`AB <0302.g1:/ A B />` meets :g:`MN <0302.g1:< M N >>`, it must meet it at some point in :g:`CD <0302.g1:/ C D />`. But this is impossible, since :g:`AB <0302.g1:/ A B />` is parallel to :g:`CD <0302.g1:/ C D />`. Therefore :g:`AB <0302.g1:/ A B />` will not meet the plane :g:`MN <0302.g1:< M N >>`, and is therefore parallel to it. [This proposition and the proof are in :person:`Legendre <legendre>`.]

.. picture:: 0302.g1.svg
   :alt: Book XI, Proposition 15 Commentary 1

The following theorems follow as corollaries.

2. *Through a given straight line a plane can be drawn parallel to any other given straight line ; and, if the lines are not parallel, only one such plane can be drawn.*

We have simply to draw through any point on the first line a straight line parallel to the second line and then pass a plane through these two intersecting lines. This plane is then, by the above proposition, parallel to the second given straight line.

3. *Through a given point a plane can be drawn parallel to any two straight lines in space ; and, if the latter are not parallel, only one such plane can be drawn.*

Here we draw through the point straight lines parallel respectively to the given straight lines and then draw a plane through the lines so drawn.

Next we have the partial converse of the first proposition above.

4. *If a straight line is parallel to a plane, it is also parallel to the intersection of any plane through it with the given plane.*

Let :g:`AB <0302.g2:/ A B />` be parallel to the plane :g:`MN <0302.g2:< M N >>`, and let any plane through :g:`AB <0302.g2:/ A B />` intersect :g:`MN <0302.g2:< M N >>` in :g:`CD <0302.g2:/ C D />`. Now :g:`AB <0302.g2:/ A B />` and :g:`CD <0302.g2:/ C D />` cannot meet, because, if they did, :g:`AB <0302.g2:/ A B />` would meet the plane :g:`MN <0302.g2:< M N >>`. And :g:`AB <0302.g2:/ A B />`, :g:`CD <0302.g2:/ C D />` are in one plane. Therefore :g:`AB <0302.g2:/ A B />`, :g:`CD <0302.g2:/ C D />` are parallel. From this follows as a corollary :

5. *If each of two intersecting straight lines is parallel to a given plane, the plane containing them is parallel to the given plane.*

Let :g:`AB <0303.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`AC <0303.g1:/ A C />` be parallel to the plane :g:`MN <0303.g1:< M N >>`. Then, if the plane :g:`ABC <0303.g1:< A B C >>` were to meet the plane :g:`MN <0303.g1:< M N >>`, the intersection would be parallel both to :g:`AB <0303.g1:/ A B />` and to :g:`AC <0303.g1:/ A C />` : which is impossible.

.. picture:: 0303.g1.svg
   :alt: Book XI, Proposition 15 Commentary 3

Lastly, we have Euclid's proposition.

6. *If two straight lines forming an angle are respectively parallel to two other straight lines forming an angle, the plane of the first angle is parallel to the plane of the second.*

Let :g:`ABC <0303.g2:< A B C )>`, :g:`DEF <0303.g2:< D E F )>` be the angles formed by straight lines parallel to one another respectively. Then, since :g:`AB <0303.g2:/ A B />` is parallel to :g:`DE <0303.g2:/ D E />`, the plane of :g:`DEF <0303.g2:< D E F >>` is parallel to :g:`AB <0303.g2:/ A B />` [(1) above]. Similarly the plane of :g:`DEF <0303.g2:< D E F >>` is parallel to :g:`BC <0303.g2:/ B C />`. Hence the plane of :g:`DEF <0303.g2:< D E F >>` is parallel to the plane of :g:`ABC <0303.g2:< A B C >>` [(5)].

.. picture:: 0303.g2.svg
   :alt: Book XI, Proposition 15 Commentary 4

:person:`Legendre <legendre>` arrives at the result by yet another method. He first proves Eucl. XI. 16 to the effect that, *if two parallel planes are cut by a third, the lines of intersection are parallel*, and then deduces from this that, *if two parallel straight lines are terminated by two parallel planes, the straight lines are equal in length.*

(The latter inference is obvious because the plane through the parallels cuts the parallel planes in parallel lines ; which therefore, with the given parallel lines, form a parallelogram.)

:person:`Legendre <legendre>` is now in a position to prove Euclid's proposition XI. 15. If :g:`ABC <0303.g3:< A B C )>`, :g:`DEF <0303.g3:< D E F )>` be the angles, make :g:`AB <0303.g3:/ A B />` :eq:`equal <0303.g3:/ A B / = 0303.g3:/ D E />` to :g:`DE <0303.g3:/ D E />`, and :g:`BC <0303.g3:/ B C />` :eq:`equal <0303.g3:/ B C / = 0303.g3:/ E F />` to :g:`EF <0303.g3:/ E F />`, and join :g:`CA <0303.g3:/ C A />`, :g:`FD <0303.g3:/ F D />`, :g:`BE <0303.g3:/ B E />`, :g:`CF <0303.g3:/ C F />`, :g:`AD <0303.g3:/ A D />`. Then, as in Eucl. XI. 10, the triangles :g:`ABC <0303.g3:< A B C >>`, :g:`DEF <0303.g3:< D E F >>` are :eq:`equal <0303.g3:< A B C > = 0303.g3:< D E F >>` in all respects ; and :g:`AD <0303.g3:/ A D />`, :g:`BE <0303.g3:/ B E />`, :g:`CF <0303.g3:/ C F />` are all :eq:`equal <0303.g3:/ A D / = 0303.g3:/ B E / = 0303.g3:/ C F />`. It is now proved that the planes are parallel by :term:`*reductio ad absurdum* <reductio-ad-absurdum>` from the last preceding result. For, if the plane :g:`ABC <0303.g3:< A B C >>` is not parallel to the plane :g:`DEF <0303.g3:< D E F >>`, let the plane drawn through :g:`B <0303.g3:B>` parallel to the plane :g:`DEF <0303.g3:< D E F >>` meet :g:`CF <0303.g3:/ C F />`, :g:`AD <0303.g3:/ A D />` in :g:`H <0303.g3:H>`, :g:`G <0303.g3:G>` respectively. Then, by the last result :g:`BE <0303.g3:/ B E />`, :g:`HF <0303.g3:/ H F />`, :g:`GD <0303.g3:/ G D />` will all be :eq:`equal <0303.g3:/ B E / = 0303.g3:/ H F / = 0303.g3:/ G D />`. But :g:`BE <0303.g3:/ B E />`, :g:`CF <0303.g3:/ C F />`, :g:`AD <0303.g3:/ A D />` are all :eq:`equal <0303.g3:/ B E / = 0303.g3:/ C F / = 0303.g3:/ A D />` : which is impossible. Therefore etc.
