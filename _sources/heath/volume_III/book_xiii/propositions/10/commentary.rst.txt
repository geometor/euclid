Commentary on Proposition XIII.10
=================================

Back to :ref:`Proposition XIII.10 <XIII.10>`.

.. folio:: 0464
   :page: 460

:g:`ABCDE <0462.g1:< A B C D E >>` being a regular pentagon inscribed in a circle, and :g:`AG <0462.g1:/ A G />` the diameter through :g:`A <0462.g1:A>`, it follows that (arc :g:`CG <0462.g1:( C G )>`) :math:`=` (arc :g:`GD <0462.g1:( G D )>`), and :g:`CG <0462.g1:( C G )>`, :g:`GD <0462.g1:( G D )>` are sides of an inscribed regular decagon.

:g:`FHK <0462.g1:/ F H K />` being drawn perpendicular to :g:`AB <0462.g1:/ A B />`, it follows, by I. 26, that :math:`\angle\text{s}` :g:`AFK <0462.g1:< A F K )>`, :g:`BFK <0462.g1:< B F K )>` are :eq:`equal <0462.g1:< A F K ) = 0462.g1:< B F K )>`, and :g:`BK <0462.g1:/ B K />`, :g:`KA <0462.g1:/ K A />` are sides of the regular decagon.

Similarly it may be proved that, :g:`FLM <0462.g1:/ F L M />` being perpendicular to :g:`AK <0462.g1:/ A K />`, :g:`AL <0462.g1:/ A L />` :math:`=` :g:`LK <0462.g1:/ L K />`, and (arc :g:`AM <0462.g1:( A M )>`) :math:`=` (arc :g:`MK <0462.g1:( M K )>`).

The main facts to prove are that (1) the triangles :g:`ABF <0462.g1:< A B F >>`, :g:`FBN <0462.g1:< F B N >>` are similar, and (2) the triangles :g:`ABK <0462.g1:< A B K >>`, :g:`AKN <0462.g1:< A K N >>` are similar.

(1) :math:`2` (arc :g:`CG <0462.g1:( C G )>`) :math:`=` (arc :g:`CD <0462.g1:( C D )>`) :math:`=` (arc :g:`AB <0462.g1:( A B )>`) :math:`= 2` (arc :g:`BK <0462.g1:( B K )>`), or (arc :g:`CG <0462.g1:( C G )>`) :math:`=` (arc :g:`BK <0462.g1:( B K )>`) :math:`=` (arc :g:`AK <0462.g1:( A K )>`) :math:`= 2` (arc :g:`KM <0462.g1:( K M )>`). And (arc :g:`CB <0462.g1:( C B )>`) :math:`= 2` (arc :g:`BK <0462.g1:( B K )>`). Therefore, by addition, (arc :g:`BCG <0462.g1:( B C G )>`) :math:`= 2` (arc :g:`BKM <0462.g1:( B K M )>`). Therefore :math:`\angle` :g:`BFG <0462.g1:< B F G )>` :math:`= 2\,\angle` :g:`BFN <0462.g1:< B F N )>`. But :math:`\angle` :g:`BFG <0462.g1:< B F G )>` :math:`= 2\,\angle` :g:`FAB <0462.g1:< F A B )>`, so that :math:`\angle` :g:`FAB <0462.g1:< F A B )>` :math:`= \angle` :g:`BFN <0462.g1:< B F N )>`. Hence, in the :math:`\Delta\text{s}` :g:`ABF <0462.g1:< A B F >>`, :g:`FBN <0462.g1:< F B N >>`, :math:`\angle` :g:`FAB <0462.g1:< F A B )>` :math:`= \angle` :g:`BFN <0462.g1:< B F N )>`, and :math:`\angle` :g:`ABF <0462.g1:< A B F )>` is common; therefore :math:`\angle` :g:`AFB <0462.g1:< A F B )>` :math:`= \angle` :g:`BNF <0462.g1:< B N F )>`, and :math:`\Delta\text{s}` :g:`ABF <0462.g1:< A B F >>`, :g:`FBN <0462.g1:< F B N >>` are similar.

(2) Since :g:`AL <0462.g1:/ A L />` :math:`=` :g:`LK <0462.g1:/ L K />`, and the angles at :g:`L <0462.g1:L>` are right, :g:`AN <0462.g1:/ A N />` :math:`=` :g:`NK <0462.g1:/ N K />`, and :math:`\angle` :g:`NKA <0462.g1:< N K A )>` :math:`= \angle` :g:`NAK <0462.g1:< N A K )>` :math:`= \angle` :g:`KBA <0462.g1:< K B A )>`. Hence, in the :math:`\Delta\text{s}` :g:`ABK <0462.g1:< A B K >>`, :g:`AKN <0462.g1:< A K N >>`, :math:`\angle` :g:`ABK <0462.g1:< A B K )>` :math:`= \angle` :g:`AKN <0462.g1:< A K N )>`, and :math:`\angle` :g:`KAN <0462.g1:< K A N )>` is common; whence the third angles are :eq:`equal <0462.g1:< A K B ) = 0462.g1:< K N A )>`; therefore the triangles :g:`ABK <0462.g1:< A B K >>`, :g:`AKN <0462.g1:< A K N >>` are similar.

Now from the similarity of :math:`\Delta\text{s}` :g:`ABF <0462.g1:< A B F >>`, :g:`FBN <0462.g1:< F B N >>` it follows that :g:`AB <0462.g1:/ A B />` :math:`:` :g:`BF <0462.g1:/ B F />` :math:`=` :g:`BF <0462.g1:/ B F />` :math:`:` :g:`BN <0462.g1:/ B N />`, or (rect. :g:`AB <0462.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`BN <0462.g1:/ B N />`) :math:`=` (sq. on :g:`BF <0462.g1:/ B F />`).

And, from the similarity of :g:`ABK <0462.g1:< A B K >>`, :g:`AKN <0462.g1:< A K N >>`, :g:`BA <0462.g1:/ B A />` :math:`:` :g:`AK <0462.g1:/ A K />` :math:`=` :g:`AK <0462.g1:/ A K />` :math:`:` :g:`AN <0462.g1:/ A N />`, or (rect. :g:`BA <0462.g1:/ B A />`, :g:`AN <0462.g1:/ A N />`) :math:`=` (sq. on :g:`AK <0462.g1:/ A K />`).

Therefore, by addition, (rect. :g:`AB <0462.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`BN <0462.g1:/ B N />`) :math:`+` (rect. :g:`BA <0462.g1:/ B A />`, :g:`AN <0462.g1:/ A N />`) :math:`=` (sq. on :g:`BF <0462.g1:/ B F />`) :math:`+` (sq. on :g:`AK <0462.g1:/ A K />`), that is, (sq. on :g:`AB <0462.g1:/ A B />`) :math:`=` (sq. on :g:`BF <0462.g1:/ B F />`) :math:`+` (sq. on :g:`AK <0462.g1:/ A K />`).

If :math:`r` be the radius of the circle, we have seen (XIII. 9, note) that :g:`AK <0462.g1:/ A K />` :math:`= \frac{r}{2}(\sqrt{5} - 1)`.

Therefore

.. math::

   \begin{aligned}
   (\text{side of pentagon})^2 &= r^2 + \frac{r^2}{4}(6 - 2\sqrt{5}) \\
   &= \frac{r^2}{4}(10 - 2\sqrt{5}),
   \end{aligned}

so that

.. math::

   (\text{side of pentagon}) = \frac{r}{2}\sqrt{10 - 2\sqrt{5}}.
