LEMMA.
~~~~~~

.. enunciation::

   It is to be proved that, as :g:`$AB$ <0474.g1:/ A B />` is to :g:`$BC$ <0474.g1:/ B C />`, so is the square on :g:`$AD$ <0474.g1:/ A D />` to the square on :g:`$DC$ <0474.g1:/ D C />`.

----

The Lemma is with reason suspected. Euclid commonly takes more difficult theorems for granted in the stereometrical Books. It is also clumsy in itself, while, from a gloss in the proposition rejected as an interpolation, it is clear that the interpolator of the gloss had not the Lemma. With the Lemma should disappear the words “as will be proved afterwards” (p. 469).

In the figure of the proposition, the semicircle really represents half of a section of the sphere through its centre and one edge of the inscribed tetrahedron (:g:`$AD$ <0472.g1:/ A D />` being the length of that edge).

The proof is in three parts, the object of which is to prove

(1) that :g:`$KEFG$ <0472.g1:< K E F G >>` is a tetrahedron with all its edges equal to :g:`$AD$ <0472.g1:/ A D />`,

(2) that it is inscribable in a sphere of diameter equal to :g:`$AB$ <0472.g1:/ A B />`,

(3) that :g:`$AB$ <0472.g1:/ A B />` $^2 = \frac{3}{2}$ :g:`$AD$ <0472.g1:/ A D />` $^2$.

To prove (1) we have to show

(a) that :g:`$KE$ <0472.g1:/ K E />` $= $ :g:`$KF$ <0472.g1:/ K F />` $= $ :g:`$KG$ <0472.g1:/ K G />` $= $ :g:`$AD$ <0472.g1:/ A D />`,

(b) that :g:`$AD$ <0472.g1:/ A D />` $= $ :g:`$EF$ <0472.g1:/ E F />`.

(a) Since :g:`$HE$ <0472.g1:/ H E />` $= $ :g:`$HF$ <0472.g1:/ H F />` $= $ :g:`$HG$ <0472.g1:/ H G />` $= $ :g:`$CD$ <0472.g1:/ C D />`, :g:`$KH$ <0472.g1:/ K H />` $= $ :g:`$AC$ <0472.g1:/ A C />`, and $\angle\text{s}$ :g:`$ACD$ <0472.g1:< A C D )>`, :g:`$KHE$ <0472.g1:< K H E )>`, :g:`$KHF$ <0472.g1:< K H F )>`, :g:`$KHG$ <0472.g1:< K H G )>` are right, $\Delta\text{s}$ :g:`$ACD$ <0472.g1:< A C D >>`, :g:`$KHE$ <0472.g1:< K H E >>`, :g:`$KHF$ <0472.g1:< K H F >>`, :g:`$KHG$ <0472.g1:< K H G >>` are equal in all respects; therefore :g:`$KE$ <0472.g1:/ K E />` $= $ :g:`$KF$ <0472.g1:/ K F />` $= $ :g:`$KG$ <0472.g1:/ K G />` $= $ :g:`$AD$ <0472.g1:/ A D />`.

(b) Since :g:`$AB$ <0472.g1:/ A B />` $= 3$ :g:`$BC$ <0472.g1:/ B C />`, and :g:`$AB$ <0472.g1:/ A B />` $:$ :g:`$BC$ <0472.g1:/ B C />` $= $ :g:`$AB$ <0472.g1:/ A B />` $\cdot$ :g:`$AC$ <0472.g1:/ A C />` $:$ :g:`$AC$ <0472.g1:/ A C />` $\cdot$ :g:`$CB$ <0472.g1:/ C B />` $= $ :g:`$AD$ <0472.g1:/ A D />` $^2 : $ :g:`$CD$ <0472.g1:/ C D />` $^2$, it follows that :g:`$AD$ <0472.g1:/ A D />` $^2 = 3$ :g:`$CD$ <0472.g1:/ C D />` $^2$. But [XIII. 12] :g:`$EF$ <0472.g1:/ E F />` $^2 = 3$ :g:`$EH$ <0472.g1:/ E H />` $^2$; and :g:`$EH$ <0472.g1:/ E H />` $= $ :g:`$CD$ <0472.g1:/ C D />`, by construction

.. _folio-iii-0476:

.. folio:: 0476
   :page: 472

Therefore :g:`$AD$ <0472.g1:/ A D />` $= $ :g:`$EF$ <0472.g1:/ E F />`. Thus :g:`$EFGK$ <0472.g1:< E F G K >>` is a regular tetrahedron.

(2) We now observe the usefulness of Euclid’s description of a sphere [in XI. Def. 14].

Producing :g:`$KH$ <0472.g1:/ K H />` ($= $ :g:`$AC$ <0472.g1:/ A C />`) to :g:`$L$ <0472.g1:L>` so that :g:`$HL$ <0472.g1:/ H L />` $= $ :g:`$CB$ <0472.g1:/ C B />`, we have :g:`$KL$ <0472.g1:/ K L />` equal to :g:`$AB$ <0472.g1:/ A B />`; thus :g:`$KL$ <0472.g1:/ K L />` is a diameter of the sphere which should circumscribe our tetrahedron, and we have only to prove that :g:`$E$ <0472.g1:E>`, :g:`$F$ <0472.g1:F>`, :g:`$G$ <0472.g1:G>` lie on semicircles described on :g:`$KL$ <0472.g1:/ K L />` as diameter.

E.g. for the point :g:`$E$ <0472.g1:E>`, since :g:`$AC$ <0472.g1:/ A C />` $:$ :g:`$CD$ <0472.g1:/ C D />` $= $ :g:`$CD$ <0472.g1:/ C D />` $:$ :g:`$CB$ <0472.g1:/ C B />`, while :g:`$AC$ <0472.g1:/ A C />` $= $ :g:`$KH$ <0472.g1:/ K H />`, :g:`$CD$ <0472.g1:/ C D />` $= $ :g:`$HE$ <0472.g1:/ H E />`, :g:`$CB$ <0472.g1:/ C B />` $= $ :g:`$HL$ <0472.g1:/ H L />`, we have :g:`$KH$ <0472.g1:/ K H />` $:$ :g:`$HE$ <0472.g1:/ H E />` $= $ :g:`$HE$ <0472.g1:/ H E />` $:$ :g:`$HL$ <0472.g1:/ H L />`, or :g:`$KH$ <0472.g1:/ K H />` $\cdot$ :g:`$HL$ <0472.g1:/ H L />` $= $ :g:`$HE$ <0472.g1:/ H E />` $^2$, whence, the angles :g:`$KHE$ <0472.g1:< K H E )>`, :g:`$EHL$ <0472.g1:< E H L )>` being right, :g:`$EKL$ <0472.g1:< E K L >>` is a triangle right-angled at :g:`$E$ <0472.g1:E>` [cf. VI. 8].

Hence :g:`$E$ <0472.g1:E>` lies on a semicircle on :g:`$KL$ <0472.g1:/ K L />` as diameter. Similarly for :g:`$F$ <0472.g1:F>`, :g:`$G$ <0472.g1:G>`. Thus a semicircle on :g:`$KL$ <0472.g1:/ K L />` as diameter revolving round :g:`$KL$ <0472.g1:/ K L />` passes successively through :g:`$E$ <0472.g1:E>`, :g:`$F$ <0472.g1:F>`, :g:`$G$ <0472.g1:G>`.

(3) :g:`$AB$ <0472.g1:/ A B />` $= 3$ :g:`$BC$ <0472.g1:/ B C />`; therefore :g:`$BA$ <0472.g1:/ B A />` $= \frac{3}{2}$ :g:`$AC$ <0472.g1:/ A C />`. And :g:`$BA$ <0472.g1:/ B A />` $:$ :g:`$AC$ <0472.g1:/ A C />` $= $ :g:`$BA$ <0472.g1:/ B A />` $^2 : $ :g:`$BA$ <0472.g1:/ B A />` $\cdot$ :g:`$AC$ <0472.g1:/ A C />` $= $ :g:`$BA$ <0472.g1:/ B A />` $^2 : $ :g:`$AD$ <0472.g1:/ A D />` $^2$. Therefore :g:`$BA$ <0472.g1:/ B A />` $^2 = \frac{3}{2}$ :g:`$AD$ <0472.g1:/ A D />` $^2$.

If $r$ be the radius of the circumscribed sphere,

$$\text{(edge of tetrahedron)} = \frac{2\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{3}}\cdot r = \frac{2}{3}\sqrt{6}\cdot r.$$

It will be observed that, although in these cases Euclid’s construction is equivalent to inscribing the particular regular solid in a given sphere, he does not actually construct the solid in the sphere but constructs a solid which a sphere *equal* to the given sphere will circumscribe. :person:`Pappus <pappus>`, on the other hand, in dealing with the same problems, actually constructs the respective solids in the given spheres. His method is to find circular sections in the given spheres containing a certain number of the angular points of the given solids. His solutions are interesting, although they require a knowledge of some properties of a sphere which are of course not found in the :cite:`*Elements* <euclid:elements>` but belonged to treatises such as the :cite:`*Sphaerica* <theodosius:sphaerica>` of :person:`Theodosius <theodosius>`.

Pappus’ solution of the problem of Eucl. XIII. 13.
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

In order to inscribe a regular pyramid or tetrahedron in a given sphere, :person:`Pappus <pappus>` (III. pp. 142—144) finds two circular sections equal and parallel to one another, each of which contains one of two opposite edges as its diameter. In this and the other similar problems he proceeds in the orthodox manner by
analysis and synthesis. The following is a reproduction of his solution of this case.

.. _folio-iii-0477:

.. folio:: 0477
   :page: 473

*Analysis.*

Suppose the problem solved, :g:`$A$ <0477.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0477.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0477.g1:C>`, :g:`$D$ <0477.g1:D>` being the angular points of the required pyramid.

Through :g:`$A$ <0477.g1:A>` draw :g:`$EF$ <0477.g1:/ E F />` parallel to :g:`$CD$ <0477.g1:/ C D />`; this will make equal angles with :g:`$AC$ <0477.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$AD$ <0477.g1:/ A D />`; and, since :g:`$AB$ <0477.g1:/ A B />` does so too, :g:`$EF$ <0477.g1:/ E F />` is perpendicular to :g:`$AB$ <0477.g1:/ A B />` [:person:`Pappus <pappus>` has a lemma for this, p. 140, 12—24], and is therefore a tangent to the sphere (for :g:`$EF$ <0477.g1:/ E F />` is parallel to :g:`$CD$ <0477.g1:/ C D />`, the base of the triangle :g:`$ACD$ <0477.g1:< A C D >>`, and therefore touches the circle circumscribing it, while it also touches the circular section :g:`$AB$ <0477.g1:( A B )>` made by the plane passing through :g:`$AB$ <0477.g1:/ A B />` and :g:`$EF$ <0477.g1:/ E F />` perpendicular to it).

.. picture:: 0477.g1.svg
   :alt: Pappus' construction of regular tetrahedron in a sphere

Similarly :g:`$GH$ <0477.g1:/ G H />` drawn through :g:`$D$ <0477.g1:D>` parallel to :g:`$AB$ <0477.g1:/ A B />` touches the sphere.

And the plane through :g:`$GH$ <0477.g1:/ G H />`, :g:`$CD$ <0477.g1:/ C D />` makes a circular section equal and parallel to :g:`$AB$ <0477.g1:( A B )>`.

Through the centre :g:`$K$ <0477.g1:K>` of that circular section, and in the plane of the section, draw :g:`$LM$ <0477.g1:/ L M />` perpendicular to :g:`$CD$ <0477.g1:/ C D />` and therefore parallel to :g:`$AB$ <0477.g1:/ A B />`. Join :g:`$BL$ <0477.g1:/ B L />`, :g:`$BM$ <0477.g1:/ B M />`.

:g:`$BM$ <0477.g1:/ B M />` is then perpendicular to :g:`$AB$ <0477.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$LM$ <0477.g1:/ L M />`, and :g:`$LB$ <0477.g1:/ L B />` is a diameter of the sphere. Join :g:`$MC$ <0477.g1:/ M C />`.

Then :g:`$LM$ <0477.g1:/ L M />` $^2 = 2$ :g:`$MC$ <0477.g1:/ M C />` $^2$, and :g:`$BC$ <0477.g1:/ B C />` $= $ :g:`$AB$ <0477.g1:/ A B />` $= $ :g:`$LM$ <0477.g1:/ L M />`, so that :g:`$BC$ <0477.g1:/ B C />` $^2 = 2$ :g:`$MC$ <0477.g1:/ M C />` $^2$.

And :g:`$BM$ <0477.g1:/ B M />`, being perpendicular to the plane of the circle :g:`$LM$ <0477.g1:( L M )>`, is perpendicular to :g:`$CM$ <0477.g1:/ C M />`, whence :g:`$BC$ <0477.g1:/ B C />` $^2 = $ :g:`$BM$ <0477.g1:/ B M />` $^2 + $ :g:`$MC$ <0477.g1:/ M C />` $^2$, so that :g:`$BM$ <0477.g1:/ B M />` $= $ :g:`$MC$ <0477.g1:/ M C />`. But :g:`$BC$ <0477.g1:/ B C />` $= $ :g:`$LM$ <0477.g1:/ L M />`; therefore :g:`$LM$ <0477.g1:/ L M />` $^2 = 2$ :g:`$BM$ <0477.g1:/ B M />` $^2$.

And, since the angle :g:`$LMB$ <0477.g1:< L M B )>` is right, :g:`$BL$ <0477.g1:/ B L />` $^2 = $ :g:`$LM$ <0477.g1:/ L M />` $^2 + $ :g:`$MB$ <0477.g1:/ M B />` $^2 = \frac{3}{2}$ :g:`$LM$ <0477.g1:/ L M />` $^2$.

*Synthesis.*

Draw two parallel circular sections of the sphere with diameter $d'$, such that

$$d'^2 = \frac{2}{3}d^2,$$

where $d$ is the diameter of the sphere.

[This is easily done by dividing :g:`$BL$ <0477.g1:/ B L />`, any diameter of the sphere, at :g:`$P$ <0477.g1:P>`, so that :g:`$LP$ <0477.g1:/ L P />` $= 2$ :g:`$PB$ <0477.g1:/ P B />`, and then drawing :g:`$PM$ <0477.g1:/ P M />` at right angles to :g:`$LB$ <0477.g1:/ L B />` meeting the great circle :g:`$LMB$ <0477.g1:( L M B )>` of the sphere in :g:`$M$ <0477.g1:M>`. Then :g:`$LM$ <0477.g1:/ L M />` $^2 : $ :g:`$LB$ <0477.g1:/ L B />` $^2 = $ :g:`$LP$ <0477.g1:/ L P />` $:$ :g:`$LB$ <0477.g1:/ L B />` $= 2 : 3$.]

Draw sections through :g:`$M$ <0477.g1:M>`, :g:`$B$ <0477.g1:B>` perpendicular to :g:`$MB$ <0477.g1:/ M B />`, and in these sections respectively draw the parallel diameters :g:`$LM$ <0477.g1:/ L M />`, :g:`$AB$ <0477.g1:/ A B />`.

Lastly, in the section :g:`$LM$ <0477.g1:( L M )>` draw :g:`$CD$ <0477.g1:/ C D />` through the centre :g:`$K$ <0477.g1:K>` perpendicular to :g:`$LM$ <0477.g1:/ L M />`.

:g:`$ABCD$ <0477.g1:< A B C D >>` is then the required regular pyramid or tetrahedron.
