Commentary on Proposition XIII.18
=================================

Back to :ref:`Proposition XIII.18 <XIII.18>`.

.. folio:: 0511
   :page: 507

.. enunciation::

   I say next that *no other figure, besides the said five figures, can be constructed which is contained by equilateral and equiangular figures equal to one another.*

.. assertion::

   For a solid angle cannot be constructed with two triangles, or indeed planes.

.. assertion::

   With three triangles the angle of the pyramid is constructed, with four the angle of the octahedron, and with five the angle of the icosahedron ; but a solid angle cannot be formed by six equilateral and equiangular triangles placed together at one point,

.. _folio-iii-0512:

.. folio:: 0512
   :page: 508

for, the angle of the equilateral triangle being two-thirds of a right angle, the six will be equal to four right angles: which is impossible, for any solid angle is contained by angles less than four right angles. :just:`[XI. 21] <XI.21>`

For the same reason, neither can a solid angle be constructed by more than six plane angles.

By three squares the angle of the cube is contained, but by four it is impossible for a solid angle to be contained, for they will again be four right angles.

By three equilateral and equiangular pentagons the angle of the dodecahedron is contained; but by four such it is impossible for any solid angle to be contained, for, the angle of the equilateral pentagon being a right angle and a fifth, the four angles will be greater than four right angles: which is impossible.

Neither again will a solid angle be contained by other polygonal figures by reason of the same absurdity.

.. assertion::

   Therefore etc.

.. qed::

   :just:`Q. E. D.`

LEMMA.
~~~~~~

.. enunciation::

   But that the angle of the equilateral and equiangular pentagon is a right angle and a fifth we must prove thus.

Let :g:`ABCDE <0512.g1:< A B C D E >>` be an :eq:`equilateral <0512.g1:/ A B / = 0512.g1:/ B C / = 0512.g1:/ C D / = 0512.g1:/ D E / = 0512.g1:/ E A />` and equiangular pentagon, let the circle :g:`ABCDE <0512.g1:( A B C D E )>` be circumscribed about it, let its centre :g:`F <0512.g1:F>` be taken, and let :g:`FA <0512.g1:/ F A />`, :g:`FB <0512.g1:/ F B />`, :g:`FC <0512.g1:/ F C />`, :g:`FD <0512.g1:/ F D />`, :g:`FE <0512.g1:/ F E />` be joined.

.. assertion::

   Therefore they bisect the angles of the pentagon at :g:`A <0512.g1:A>`, :g:`B <0512.g1:B>`, :g:`C <0512.g1:C>`, :g:`D <0512.g1:D>`, :g:`E <0512.g1:E>`.

.. subclause::

   And, since the angles at :g:`F <0512.g1:F>` are equal to four right angles and are :eq:`equal <0512.g1:< A F B ) = 0512.g1:< B F C ) = 0512.g1:< C F D ) = 0512.g1:< D F E ) = 0512.g1:< E F A )>`,

.. picture:: 0512.g1.svg
   :alt: Lemma Pentagon Diagram

.. _folio-iii-0513:

.. folio:: 0513
   :page: 509

.. assertion::

   therefore one of them, as the angle :g:`AFB <0512.g1:< A F B )>`, is one right angle less a fifth; therefore the remaining angles :g:`FAB <0512.g1:< F A B )>`, :g:`ABF <0512.g1:< A B F )>` consist of one right angle and a fifth.

.. subclause::

   But the angle :g:`FAB <0512.g1:< F A B )>` is :eq:`equal <0512.g1:< F A B ) = 0512.g1:< F B C )>` to the angle :g:`FBC <0512.g1:< F B C )>`;

.. assertion::

   therefore the whole angle :g:`ABC <0512.g1:< A B C )>` of the pentagon consists of one right angle and a fifth.

We have seen in the preceding notes that, if $r$ be the radius of the sphere circumscribing the five solid figures,

$$\begin{aligned}
(\text{edge of tetrahedron}) &= \tfrac{2}{3} \sqrt{6} \cdot r, \\
(\text{edge of octahedron}) &= \sqrt{2} \cdot r, \\
(\text{edge of cube}) &= \tfrac{2}{3} \sqrt{3} \cdot r, \\
(\text{edge of icosahedron}) &= \tfrac{r}{5} \sqrt{10(5-\sqrt{5})}, \\
(\text{edge of dodecahedron}) &= \tfrac{r}{3} (\sqrt{15}-\sqrt{3}).
\end{aligned}$$

Euclid here exhibits the edges of all the five regular solids in one figure.

(1) Make :g:`AD <0507.g1:/ A D />` equal to $2$ :g:`DB <0507.g1:/ D B />`. Thus :g:`BA <0507.g1:/ A B />` $= \tfrac{3}{2}$ :g:`AD <0507.g1:/ A D />`, and :g:`BA <0507.g1:/ A B />` $:$ :g:`AD <0507.g1:/ A D />` $=$ :g:`BA <0507.g1:/ A B />` $^2 :$ :g:`AF <0507.g1:/ A F />` $^2$; therefore :g:`BA <0507.g1:/ A B />` $^2 = \tfrac{3}{2}$ :g:`AF <0507.g1:/ A F />` $^2$. Thus :g:`AF <0507.g1:/ A F />` $= \sqrt{\tfrac{2}{3}} \cdot 2r = \tfrac{2}{3} \sqrt{6} \cdot r = (\text{edge of tetrahedron})$.

(2) :g:`AB <0507.g1:/ A B />` $^2 :$ :g:`BF <0507.g1:/ F B />` $^2 =$ :g:`AB <0507.g1:/ A B />` $:$ :g:`BD <0507.g1:/ D B />` $= 3 : 1$. Therefore :g:`BF <0507.g1:/ F B />` $^2 = \tfrac{1}{3}$ :g:`AB <0507.g1:/ A B />` $^2$, or :g:`BF <0507.g1:/ F B />` $= \frac{2}{\sqrt{3}} \cdot r = \tfrac{2}{3} \sqrt{3} \cdot r = (\text{edge of cube})$.

(3) :g:`AB <0507.g1:/ A B />` $^2 = 2$ :g:`BE <0507.g1:/ E B />` $^2$. Therefore :g:`BE <0507.g1:/ E B />` $= \sqrt{2} \cdot r = (\text{edge of octahedron})$.

(4) Draw :g:`AG <0507.g1:/ A G />` perpendicular and :eq:`equal <0507.g1:/ A G / = 0507.g1:/ A B />` to :g:`AB <0507.g1:/ A B />`. Join :g:`GC <0507.g1:/ G C />`, meeting the semicircle in :g:`H <0507.g1:H>`, and draw :g:`HK <0507.g1:/ H K />` perpendicular to :g:`AB <0507.g1:/ A B />`. Then :g:`GA <0507.g1:/ A G />` $= 2$ :g:`AC <0507.g1:/ A C />`; therefore, by similar triangles, :g:`HK <0507.g1:/ H K />` $= 2$ :g:`KC <0507.g1:/ C K />`. Hence :g:`HK <0507.g1:/ H K />` $^2 = 4$ :g:`KC <0507.g1:/ C K />` $^2$, and therefore $5$ :g:`KC <0507.g1:/ C K />` $^2 =$ :g:`HK <0507.g1:/ H K />` $^2 +$ :g:`KC <0507.g1:/ C K />` $^2$ $= $ :g:`HC <0507.g1:/ H C />` $^2$ $= $ :g:`CB <0507.g1:/ C B />` $^2$.

Again, since :g:`AB <0507.g1:/ A B />` $= 2$ :g:`CB <0507.g1:/ C B />`, and :g:`AD <0507.g1:/ A D />` $= 2$ :g:`DB <0507.g1:/ D B />`, by subtraction, :g:`BD <0507.g1:/ D B />` $= 2$ :g:`DC <0507.g1:/ C D />`, or :g:`BC <0507.g1:/ C B />` $= 3$ :g:`DC <0507.g1:/ C D />`.

.. _folio-iii-0514:

.. folio:: 0514
   :page: 510

Therefore $9$ :g:`DC <0507.g1:/ C D />` $^2 =$ :g:`BC <0507.g1:/ C B />` $^2$ $= 5$ :g:`KC <0507.g1:/ C K />` $^2$.

Hence :g:`KC <0507.g1:/ C K />` $>$ :g:`CD <0507.g1:/ C D />`.

Make :g:`CL <0507.g1:/ C L />` :eq:`equal <0507.g1:/ C L / = 0507.g1:/ C K />` to :g:`KC <0507.g1:/ C K />`, draw :g:`LM <0507.g1:/ L M />` at right angles to :g:`AB <0507.g1:/ A B />`, and join :g:`AM <0507.g1:/ A M />`, :g:`MB <0507.g1:/ M B />`.

Since :g:`CB <0507.g1:/ C B />` $^2 = 5$ :g:`KC <0507.g1:/ C K />` $^2$, :g:`AB <0507.g1:/ A B />` $^2 = 5$ :g:`KL <0507.g1:/ K L />` $^2$.

It follows that :g:`KL <0507.g1:/ K L />` ($= \sqrt{\tfrac{4}{5}} \cdot r$) is the radius of, or the side of the regular hexagon in, the circle containing the pentagonal sections of the icosahedron. :just:`[XIII. 16] <XIII.16>`

And, since $2r = (\text{side of hexagon}) + 2 (\text{side of decagon in same circle})$ :just:`[XIII. 16, Por.]` :g:`AK <0507.g1:/ A K />` $=$ :g:`LB <0507.g1:/ L B />` $= (\text{side of decagon in the said circle})$.

But :g:`LM <0507.g1:/ L M />` $=$ :g:`HK <0507.g1:/ H K />` $=$ :g:`KL <0507.g1:/ K L />` $= (\text{side of hexagon in circle})$.

Therefore :g:`LM <0507.g1:/ L M />` $^2 +$ :g:`LB <0507.g1:/ L B />` $^2 (=$ :g:`BM <0507.g1:/ M B />` $^2) = (\text{side of pentagon in circle})^2$ :just:`[XIII. 10] <XIII.10>` $= (\text{edge of icosahedron})^2$, and :g:`BM <0507.g1:/ M B />` $= (\text{edge of icosahedron})$.

[More shortly, :g:`HK <0507.g1:/ H K />` $= 2$ :g:`KC <0507.g1:/ C K />`, whence :g:`HK <0507.g1:/ H K />` $^2 = 4$ :g:`KC <0507.g1:/ C K />` $^2$, and $5$ :g:`KC <0507.g1:/ C K />` $^2 =$ :g:`HC <0507.g1:/ H C />` $^2 = r^2$.

Also :g:`AK <0507.g1:/ A K />` $= r -$ :g:`CK <0507.g1:/ C K />` $= r \left( 1 - \frac{1}{\sqrt{5}} \right)$.

Thus :g:`BM <0507.g1:/ M B />` $^2 =$ :g:`HK <0507.g1:/ H K />` $^2 +$ :g:`AK <0507.g1:/ A K />` $^2$ $= \tfrac{4}{5} r^2 + r^2 \left( 1 - \frac{1}{\sqrt{5}} \right)^2$ $= r^2 \left( \frac{10}{5} - \frac{2}{\sqrt{5}} \right)$ $= \frac{r^2}{5} (10 - 2\sqrt{5})$, and :g:`BM <0507.g1:/ M B />` $= \frac{r}{5} \sqrt{10(5-\sqrt{5})} = (\text{edge of icosahedron})$.]

(5) Cut :g:`BF <0507.g1:/ F B />` (the edge of the cube) in extreme and mean ratio at :g:`N <0507.g1:N>`. Then, if :g:`BN <0507.g1:/ N B />` be the greater segment, :g:`BN <0507.g1:/ N B />` $= (\text{edge of dodecahedron})$. :just:`[XIII. 17] <XIII.17>`

[Solving, we obtain :g:`BN <0507.g1:/ N B />` $= \frac{\sqrt{5}-1}{2} \cdot$ :g:`BF <0507.g1:/ F B />` $= \frac{\sqrt{5}-1}{2} \cdot \frac{2}{\sqrt{3}} \cdot r$ $= \frac{r}{3} (\sqrt{15}-\sqrt{3})$ $= (\text{edge of dodecahedron})$.]

.. _folio-iii-0515:

.. folio:: 0515
   :page: 511

(6) If $t, o, c$ are the edges of the tetrahedron, octahedron and cube respectively,

$$4r^2 = \tfrac{3}{2} t^2 = 2o^2 = 3c^2.$$

If each of these equals is put equal to $X$,

$$\begin{aligned}
4r^2 &= X, \\
t^2 &= \tfrac{2}{3} \cdot X, \\
o^2 &= \tfrac{1}{2} \cdot X, \\
c^2 &= \tfrac{1}{3} \cdot X,
\end{aligned}$$

whence

$$4r^2 : t^2 : o^2 : c^2 = 6 : 4 : 3 : 2,$$

and the ratios between $2r, t, o, c$ are all rational (in Euclid's sense). The ratios between these and the edges of the icosahedron and the dodecahedron are *irrational*.

(7) To prove that

$$(\text{edge of icosahedron}) > (\text{edge of dodecahedron}),$$

i.e. that :g:`MB <0507.g1:/ M B />` $>$ :g:`NB <0507.g1:/ N B />`.

.. subclause::

   By similar $\triangle\text{s}$ :g:`FDB <0507.g1:< F D B >>`, :g:`AFB <0507.g1:< A F B >>`,

.. assertion::

   :g:`DB <0507.g1:/ D B />` $:$ :g:`BF <0507.g1:/ F B />` $=$ :g:`BF <0507.g1:/ F B />` $:$ :g:`BA <0507.g1:/ A B />`, or :g:`DB <0507.g1:/ D B />` $:$ :g:`BA <0507.g1:/ A B />` $=$ :g:`DB <0507.g1:/ D B />` $^2 :$ :g:`BF <0507.g1:/ F B />` $^2$.

.. subclause::

   But $3$ :g:`DB <0507.g1:/ D B />` $=$ :g:`BA <0507.g1:/ A B />`;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`BF <0507.g1:/ F B />` $^2 = 3$ :g:`DB <0507.g1:/ D B />` $^2$.

.. subclause::

   By hypothesis, :g:`AD <0507.g1:/ A D />` $^2 = 4$ :g:`DB <0507.g1:/ D B />` $^2$;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`AD <0507.g1:/ A D />` $>$ :g:`BF <0507.g1:/ F B />`, and, :term:`*a fortiori* <a-fortiori>`, :g:`AL <0507.g1:/ A L />` $>$ :g:`FB <0507.g1:/ F B />`.

.. subclause::

   Now :g:`LK <0507.g1:/ K L />` is the side of a hexagon, and :g:`AK <0507.g1:/ A K />` the side of a decagon in the same circle;

.. assertion::

   therefore, when :g:`AL <0507.g1:/ A L />` is divided in extreme and mean ratio, :g:`KL <0507.g1:/ K L />` is the greater segment.

.. subclause::

   And, when :g:`FB <0507.g1:/ F B />` is divided in extreme and mean ratio, :g:`BN <0507.g1:/ N B />` is the greater segment.

.. assertion::

   Therefore, since :g:`AL <0507.g1:/ A L />` $>$ :g:`BF <0507.g1:/ F B />`, :g:`KL <0507.g1:/ K L />` $>$ :g:`BN <0507.g1:/ N B />`, or :g:`LM <0507.g1:/ L M />` $>$ :g:`BN <0507.g1:/ N B />`.

.. assertion::

   And therefore, :term:`*a fortiori* <a-fortiori>`, :g:`MB <0507.g1:/ M B />` $>$ :g:`BN <0507.g1:/ N B />`.
