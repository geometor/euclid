Bibliography¶

Primary Editions, Manuscript Archetypes & Scholarly Literature

This unified bibliography brings together the authoritative Greek text editions, landmark translations, and manuscript traditions underpinning the GEOMETOR editions of Euclid’s Elements and Data.

Primary Editions & Archival Witnesses¶

Euclidis Opera Omnia (Teubner Critical Edition)¶

  • Citation Key: heiberg:1883 / menge:1896

  • Editors: Johan Ludvig Heiberg and Heinrich Menge

  • Full Title: Euclidis Opera Omnia, 8 volumes plus Supplementum (Leipzig: B. G. Teubner, 1883–1916).

    • Vols. I–IV (1883–1885): Elementa, Books I–XIII (ed. J. L. Heiberg).

    • Vol. V (1888): Elementa, Books XIV–XV (ed. J. L. Heiberg).

    • Vol. VI (1896): Data cum commentario Marini et scholiis antiquis (ed. H. Menge).

    • Vol. VII (1895): Optica, Catoptrica, Phaenomena (ed. J. L. Heiberg).

The definitive critical edition establishing the pre-Theonine Greek text from the primary manuscript witnesses, accompanied by scholarly Latin translations, apparatus criticus, and ancient scholia.

The Thirteen Books of Euclid’s Elements (Heath 1908 / 1926)¶

  • Citation Key: heath:1926

  • Editor & Translator: Sir Thomas Little Heath

  • Full Title: The Thirteen Books of Euclid’s Elements, Translated from the Text of Heiberg with Introduction and Commentary, 3 volumes (Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 1908; 2nd edition, revised with additions, 1926; reprinted New York: Dover Publications, 1956).

    • Volume I: Introduction and Books I, II.

    • Volume II: Books III–IX.

    • Volume III: Books X–XIII and Excursus.

The standard reference edition for English-speaking scholarship, incorporating exhaustive historical introductions, textual notes, and geometric commentary.

Simson’s Critical Edition of the Elements and Data (1756 / 1762)¶

  • Citation Key: simson:1762

  • Editor & Translator: Robert Simson

  • Full Title: The Elements of Euclid, viz. the First Six Books, together with the Eleventh and Twelfth… Also the Book of Euclid’s Data, in like manner restored (Glasgow: Printed by Robert & Andrew Foulis, 1756 [Latin], 1762 [English]; 2nd ed. London: F. Wingrave, 1806).

Simson’s restoration rescued the Data from corrupted medieval translations, reconstructing numerous damaged propositions and providing the definitive foundation for analytical geometry education.

Key Manuscript Witnesses¶

  • Codex Vaticanus Graecus 190 (Codex P): Ninth-century minuscule manuscript preserved in the Vatican Library. Identified by F. Peyrard in 1808 and collated by J. L. Heiberg, this codex preserves the ancient pre-Theonine recension of both the Elements and the Data.

  • Codex Bodleianus D’Orville 301: Written in Constantinople in 888 CE by the scribe Stephen for Arethas of Caesarea; now preserved in the Bodleian Library, Oxford. One of the oldest dated Greek manuscripts of the Elements.
