Data¶

Δεδομένα — Bilingual Critical Edition (Menge 1896 & Simson 1806)

Author: Euclid of Alexandria (fl. c. 300 BCE)
Protheoria: Marinus of Neapolis (c. 440 – c. 495 CE)
Critical Greek Edition: Heinrich Menge (Euclidis Opera Omnia, Vol. VI, Leipzig: B. G. Teubner, 1896)
Critical Reconstruction: Robert Simson (Glasgow 1762 / London 1806)

Composed by Euclid of Alexandria alongside the thirteen books of the Elements, the Data (Δεδομένα, Dedomena) is the foundational grammar of Greek geometric analysis: given certain magnitudes, ratios, angles, or loci in hypothesis, what further geometric entities are thereby uniquely determined (πόριμον)?

Functional Architecture of the Data¶

Interactive architecture map: Protheoria & Definitions 1–15 grounds the modes of givenness alongside Euclid's Elements I–VI. From these rise Magnitudes & Ratios (D.1–24) and Position & Parallel Loci (D.25–38), converging in Triangle & Polygon Species (D.39–55), branching through Area Application & Cosine Metric (D.56–67) and Proportional Figures (D.68–83), and culminating in Quadratic Systems & Circle Loci (D.84–94)—powering Pappus's Treasury of Analysis (Synagoge VII).
