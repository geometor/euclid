Proposition 10 [ι΄ · Prop. XVI]¶

If a magnitude be greater than a magnitude by a given magnitude than in ratio, the compounded magnitude will also be greater than the same by a given magnitude than in ratio; and if the compounded magnitude be greater than the same by a given magnitude than in ratio, either the remainder is also greater than the same by a given magnitude than in ratio, or the remainder together with the following magnitude to which the other has a given ratio is given.

Functional Signature: Given((AB - D) : BC = k) <-> Determined(((AB+BC) - D) : BC = k+1 | (AB + BE = D where BC:BE given)) · Dependencies: [Data Def. 9], [Data Def. 11], [Data 3], [Data 4], [Data 5], [Data 6], [V.18], [V.19]

First, let the magnitude \(AB\) be greater by a given magnitude than in ratio to \(BC\). I say that the compound \(AC\) is also greater by a given magnitude than in ratio to the same \(BC\).

From \(AB\) let the given magnitude \(AD\) be subtracted ([Data Def. 11]); then the ratio of the remainder \(DB\) to \(BC\) is given. Componendo, the ratio \(DC : BC\) is given ([Data 6], [V.18]). And since \(AD\) is given, the whole \(CA\) is greater than \(DC\) by the given magnitude \(AD\), and hence \(AC\) is greater by a given magnitude than in ratio to \(BC\) ([Data Def. 11]).

Conversely, let the compound \(AC\) be greater by a given magnitude than in ratio to \(BC\). I say that the remaining part \(AB\) is either greater by a given magnitude than in ratio to \(BC\), or else \(AB\) together with a magnitude to which \(BC\) has a given ratio is given.

Subtract the given excess from \(AC\) ([Data Def. 11]). This given magnitude is either less than \(AB\) or greater than \(AB\) (if equal to \(AB\), then \(BC\) itself is in a given ratio to \(BC\), a boundary case). First let it be less than \(AB\), and let it be \(AD\): then \(DC : CB\) is given, so separando \(DB : BC\) is given ([Data 5], [V.17]); and \(AD\) is given, so \(AB\) is greater by a given magnitude than in ratio to \(BC\) ([Data Def. 11]). Next let the given magnitude be greater than \(AB\), and let \(AE\) be set equal to it (with \(E\) falling between \(B\) and \(C\)): then the remainder \(EC\) has a given ratio to \(CB\), so invertendo \(BC : EC\) is given, and convertendo \(BC : BE\) is given ([Data 5]). Yet \(AE = AB + BE\) is given; therefore \(AB\) together with \(BE\)—to which \(BC\) has a given ratio—is given.
