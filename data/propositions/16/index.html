Proposition 16 [ιϛ΄ · Prop. XX]¶

If two magnitudes have a given ratio to one another, and from one of them a given magnitude be subtracted while to the other a given magnitude be added, the whole will be greater than the remainder by a given magnitude than in ratio.

Functional Signature: Given(Ratio(A : C) = r, Mag(d1), Mag(d2)) -> Determined(((A+d2) - (r*d1+d2)) : (C-d1) = r) · Dependencies: [Data Def. 11], [Data 2], [Data 3], [V.19]

Let two magnitudes $AB$ and $CD$ have a given ratio to one another; from $AB$ let the given magnitude $AE$ be subtracted, and to $CD$ let the given magnitude $DF$ be added. I say that the remainder $EB$ is less by a given magnitude than in ratio to the whole $CF$.

Since the ratio $AB : CD$ is given, make $GB : DF = AB : CD$, adjoining $GB$ to $AB$ at $B$ ([Data Def. 2]). Because $DF$ is given and $GB : DF$ is given, $GB$ is given ([Data 2]). Since $AE$ is also given, the sum $AE + GB$ is given ([Data 3]). Now since $AB : CD = GB : DF$, the compound $AG = AB + GB$ is to the compound $CF = CD + DF$ in the given ratio $AB : CD$ ([V.12]). Yet $AG = EB + (AE + GB)$ exceeds the remainder $EB$ by the given magnitude $AE + GB$. Therefore $EB$, together with the given magnitude $AE + GB$, has a given ratio to $CF$; that is, $EB$ is less by a given magnitude than in ratio to $CF$ ([Data Def. 12]).
