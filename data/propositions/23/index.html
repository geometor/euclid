Proposition 23 [κγ΄ · Prop. XII]¶

If a whole have to a whole a given ratio, and the parts have to the parts given ratios, yet not the same ratios, all the parts will have given ratios to all.

Functional Signature: Given(AB:CD = r0, AE:CF = r1 != r0, EB:FD = r2) -> Determined(All pairwise ratios) · Dependencies: [Data 5], [Data 8], [V.11], [V.16], [V.19]

Let the whole $AB$ have a given ratio to the whole $CD$, and let each of the parts $AE, EB$ of $AB$ have given ratios to the parts $CF, FD$ of $CD$ respectively, but not the same ratios (μὴ τοὺς αὐτοὺς δέ, so $AE : CF \neq EB : FD$). I say that all have given ratios to all.

Make $AE : CG = AB : CD$ ([Data Def. 2]). Since $AB : CD$ and $AE : CF$ are distinct ratios, $CG \neq CF$; suppose $G$ falls beyond $F$ on $CD$ (so $CG > CF$). Because $AE : CF$ is given and $AE : CG (= AB : CD)$ is given, the ratio $CF : CG$ is given ([Data 8]), whence convertendo $CF : FG$ is given ([Data 5]). Moreover, since whole $AB$ is to whole $CD$ as subtracted $AE$ is to subtracted $CG$, the remainder $EB$ is to the remainder $GD$ as $AB$ is to $CD$ ([V.19]), so $EB : GD$ is given. Yet $EB : FD$ is also given by hypothesis; hence $FD : GD$ is given ([Data 8]), and separando $FG : GD$ (and thus $FG : FD$) is given ([Data 5]). Combining $CF : FG$ and $FG : FD$ yields $CF : FD$ given ([Data 8]), whence $CD : CF$ and $CD : FD$ are given ([Data 6]). Chaining with the given ratios $AE : CF$ and $EB : FD$ gives $AE : EB$ and every pairwise ratio among $AB, AE, EB, CD, CF, FD$ ([Data 8], [Data 9]).
