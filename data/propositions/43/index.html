Proposition 43 [μγ΄ · Prop. XLVI]¶

If of a right-angled triangle the sides about one of the acute angles have a given ratio to one another, the triangle is given in species.

Functional Signature: Given(Angle(A) = pi/2, Ratio(hypotenuse c : leg b)) -> Determined(Species(Triangle ABC)) · Dependencies: [Data Def. 3], [Data 2], [Data 25], [Data 26], [Data 27], [Data 29], [Data 31], [I.47], [VI.7]

Let $\triangle ABC$ be right-angled at $A$ ($\angle BAC = 1\text{ rt.}$), and let the sides $BC$ and $BA$ about one of the other angles $\angle ABC$ have a given ratio $BC : BA$. I say that $\triangle ABC$ is given in species.

Set out a straight line $DE$ given in position and magnitude, and on diameter $DE$ describe the semicircle $DGE$ ([Post. 3]), whose centre (the midpoint of $DE$, [Data 7], [Data 27]) and radius are given, so the semicircle $DGE$ is given in position ([Data Def. 6]). Make $DE : F = BC : BA$ ([Data Def. 2]); since $DE$ is given and $BC : BA$ is given (with $BC > BA$ by [I.19]), $F$ is given in magnitude ([Data 2]) and $F < DE$. With centre $D$ and radius $DG = F$, describe a circle meeting the semicircle $DGE$ at the unique point $G$ ([Data 25]), and join $DG, GE$ ([Data 26]). Then $\triangle DGE$ is given in species ([Data 39]) and $\angle DGE$ in the semicircle is right ([III.31]). In $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle DGE$, $\angle BAC = \angle DGE = 1\text{ rt.}$, $BC : BA = DE : DG$, and the angles $\angle ACB, \angle DEG$ opposite $BA, DG$ are both acute ([I.17]); hence $\triangle ABC \sim \triangle DGE$ ([VI.7]), so $\triangle ABC$ is given in species.
