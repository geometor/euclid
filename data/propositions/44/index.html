Proposition 44 [μδ΄ · Prop. XLVII]¶

If a triangle have one angle given, and the sides about another angle have a given ratio to one another, the triangle is given in species.

Functional Signature: Given(Angle(A), Ratio(AB : BC), Sign(cos C)) -> Determined(Species(Triangle ABC)) [SSA diorismos] · Dependencies: [Data Def. 3], [Data 2], [Data 25], [Data 26], [Data 27], [Data 29], [Data 31], [I.13], [VI.7]

Let $\triangle ABC$ have one given angle $\angle ABC$, and let the sides $AB$ and $AC$ about another angle $\angle BAC$ have a given ratio $AB : AC$—assuming the specification (diorismos) whether $\angle ACB$ is obtuse or acute is fixed. I say that $\triangle ABC$ is given in species.

From $A$ drop $AD$ perpendicular to $BC$ (or $BC$ produced) ([I.12]). In right-angled $\triangle ABD$, $\angle ADB = 1\text{ rt.}$ and $\angle ABD$ (or its supplement) is given, so all angles of $\triangle ABD$ are given ([I.32]), whence $\triangle ABD$ is given in species ([Data 40]). Therefore the ratio $BA : AD$ is given ([Data Def. 3]). Yet the ratio $CA : AB$ is given by hypothesis; ex aequali, the ratio $CA : AD$ is given ([Data 8]). Now $\triangle ADC$ is right-angled at $D$ with the ratio of hypotenuse $CA$ to leg $AD$ given; hence $\triangle ADC$ is given in species ([Data 43]), so the acute angle $\angle ACD$ is given! Depending on the single branch specified (whether $D$ falls on $BC$ with $\angle ACB$ acute, or on $BC$ produced with $\angle ACB = 2\text{ rt.} - \angle ACD$ obtuse), $\angle ACB$ is uniquely given. Together with given $\angle ABC$, all angles of $\triangle ABC$ are given ([I.32]), so $\triangle ABC$ is given in species ([Data 40]).
