Proposition 68 [ξη΄ · Prop. LXV]¶

If two equiangular parallelograms have a given ratio to one another, and one side have to one side a given ratio, the remaining side will also have to the remaining side a given ratio.

Functional Signature: Given(Equiangular(P1, P2), Ratio(P1 : P2), Ratio(s1 : t1)) -> Determined(Ratio(s2 : t2)) · Dependencies: [Data 2], [Data 8], [Data 56], [I.31], [VI.1], [VI.14]

Let two equiangular parallelograms $AB$ ($= ACDB$) and $EF$ ($= EGHF$) have a given area ratio $AB : EF$, and let one side $BD$ have a given ratio to one side $FH$. I say that the remaining side $CD$ also has a given ratio to the remaining side $GH$.

Along $CD$ (produced if necessary) apply to $BD$ the equiangular parallelogram $BDKL$ equal in area to $EF$ ([I.44]). Because $AB : EF$ is given and $BDKL = EF$, the ratio $AB : BDKL$ is given; since $AB$ and $BDKL$ share the base $BD$ in the same angle, $CD : DK = AB : BDKL$ ([VI.1]), so $CD : DK$ is given. Moreover, because $BDKL$ and $EF$ are equal and equiangular, their sides about the equal angles are reciprocally proportional: $BD : FH = GH : DK$ ([VI.14]). Since $BD : FH$ is given, $GH : DK$ is given; combining $CD : DK$ with $DK : GH$ yields $CD : GH$ given ([Data 8]).
