Proposition 86 [πϛ΄ · Prop. LXXXIX (cf. LXXXVII–LXXXVIII)]¶

If two straight lines contain a given area in a given angle, and the square on one be greater than the square on the other by a given area than in ratio, each of them will also be given.

Functional Signature: Given(Angle B, x*y*sin B = S, (y^2 - D) : x^2 = k) -> Determined(Mag(x), Mag(y)) · Dependencies: [Data 1], [Data 2], [Data 8], [Data 55], [Data 57], [Data 58], [Data 59], [Data 68], [II.4], [II.6], [VI.1], [VI.17], [VI.20]

Let two straight lines $AB$ and $BC$ contain a given area $AB \cdot BC$ in a given angle $\angle ABC$, and let the square $BC^2$ be greater by a given area than in ratio to the square $AB^2$. I say that each of $AB$ and $BC$ is given in magnitude.

Since $BC^2$ is greater by a given area than in ratio to $AB^2$, subtract the given area—expressed as a rectangle $BC \cdot BD$ applied to $BC$ ([I.44]), so $BC^2 - BC \cdot BD = BC \cdot DC$ ([II.2]) has a given ratio to $AB^2$ ([Data Def. 11]). Moreover, since the parallelogram $ABEC$ in the given angle $\angle ABC$ is given in magnitude, the rectangle $AB \cdot BC$ also is given in magnitude (having a given ratio to $ABEC$ by [Data 66] and [Data 2]), while $BC \cdot BD$ is given in magnitude! Therefore the ratio $(BC \cdot BD) : (AB \cdot BC) = BD : AB$ ([Data 1], [VI.1]) is given, whence $BD^2 : AB^2$ is given ([Data 50]). Combining $(BC \cdot DC) : AB^2$ with $AB^2 : BD^2$ gives $(BC \cdot DC) : BD^2$ given ([Data 8]), so $4\,(BC \cdot DC) : BD^2$ is given. Yet by [II.8] (taking $E$ with $BE = BD$, or directly $(BC + DC)^2 = (2\,BC - BD)^2 = 4\,BC \cdot DC + BD^2$!), adding $BD^2$ componendo ([Data 6]) gives $(2\,BC - BD)^2 : BD^2$ given! Extracting the square root via [Data 54] yields $(2\,BC - BD) : BD$ given, whence componendo $2\,BC : BD$ and $BC : BD$ are given ([Data 6]). Combining $BC : BD$ with $BD : AB$ gives $BC : AB$ given ([Data 8]). Thus the given parallelogram $ABEC$ has given angles and given side ratio $BC : AB$, so $ABEC$ is given in species ([Data Def. 3]) as well as in magnitude; therefore $AB$ and $BC$ are given in magnitude ([Data 55]).

Interactive circle-locus diagram (Menge pp. 168–178): In circle (O, OA) with centre O and diameter CE, chord BC subtends given inscribed angle ∠BAC in △ABC, calibrated via right-angled triangle on BE (D.87–D.88). From external given point P, tangent PT (perpendicular to radius OT at T, D.90) fixes the secant power along CP (D.91).
