Proposition 87 [πζ΄ · Prop. XCI]¶

If in a circle given in magnitude a straight line be drawn cutting off a segment that accepts a given angle, the straight line drawn is given in magnitude.

Functional Signature: Given(CircleRadius R, InscribedAngle theta) -> Determined(Mag(Chord BC = 2*R*sin theta)) · Dependencies: [Data Def. 5], [Data 3], [Data 40], [Data 53], [III.21], [III.31]

In a circle $ABC$ given in magnitude ([Data Def. 5]), let a straight line $AC$ be drawn cutting off a segment $ABC$ that admits a given angle $\angle ABC$ ([Data Def. 7]). I say that the chord $AC$ is given in magnitude.

Take the centre $D$ of circle $ABC$ ([III.1]), draw the diameter $ADE$ through $A$ and $D$, and join $EC$. Since the circle $ABC$ is given in magnitude, its radius $AD$ and diameter $AE = 2\,AD$ are given in magnitude ([Data Def. 5], [Data 3]). In the semicircle $ACE$, the angle $\angle ACE$ is right ([III.31]). Moreover, either $B$ and $E$ lie in the same segment on $AC$ so $\angle AEC = \angle ABC$ ([III.21]), or they lie in opposite segments of the cyclic quadrilateral $ABCE$ so $\angle AEC = 2\\text{ rt.} - \angle ABC$ ([III.22], [Data 4]); in either case $\angle AEC$ is given. Therefore right-angled $\\triangle ACE$ has all angles given and is given in species ([Data 40]), so the ratio $AC : AE$ is given ([Data Def. 3]). Since the diameter $AE$ is given in magnitude, the chord $AC$ is given in magnitude ([Data 2]).
