Proposition 94 [ϙδ΄ · Prop. XCIX (cf. Prop. C [Stewart])]¶

If in the diameter of a circle given in position a given point be taken, and from that point a straight line be drawn to the circle, and from the section a perpendicular be drawn to the drawn line, and through the point where the perpendicular meets the circumference again a parallel be drawn to the first drawn line, both the point at which the parallel meets the diameter is given, and the rectangle contained by the parallel chords is given.

Functional Signature: Given(PosMag(Circle(H, R)), Pos(D on diam BC), ∠DAE = 90°, EF || DA) -> Determined(Pos(F = 2H - D), Mag(AD * EF = |R^2 - HD^2|)) · Dependencies: [Data Def. 6], [Data 26], [Data 27], [Data 91], [Data 92], [I.4], [I.26], [I.29], [III.31], [III.35]

In a circle $ABC$ given in position, on its diameter $BC$ let a given point $D$ be taken; through $D$ let any straight line $DA$ be drawn to the circumference at $A$, from $A$ let $AE$ be drawn at right angles to $DA$ meeting the circumference at $E$ ([I.11]), and through $E$ let $EF$ be drawn parallel to $AD$ meeting the diameter $BC$ at $F$ ([I.31]). I say that the point $F$ is given in position, and that the rectangle $AD \cdot EF$ is given in magnitude.

Produce $EF$ to meet the circumference again at $H$, and join $AH$. Because $AD \parallel EH$ and $\angle DAE$ is right, the interior alternate angle $\angle HEA$ is also right ([I.29]); therefore $HA$ is a diameter of the circle $ABC$ ([III.31]). Since $BC$ is also a diameter, their intersection $G$ is the centre of the position-given circle $ABC$, so $G$ is given in position ([Data Def. 6]). Because $D$ is also given in position, $DG$ is given in magnitude ([Data 26]). Now in $\\triangle AGD$ and $\\triangle HGF$, the radii $AG$ and $HG$ are equal, the vertical angles at $G$ are equal ([I.15]), and the alternate angles $\angle GAD = \angle GHF$ (from $AD \parallel EH$) are equal ([I.29]); hence $\\triangle AGD \cong \\triangle HGF$ ([I.26]), so $FG = DG$ and $FH = AD$. Since $DG$ is given in magnitude, $FG$ is given in magnitude along the position-given diameter $BC$ from given $G$, whence $F$ is given in position ([Data 27]). Finally, because the chord $EFH$ passes through the given interior point $F$ of the position-given circle $ABC$, the rectangle $EF \cdot FH$ is given in magnitude ([Data 92]); substituting $FH = AD$ proves that $AD \cdot EF$ is given in magnitude.
