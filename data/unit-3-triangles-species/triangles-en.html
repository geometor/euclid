Unit 3: Triangles & Polygons Given in Species (Propositions 39–55)¶

Similarity Invariants, Triangle Rigidity Criteria, and Area-to-Side Scaling — D.39–D.55 (Simson XLII–LX) · Menge (1896) pp. 66–98

Note

Edition Track (English): Constructive determination of triangles up to similarity (εἴδει) from SSS-ratios (D.39), AAA (D.40), SAS-ratio (D.41), side-ratios (D.42), right-SSA (D.43), general SSA bifurcation with obtuse/acute diorismos (D.44), sum/difference of sides (D.45–D.46), decomposing rectilinear polygons into triangles (D.47), and quadratic area-scaling of figures given in species (D.48–D.55). Proposition 39 [λθ΄ · Prop. XLII]¶

[Folio Menge 66 | p. 66]
If each of the sides of a triangle be given in magnitude, the triangle is given in species.

Functional Signature: Given(Mag(a), Mag(b), Mag(c)) -> Determined(Species(Triangle ABC)) · Dependencies: [Data Def. 1], [Data Def. 3], [Data 1], [I.8], [I.22]

Let each of the sides $AB, BC, CA$ of $\triangle ABC$ be given in magnitude. I say that $\triangle ABC$ is given in species.

Set out a straight line $DM$ given in position from a given endpoint $D$, and cut off $DE = AB$ ([I.3]), so $DE$ is given in magnitude and $E$ is given in position ([Data 27]). Similarly procure segments equal to $BC$ and $CA$, and on $DE$ construct $\triangle DEH$ with $EH = BC$ and $DH = CA$ ([I.22]): the circles $(E, BC)$ and $(D, CA)$ are both given in position ([Data Def. 6]), so their intersection $H$ (on an assigned side of $DE$) is given in position ([Data 25]), and the sides $DH, EH$ are given in position and magnitude ([Data 26]). Thus every angle and side-ratio of $\triangle DEH$ is given ([Data 1]). By [I.8] and [I.4], $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle DEH$, whence all angles and side-ratios of $\triangle ABC$ are given; therefore $\triangle ABC$ is given in species ([Data Def. 3]).

Proposition 40 [μ΄ · Prop. XLIII]¶

If each of the angles of a triangle be given in magnitude, the triangle is given in species.

Functional Signature: Given(Angle(A), Angle(B), Angle(C)) -> Determined(Species(Triangle ABC)) · Dependencies: [Data Def. 3], [Data 25], [Data 26], [Data 27], [Data 29], [I.32], [VI.4]

Let each of the angles $\angle ABC, \angle BCA, \angle CAB$ of $\triangle ABC$ be given in magnitude. I say that $\triangle ABC$ is given in species.

Set out a straight line $DE$ given in position and magnitude ([Data 26]). At the given points $D$ and $E$ on $DE$, draw straight lines $DF$ and $EF$ making $\angle EDF = \angle BAC$ and $\angle DEF = \angle ABC$ ([I.23]). Since $\angle EDF$ and $\angle DEF$ are given, the rays $DF$ and $EF$ are given in position ([Data 29]), so their intersection $F$ is given in position ([Data 25]). Therefore $DF$ and $EF$ are given in magnitude ([Data 26]), and $\triangle DEF$ has given pairwise side ratios ([Data 1]). By [I.32], the remaining angle $\angle DFE = \angle ACB$, so $\triangle ABC$ is equiangular with $\triangle DEF$ and its sides are proportional to those of $\triangle DEF$ ([VI.4]). Since all angles and side ratios of $\triangle ABC$ are given, $\triangle ABC$ is given in species ([Data Def. 3]).

Proposition 41 [μα΄ · Prop. XLIV]¶

If a triangle have one angle given, and the sides about the given angle have a given ratio to one another, the triangle is given in species.

Functional Signature: Given(Angle(A), Ratio(c : b)) -> Determined(Species(Triangle ABC)) · Dependencies: [Data Def. 3], [Data 2], [Data 25], [Data 26], [Data 27], [Data 29], [VI.6]

Let $\triangle ABC$ have one given angle $\angle BAC$, and let the sides $AB$ and $AC$ about the given angle have a given ratio $AB : AC$. I say that $\triangle ABC$ is given in species.

Set out a straight line $DE$ given in position and magnitude. At the given point $D$ on $DE$, draw $DF$ making $\angle EDF = \angle BAC$ ([I.23]); since $\angle BAC$ is given, $DF$ is given in position ([Data 29]). Make $DE : DF = AB : AC$ ([Data Def. 2]); since $DE$ is given and $AB : AC$ is given, $DF$ is given in magnitude ([Data 2]), whence $F$ is given in position ([Data 27]). Join $EF$: then $EF$ is given in position and magnitude ([Data 26]), so $\triangle DEF$ is given in species ([Data 39]). Because $\angle BAC = \angle EDF$ and $AB : AC = DE : DF$, $\triangle ABC \sim \triangle DEF$ ([VI.6]). Therefore $\triangle ABC$ is also given in species ([Data Def. 3]).

Proposition 42 [μβ΄ · Prop. XLV]¶

[Folio Menge 74 | p. 74]
If the sides of a triangle have given ratios to one another, the triangle is given in species.

Functional Signature: Given(Ratio(a : b), Ratio(b : c)) -> Determined(Species(Triangle ABC)) · Dependencies: [Data Def. 3], [Data 2], [Data 39], [I.20], [I.22], [VI.5]

Let the three sides $AB, BC, CA$ of $\triangle ABC$ have given ratios to one another. I say that $\triangle ABC$ is given in species.

Set out a given straight line $D$. Make $D : E = AB : BC$ and $D : F = AB : AC$ ([Data Def. 2]). Since $D$ is given and both ratios are given, $E$ and $F$ are given in magnitude ([Data 2]). Because $AB, BC, CA$ satisfy the triangle inequality ([I.20]) and $D, E, F$ are proportional to $AB, BC, CA$ ([Data 8]), any two of $D, E, F$ are greater than the remaining one; construct $\triangle GHK$ from sides $GH = D$, $HK = E$, $KG = F$ ([I.22]). Since each side of $\triangle GHK$ is given in magnitude, $\triangle GHK$ is given in species ([Data 39]). Because $AB : BC : CA = GH : HK : KG$, $\triangle ABC$ is equiangular and similar to $\triangle GHK$ ([VI.5]), and therefore $\triangle ABC$ is given in species ([Data Def. 3]).

Proposition 43 [μγ΄ · Prop. XLVI]¶

[Folio Menge 76 | p. 76]
If of a right-angled triangle the sides about one of the acute angles have a given ratio to one another, the triangle is given in species.

Functional Signature: Given(Angle(A) = pi/2, Ratio(hypotenuse c : leg b)) -> Determined(Species(Triangle ABC)) · Dependencies: [Data Def. 3], [Data 2], [Data 25], [Data 26], [Data 27], [Data 29], [Data 31], [I.47], [VI.7]

Let $\triangle ABC$ be right-angled at $A$ ($\angle BAC = 1\text{ rt.}$), and let the sides $BC$ and $BA$ about one of the other angles $\angle ABC$ have a given ratio $BC : BA$. I say that $\triangle ABC$ is given in species.

Set out a straight line $DE$ given in position and magnitude, and on diameter $DE$ describe the semicircle $DGE$ ([Post. 3]), whose centre (the midpoint of $DE$, [Data 7], [Data 27]) and radius are given, so the semicircle $DGE$ is given in position ([Data Def. 6]). Make $DE : F = BC : BA$ ([Data Def. 2]); since $DE$ is given and $BC : BA$ is given (with $BC > BA$ by [I.19]), $F$ is given in magnitude ([Data 2]) and $F < DE$. With centre $D$ and radius $DG = F$, describe a circle meeting the semicircle $DGE$ at the unique point $G$ ([Data 25]), and join $DG, GE$ ([Data 26]). Then $\triangle DGE$ is given in species ([Data 39]) and $\angle DGE$ in the semicircle is right ([III.31]). In $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle DGE$, $\angle BAC = \angle DGE = 1\text{ rt.}$, $BC : BA = DE : DG$, and the angles $\angle ACB, \angle DEG$ opposite $BA, DG$ are both acute ([I.17]); hence $\triangle ABC \sim \triangle DGE$ ([VI.7]), so $\triangle ABC$ is given in species.

Proposition 44 [μδ΄ · Prop. XLVII]¶

[Folio Menge 78 | p. 78]
If a triangle have one angle given, and the sides about another angle have a given ratio to one another, the triangle is given in species.

Functional Signature: Given(Angle(A), Ratio(AB : BC), Sign(cos C)) -> Determined(Species(Triangle ABC)) [SSA diorismos] · Dependencies: [Data Def. 3], [Data 2], [Data 25], [Data 26], [Data 27], [Data 29], [Data 31], [I.13], [VI.7]

Let $\triangle ABC$ have one given angle $\angle ABC$, and let the sides $AB$ and $AC$ about another angle $\angle BAC$ have a given ratio $AB : AC$—assuming the specification (diorismos) whether $\angle ACB$ is obtuse or acute is fixed. I say that $\triangle ABC$ is given in species.

From $A$ drop $AD$ perpendicular to $BC$ (or $BC$ produced) ([I.12]). In right-angled $\triangle ABD$, $\angle ADB = 1\text{ rt.}$ and $\angle ABD$ (or its supplement) is given, so all angles of $\triangle ABD$ are given ([I.32]), whence $\triangle ABD$ is given in species ([Data 40]). Therefore the ratio $BA : AD$ is given ([Data Def. 3]). Yet the ratio $CA : AB$ is given by hypothesis; ex aequali, the ratio $CA : AD$ is given ([Data 8]). Now $\triangle ADC$ is right-angled at $D$ with the ratio of hypotenuse $CA$ to leg $AD$ given; hence $\triangle ADC$ is given in species ([Data 43]), so the acute angle $\angle ACD$ is given! Depending on the single branch specified (whether $D$ falls on $BC$ with $\angle ACB$ acute, or on $BC$ produced with $\angle ACB = 2\text{ rt.} - \angle ACD$ obtuse), $\angle ACB$ is uniquely given. Together with given $\angle ABC$, all angles of $\triangle ABC$ are given ([I.32]), so $\triangle ABC$ is given in species ([Data 40]).

Proposition 45 [με΄ · Prop. XLVIII]¶

[Folio Menge 82 | p. 82]
If a triangle have one angle given, and both the sides together about the given angle have a given ratio to the remaining side, the triangle is given in species.

Functional Signature: Given(Angle(A), Ratio((b + c) : a)) -> Determined(Species(Triangle ABC)) · Dependencies: [Data 40], [Data 44], [I.5], [I.32]

Let $\triangle ABC$ have one given angle $\angle BAC$, and let the compound $(BA + AC)$ of the two sides about the given angle have a given ratio to the remaining side $BC$. I say that $\triangle ABC$ is given in species.

Bisect $\angle BAC$ by the straight line $AD$ meeting $BC$ at $D$ ([I.9]). Since $\angle BAC$ is given, its half $\angle BAD$ is given ([Data 7]). Because $AD$ bisects $\angle BAC$, $BA : AC = BD : DC$ ([VI.3]). Alternando $BA : BD = AC : DC$ ([V.16]), and componendo $(BA + AC) : BC = BA : BD$ ([V.12]). Since $(BA + AC) : BC$ is given by hypothesis, the ratio $BA : BD$ is given. Applying [Data 44] to $\triangle ABD$ (with given angle $\angle BAD$ and given ratio $BA : BD$, up to the symmetric interchange of $AB$ and $AC$) determines $\triangle ABD$ in species, whence $\angle ABD$ is given; together with given $\angle BAC$, $\triangle ABC$ is given in species ([Data 40]).

Proposition 46 [μϛ΄ · Prop. XLIX]¶

[Folio Menge 84 | p. 84]
If a triangle have one angle given, and the sides together about another angle have a given ratio to the remaining side, the triangle is given in species.

Functional Signature: Given(Angle(B), Ratio((c + b) : a)) -> Determined(Species(Triangle ABC)) · Dependencies: [Data 40], [Data 41], [I.5], [I.9], [I.32]

Let $\triangle ABC$ have one given angle $\angle ABC$, and let the compound $(BA + AC)$ of the sides about another angle $\angle BAC$ have a given ratio to the remaining side $BC$. I say that $\triangle ABC$ is given in species.

Bisect $\angle BAC$ by $AD$ meeting $BC$ at $D$ ([I.9]). Since $BA : AC = BD : DC$ ([VI.3]), alternando and componendo $(BA + AC) : BC = AB : BD$ ([V.16], [V.12]). Because $(BA + AC) : BC$ is given, the ratio $AB : BD$ is given. Now in $\triangle ABD$, the given angle $\angle ABD$ ($= \angle ABC$) is itself the included angle between $AB$ and $BD$; therefore $\triangle ABD$ is uniquely given in species ([Data 41]). Hence $\angle BAD$ is given, its double $\angle BAC$ is given ([Data 3]), and from given $\angle ABC$ and $\angle BAC$ the triangle $\triangle ABC$ is given in species ([Data 40]).

Proposition 47 [μζ΄ · Prop. LI]¶

Rectilineal figures given in species are divided into triangles given in species.

Functional Signature: Given(Species(n-gon P)) -> Determined(Species(ear-triangulation T_1 .. T_{n-2})) · Dependencies: [Data Def. 3], [Data 4], [Data 8], [Data 41], [I.32], [VI.20]

Rectilinear figures given in species are divided into triangles given in species. Let the polygon $ABCDE$ be given in species ([Data Def. 3]). I say that $ABCDE$ is divided into triangles given in species.

Join the diagonals $BE$ and $EC$ ([Post. 1]). Since $ABCDE$ is given in species, the angle $\angle BAE$ is given and the ratio $BA : AE$ is given ([Data Def. 3]). Therefore $\triangle BAE$ is given in species ([Data 41]), whence $\angle ABE$ is given and the ratio $AB : BE$ is given. Subtracting given $\angle ABE$ from the given polygon angle $\angle ABC$ leaves $\angle EBC$ given ([Data 4]). Moreover, since $EB : BA$ is given and $AB : BC$ is given ([Data Def. 3]), the ratio $EB : BC$ is given ([Data 8]). Thus $\triangle EBC$ has given angle $\angle EBC$ and given ratio $EB : BC$, so $\triangle EBC$ is given in species ([Data 41]). For the very same reason $\triangle ECD$ is also given in species; therefore $ABCDE$ has been partitioned into triangles given in species.

Proposition 48 [μη΄ · Prop. LII]¶

If on the same straight line two triangles given in species be described, they will have a given ratio to one another.

Functional Signature: Given(Species(△ABC), Species(△ABD), SharedBase(AB)) -> Determined(Ratio(△ABC : △ABD)) · Dependencies: [Data Def. 3], [Data 8], [Data 28], [Data 30], [I.37], [VI.1], [VI.4]

On the same straight line $AB$, let two triangles $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle ABD$ given in species be described. I say that $\triangle ABC$ has a given ratio to $\triangle ABD$.

Through $A$ draw $AE$ perpendicular to $AB$ ([I.11]), and through $C$ and $D$ draw $CE$ and $DF$ parallel to $AB$ meeting the line $AE$ (produced as needed) at $E$ and $F$ ([I.31]). Since $\triangle ABC$ is given in species, dropping altitude $CG \perp AB$ (with $CG = AE$ by [I.34]) gives right-angled $\triangle AGC$ in species ([Data 40]), so the ratio $AE : AB$ ($= CG : AB$ via $CG : AC$ and $AC : AB$, [Data 8]) is given. For the same reason, since $\triangle ABD$ is given in species, the ratio $AB : AF$ is given. Combining $AE : AB$ and $AB : AF$ yields $AE : AF$ given ([Data 8]). Yet triangles $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle ABD$ on the same base $AB$ are to one another as their altitudes $AE$ and $AF$ (specifically, completing parallelograms $ABHE$ and $ABKF$, $\triangle ABC : \triangle ABD = \square ABHE : \square ABKF = AE : AF$ by [I.41] and [VI.1]). Therefore the ratio $\triangle ABC : \triangle ABD$ is given ([Data Def. 2]).

Proposition 49 [μθ΄ · Prop. LIII]¶

[Folio Menge 88 | p. 88]
If on the same straight line any two rectilineal figures given in species be described, they will have a given ratio to one another.

Functional Signature: Given(Species(P1 on AB), Species(P2 on AB)) -> Determined(Ratio(Area(P1) : Area(P2))) · Dependencies: [Data 8], [Data 22], [Data 47], [Data 48], [VI.20]

On the same straight line $AB$, let any two rectilinear figures $ABCDE$ and $ABFG$ given in species be described. I say that $ABCDE$ has a given ratio to $ABFG$.

Divide each figure into triangles given in species by drawing diagonals from $A$ and $B$, say $ABCDE = \triangle ABE + \triangle EBC + \triangle ECD$ and $ABFG = \triangle ABF + \triangle AFG$ ([Data 47]). Because similar polygons and species-given sub-triangles on chained diagonals have given side-squared ratios ([VI.19]), $\triangle ECD : \triangle EBC$ and $\triangle EBC : \triangle ABE$ are given, so by componendo ([Data 6], [Data 22]) the whole polygon $ABCDE$ has a given ratio to the base triangle $\triangle ABE$. For the same reason $ABFG$ has a given ratio to $\triangle ABF$. Yet $\triangle ABE$ and $\triangle ABF$ are two species-given triangles on the same straight line $AB$, so $\triangle ABE : \triangle ABF$ is given ([Data 48]). Chaining $ABCDE : \triangle ABE$, $\triangle ABE : \triangle ABF$, and $\triangle ABF : ABFG$ via [Data 8] proves that $ABCDE : ABFG$ is given.

Proposition 50 [ν΄ · Prop. LIV]¶

If two straight lines have a given ratio to one another, the rectilineal figures similar and similarly described upon them will also have a given ratio to one another.

Functional Signature: Given(Ratio(AB : CD) = r, Similar(E, F)) -> Determined(Ratio(Area(E) : Area(F)) = r^2) · Dependencies: [Data 2], [Data 8], [VI.11], [VI.19], [VI.20]

Let two straight lines $AB$ and $CD$ have a given ratio $AB : CD$, and on them let similar and similarly situated rectilinear figures $E$ and $F$ be described. I say that $E$ has a given ratio to $F$.

Take the third proportional $G$ to $AB$ and $CD$, so that $AB : CD = CD : G$ ([VI.11]). Since $AB : CD$ is given, $CD : G$ is given; hence ex aequali $AB : G$ is given ([Data 8]). Yet because $E$ and $F$ are similar and similarly situated rectilinear figures on $AB$ and $CD$, $E$ has to $F$ the duplicate ratio of $AB$ to $CD$, namely $E : F = AB : G$ ([VI.19] Cor., [VI.20] Cor.). Since $AB : G$ is given, the ratio $E : F$ is given ([Data Def. 2]).

Proposition 51 [να΄ · Prop. LV]¶

[Folio Menge 92 | p. 92]
If two straight lines have a given ratio to one another, and upon them be described any rectilineal figures given in species, those figures will have a given ratio to one another.

Functional Signature: Given(Ratio(AB : CD), Species(E), Species(F)) -> Determined(Ratio(Area(E) : Area(F))) · Dependencies: [Data 8], [Data 49], [Data 50], [VI.18], [VI.20]

Let two straight lines $AB$ and $CD$ have a given ratio $AB : CD$, and on them let any two rectilinear figures $E$ (on $AB$) and $F$ (on $CD$), each given in species, be described. I say that $E$ has a given ratio to $F$.

On $AB$ describe a rectilinear figure $G$ similar and similarly situated to $F$ ([VI.18]). Because $F$ is given in species and $G \sim F$, $G$ is also given in species ([Data Def. 3]). Now $E$ and $G$ are two rectilinear figures given in species described on the same straight line $AB$; therefore the ratio $E : G$ is given ([Data 49]). Moreover, $G$ and $F$ are similar and similarly situated figures described on $AB$ and $CD$, which have the given ratio $AB : CD$; therefore the ratio $G : F$ is given ([Data 50]). Combining $E : G$ and $G : F$ yields $E : F$ given ([Data 8]).

Proposition 52 [νβ΄ · Prop. LVI]¶

[Folio Menge 94 | p. 94]
If on a straight line given in magnitude a rectilineal figure given in species be described, it is given in magnitude.

Functional Signature: Given(Mag(AB), Species(P on AB)) -> Determined(Mag(Area(P))) · Dependencies: [Data Def. 1], [Data 2], [Data 49], [I.46]

On a straight line $AB$ given in magnitude, let a rectilinear figure $ACDEB$ given in species be described. I say that $ACDEB$ is given in magnitude.

On $AB$ describe the square $AFGB$ ([I.46]). Because all angles of a square are right and all its sides are equal ($1:1$), the square $AFGB$ is given in species ([Data Def. 3]), and since $AB$ is given in magnitude, the square $AFGB$ is directly constructible equal to itself and so is given in magnitude ([Data Def. 1]). Now $ACDEB$ and $AFGB$ are two figures given in species on the same straight line $AB$; hence the ratio $ACDEB : AFGB$ is given ([Data 49]). Because $AFGB$ is given in magnitude, $ACDEB$ is also given in magnitude ([Data 2]).

Proposition 53 [νγ΄ · Prop. LVII]¶

If two figures be given in species, and one side of one have a given ratio to one side of the other, the remaining sides will also have given ratios to the remaining sides.

Functional Signature: Given(Species(A), Species(B), Ratio(s1(A) : s1(B))) -> Determined(Ratio(si(A) : sj(B))) · Dependencies: [Data Def. 3], [Data 8], [V.22]

Let two rectilinear figures $A$ and $B$ be given in species, and let two sides—say $CD$ of $A$ and $EF$ of $B$—have a given ratio $CD : EF$. I say that the remaining sides also have given ratios to the remaining sides.

Let $GH$ be any other side of $A$ and $KL$ any other side of $B$. Since $A$ is given in species, the ratio of its own sides $GH : CD$ is given ([Data Def. 3]). By hypothesis, the ratio $CD : EF$ is given. And since $B$ is given in species, the ratio $EF : KL$ is given ([Data Def. 3]). Chaining $GH : CD$, $CD : EF$, and $EF : KL$ via [Data 8] proves that the ratio $GH : KL$ is given.

Proposition 54 [νδ΄ · Prop. LIX]¶

If two figures given in species have a given ratio to one another, their sides will also have a given ratio to one another.

Functional Signature: Given(Species(A), Species(B), Ratio(Area(A) : Area(B))) -> Determined(Ratio(si(A) : sj(B))) · Dependencies: [Data 8], [Data 24], [Data 49], [Data 53], [VI.18], [VI.20]

Let two rectilinear figures $A$ (on side $CD$) and $B$ (on side $EF$) be given in species, and let the area ratio $A : B$ be given. I say that their sides also have given ratios to one another.

First, if $A$ and $B$ are similar, take the third proportional $G$ to $CD$ and $EF$ so $CD : EF = EF : G$ ([VI.11]). Then $CD : G = A : B$ ([VI.20] Cor.), which is given! Thus three straight lines $CD, EF, G$ are in continued proportion and the first $CD$ has a given ratio to the third $G$; by [Data 24], $CD$ has a given ratio to the second $EF$. Next, if $A$ and $B$ are not similar, on $CD$ describe a figure $H$ similar and similarly situated to $B$ ([VI.18]), so $H$ is given in species. On the same side $CD$, $A : H$ is given ([Data 49]); combining with given $A : B$ gives $H : B$ given ([Data 8]). Since $H \sim B$ on corresponding sides $CD$ and $EF$, the first case yields $CD : EF$ given, and [Data 53] gives the ratios of all remaining sides.

Proposition 55 [νε΄ · Prop. LX]¶

[Folio Menge 98 | p. 98]
If an area be given in species and in magnitude, its sides will also be given in magnitude.

Functional Signature: Given(Species(A), Mag(Area(A))) -> Determined(Mag(sides(A))) · Dependencies: [Data 1], [Data 2], [Data 52], [Data 54], [I.46], [VI.18]

Let a rectilinear figure $A$ be given both in species and in magnitude. I say that its sides are also given in magnitude.

Let $BC$ be a side of $A$. Set out a straight line $DE$ given in magnitude, and on $DE$ describe a rectilinear figure $F$ similar and similarly situated to $A$ ([VI.18]). Since $A$ is given in species, $F$ is also given in species ([Data Def. 3]); and since $F$ is described on the given straight line $DE$, $F$ is given in magnitude ([Data 52]). Because both $A$ and $F$ are given in magnitude, their ratio $A : F$ is given ([Data 1]). Therefore, by [Data 54], their homologous sides have a given ratio $BC : DE$. Since $DE$ is given in magnitude, $BC$ is given in magnitude ([Data 2]), and since $A$ is given in species all remaining sides have given ratios to $BC$ and so are given in magnitude ([Data 2]).
