Unit 4: Application of Areas, Quadratics & Cosine Projections (Propositions 56–67) — Philological & Critical Notes¶

1. Geometric Algebra of Application of Areas: Parabolē, Elleipsis, and Hyperbolē (D.57–D.60)¶

Unit 4 (D.56–D.67) translates the constructive application of areas (παραβολὴ τῶν χωρίων, Elements II.5–6 & VI.27–29) into analytical root-determination theorems:

  • Parabolē (παραβολή, D.57): Exact application $a \cdot x = S$ in a given angle $\\theta$ solves the linear equation $x = S / (a \sin\\theta)$.

  • Elleipsis (ἔλλειψις, D.58): Elliptic application $(a - x)\cdot (\lambda x) = S$ solves the quadratic equation $\lambda x^2 - \lambda a x + S = 0$ by completing the gnomon on the half-segment $a/2$ ([II.5], [VI.28]), subject to the discriminant diorismos $S \le \lambda (a/2)^2$ ([VI.27]).

  • Hyperbolē (ὑπερβολή, D.59): Hyperbolic application $(a + x)\cdot (\lambda x) = S$ solves $\lambda x^2 + \lambda a x = S$ unconditionally via [II.6] and [VI.29]. When $S = a^2$ and the excess is a square ($\lambda = 1$), $x(a + x) = a^2$ determines the golden section $x = a/\phi \in \mathbb{Q}(\phi)$ ([II.11], [VI.30])!

As Proclus (In Eucl. 419.15) and Pappus record, Apollonius of Perga borrowed the very names parabola, ellipse, and hyperbola directly from this Pythagorean-Euclidean triad.

2. Area-to-Species Bridges (D.61–D.63) & Textual Notes¶

Propositions D.61 and D.62 bridge area-ratios back to species: if a parallelogram $CD$ in a given angle is applied either to a side of a species-given figure (D.61) or to a segment having a given ratio to a side of a species-given figure (D.62), and the area ratio is given, then $CD$ itself is given in species. Simson (Props. LXIX–LXX, p. 464) retained both (D.61–62) while omitting D.63 (species-given figures on the three sides of a species-given triangle have given ratios to one another) as an immediate corollary of D.51; Menge preserved D.63 from Vat. gr. 190.

3. The Euclidean Law of Cosines, Sine Area Formula & Half-Angle Excess (D.64–D.67)¶

Propositions D.64–D.67 form the trigonometric heart of Greek plane geometry (visualized in data_05_cosine_projection.svg):

  • Obtuse & Acute Cosine Laws (D.64–D.65): By combining [Elements II.12] and [II.13] with species-given right-triangle projections (D.40), Euclid proves that $|a^2 + c^2 - b^2| : (a c) = 2\, BD : AB = 2|\cos B|$ is given whenever $\angle B$ is given—without ever needing symbolic trigonometric tables!

  • Sine Area Law (D.66): Proves $(AB \cdot BC) : \\triangle ABC = 2/\sin B$ is given whenever $\angle B$ is given.

  • Compound-Side Quadratic Excess (D.67, Simson Prop. LXXVI): Proves $((b + c)^2 - a^2) : \\triangle ABC$ is given whenever $\angle A$ is given. Algebraically, $(b+c)^2 - a^2 = 2bc(1 + \cos A)$ while $\\triangle ABC = \frac{1}{2}bc\sin A$, so their constant ratio is $4(1+\cos A)/\sin A = 4\cot(A/2)$! In Vat. gr. 190 (Menge pp. 122–126), Euclid establishes this synthetically without trigonometry by extending $BA$ to $D$ with $AD = AC$ and applying [II.6].
