Unit 5: Proportional Parallelograms, Triangles & Four-Line Relations (Propositions 68–83) — Philological & Critical Notes¶

1. Compound Ratios & Oblique Shear Invariance (D.68–D.75)¶

Unit 5 (D.68–D.83) lifts the theory of equiangular parallelograms ([Elements VI.14], [VI.23]) to arbitrary given angles. Euclid’s key geometric operator in D.69, D.70, D.73, and D.74 is an area-preserving Cavalieri shear ([I.35]) on a fixed base $BC$: shearing a parallelogram from given angle $\\theta_1$ to given angle $\\theta_2$ introduces a corner triangle $\\triangle ABP$ whose angles are given, so its slant-side ratio $BA : BP = \sin\\theta_2 / \sin\\theta_1$ is given by D.40!

In his philological commentary (pp. 464–466), Robert Simson showed that late Greek scribes had slightly garbled the reciprocally-proportional clauses of D.73 and D.74, and consolidated them cleanly in Simson Props. LXIII–LXVII.

2. Manuscript Doublets (D.77–D.78) & the Inscribed-Angle Locus Theorem (D.79–D.80)¶

  • Doublets in Codex P: D.77 repeats the enunciation of D.54 with an alternative square-calibration proof, and D.78 specializes D.62 to rectangles; Simson omitted both as scholiastic doublets, whereas Menge (pp. 148–152) printed them in their traditional numbering so all ancient citations from Pappus remain intact.

  • The Inscribed-Angle & Parallel-Chord Masterpiece (D.79–D.80): D.80 (Simson Prop. LXXVIII) proves that $\\triangle ABC$ is given in species if $\angle A$ and the ratio $(AB \cdot AC) : BC^2$ are given. Euclid prepares this via the pure locus lemma D.79: two triangles with equal vertex angles $\angle A = \angle D$ and equal base-to-altitude ratios $BC : AG = EF : DH$ can be superposed onto the same circular segment ([III.21], [III.33]), where equal altitudes cut the arc in a parallel line symmetric across the perpendicular bisector! Simson admired this cluster so deeply that he added four companion theorems (Props. LXXVII, LXXIX–LXXXI).

3. Four-Line Proportional Permutations & Apollonian Analysis (D.81–D.83)¶

Propositions D.81–D.83 provide the algebraic rewrite rules for proportions $A : B = C : D$ when one or more terms are replaced by lines having given ratios to them. As Pappus (Synagoge Book VII) demonstrates across his lemmas to Apollonius's Cutting off of a Ratio (Λόγου ἀποτομή), Cutting off of an Area (Χωρίου ἀποτομή), and Determinate Section (Διωρισμένη τομή), D.82 and D.83 allow an analyst to absorb arbitrary given multiplicative constants into auxiliary segments without breaking a four-term proportion.
