.. _D.15:

Proposition 15 [ιε΄ · Prop. XIX]
--------------------------------

.. enunciation::

   If two magnitudes have a given ratio to one another, and a given magnitude be subtracted from each of them, either the remainders will have a given ratio to one another, or one will be greater than the other by a given magnitude than in ratio.

*Functional Signature*: ``Given(Ratio(A : C) = r, Mag(d1), Mag(d2)) -> Determined(Ratio(A-d1 : C-d2) | GreaterThanInRatio)`` · *Dependencies*: :just:`[Data Def. 11] <D.def.11>`, :just:`[Data 1] <D.1>`, :just:`[Data 2] <D.2>`, :just:`[Data 4] <D.4>`, :just:`[Data 8] <D.8>`, :just:`[V.19] <V.19>`

Let two magnitudes :math:`AB` and :math:`CD` have a given ratio to one another; from :math:`AB` let the given magnitude :math:`AE` be subtracted, and to :math:`CD` let the given magnitude :math:`DF` be added, forming :math:`EB` and :math:`CF`. I say that the whole :math:`CF` is greater by a given magnitude than in ratio to the remainder :math:`EB`.

Since :math:`AE` is given and the ratio :math:`AB : CD` is given, make :math:`AE : CG = AB : CD` (:just:`[Data Def. 2] <D.def.2>`). Because :math:`AE` is given and :math:`AE : CG` is given, :math:`CG` is given (:just:`[Data 2] <D.2>`); and since :math:`DF` is also given, the sum :math:`CG + DF` is given (:just:`[Data 3] <D.3>`). Moreover, since whole :math:`AB` is to whole :math:`CD` as subtracted :math:`AE` is to subtracted :math:`CG`, the remainder :math:`EB` is to the remainder :math:`GD` as :math:`AB` is to :math:`CD` (:just:`[V.19] <V.19>`), so the ratio :math:`GD : EB` is given (and if :math:`CG \ge CD`, adjoining the auxiliary proportional beyond :math:`C` gives the identical sum of given offsets). Since :math:`CF = GD + (CG + DF)` exceeds :math:`GD` by the given magnitude :math:`CG + DF`, the whole :math:`CF` is greater by a given magnitude than in ratio to :math:`EB` (:just:`[Data Def. 11] <D.def.11>`).
