Proposition 54 [νδ΄ · Prop. LIX]
--------------------------------

.. enunciation::

   If two figures given in species have a given ratio to one another, their sides will also have a given ratio to one another.

*Functional Signature*: ``Given(Species(A), Species(B), Ratio(Area(A) : Area(B))) -> Determined(Ratio(si(A) : sj(B)))`` · *Dependencies*: :just:`[Data 8] <D.8>`, :just:`[Data 24] <D.24>`, :just:`[Data 49] <D.49>`, :just:`[Data 53] <D.53>`, :just:`[VI.18] <VI.18>`, :just:`[VI.20] <VI.20>`

Let two rectilinear figures :math:`A` (on side :math:`CD`) and :math:`B` (on side :math:`EF`) be given in species, and let the area ratio :math:`A : B` be given. I say that their sides also have given ratios to one another.

First, if :math:`A` and :math:`B` are similar, take the third proportional :math:`G` to :math:`CD` and :math:`EF` so :math:`CD : EF = EF : G` (:just:`[VI.11] <VI.11>`). Then :math:`CD : G = A : B` (:just:`[VI.20] <VI.20>` Cor.), which is given! Thus three straight lines :math:`CD, EF, G` are in continued proportion and the first :math:`CD` has a given ratio to the third :math:`G`; by :just:`[Data 24] <D.24>`, :math:`CD` has a given ratio to the second :math:`EF`. Next, if :math:`A` and :math:`B` are not similar, on :math:`CD` describe a figure :math:`H` similar and similarly situated to :math:`B` (:just:`[VI.18] <VI.18>`), so :math:`H` is given in species. On the same side :math:`CD`, :math:`A : H` is given (:just:`[Data 49] <D.49>`); combining with given :math:`A : B` gives :math:`H : B` given (:just:`[Data 8] <D.8>`). Since :math:`H \sim B` on corresponding sides :math:`CD` and :math:`EF`, the first case yields :math:`CD : EF` given, and :just:`[Data 53] <D.53>` gives the ratios of all remaining sides.
