Proposition 71 [οα΄ · Prop. LXVIII Cor. 1]
------------------------------------------

.. folio:: Menge 134
   :page: 134

.. enunciation::

   If of two triangles the sides about equal angles, or about unequal yet given angles, have given ratios to one another, the triangles themselves also have a given ratio to one another.

*Functional Signature*: ``Given(Angle(A), Angle(D), Ratio(c1 : c2), Ratio(b1 : b2)) -> Determined(Ratio(△1 : △2))`` · *Dependencies*: :just:`[Data 8] <D.8>`, :just:`[Data 70] <D.70>`, :just:`[I.31] <I.31>`, :just:`[I.34] <I.34>`, :just:`[V.15] <V.15>`

Let two triangles :math:`\\triangle ABC` and :math:`\\triangle DEF` have their sides about either equal angles (:math:`\angle B = \angle E`) or given unequal angles (:math:`\angle B, \angle E`) in given ratios (:math:`AB : DE` given and :math:`BC : EF` given). I say that the area ratio :math:`\\triangle ABC : \\triangle DEF` is given.

Complete the parallelograms :math:`ABCG` and :math:`DEFH` by drawing parallels to the opposite sides (:just:`[I.31] <I.31>`). Then :math:`ABCG` and :math:`DEFH` are parallelograms having either equal angles :math:`\angle B = \angle E` or given angles :math:`\angle B, \angle E`, with :math:`AB : DE` and :math:`BC : EF` given; therefore by :just:`[Data 70] <D.70>`, the ratio :math:`ABCG : DEFH` is given. Since each parallelogram is double its triangle (:just:`[I.34] <I.34>`), :math:`\\triangle ABC : \\triangle DEF = ABCG : DEFH` (:just:`[V.15] <V.15>`), so :math:`\\triangle ABC : \\triangle DEF` is given.
