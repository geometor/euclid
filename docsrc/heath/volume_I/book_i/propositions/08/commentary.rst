Commentary on Proposition I.8
=============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition I.8 <I.8>`.

.. folio:: 0276
   :page: 262

As pointed out above (p. 257) I. 8 is a *partial* converse of I. 4.

It is to be observed that in I. 8 :person:`Euclid <euclid>` is satisfied with proving the equality of the vertical angles and does not, as in I. 4, add that the triangles are equal, and the remaining angles are equal respectively. The reason is no doubt (as pointed out by :person:`Proclus <proclus>` and by :person:`Savile <savile>` after him) that, when once the vertical angles are proved equal, the rest follows from I. 4, and there is no object in proving again what has been proved already.

:person:`Aristotle <aristotle>` has an allusion to the theorem of this proposition in *Meteorologica* III. 3, 373 a 5—16. He is speaking of the rainbow and observes that, if equal rays be reflected from one and the same point to one and the same point, the points at which reflection takes place are on the circumference of a circle. “For let the broken lines :g:`ACB <0277.g1:< A C B >>`, :g:`AFB <0277.g1:< A F B >>`, :g:`ADB <0277.g1:< A D B >>` be all reflected from the point :g:`A <0277.g1:A>` to the point :g:`B <0277.g1:B>` (in such a way that) :g:`AC <0277.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`AF <0277.g1:/ A F />`, :g:`AD <0277.g1:/ A D />` are all equal to one another, and the lines (terminating) at :g:`B <0277.g1:B>`, i.e. :g:`CB <0277.g1:/ C B />`, :g:`FB <0277.g1:/ F B />`, :g:`DB <0277.g1:/ D B />`, are likewise all equal; and let :g:`AEB <0277.g1:/ A E B />` be joined. It follows that the triangles are *equal*; for they are upon the equal (base) :g:`AEB <0277.g1:/ A E B />`.”

:person:`Heiberg <heiberg>` (*Mathematisches zu Aristoteles*, p. 18) thinks that the form of the conclusion quoted is an indication that in the corresponding proposition to Eucl. I. 8, as it lay before :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>`, it was maintained that the triangles were equal, and not only the angles, and “we see here therefore, in a clear example, how the stones of the ancient fabric were recut for the rigid structure of his
*Elements*.” I do not, however, think that this inference from Aristotle’s language as to the form of the pre-Euclidean proposition is safe. Thus if we, nowadays, were arguing from the data in the passage of :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>`, we should doubtless infer directly that the triangles are equal in all respects, quoting I. 8 alone. Besides, Aristotle’s language is rather careless, as the next sentences of the same passage show. “Let perpendiculars,” he says, “be drawn to :g:`AEB <0277.g1:/ A E B />` from the angles, :g:`CE <0277.g1:/ C E />` from :g:`C <0277.g1:C>`, :g:`FE <0277.g1:/ F E />` from :g:`F <0277.g1:F>` and :g:`DE <0277.g1:/ D E />` from :g:`D <0277.g1:D>`. These, then, are equal; for they are all in equal triangles, and in one plane; for all of them are perpendicular to :g:`AEB <0277.g1:/ A E B />`, and they meet at one point :g:`E <0277.g1:E>`. Therefore the (line) drawn (through :g:`C <0277.g1:C>`, :g:`F <0277.g1:F>`, :g:`D <0277.g1:D>`) will be a circle, and its centre (will be) :g:`E <0277.g1:E>`.” :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>` should obviously have proved that the three perpendiculars *will* meet at one point :g:`E <0277.g1:E>` on :g:`AEB <0277.g1:/ A E B />` before he spoke of drawing the perpendiculars :g:`CE <0277.g1:/ C E />`, :g:`FE <0277.g1:/ F E />`, :g:`DE <0277.g1:/ D E />`. This of course follows from their being “in equal triangles” (by means of Eucl. I. 26); and then, from the fact that the perpendiculars meet at one point on :g:`AB <0277.g1:/ A B />`, it can be inferred that all three are in one plane.

Philo's proof of I. 8.
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

This alternative proof avoids the use of I. 7, and it is elegant; but it is inconvenient in one respect, since three cases have to be distinguished. :person:`Proclus <proclus>` gives the proof in the following order (pp. 266, 15—268, 14).

Let :g:`ABC <0277.g2:< A B C >>`, :g:`DEF <0277.g2:< D E F >>` be two triangles having the sides :g:`AB <0277.g2:/ A B />`, :g:`AC <0277.g2:/ A C />` equal to the sides :g:`DE <0277.g2:/ D E />`, :g:`DF <0277.g2:/ D F />` respectively, and the base :g:`BC <0277.g2:/ B C />` equal to the base :g:`EF <0277.g2:/ E F />`.

Let the triangle :g:`ABC <0277.g2:< A B C >>` be applied to the triangle :g:`DEF <0277.g2:< D E F >>`, so that :g:`B <0277.g2:B>` is placed on :g:`E <0277.g2:E>` and :g:`BC <0277.g2:/ B C />` on :g:`EF <0277.g2:/ E F />`, but so that :g:`A <0277.g2:A>` falls on the opposite side of :g:`EF <0277.g2:/ E F />` from :g:`D <0277.g2:D>`, taking the position :g:`G <0277.g2:G>`. Then :g:`C <0277.g2:C>` will coincide with :g:`F <0277.g2:F>`, since :g:`BC <0277.g2:/ B C />` is equal to :g:`EF <0277.g2:/ E F />`.

Now :g:`FG <0277.g2:/ F G />` will either be in a straight line with :g:`DF <0277.g2:/ D F />`, or make an angle with it, and in the latter case the angle will either be *interior* (κατὰ τὸ ἐντός) to the figure or *exterior* (κατὰ τὸ ἐκτός).

.. picture:: 0277.g2.svg
   :alt: Philo Proof of I. 8 Case I

I. Let :g:`FG <0277.g2:/ F G />` be in a straight line with :g:`DF <0277.g2:/ D F />`. Then, since :g:`DE <0277.g2:/ D E />` is equal to :g:`EG <0277.g2:/ E G />`, and :g:`DFG <0277.g2:/ D F G />` is a straight line, :g:`DEG <0277.g2:< D E G >>` is an isosceles triangle, and the angle at :g:`D <0277.g2:< E D G )>` is equal to the angle at :g:`G <0277.g2:< E G D )>`. [I. 5].

.. picture:: 0277.g3.svg
   :alt: Philo Proof of I. 8 Case II

II. Let :g:`DF <0277.g3:/ D F />`, :g:`FG <0277.g3:/ F G />` form an angle *interior* to the figure. Let :g:`DG <0277.g3:/ D G />` be joined. Then, since :g:`DE <0277.g3:/ D E />`, :g:`EG <0277.g3:/ E G />` are equal, the angle :g:`EDG <0277.g3:< E D G )>` is equal to the angle :g:`EGD <0277.g3:< E G D )>`. Again, since :g:`DF <0277.g3:/ D F />` is equal to :g:`FG <0277.g3:/ F G />`, the angle :g:`FDG <0277.g3:< F D G )>` is equal to the angle :g:`FGD <0277.g3:< F G D )>`. Therefore, by addition, the whole angle :g:`EDF <0277.g3:< E D F )>` is equal to the whole angle :g:`EGF <0277.g3:< E G F )>`.

III. Let :g:`DF <0278.g1:/ D F />`, :g:`FG <0278.g1:/ F G />` form an angle *exterior* to the figure. Let :g:`DG <0278.g1:/ D G />` be joined. The proof proceeds as in the last case, except that subtraction takes the place of addition, and the remaining angle :g:`EDF <0278.g1:< E D F )>` is equal to the remaining angle :g:`EGF <0278.g1:< E G F )>`.

Therefore in all three cases the angle :g:`EDF <0278.g1:< E D F )>` is equal to the angle :g:`EGF <0278.g1:< E G F )>`, that is, to the angle :g:`BAC <0278.g1:< B A C )>`.

It will be observed that, in accordance with the practice of the Greek geometers in not recognising as an “angle” any angle not less than two right angles, the re-entrant angle of the quadrilateral :g:`DEGF <0278.g1:< D E G F >>` is ignored and the angle :g:`DFG <0278.g1:< D F G )>` is said to be *outside* the figure.
