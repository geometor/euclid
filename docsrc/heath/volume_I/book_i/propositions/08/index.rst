PROPOSITION 8.
==============

.. enunciation::

   If two :term:`triangles <trilateral>` have the two sides equal to two sides respectively, and have also the base equal to the base, they will also have the :term:`angles <plane-angle>` equal which are contained by the equal :term:`straight lines <straight-line>`.

.. picture:: 0275.g1.svg
   :alt: Proposition 8 figure

Let :g:`ABC <0275.g1:< A B C >>`, :g:`DEF <0275.g1:< D E F >>` be two triangles having the two sides :g:`AB <0275.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`AC <0275.g1:/ A C />` :eq:`equal <0275.g1:/ A B / = 0275.g1:/ D E /; 0275.g1:/ A C / = 0275.g1:/ D F />` to the two sides :g:`DE <0275.g1:/ D E />`, :g:`DF <0275.g1:/ D F />` respectively, namely :g:`AB <0275.g1:/ A B />` to :g:`DE <0275.g1:/ D E />`, and :g:`AC <0275.g1:/ A C />` to :g:`DF <0275.g1:/ D F />`; and let them have the base :g:`BC <0275.g1:/ B C />` :eq:`equal <0275.g1:/ B C / = 0275.g1:/ E F />` to the base :g:`EF <0275.g1:/ E F />`;

I say that the angle :g:`BAC <0275.g1:< B A C )>` is also :eq:`equal <0275.g1:< B A C ) = 0275.g1:< E D F )>` to the angle :g:`EDF <0275.g1:< E D F )>`.

For, if the triangle :g:`ABC <0275.g1:< A B C >>` be :coincide:`applied <0275.g1:< A B C > ≡ 0275.g1:< D E F >>` to the triangle :g:`DEF <0275.g1:< D E F >>`, and if the :term:`point <point>` :g:`B <0275.g1:B>` be :coincide:`placed on <0275.g1:B ≡ 0275.g1:E; 0275.g1:/ B C / ≡ 0275.g1:/ E F />` the point :g:`E <0275.g1:E>` and the straight line :g:`BC <0275.g1:/ B C />` on :g:`EF <0275.g1:/ E F />`,

.. subclause::

   the point :g:`C <0275.g1:C>` will also :coincide:`coincide <0275.g1:C ≡ 0275.g1:F>` with :g:`F <0275.g1:F>`,

.. assertion::

   because :g:`BC <0275.g1:/ B C />` is :eq:`equal <0275.g1:/ B C / = 0275.g1:/ E F />` to :g:`EF <0275.g1:/ E F />`.

.. folio:: 0276
   :page: 262

Then, :g:`BC <0275.g1:/ B C />` :coincide:`coinciding <0275.g1:/ B C / ≡ 0275.g1:/ E F />` with :g:`EF <0275.g1:/ E F />`,

.. subclause::

   :g:`BA <0275.g1:/ B A />`, :g:`AC <0275.g1:/ A C />`\ [#f1]_ will also :coincide:`coincide <0275.g1:/ B A / ≡ 0275.g1:/ E D /; 0275.g1:/ A C / ≡ 0275.g1:/ D F />` with :g:`ED <0275.g1:/ E D />`, :g:`DF <0275.g1:/ D F />`;

for, if the base :g:`BC <0275.g1:/ B C />` :coincide:`coincides <0275.g1:/ B C / ≡ 0275.g1:/ E F />` with the base :g:`EF <0275.g1:/ E F />`, and the sides :g:`BA <0275.g1:/ B A />`, :g:`AC <0275.g1:/ A C />` do not coincide with :g:`ED <0275.g1:/ E D />`, :g:`DF <0275.g1:/ D F />` but fall beside them\ [#f2]_ as :g:`EG <0275.g1:/ E G />`, :g:`GF <0275.g1:/ G F />`,

.. subclause::

   then, given two straight lines constructed on a straight line (from its :term:`extremities <extremity>`) and meeting in a point, there will have been constructed on the same straight line (from its extremities), and on the same side of it, two other straight lines meeting in another point and :eq:`equal <0275.g1:/ E D / = 0275.g1:/ E G /; 0275.g1:/ D F / = 0275.g1:/ G F />` to the former two respectively, namely each to that which has the same extremity with it.

But they cannot be so constructed. :just:`[I. 7] <I.7>`

Therefore it is not possible that, if the base :g:`BC <0275.g1:/ B C />` be :coincide:`applied <0275.g1:/ B C / ≡ 0275.g1:/ E F />` to the base :g:`EF <0275.g1:/ E F />`, the sides :g:`BA <0275.g1:/ B A />`, :g:`AC <0275.g1:/ A C />` should not coincide with :g:`ED <0275.g1:/ E D />`, :g:`DF <0275.g1:/ D F />`;

.. assertion::

   they will therefore :coincide:`coincide <0275.g1:/ B A / ≡ 0275.g1:/ E D /; 0275.g1:/ A C / ≡ 0275.g1:/ D F />`,

so that the angle :g:`BAC <0275.g1:< B A C )>` will also :coincide:`coincide <0275.g1:< B A C ) ≡ 0275.g1:< E D F )>` with the angle :g:`EDF <0275.g1:< E D F )>`, and will be :eq:`equal <0275.g1:< B A C ) = 0275.g1:< E D F )>` to it. :just:`[C.N. 4] <I.cn.4>`

.. qed::

   If therefore etc. :just:`Q. E. D.`

.. [#f1] **BA, AC.** The text has here “:g:`BA <0275.g1:/ B A />`, :g:`CA <0275.g1:/ C A />`.”

.. [#f2] **fall beside them.** The Greek has the future, παραλλάξουσι. παραλλάττω means “to pass by without touching,” “to miss” or “to deviate.”
