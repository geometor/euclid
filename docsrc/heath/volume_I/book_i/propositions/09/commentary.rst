Commentary on Proposition I.9
=============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition I.9 <I.9>`.

.. folio:: 0278
   :page: 264

It will be observed from the translation of this proposition that :person:`Euclid <euclid>` does not say, in his description of the construction, that the equilateral triangle should be constructed on the side of :g:`$DE$ <0278.g2:/ D E />` opposite to :g:`$A$ <0278.g2:A>`; he leaves this to be inferred from his figure. There is no particular value in :person:`Proclus <proclus>`’ explanation as to how we should proceed in case any one should assert that he could not recognise the existence of any space below :g:`$DE$ <0278.g2:/ D E />`. He supposes, then, the equilateral triangle described on the side of :g:`$DE$ <0278.g2:/ D E />` towards :g:`$A$ <0278.g2:A>`, and hence has to consider three cases according as the vertex of the equilateral triangle falls on :g:`$A$ <0278.g2:A>`, above :g:`$A$ <0278.g2:A>` or below it. The second and third cases do not differ
substantially from Euclid’s. In the first case, where :g:`$ADE$ <0279.g1:< A D E >>` is the equilateral triangle constructed on :g:`$DE$ <0279.g1:/ D E />`, take any point :g:`$F$ <0279.g1:F>` on :g:`$AD$ <0279.g1:/ A D />`, and from :g:`$AE$ <0279.g1:/ A E />` cut off :g:`$AG$ <0279.g1:/ A G />` equal to :g:`$AF$ <0279.g1:/ A F />`. Join :g:`$DG$ <0279.g1:/ D G />`, :g:`$EF$ <0279.g1:/ E F />` meeting in :g:`$H$ <0279.g1:H>`; and join :g:`$AH$ <0279.g1:/ A H />`. Then :g:`$AH$ <0279.g1:/ A H />` is the bisector required.

:person:`Proclus <proclus>` also answers the possible objection that might be raised to Euclid’s proof on the ground that it assumes that, if the equilateral triangle be described on the side of :g:`$DE$ <0279.g2:/ D E />` opposite to :g:`$A$ <0279.g2:A>`, its vertex :g:`$F$ <0279.g2:F>` will lie within the angle :g:`$BAC$ <0279.g2:< B A C )>`. The objector is supposed to argue that this is not necessary, but that :g:`$F$ <0279.g2:F>` might fall either on one of the lines forming the angle or outside it altogether. The two cases are disposed of thus.

Suppose :g:`$F$ <0279.g2:F>` to fall as shown in the two figures below respectively.

.. picture:: 0279.g2.svg
   :alt: Proclus Objections to I. 9 Figures

Then, since :g:`$FD$ <0279.g2:/ F D />` is equal to :g:`$FE$ <0279.g2:/ F E />`, the angle :g:`$FDE$ <0279.g2:< F D E )>` is equal to the angle :g:`$FED$ <0279.g2:< F E D )>`. Therefore the angle :g:`$CED$ <0279.g2:< C E D )>` is greater than the angle :g:`$FDE$ <0279.g2:< F D E )>`; and, in the second figure, *a fortiori*, the angle :g:`$CED$ <0279.g2:< C E D )>` is greater than the angle :g:`$BDE$ <0279.g2:< B D E )>`. But, since :g:`$ADE$ <0279.g2:< A D E >>` is an isosceles triangle, and the equal sides are produced, the angles under the base are equal, i.e., the angle :g:`$CED$ <0279.g2:< C E D )>` is equal to the angle :g:`$BDE$ <0279.g2:< B D E )>`. But the angle :g:`$CED$ <0279.g2:< C E D )>` was proved greater: which is impossible.

Here then is the second case in which, in :person:`Proclus <proclus>`’ view, the second part of I. 5 is useful for refuting objections.

On this proposition :person:`Proclus <proclus>` takes occasion (p. 271, 15—19) to emphasize the fact that the given angle must be *rectilineal*, since the bisection of any sort of angle (including angles made by curves with one another or with straight lines) is not matter for an elementary treatise, besides which it is questionable whether such bisection is always possible. “Thus it is difficult to say whether it is possible to bisect the so-called *horn-like angle*” (formed by the circumference of a circle and a tangent to it).

Trisection of an angle.
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Further it is here that :person:`Proclus <proclus>` gives us his valuable historical note about the *trisection* of any acute angle, which (as well as the division of an angle in any given ratio) requires resort to other curves than circles, i.e. curves of the species which, after :person:`Geminus <geminus>`, he calls “mixed.” “This,” he says (p. 272, 1—12), “is shown by those who have set themselves the task of trisecting such a given rectilineal angle. For Nicomedes trisected any rectilineal angle by means of the *conchoidal* lines, the origin, order, and properties of which he has handed down to us, being himself the discoverer of their peculiarity. Others have done the same thing by means of the *quadratrices* of Hippias and Nicomedes, thereby again using ‘mixed’ curves. But others, starting from the Archimedean spirals, cut a given rectilineal angle in a given ratio.”

(a) Trisection by means of the conchoid.
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

I have already spoken of the conchoid of Nicomedes (note on Def. 2, pp. 160—1); it remains to show how it could be used for trisecting an angle. :person:`Pappus <pappus>` explains this (iv. pp. 274—5) as follows.

Let :g:`$ABC$ <0280.g1:< A B C )>` be the given acute angle, and from any point :g:`$A$ <0280.g1:A>` in :g:`$AB$ <0280.g1:/ A B />` draw :g:`$AC$ <0280.g1:/ A C />` perpendicular to :g:`$BC$ <0280.g1:/ B C />`.

.. picture:: 0280.g1.svg
   :alt: Trisection of Angle by Conchoid of Nicomedes

Complete the parallelogram :g:`$FBCA$ <0280.g1:< F B C A >>` and produce :g:`$FA$ <0280.g1:/ F A />` to a point :g:`$E$ <0280.g1:E>` such that, if :g:`$BE$ <0280.g1:/ B E />` be joined, :g:`$BE$ <0280.g1:/ B E />` intercepts between :g:`$AC$ <0280.g1:/ A C />` and :g:`$AE$ <0280.g1:/ A E />` a length :g:`$DE$ <0280.g1:/ D E />` equal to twice :g:`$AB$ <0280.g1:/ A B />`.

I say that the angle :g:`$EBC$ <0280.g1:< E B C )>` is one-third of the angle :g:`$ABC$ <0280.g1:< A B C )>`.

For, joining :g:`$A$ <0280.g1:A>` to :g:`$G$ <0280.g1:G>`, the middle point of :g:`$DE$ <0280.g1:/ D E />`, we have the three straight lines :g:`$AG$ <0280.g1:/ A G />`, :g:`$DG$ <0280.g1:/ D G />`, :g:`$EG$ <0280.g1:/ E G />` equal, and the angle :g:`$AGD$ <0280.g1:< A G D )>` is double of the angle :g:`$AED$ <0280.g1:< A E D )>` or :g:`$EBC$ <0280.g1:< E B C )>`.

But :g:`$DE$ <0280.g1:/ D E />` is double of :g:`$AB$ <0280.g1:/ A B />`; therefore :g:`$AG$ <0280.g1:/ A G />`, which is equal to :g:`$DG$ <0280.g1:/ D G />`, is equal to :g:`$AB$ <0280.g1:/ A B />`. Hence the angle :g:`$AGD$ <0280.g1:< A G D )>` is equal to the angle :g:`$ABG$ <0280.g1:< A B G )>`. Therefore the angle :g:`$ABD$ <0280.g1:< A B D )>` is also double of the angle :g:`$EBC$ <0280.g1:< E B C )>`; so that the angle :g:`$EBC$ <0280.g1:< E B C )>` is one-third of the angle :g:`$ABC$ <0280.g1:< A B C )>`.

So far :person:`Pappus <pappus>`, who reduces the construction to the drawing of :g:`$BE$ <0280.g1:/ B E />` so that :g:`$DE$ <0280.g1:/ D E />` shall be equal to twice :g:`$AB$ <0280.g1:/ A B />`.

This is what the conchoid constructed with :g:`$B$ <0280.g1:B>` as *pole*, :g:`$AC$ <0280.g1:/ A C />` as *directrix*, and *distance* equal to twice :g:`$AB$ <0280.g1:/ A B />` enables us to do; for that conchoid cuts :g:`$AE$ <0280.g1:/ A E />` in the required point :g:`$E$ <0280.g1:E>`.

(b) Use of the quadratrix.
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

The plural *quadratrices* in the above passage is a Hellenism for the singular *quadratrix*, which was a curve discovered by Hippias of Elis about 420 B.C. According to :person:`Proclus <proclus>` (:cite:`p. 356, 11 <proclus:in-euclid:356.11>`) Hippias proved its properties; and we are told (1) in the passage quoted above that Nicomedes also investigated it and that it was used for trisecting an angle, and (2) by :person:`Pappus <pappus>` (iv. pp. 250, 33—252, 4) that it was used by :person:`Dinostratus <dinostratus>` and Nicomedes and some more recent writers for squaring the circle, whence its name. It is described thus (:person:`Pappus <pappus>` iv. p. 252).

Suppose that :g:`$ABCD$ <0280.g2:< A B C D >>` is a square and :g:`$BED$ <0280.g2:( B E D )>` a quadrant of a circle with centre :g:`$A$ <0280.g2:A>`.

.. picture:: 0280.g2.svg
   :alt: Quadratrix of Hippias Diagram

Suppose (1) that a radius of the circle moves uniformly about :g:`$A$ <0280.g2:A>` from the position :g:`$AB$ <0280.g2:/ A B />` to the position :g:`$AD$ <0280.g2:/ A D />`, and (2) that *in the same time* the line :g:`$BC$ <0280.g2:/ B C />` moves uniformly, always parallel to itself, and with its extremity :g:`$B$ <0280.g2:B>` moving along :g:`$BA$ <0280.g2:/ B A />`, from the position :g:`$BC$ <0280.g2:/ B C />` to the position :g:`$AD$ <0280.g2:/ A D />`.

Then the radius :g:`$AE$ <0280.g2:/ A E />` and the moving line :g:`$BC$ <0280.g2:/ B C />` determine at any instant by their intersection a point :g:`$F$ <0280.g2:F>`.

The locus of :g:`$F$ <0280.g2:F>` is the *quadratrix*.

.. _folio-i-0281:

.. folio:: 0281
   :page: 267

The property of the curve is that, if :g:`$F$ <0280.g2:F>` is any point, the arc :g:`$BED$ <0280.g2:( B E D )>` is to the arc :g:`$ED$ <0280.g2:( E D )>` as :g:`$AB$ <0280.g2:/ A B />` is to :g:`$FH$ <0280.g2:/ F H />`.

In other words, if :g:`$\phi$ <0280.g2:< F A D )>` is the angle :g:`$FAD$ <0280.g2:< F A D )>`, :g:`$\rho$ <0280.g2:/ A F />` the radius vector :g:`$AF$ <0280.g2:/ A F />` and :g:`$a$ <0280.g2:/ A D />` the side of the square,

$$(\rho \sin \phi)/a = \phi/\frac{1}{2}\pi.$$

Now the angle :g:`$EAD$ <0280.g2:< E A D )>` can not only be *trisected* but *divided in any given ratio* by means of the quadratrix (:person:`Pappus <pappus>` iv. p. 286).

For let :g:`$FH$ <0280.g2:/ F H />` be divided at :g:`$K$ <0280.g2:K>` in the given ratio.

Draw :g:`$KL$ <0280.g2:/ K L />` parallel to :g:`$AD$ <0280.g2:/ A D />`, meeting the curve in :g:`$L$ <0280.g2:L>`; join :g:`$AL$ <0280.g2:/ A L />` and produce it to meet the circle in :g:`$N$ <0280.g2:N>`.

Then the angles :g:`$EAN$ <0280.g2:< E A N )>`, :g:`$NAD$ <0280.g2:< N A D )>` are in the ratio of :g:`$FK$ <0280.g2:/ F K />` to :g:`$KH$ <0280.g2:/ K H />`, as is easily proved.

(c) Use of the *spiral of Archimedes*.
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

The trisection of an angle, or the division of an angle in any ratio, by means of the *spiral of Archimedes* is of course an equally simple matter.  Suppose any angle included between the two radii vectores $OA$ and $OB$ of the spiral, and let it be required to cut the angle $AOB$ in a given ratio.  Since the radius vector increases proportionally with the angle described by the vector which generates the curve (reckoned from the original position of the vector coinciding with the initial line to the particular position assumed), we have only to take the radius vector $OB$ (the greater of the two $OA$, $OB$), mark off $OC$ along it equal to $OA$, cut $CB$ in the given ratio (at $D$ say), and then draw the circle with centre $O$ and radius $OD$ cutting the spiral in $E$.  Then $OE$ will divide the angle $AOB$ in the required manner.
