Commentary on Proposition I.19
==============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition I.19 <I.19>`.

.. folio:: 0298
   :page: 284

This proposition, like I. 6, can be proved by merely *logical* deduction from I. 5 and I. 18 taken together, as pointed out by :person:`De Morgan <de-morgan>`. The general form of the argument used by :person:`De Morgan <de-morgan>` is given in his *Formal Logic* (1847), p. 25, thus:

“*Hypothesis.* Let there be any number of propositions or assertions—three for instance, $X$, $Y$ and $Z$—of which it is the property that one or the other must be true, *and one only*. Let there be three other propositions $P$, $Q$ and $R$ of which it is also the property that one, and one only, must be true. Let it be a connexion of those assertions that:

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\text{when } X \text{ is true, } P \text{ is true,} \\
&\text{when } Y \text{ is true, } Q \text{ is true,} \\
&\text{when } Z \text{ is true, } R \text{ is true.}
\end{aligned}
$$

*Consequence:* then it follows that,

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\text{when } P \text{ is true, } X \text{ is true,} \\
&\text{when } Q \text{ is true, } Y \text{ is true,} \\
&\text{when } R \text{ is true, } Z \text{ is true.”}
\end{aligned}
$$

To apply this to the case before us, let us denote the sides of the triangle :g:`ABC <0298.g1:< A B C >>` by :g:`a <0298.g1:/ B C />`, :g:`b <0298.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`c <0298.g1:/ A B />`, and the angles opposite to these sides by :g:`A <0298.g1:< B A C )>`, :g:`B <0298.g1:< A B C )>`, :g:`C <0298.g1:< A C B )>` respectively, and suppose that :g:`a <0298.g1:/ B C />` is the base.

Then we have the three propositions,

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\text{when } b \text{ is equal to } c\text{, } B \text{ is equal to } C\text{,} & [\textbf{I. } 5] \\
&\left.
\begin{aligned}
&\text{when } b \text{ is greater than } c\text{, } B \text{ is greater than } C\text{,} \\
&\text{when } b \text{ is less than } c\text{, } B \text{ is less than } C\text{,}
\end{aligned}
\right\} & [\textbf{I. } 18]
\end{aligned}
$$

and it follows logically that,

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\text{when } B \text{ is equal to } C\text{, } b \text{ is equal to } c\text{,} & [\textbf{I. } 6] \\
&\left.
\begin{aligned}
&\text{when } B \text{ is greater than } C\text{, } b \text{ is greater than } c\text{,} \\
&\text{when } B \text{ is less than } C\text{, } b \text{ is less than } c\text{.}
\end{aligned}
\right\} & [\textbf{I. } 19]
\end{aligned}
$$

**Reductio ad absurdum by exhaustion.** Here, says :person:`Proclus <proclus>` (:cite:`p. 318, 16—23 <proclus:in-euclid:318.16-23>`), :person:`Euclid <euclid>` proves the impossibility “by means of division” (ἐκ διαιρέσεως). This means simply the separation of different hypotheses, each of which is inconsistent with the truth of the theorem to be proved, and which therefore must be successively shown to be impossible. If a straight line is not greater than a straight line, it must be either equal to it or less; thus in a *reductio ad absurdum* intended to prove such a theorem as I. 19 it is necessary to dispose successively of *two* hypotheses inconsistent with the truth of the theorem.

**Alternative (direct) proof.** :person:`Proclus <proclus>` gives a direct proof (pp. 319—321) which :person:`an-Nairīzī <an-nairizi>` also has and attributes to :person:`Heron <heron>`. It requires a lemma and is consequently open to the slight objection of separating a theorem from its converse. But the lemma and proof are worth giving.

*Lemma.*

*If an angle of a triangle be bisected and the straight line bisecting it meet the base and divide it into unequal parts, the sides containing the angle will be unequal, and the greater will be that which meets the greater segment of the base, and the less that which meets the less.*

Let :g:`AD <0299.g1:/ A D />`, the bisector of the angle :g:`A <0299.g1:< B A C )>` of the triangle :g:`ABC <0299.g1:< A B C >>`, meet :g:`BC <0299.g1:/ B C />` in :g:`D <0299.g1:D>`, making :g:`CD <0299.g1:/ C D />` greater than :g:`BD <0299.g1:/ B D />`. I say that :g:`AC <0299.g1:/ A C />` is greater than :g:`AB <0299.g1:/ A B />`.

.. picture:: 0299.g1.svg
   :alt: Heron/an-Nairizi Lemma for Direct Proof of I. 19 Figure

Produce :g:`AD <0299.g1:/ A D />` to :g:`E <0299.g1:E>` so that :g:`DE <0299.g1:/ D E />` is :eq:`equal <0299.g1:/ D E / = 0299.g1:/ A D />` to :g:`AD <0299.g1:/ A D />`. And, since :g:`DC <0299.g1:/ D C />` is greater than :g:`BD <0299.g1:/ B D />`, cut off :g:`DF <0299.g1:/ D F />` :eq:`equal <0299.g1:/ D F / = 0299.g1:/ B D />` to :g:`BD <0299.g1:/ B D />`. Join :g:`EF <0299.g1:/ E F />` and produce it to :g:`G <0299.g1:G>`. Then, since the two sides :g:`AD <0299.g1:/ A D />`, :g:`DB <0299.g1:/ D B />` are :eq:`equal <0299.g1:/ A D / = 0299.g1:/ E D /; 0299.g1:/ D B / = 0299.g1:/ D F />` to the two sides :g:`ED <0299.g1:/ E D />`, :g:`DF <0299.g1:/ D F />`, and the vertical angles at :g:`D <0299.g1:D>` are :eq:`equal <0299.g1:< A D B ) = 0299.g1:< E D F )>`, :g:`AB <0299.g1:/ A B />` is :eq:`equal <0299.g1:/ A B / = 0299.g1:/ E F /; 0299.g1:< D E F ) = 0299.g1:< B A D ) = 0299.g1:< D A G )>` to :g:`EF <0299.g1:/ E F />`, and the angle :g:`DEF <0299.g1:< D E F )>` to the angle :g:`BAD <0299.g1:< B A D )>`, i.e. to the angle :g:`DAG <0299.g1:< D A G )>` (by hypothesis). Therefore :g:`AG <0299.g1:/ A G />` is :eq:`equal <0299.g1:/ A G / = 0299.g1:/ E G />` to :g:`EG <0299.g1:/ E G />`, and therefore greater than :g:`EF <0299.g1:/ E F />`, or :g:`AB <0299.g1:/ A B />`. Hence, *a fortiori*, :g:`AC <0299.g1:/ A C />` is greater than :g:`AB <0299.g1:/ A B />`.

**Proof of I. 19.**

Let :g:`ABC <0300.g1:< A B C >>` be a triangle in which the angle :g:`ABC <0300.g1:< A B C )>` is greater than the angle :g:`ACB <0300.g1:< A C B )>`. Bisect :g:`BC <0300.g1:/ B C />` at :g:`D <0300.g1:D>`, join :g:`AD <0300.g1:/ A D />`, and produce it to :g:`E <0300.g1:E>` so that :g:`DE <0300.g1:/ D E />` is :eq:`equal <0300.g1:/ D E / = 0300.g1:/ A D />` to :g:`AD <0300.g1:/ A D />`. Join :g:`BE <0300.g1:/ B E />`.

.. picture:: 0300.g1.svg
   :alt: Heron/an-Nairizi Proof of I. 19 Figure

Then the two sides :g:`BD <0300.g1:/ B D />`, :g:`DE <0300.g1:/ D E />` are :eq:`equal <0300.g1:/ B D / = 0300.g1:/ C D /; 0300.g1:/ D E / = 0300.g1:/ D A />` to the two sides :g:`CD <0300.g1:/ C D />`, :g:`DA <0300.g1:/ D A />`, and the vertical angles at :g:`D <0300.g1:D>` are :eq:`equal <0300.g1:< A D B ) = 0300.g1:< E D C )>`; therefore :g:`BE <0300.g1:/ B E />` is :eq:`equal <0300.g1:/ B E / = 0300.g1:/ A C /; 0300.g1:< D B E ) = 0300.g1:< A C B )>` to :g:`AC <0300.g1:/ A C />`, and the angle :g:`DBE <0300.g1:< D B E )>` to the angle at :g:`C <0300.g1:< A C B )>`. But the angle at :g:`C <0300.g1:< A C B )>` is less than the angle :g:`ABC <0300.g1:< A B C )>`; therefore the angle :g:`DBE <0300.g1:< D B E )>` is less than the angle :g:`ABD <0300.g1:< A B D )>`. Hence, if :g:`BF <0300.g1:/ B F />` bisect the angle :g:`ABE <0300.g1:< A B E )>`, :g:`BF <0300.g1:/ B F />` meets :g:`AE <0300.g1:/ A E />` between :g:`A <0300.g1:A>` and :g:`D <0300.g1:D>`. Therefore :g:`EF <0300.g1:/ E F />` is greater than :g:`FA <0300.g1:/ F A />`. It follows, by the lemma, that :g:`BE <0300.g1:/ B E />` is greater than :g:`BA <0300.g1:/ B A />`, that is, :g:`AC <0300.g1:/ A C />` is greater than :g:`AB <0300.g1:/ A B />`.
