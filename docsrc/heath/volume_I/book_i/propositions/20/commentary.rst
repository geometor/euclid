Commentary on Proposition I.20
==============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition I.20 <I.20>`.

.. folio:: 0301
   :page: 287

It was the habit of the Epicureans, says :person:`Proclus <proclus>` (:cite:`p. 322 <proclus:in-euclid:322>`), to ridicule this theorem as being evident even to an ass and requiring no proof, and their allegation that the theorem was “known” (γνώριμον) even to an ass was based on the fact that, if fodder is placed at one angular point and the ass at another, he does not, in order to get to his food, traverse the two sides of the triangle but only the one side separating them (an argument which makes :person:`Savile <savile>` exclaim that its authors were “*digni ipsi, qui cum Asino foenum essent*,” p. 78). :person:`Proclus <proclus>` replies truly that a mere perception of the truth of the theorem is a different thing from a scientific proof of it and a knowledge of the reason *why* it is true. Moreover, as :person:`Simson <simson>` says, the number of axioms should not be increased without necessity.

**Alternative Proofs.**

:person:`Heron <heron>` and :person:`Porphyry <porphyry>`, we are told (:person:`Proclus <proclus>`, :cite:`pp. 323–6 <proclus:in-euclid:323-6>`), proved this theorem in *different* ways as follows, without producing one of the sides.

*First proof.*

Let :g:`$ABC$ <0301.g1:< A B C >>` be the triangle, and let it be required to prove that the sides :g:`$BA$ <0301.g1:/ B A />`, :g:`$AC$ <0301.g1:/ A C />` are greater than :g:`$BC$ <0301.g1:/ B C />`.

Bisect the angle :g:`$BAC$ <0301.g1:< B A C )>` by :g:`$AD$ <0301.g1:/ A D />` meeting :g:`$BC$ <0301.g1:/ B C />` in :g:`$D$ <0301.g1:D>`.

.. picture:: 0301.g1.svg
   :alt: Folio 0301 Figure 1 - First Alternative Proof of I. 20 (Heron and Porphyry)

Then, in the triangle :g:`$ABD$ <0301.g1:< A B D >>`, the exterior angle :g:`$ADC$ <0301.g1:< A D C )>` is greater than the interior and opposite angle :g:`$BAD$ <0301.g1:< B A D )>`, [I. 16] that is, greater than the angle :g:`$DAC$ <0301.g1:< D A C )>`.

Therefore the side :g:`$AC$ <0301.g1:/ A C />` is greater than the side :g:`$CD$ <0301.g1:/ C D />`. [I. 19]

Similarly we can prove that :g:`$AB$ <0301.g1:/ A B />` is greater than :g:`$BD$ <0301.g1:/ B D />`.

Hence, by addition, :g:`$BA$ <0301.g1:/ B A />`, :g:`$AC$ <0301.g1:/ A C />` are greater than :g:`$BC$ <0301.g1:/ B C />`.

*Second proof.*

This, like the first proof, is direct. There are several cases to be considered.

(1) If the triangle is *equilateral*, the truth of the proposition is obvious.

(2) If the triangle is *isosceles*, the proposition needs no proof in the case (*a*) where each of the equal sides is greater than the base.

(*b*) If the base is greater than either of the other sides, we have to prove that the sum of the two equal sides is greater than the base. Let :g:`$BC$ <0301.g2:/ B C />` be the base in such a triangle.

Cut off from :g:`$BC$ <0301.g2:/ B C />` a length :g:`$BD$ <0301.g2:/ B D />` equal to :g:`$AB$ <0301.g2:/ A B />`, and join :g:`$AD$ <0301.g2:/ A D />`.

.. picture:: 0301.g2.svg
   :alt: Folio 0301 Figure 2 - Second Alternative Proof of I. 20

Then, in the triangle :g:`$ADB$ <0301.g2:< A D B >>`, the exterior angle :g:`$ADC$ <0301.g2:< A D C )>` is greater than the interior and opposite angle :g:`$BAD$ <0301.g2:< B A D )>`. [I. 16]

Similarly, in the triangle :g:`$ADC$ <0301.g2:< A D C >>`, the exterior angle :g:`$ADB$ <0301.g2:< A D B )>` is greater than the interior and opposite angle :g:`$CAD$ <0301.g2:< C A D )>`.

.. _folio-i-0302:

.. folio:: 0302
   :page: 288

By addition, the two angles :g:`$BDA$ <0301.g2:< B D A )>`, :g:`$ADC$ <0301.g2:< A D C )>` are together greater than the two angles :g:`$BAD$ <0301.g2:< B A D )>`, :g:`$DAC$ <0301.g2:< D A C )>` (or the whole angle :g:`$BAC$ <0301.g2:< B A C )>`).

Subtracting the equal angles :g:`$BDA$ <0301.g2:< B D A )>`, :g:`$BAD$ <0301.g2:< B A D )>`, we have the angle :g:`$ADC$ <0301.g2:< A D C )>` greater than the angle :g:`$CAD$ <0301.g2:< C A D )>`.

It follows that :g:`$AC$ <0301.g2:/ A C />` is greater than :g:`$CD$ <0301.g2:/ C D />`; [I. 19] and, adding the equals :g:`$AB$ <0301.g2:/ A B />`, :g:`$BD$ <0301.g2:/ B D />` respectively, we have :g:`$BA$ <0301.g2:/ B A />`, :g:`$AC$ <0301.g2:/ A C />` together greater than :g:`$BC$ <0301.g2:/ B C />`.

(3) If the triangle be *scalene*, we can arrange the sides in order of length. Suppose :g:`$BC$ <0301.g2:/ B C />` is the greatest, :g:`$AB$ <0301.g2:/ A B />` the intermediate and :g:`$AC$ <0301.g2:/ A C />` the least side. Then it is obvious that :g:`$AB$ <0301.g2:/ A B />`, :g:`$BC$ <0301.g2:/ B C />` are together greater than :g:`$AC$ <0301.g2:/ A C />`, and :g:`$BC$ <0301.g2:/ B C />`, :g:`$CA$ <0301.g2:/ C A />` together greater than :g:`$AB$ <0301.g2:/ A B />`.

It only remains therefore to prove that :g:`$CA$ <0301.g2:/ C A />`, :g:`$AB$ <0301.g2:/ A B />` are together greater than :g:`$BC$ <0301.g2:/ B C />`.

We cut off from :g:`$BC$ <0301.g2:/ B C />` a length :g:`$BD$ <0301.g2:/ B D />` equal to the adjacent side, join :g:`$AD$ <0301.g2:/ A D />`, and proceed exactly as in the above case of the isosceles triangle.

*Third proof.*

This proof is by *reductio ad absurdum*.

Suppose that :g:`$BC$ <0302.g1:/ B C />` is the greatest side and, as before, we have to prove that :g:`$BA$ <0302.g1:/ B A />`, :g:`$AC$ <0302.g1:/ A C />` are greater than :g:`$BC$ <0302.g1:/ B C />`.

If they are not, they must be either equal to or less than :g:`$BC$ <0302.g1:/ B C />`.

(1) Suppose :g:`$BA$ <0302.g1:/ B A />`, :g:`$AC$ <0302.g1:/ A C />` are together equal to :g:`$BC$ <0302.g1:/ B C />`.

From :g:`$BC$ <0302.g1:/ B C />` cut off :g:`$BD$ <0302.g1:/ B D />` equal to :g:`$BA$ <0302.g1:/ B A />`, and join :g:`$AD$ <0302.g1:/ A D />`.

.. picture:: 0302.g1.svg
   :alt: Folio 0302 Figure 1 - Third Alternative Proof of I. 20 (Case 1)

It follows from the hypothesis that :g:`$DC$ <0302.g1:/ D C />` is equal to :g:`$AC$ <0302.g1:/ A C />`.

Then, since :g:`$BA$ <0302.g1:/ B A />` is equal to :g:`$BD$ <0302.g1:/ B D />`, the angle :g:`$BDA$ <0302.g1:< B D A )>` is equal to the angle :g:`$BAD$ <0302.g1:< B A D )>`.

Similarly, since :g:`$AC$ <0302.g1:/ A C />` is equal to :g:`$CD$ <0302.g1:/ C D />`, the angle :g:`$CDA$ <0302.g1:< C D A )>` is equal to the angle :g:`$CAD$ <0302.g1:< C A D )>`.

By addition, the angles :g:`$BDA$ <0302.g1:< B D A )>`, :g:`$ADC$ <0302.g1:< A D C )>` are together equal to the whole angle :g:`$BAC$ <0302.g1:< B A C )>`.

That is, the angle :g:`$BAC$ <0302.g1:< B A C )>` is equal to two right angles: which is impossible.

(2) Suppose :g:`$BA$ <0302.g2:/ B A />`, :g:`$AC$ <0302.g2:/ A C />` are together less than :g:`$BC$ <0302.g2:/ B C />`.

From :g:`$BC$ <0302.g2:/ B C />` cut off :g:`$BD$ <0302.g2:/ B D />` equal to :g:`$BA$ <0302.g2:/ B A />`, and from :g:`$CB$ <0302.g2:/ C B />` cut off :g:`$CE$ <0302.g2:/ C E />` equal to :g:`$CA$ <0302.g2:/ C A />`. Join :g:`$AD$ <0302.g2:/ A D />`, :g:`$AE$ <0302.g2:/ A E />`.

.. picture:: 0302.g2.svg
   :alt: Folio 0302 Figure 2 - Third Alternative Proof of I. 20 (Case 2)

In this case, we prove in the same way that the angle :g:`$BDA$ <0302.g2:< B D A )>` is equal to the angle :g:`$BAD$ <0302.g2:< B A D )>`, and the angle :g:`$CEA$ <0302.g2:< C E A )>` to the angle :g:`$CAE$ <0302.g2:< C A E )>`.

By addition, the sum of the angles :g:`$BDA$ <0302.g2:< B D A )>`, :g:`$AEC$ <0302.g2:< A E C )>` is equal to the sum of the angles :g:`$BAD$ <0302.g2:< B A D )>`, :g:`$CAE$ <0302.g2:< C A E )>`.

Now, by I. 16, the angle :g:`$BDA$ <0302.g2:< B D A )>` is greater than the angle :g:`$DAC$ <0302.g2:< D A C )>`, and therefore, *a fortiori*, greater than the angle :g:`$EAC$ <0302.g2:< E A C )>`.

Similarly the angle :g:`$AEC$ <0302.g2:< A E C )>` is greater than the angle :g:`$BAD$ <0302.g2:< B A D )>`.

Hence the sum of the angles :g:`$BDA$ <0302.g2:< B D A )>`, :g:`$AEC$ <0302.g2:< A E C )>` is greater than the sum of the angles :g:`$BAD$ <0302.g2:< B A D )>`, :g:`$EAC$ <0302.g2:< E A C )>`.

But the former sum was also equal to the latter: which is impossible.
