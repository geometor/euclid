Commentary on Proposition I.21
==============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition I.21 <I.21>`.

.. _folio-i-0304:

.. folio:: 0304

The editors generally call attention to the fact that the lines drawn within the triangle in this proposition must be drawn, as the enunciation says, from the *ends* of the side; otherwise it is not necessary that their sum should be less than that of the remaining sides of the triangle. :person:`Proclus <proclus>` (:cite:`p. 327, 12 <proclus:in-euclid:327.12>` sqq.) gives a simple illustration.

.. picture:: 0304.g1.svg
   :alt: Folio 0304 Figure 1 - Proclus Counterexample for Straight Lines not from Extremities

Let :g:`$ABC$ <0304.g1:< A B C >>` be a right-angled triangle. Take any point :g:`$D$ <0304.g1:D>` on :g:`$BC$ <0304.g1:/ B C />`, join :g:`$DA$ <0304.g1:/ D A />`, and cut off from it :g:`$DE$ <0304.g1:/ D E />` equal to :g:`$AB$ <0304.g1:/ A B />`. Bisect :g:`$AE$ <0304.g1:/ A E />` at :g:`$F$ <0304.g1:F>`, and join :g:`$FC$ <0304.g1:/ F C />`. Then shall :g:`$CF$ <0304.g1:/ C F />`, :g:`$FD$ <0304.g1:/ F D />` be together greater than :g:`$CA$ <0304.g1:/ C A />`, :g:`$AB$ <0304.g1:/ A B />`. For :g:`$CF$ <0304.g1:/ C F />`, :g:`$FE$ <0304.g1:/ F E />` are equal to :g:`$CF$ <0304.g1:/ C F />`, :g:`$FA$ <0304.g1:/ F A />`, and therefore greater than :g:`$CA$ <0304.g1:/ C A />`. Add the equals :g:`$ED$ <0304.g1:/ E D />`, :g:`$AB$ <0304.g1:/ A B />` respectively; therefore :g:`$CF$ <0304.g1:/ C F />`, :g:`$FD$ <0304.g1:/ F D />` are together greater than :g:`$CA$ <0304.g1:/ C A />`, :g:`$AB$ <0304.g1:/ A B />`.

:person:`Pappus <pappus>` gives the same proposition as that just proved, but follows it up by a number of others more elaborate in character, selected apparently from “the so-called paradoxes” of one Erycinus (:person:`Pappus <pappus>`, III. p. 106 sqq.). Thus he proves the following:

1. In any triangle, except an equilateral triangle or an isosceles triangle with base less than one of the other sides, it is possible to construct on the base and within the triangle two straight lines the sum of which is equal to the sum of the other two sides of the triangle.
2. In any triangle in which it is possible to construct two straight lines on the base which are equal to the sum of the other two sides of the triangle it is also possible to construct two others the sum of which is *greater* than that sum.
3. Under the same conditions, if the base is greater than either of the other two sides, two straight lines can be constructed in the manner described which are respectively *greater* than the other two sides of the triangle; and the lines may be constructed so as to be respectively *equal* to the two sides, if one of those two sides is less than the other and each of them less than the base.
4. The lines may be so constructed that their sum will bear to the sum of the two sides of the triangle any ratio less than 2 : 1.

As a specimen of the proofs we will give that of the proposition which has been numbered (1) for the case where the triangle is isosceles (:person:`Pappus <pappus>`, III. pp. 108—110).

Let :g:`$ABC$ <0304.g2:< A B C >>` be an isosceles triangle in which the base :g:`$AC$ <0304.g2:/ A C />` is greater than either of the equal sides :g:`$AB$ <0304.g2:/ A B />`, :g:`$BC$ <0304.g2:/ B C />`. With centre :g:`$A$ <0304.g2:A>` and radius :g:`$AB$ <0304.g2:/ A B />` describe a circle meeting :g:`$AC$ <0304.g2:/ A C />` in :g:`$D$ <0304.g2:D>`. Draw any radius :g:`$AEF$ <0304.g2:/ A E F />` such that it meets :g:`$BC$ <0304.g2:/ B C />` in a point :g:`$F$ <0304.g2:F>` outside the circle. Take any point :g:`$G$ <0304.g2:G>` on :g:`$EF$ <0304.g2:/ E F />`, and through it draw :g:`$GH$ <0304.g2:/ G H />` parallel to :g:`$AC$ <0304.g2:/ A C />`. Take any point :g:`$K$ <0304.g2:K>` on :g:`$GH$ <0304.g2:/ G H />`, and draw :g:`$KL$ <0304.g2:/ K L />` parallel to :g:`$FA$ <0304.g2:/ F A />` meeting :g:`$AC$ <0304.g2:/ A C />` in :g:`$L$ <0304.g2:L>`. From :g:`$BC$ <0304.g2:/ B C />` cut off :g:`$BN$ <0304.g2:/ B N />` equal to :g:`$EG$ <0304.g2:/ E G />`. Thus :g:`$AG$ <0304.g2:/ A G />`, or :g:`$LK$ <0304.g2:/ L K />`, is equal to the sum of :g:`$AB$ <0304.g2:/ A B />`, :g:`$BN$ <0304.g2:/ B N />`, and :g:`$CN$ <0304.g2:/ C N />` is less than :g:`$LK$ <0304.g2:/ L K />`. Now :g:`$GF$ <0304.g2:/ G F />`, :g:`$FH$ <0304.g2:/ F H />` are together greater than :g:`$GH$ <0304.g2:/ G H />`, and :g:`$CH$ <0304.g2:/ C H />`, :g:`$HK$ <0304.g2:/ H K />` together greater than :g:`$CK$ <0304.g2:/ C K />`. Therefore, by addition, :g:`$CF$ <0304.g2:/ C F />`, :g:`$FG$ <0304.g2:/ F G />`, :g:`$HK$ <0304.g2:/ H K />` are together greater than :g:`$CK$ <0304.g2:/ C K />`, :g:`$HG$ <0304.g2:/ H G />`. Subtracting :g:`$HK$ <0304.g2:/ H K />` from each side, we see that :g:`$CF$ <0304.g2:/ C F />`, :g:`$FG$ <0304.g2:/ F G />` are together greater than :g:`$CK$ <0304.g2:/ C K />`, :g:`$KG$ <0304.g2:/ K G />`; therefore, if we add :g:`$AG$ <0304.g2:/ A G />` to each, :g:`$AF$ <0304.g2:/ A F />`, :g:`$FC$ <0304.g2:/ F C />` are together greater than :g:`$AG$ <0304.g2:/ A G />`, :g:`$GK$ <0304.g2:/ G K />`, :g:`$KC$ <0304.g2:/ K C />`. And :g:`$AB$ <0304.g2:/ A B />`, :g:`$BC$ <0304.g2:/ B C />` are together greater than :g:`$AF$ <0304.g2:/ A F />`, :g:`$FC$ <0304.g2:/ F C />`. [I. 21] Therefore :g:`$AB$ <0304.g2:/ A B />`, :g:`$BC$ <0304.g2:/ B C />` are together greater than :g:`$AG$ <0304.g2:/ A G />`, :g:`$GK$ <0304.g2:/ G K />`, :g:`$KC$ <0304.g2:/ K C />`. But, by construction, :g:`$AB$ <0304.g2:/ A B />`, :g:`$BN$ <0304.g2:/ B N />` are together equal to :g:`$AG$ <0304.g2:/ A G />`; therefore, by subtraction, :g:`$NC$ <0304.g2:/ N C />` is greater than :g:`$GK$ <0304.g2:/ G K />`, :g:`$KC$ <0304.g2:/ K C />`, and *a fortiori* greater than :g:`$KC$ <0304.g2:/ K C />`. Take on :g:`$KC$ <0304.g2:[ K C ]>` produced a point :g:`$M$ <0304.g2:M>` such that :g:`$KM$ <0304.g2:/ K M />` is equal to :g:`$NC$ <0304.g2:/ N C />`; with centre :g:`$K$ <0304.g2:K>` and radius :g:`$KM$ <0304.g2:/ K M />` describe a circle meeting :g:`$CL$ <0304.g2:/ C L />` in :g:`$O$ <0304.g2:O>`, and join :g:`$KO$ <0304.g2:/ K O />`. Then shall :g:`$LK$ <0304.g2:/ L K />`, :g:`$KO$ <0304.g2:/ K O />` be equal to :g:`$AB$ <0304.g2:/ A B />`, :g:`$BC$ <0304.g2:/ B C />`. For, by construction, :g:`$LK$ <0304.g2:/ L K />` is equal to the sum of :g:`$AB$ <0304.g2:/ A B />`, :g:`$BN$ <0304.g2:/ B N />`, and :g:`$KO$ <0304.g2:/ K O />` is equal to :g:`$NC$ <0304.g2:/ N C />`; therefore :g:`$LK$ <0304.g2:/ L K />`, :g:`$KO$ <0304.g2:/ K O />` are together equal to :g:`$AB$ <0304.g2:/ A B />`, :g:`$BC$ <0304.g2:/ B C />`.

It is after I. 21 that (as remarked by :person:`De Morgan <de-morgan>`) the important proposition about the perpendicular and obliques drawn from a point to a straight line of unlimited length is best introduced:

*Of all straight lines that can be drawn to a given straight line of unlimited length from a given point without it:* *(a) the perpendicular is the shortest;* *(b) of the obliques, that is the greater the foot of which is further from the perpendicular;* *(c) given one oblique, only one other can be found of the same length, namely that the foot of which is equally distant with the foot of the given one from the perpendicular, but on the other side of it.*

Let :g:`$A$ <0305.g1:A>` be the given point, :g:`$BC$ <0305.g1:[ B C ]>` the given straight line; let :g:`$AD$ <0305.g1:/ A D />` be the perpendicular from :g:`$A$ <0305.g1:A>` on :g:`$BC$ <0305.g1:[ B C ]>`, and :g:`$AE$ <0305.g1:/ A E />`, :g:`$AF$ <0305.g1:/ A F />` any two obliques of which :g:`$AF$ <0305.g1:/ A F />` makes the greater angle with :g:`$AD$ <0305.g1:/ A D />`.

Produce :g:`$AD$ <0305.g1:/ A D />` to :g:`$A'$ <0305.g1:A'>`, making :g:`$A'D$ <0305.g1:/ A' D />` equal to :g:`$AD$ <0305.g1:/ A D />`, and join :g:`$A'E$ <0305.g1:/ A' E />`, :g:`$A'F$ <0305.g1:/ A' F />`. Then the triangles :g:`$ADE$ <0305.g1:< A D E >>`, :g:`$A'DE$ <0305.g1:< A' D E >>` are equal in all respects; and so are the triangles :g:`$ADF$ <0305.g1:< A D F >>`, :g:`$A'DF$ <0305.g1:< A' D F >>`.

.. picture:: 0305.g1.svg
   :alt: Folio 0305 Figure 1 - De Morgan Perpendicular and Obliques from a Point

Now (1) in the triangle :g:`$AEA'$ <0305.g1:< A E A' >>` the two sides :g:`$AE$ <0305.g1:/ A E />`, :g:`$EA'$ <0305.g1:/ E A' />` are greater than :g:`$AA'$ <0305.g1:/ A A' />` [I. 20], that is, twice :g:`$AE$ <0305.g1:/ A E />` is greater than twice :g:`$AD$ <0305.g1:/ A D />`.

Therefore :g:`$AE$ <0305.g1:/ A E />` is greater than :g:`$AD$ <0305.g1:/ A D />`.

(2) Since :g:`$AE$ <0305.g1:/ A E />`, :g:`$A'E$ <0305.g1:/ A' E />` are drawn to :g:`$E$ <0305.g1:E>`, a point within the triangle :g:`$AFA'$ <0305.g1:< A F A' >>`, :g:`$AF$ <0305.g1:/ A F />`, :g:`$FA'$ <0305.g1:/ F A' />` are together greater than :g:`$AE$ <0305.g1:/ A E />`, :g:`$EA'$ <0305.g1:/ E A' />`, [I. 21] or twice :g:`$AF$ <0305.g1:/ A F />` is greater than twice :g:`$AE$ <0305.g1:/ A E />`. Therefore :g:`$AF$ <0305.g1:/ A F />` is greater than :g:`$AE$ <0305.g1:/ A E />`.

(3) Along :g:`$DB$ <0305.g1:/ D B />` measure off :g:`$DG$ <0305.g1:/ D G />` equal to :g:`$DF$ <0305.g1:/ D F />`, and join :g:`$AG$ <0305.g1:/ A G />`. The triangles :g:`$AGD$ <0305.g1:< A G D >>`, :g:`$AFD$ <0305.g1:< A F D >>` are then equal in all respects, so that the angles :g:`$GAD$ <0305.g1:< G A D )>`, :g:`$FAD$ <0305.g1:< F A D )>` are equal, and :g:`$AG$ <0305.g1:/ A G />` is equal to :g:`$AF$ <0305.g1:/ A F />`.
