Commentary on Proposition I.22
==============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition I.22 <I.22>`.

.. folio:: 0307
   :page: 293

It was early observed that :person:`Euclid <euclid>` assumes, without giving any reason, that the circles drawn as described will meet if the condition that any two of the straight lines :g:`A <0306.g1:A>`, :g:`B <0306.g1:B>`, :g:`C <0306.g1:C>` are together greater than the third be fulfilled. :person:`Proclus <proclus>` (:cite:`p. 331, 8 <proclus:in-euclid:331.8>` sqq.) argues the matter by means of *reductio ad absurdum*, but does not exhaust the possible hypotheses inconsistent with the contention. He says the circles must do one of three things, (1) cut one another, (2) touch one another, (3) stand apart (διεστάναι) from one another. He then considers the hypotheses (*a*) of their touching externally, (*b*) of their being separated from one another by a space. He should have considered also the hypothesis (*c*) of one circle touching the other *internally* or lying entirely within the other without touching. These three hypotheses being successively disproved, it follows that the circles must meet (this is the line taken by Camerer and :person:`Todhunter <todhunter>`).

:person:`Simson <simson>` says: “Some authors blame :person:`Euclid <euclid>` because he does not demonstrate that the two circles made use of in the construction of this problem must cut one another: but this is very plain from the determination he has given, namely, that any two of the straight lines :g:`DF <0306.g1:/ D F />`, :g:`FG <0306.g1:/ F G />`, :g:`GH <0306.g1:/ G H />` must be greater than the third. For who is so dull, though only beginning to learn the Elements, as not to perceive that the circle described from the centre :g:`F <0306.g1:F>`, at the distance :g:`FD <0306.g1:/ F D />`, must meet :g:`FH <0306.g1:/ F H />` betwixt :g:`F <0306.g1:F>` and :g:`H <0306.g1:H>`, because :g:`FD <0306.g1:/ F D />` is less than :g:`FH <0306.g1:/ F H />`; and that, for the like reason, the circle described from the
centre :g:`G <0306.g1:G>` at the distance :g:`GH <0306.g1:/ G H />` must meet :g:`DG <0306.g1:/ D G />` betwixt :g:`D <0306.g1:D>` and :g:`G <0306.g1:G>`; and that these circles must meet one another, because :g:`FD <0306.g1:/ F D />` and :g:`GH <0306.g1:/ G H />` are together greater than :g:`FG <0306.g1:/ F G />`.”

We have in fact only to satisfy ourselves that one of the circles, e.g. that with centre :g:`G <0306.g1:G>`, has at least one point of its circumference outside the other circle and also at least one point of its circumference inside the same circle; and this is best shown with reference to the points in which the first circle cuts the straight line :g:`DE <0306.g1:[ D E ]>`. For (1) :g:`FH <0306.g1:/ F H />`, being equal to the sum of :g:`B <0306.g1:B>` and :g:`C <0306.g1:C>`, is greater than :g:`A <0306.g1:A>`, i.e. than the radius of the circle with centre :g:`F <0306.g1:F>`, and therefore :g:`H <0306.g1:H>` is outside that circle. (2) If :g:`GM <0306.g1:/ G M />` be measured along :g:`GF <0306.g1:/ G F />` :eq:`equal <0306.g1:/ G M / = 0306.g1:/ G H / = 0306.g1:C>` to :g:`GH <0306.g1:/ G H />` or :g:`C <0306.g1:C>`, then, since :g:`GM <0306.g1:/ G M />` is either (*a*) less or (*b*) greater than :g:`GF <0306.g1:/ G F />`, :g:`M <0306.g1:M>` will fall either (*a*) between :g:`G <0306.g1:G>` and :g:`F <0306.g1:F>` or (*b*) beyond :g:`F <0306.g1:F>` towards :g:`D <0306.g1:D>`; in the first case (*a*) the sum of :g:`FM <0306.g1:/ F M />` and :g:`C <0306.g1:C>` is equal to :g:`FG <0306.g1:/ F G />` and therefore less than the sum of :g:`A <0306.g1:A>` and :g:`C <0306.g1:C>`, so that :g:`FM <0306.g1:/ F M />` is less than :g:`A <0306.g1:A>` or :g:`FD <0306.g1:/ F D />`; in the second case (*b*) the sum of :g:`MF <0306.g1:/ M F />` and :g:`FG <0306.g1:/ F G />`, i.e. the sum of :g:`MF <0306.g1:/ M F />` and :g:`B <0306.g1:B>`, is equal to :g:`GM <0306.g1:/ G M />` or :g:`C <0306.g1:C>`, and therefore less than the sum of :g:`A <0306.g1:A>` and :g:`B <0306.g1:B>`, so that :g:`MF <0306.g1:/ M F />` is less than :g:`A <0306.g1:A>` or :g:`FD <0306.g1:/ F D />`; hence in either case :g:`M <0306.g1:M>` falls within the circle with centre :g:`F <0306.g1:F>`.

It being now proved that the circumference of the circle with centre :g:`G <0306.g1:G>` has at least one point outside, and at least one point inside, the circle with centre :g:`F <0306.g1:F>`, we have only to invoke the Principle of Continuity, as we have to do in **I. 1** (cf. the note on that proposition, p. 242, where the necessary postulate is stated in the form suggested by :person:`Killing <killing>`).

That the construction of the proposition gives only *two* points of intersection between the circles, and therefore only two triangles satisfying the condition, one on each side of :g:`FG <0306.g1:/ F G />`, is clear from **I. 7**, which, as before pointed out, takes the place, in Book **I.**, of **III. 10** proving that two circles cannot intersect in more points than two.
