Commentary on Proposition I.23
==============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition I.23 <I.23>`.

.. folio:: 0309
   :page: 295

This problem was, according to :person:`Eudemus <eudemus>` (see :person:`Proclus <proclus>`, :cite:`p. 333, 5 <proclus:in-euclid:333.5>`), “rather the discovery of :person:`Oenopides <oenopides>`,” from which we must apparently infer, not that :person:`Oenopides <oenopides>` was the first to find any solution of it, but that it was he who discovered the particular solution given by :person:`Euclid <euclid>`. (Cf. :person:`Bretschneider <bretschneider>`, p. 65.)

The editors do not seem to have noticed the fact that the construction of the triangle assumed in this proposition is not exactly the construction given in **I.** 22. We have here to construct a triangle on a certain finite straight line :g:`$AG$ <0308.g1:/ A G />` as base; in **I.** 22 we have only to construct a triangle with sides of given length without any restriction as to how it is to be placed. Thus in **I.** 22 we set out any line whatever and measure successively three lengths along it beginning from the given extremity, and what we must regard as the base is the intermediate length, not the length beginning at the given extremity, of the straight line arbitrarily set out. Here the base is a given straight line abutting at a given point. Thus the construction has to be modified somewhat from that of the preceding proposition.

.. picture:: 0309.g1.svg
   :alt: Folio 0309 Figure 1 - Modified Construction of I. 23 on Given Finite Base

We must measure :g:`$AG$ <0309.g1:/ A G />` along :g:`$AB$ <0309.g1:/ A B />` so that :g:`$AG$ <0309.g1:/ A G />` is :eq:`equal <0309.g1:/ A G / = 0309.g1:/ C E />` to :g:`$CE$ <0309.g1:/ C E />` (or :g:`$CD$ <0309.g1:/ C D />`), and :g:`$GH$ <0309.g1:/ G H />` along :g:`$GB$ <0309.g1:/ G B />` :eq:`equal <0309.g1:/ G H / = 0309.g1:/ D E />` to :g:`$DE$ <0309.g1:/ D E />`; and then we must produce :g:`$BA$ <0309.g1:[ B A ]>`, in the opposite direction, to :g:`$F$ <0309.g1:F>`, so that :g:`$AF$ <0309.g1:/ A F />` is :eq:`equal <0309.g1:/ A F / = 0309.g1:/ C D />` to :g:`$CD$ <0309.g1:/ C D />` (or :g:`$CE$ <0309.g1:/ C E />`, if :g:`$AG$ <0309.g1:/ A G />` has been made :eq:`equal <0309.g1:/ A G / = 0309.g1:/ C D />` to :g:`$CD$ <0309.g1:/ C D />`).

Then, by drawing circles (1) with centre :g:`$A$ <0309.g1:A>` and radius :g:`$AF$ <0309.g1:/ A F />`, (2) with centre :g:`$G$ <0309.g1:G>` and radius :g:`$GH$ <0309.g1:/ G H />`, we determine :g:`$K$ <0309.g1:K>`, one of their points of intersection, and we prove that the triangle :g:`$KAG$ <0309.g1:< K A G >>` is :eq:`equal <0309.g1:< K A G > = 0309.g1:< D C E >>` in all respects to the triangle :g:`$DCE$ <0309.g1:< D C E >>`, and then that the angle at :g:`$A$ <0309.g1:< K A G )>` is :eq:`equal <0309.g1:< K A G ) = 0309.g1:< D C E )>` to the angle :g:`$DCE$ <0309.g1:< D C E )>`.

I think that :person:`Proclus <proclus>` must (though he does not say so) have felt the same difficulty with regard to the use in **I.** 23 of the result of **I.** 22, and that this is probably the reason why he gives over again the construction which I have given above, with the remark (p. 334, 6) that “you may obtain the construction of the triangle in a more instructive manner (διδασκαλικώτερον) as follows.”

:person:`Proclus <proclus>` objects to the procedure of :person:`Apollonius <apollonius>` in constructing an angle under the same conditions, and certainly, if he quotes :person:`Apollonius <apollonius>` correctly, the latter’s exposition must have been somewhat slipshod.

.. folio:: 0310
   :page: 296

“He takes an angle :g:`$CDE$ <0310.g1:< C D E )>` at random,” says :person:`Proclus <proclus>` (:cite:`p. 335, 19 <proclus:in-euclid:335.19>` sqq.), “and a straight line :g:`$AB$ <0310.g1:/ A B />`, and with centre :g:`$D$ <0310.g1:D>` and distance :g:`$CD$ <0310.g1:/ C D />` describes the circumference :g:`$CE$ <0310.g1:( C E )>`, and in the same way with centre :g:`$A$ <0310.g1:A>` and distance :g:`$AB$ <0310.g1:/ A B />` the circumference :g:`$FB$ <0310.g1:( F B )>`. Then, cutting off :g:`$FB$ <0310.g1:( F B )>` :eq:`equal <0310.g1:( F B ) = 0310.g1:( C E )>` to :g:`$CE$ <0310.g1:( C E )>`, he joins :g:`$AF$ <0310.g1:/ A F />`. And he declares that the angles :g:`$A$ <0310.g1:< F A B )>`, :g:`$D$ <0310.g1:< C D E )>` standing on :eq:`equal <0310.g1:( F B ) = 0310.g1:( C E )>` circumferences are :eq:`equal <0310.g1:< F A B ) = 0310.g1:< C D E )>`.”

.. picture:: 0310.g1.svg
   :alt: Folio 0310 Figure 1 - Apollonius Construction of Equal Angle by Circular Arcs

In the first place, as :person:`Proclus <proclus>` remarks, it should be premised that :g:`$AB$ <0310.g1:/ A B />` is :eq:`equal <0310.g1:/ A B / = 0310.g1:/ C D />` to :g:`$CD$ <0310.g1:/ C D />` in order that the circles may be equal; and the use of Book **III.** for such an elementary construction is objectionable. The omission to state that :g:`$AB$ <0310.g1:/ A B />` must be taken :eq:`equal <0310.g1:/ A B / = 0310.g1:/ C D />` to :g:`$CD$ <0310.g1:/ C D />` was no doubt a slip, if it occurred. And, as regards the :eq:`equal <0310.g1:< F A B ) = 0310.g1:< C D E )>` angles “standing on :eq:`equal <0310.g1:( F B ) = 0310.g1:( C E )>` circumferences,” it would seem possible that :person:`Apollonius <apollonius>` said this *in explanation*, for the sake of brevity, rather than by way of proof. It seems to me probable that his construction was only given from the point of view of *practical*, not theoretical, geometry. It really comes to the same thing as Euclid’s except that :g:`$DC$ <0310.g1:/ D C />` is taken :eq:`equal <0310.g1:/ D C / = 0310.g1:/ D E />` to :g:`$DE$ <0310.g1:/ D E />`. For cutting off the arc :g:`$BF$ <0310.g1:( B F )>` :eq:`equal <0310.g1:( B F ) = 0310.g1:( C E )>` to the arc :g:`$CE$ <0310.g1:( C E )>` can only be meant in the sense of measuring the chord :g:`$CE$ <0310.g1:/ C E />`, say, with a pair of compasses, and then drawing a circle with centre :g:`$B$ <0310.g1:B>` and radius :eq:`equal <0310.g1:/ B F / = 0310.g1:/ C E />` to the chord :g:`$CE$ <0310.g1:/ C E />`. :person:`Apollonius <apollonius>`’ direction was therefore probably intended as a practical short cut, avoiding the actual drawing of the chords :g:`$CE$ <0310.g1:/ C E />`, :g:`$BF$ <0310.g1:/ B F />`, which, as well as a proof of the equality in all respects of the triangles :g:`$CDE$ <0310.g1:< C D E >>`, :g:`$BAF$ <0310.g1:< B A F >>`, would be required to establish *theoretically* the correctness of the construction.
