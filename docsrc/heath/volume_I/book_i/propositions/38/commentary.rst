Commentary on Proposition 38
============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition 38 <I.38>`.

.. folio:: 0348
   :page: 334

On this proposition Proclus remarks (pp. 405—6) that Euclid seems to him to have given in VI. 1 one proof including all the four theorems from I. 35 to I. 38, and that most people had failed to notice this. When Euclid, he says, proves that triangles and parallelograms of the same altitude have to one another the same ratio as their bases, he simply proves all these propositions more generally by the use of proportion; for of course to be of the same altitude is equivalent to being in the same parallels. It is true that VI. 1 generalises these propositions, but it must be observed that it does not prove the propositions themselves, as Proclus seems to imply; they are in fact assumed in order to prove VI. 1.

Comparison of areas of triangles of I. 24.
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

The theorem already mentioned as given by Proclus on I. 24 (pp. 340—4) is placed here by Heron, who also enunciates it more clearly (an-Nairīzī, ed. Besthorn-Heiberg, pp. 155—161, ed. Curtze, pp. 75—8).

*If in two triangles two sides of the one be equal to two sides of the other respectively, and the angle of the one be greater than the angle of the other, namely the angles contained by the equal sides, then, (1) if the sum of the two angles contained by the equal sides is equal to two right angles, the two triangles are equal to one another; (2) if less than two right angles, the triangle which has the greater angle is also itself greater than the other; (3) if greater than two right angles, the triangle which has the less angle is greater than the other triangle.*

.. picture:: 0348.g1.svg
   :alt: Folio 0348 Figure 1 - Proposition 38 Commentary (Heron)

Let two triangles :g:`$ABC$ <0348.g1:< A B C >>`, :g:`$DEF$ <0348.g1:< D E F >>` have the sides :g:`$AB$ <0348.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$AC$ <0348.g1:/ A C />` respectively equal to :g:`$DE$ <0348.g1:/ D E />`, :g:`$DF$ <0348.g1:/ D F />`.

(1) First, suppose that the angles at :g:`$A$ <0348.g1:A>` and :g:`$D$ <0348.g1:D>` in the triangles :g:`$ABC$ <0348.g1:< A B C >>`, :g:`$DEF$ <0348.g1:< D E F >>` are together equal to two right angles.

| Heron's construction is now as follows.
| Make the angle :g:`$EDG$ <0348.g1:< E D G )>` equal to the angle :g:`$BAC$ <0348.g1:< B A C )>`.
| Draw :g:`$FH$ <0348.g1:/ F H />` parallel to :g:`$ED$ <0348.g1:/ E D />` meeting :g:`$DG$ <0348.g1:/ D G />` in :g:`$H$ <0348.g1:H>`.
| Join :g:`$EH$ <0348.g1:/ E H />`.

.. folio:: 0349
   :page: 335

| Then, since the angles :g:`$BAC$ <0348.g1:< B A C )>`, :g:`$EDF$ <0348.g1:< E D F )>` are equal to two right angles, the angles :g:`$EDH$ <0348.g1:< E D H )>`, :g:`$EDF$ <0348.g1:< E D F )>` are equal to two right angles.
| But so are the angles :g:`$EDH$ <0348.g1:< E D H )>`, :g:`$DHF$ <0348.g1:< D H F )>`.
| Therefore the angles :g:`$EDF$ <0348.g1:< E D F )>`, :g:`$DHF$ <0348.g1:< D H F )>` are equal.
| And the alternate angles :g:`$EDF$ <0348.g1:< E D F )>`, :g:`$DFH$ <0348.g1:< D F H )>` are equal. [I. 29]
| Therefore the angles :g:`$DHF$ <0348.g1:< D H F )>`, :g:`$DFH$ <0348.g1:< D F H )>` are equal,
| and :g:`$DF$ <0348.g1:/ D F />` is equal to :g:`$DH$ <0348.g1:/ D H />`. [I. 6]
| Hence the two sides :g:`$ED$ <0348.g1:/ E D />`, :g:`$DH$ <0348.g1:/ D H />` are equal to the two sides :g:`$BA$ <0348.g1:/ B A />`, :g:`$AC$ <0348.g1:/ A C />`; and the included angles are equal.
| Therefore the triangles :g:`$ABC$ <0348.g1:< A B C >>`, :g:`$DEH$ <0348.g1:< D E H >>` are equal in all respects.
| And the triangles :g:`$DEF$ <0348.g1:< D E F >>`, :g:`$DEH$ <0348.g1:< D E H >>` between the same parallels are equal. [I. 37]
| Therefore the triangles :g:`$ABC$ <0348.g1:< A B C >>`, :g:`$DEF$ <0348.g1:< D E F >>` are equal.
| [Proclus takes the construction of Eucl. I. 24, i.e., he makes :g:`$DH$ <0348.g1:/ D H />` equal to :g:`$DF$ <0348.g1:/ D F />` and then proves that :g:`$ED$ <0348.g1:/ E D />`, :g:`$FH$ <0348.g1:/ F H />` are parallel.]

| (2) Suppose the angles :g:`$BAC$ <0349.g1:< B A C )>`, :g:`$EDF$ <0349.g1:< E D F )>` together less than two right angles.
| As before, make the angle :g:`$EDG$ <0349.g1:< E D G )>` equal to the angle :g:`$BAC$ <0349.g1:< B A C )>`, draw :g:`$FH$ <0349.g1:/ F H />` parallel to :g:`$ED$ <0349.g1:/ E D />`, and join :g:`$EH$ <0349.g1:/ E H />`.

.. picture:: 0349.g1.svg
   :alt: Folio 0349 Figure 1 - Proposition 38 Commentary (Heron, Case 2)

| In this case the angles :g:`$EDH$ <0349.g1:< E D H )>`, :g:`$EDF$ <0349.g1:< E D F )>` are together less than two right angles, while the angles :g:`$EDH$ <0349.g1:< E D H )>`, :g:`$DHF$ <0349.g1:< D H F )>` are equal to two right angles. [I. 29]
| Hence the angle :g:`$EDF$ <0349.g1:< E D F )>`, and therefore the angle :g:`$DFH$ <0349.g1:< D F H )>`, is less than the angle :g:`$DHF$ <0349.g1:< D H F )>`.
| Therefore :g:`$DH$ <0349.g1:/ D H />` is less than :g:`$DF$ <0349.g1:/ D F />`. [I. 19]

Produce :g:`$DH$ <0349.g1:/ D H />` to :g:`$G$ <0349.g1:G>` so that :g:`$DG$ <0349.g1:/ D G />` is equal to :g:`$DF$ <0349.g1:/ D F />` or :g:`$AC$ <0349.g1:/ A C />`, and join :g:`$EG$ <0349.g1:/ E G />`.

Then the triangle :g:`$DEG$ <0349.g1:< D E G >>`, which is equal to the triangle :g:`$ABC$ <0349.g1:< A B C >>`, is greater than the triangle :g:`$DEH$ <0349.g1:< D E H >>`, and therefore greater than the triangle :g:`$DEF$ <0349.g1:< D E F >>`.

(3) Suppose the angles :g:`$BAC$ <0349.g2:< B A C )>`, :g:`$EDF$ <0349.g2:< E D F )>` together greater than two right angles.

.. picture:: 0349.g2.svg
   :alt: Folio 0349 Figure 2 - Proposition 38 Commentary (Heron, Case 3)

| We make the same construction in this case, and we prove in like manner that the angle :g:`$DHF$ <0349.g2:< D H F )>` is less than the angle :g:`$DFH$ <0349.g2:< D F H )>`,
| whence :g:`$DH$ <0349.g2:/ D H />` is greater than :g:`$DF$ <0349.g2:/ D F />` or :g:`$AC$ <0349.g2:/ A C />`.

Make :g:`$DG$ <0349.g2:/ D G />` equal to :g:`$AC$ <0349.g2:/ A C />`, and join :g:`$EG$ <0349.g2:/ E G />`.

It then follows that the triangle :g:`$DEF$ <0349.g2:< D E F >>` is greater than the triangle :g:`$ABC$ <0349.g2:< A B C >>`.

[In the second and third cases again Proclus starts from the construction in I. 24, and proves, in the second case, that the parallel, :g:`$FH$ <0349.g1:/ F H />`, to :g:`$ED$ <0349.g1:/ E D />` cuts :g:`$DG$ <0349.g1:/ D G />` and, in the third case, that it cuts :g:`$DG$ <0349.g2:/ D G />` produced.]
