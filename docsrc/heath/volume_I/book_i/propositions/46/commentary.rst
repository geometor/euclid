Commentary on Proposition I.46
==============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition I.46 <I.46>`.

.. folio:: 0362
   :page: 348

:person:`Proclus <proclus>` (:cite:`pp. 424—5 <proclus:in-euclid:424-5>`) proves that, *if squares are described on equal straight lines, the squares are equal; and, conversely, that, if two squares are equal, the straight lines are equal on which they are described*. The first proposition is immediately obvious if we divide the squares into two triangles by drawing a diagonal in each. The converse is proved as follows.

.. picture:: 0362.g1.svg
   :alt: Folio 0362 Figure 1 - Proclus Equal Squares on Equal Straight Lines

Place the two :eq:`equal <0362.g1:< A D F B > = 0362.g1:< B C E G >>` squares :g:`AF <0362.g1:< A D F B >>`, :g:`CG <0362.g1:< B C E G >>` so that :g:`AB <0362.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`BC <0362.g1:/ B C />` are in a straight line. Then, since the angles are right, :g:`FB <0362.g1:/ F B />`, :g:`BG <0362.g1:/ B G />` will also be in a straight line. Join :g:`AF <0362.g1:/ A F />`, :g:`FC <0362.g1:/ F C />`, :g:`CG <0362.g1:/ C G />`, :g:`GA <0362.g1:/ G A />`.

Now, since the squares are :eq:`equal <0362.g1:< A D F B > = 0362.g1:< B C E G >>`, the triangles :g:`ABF <0362.g1:< A B F >>`, :g:`CBG <0362.g1:< C B G >>` are :eq:`equal <0362.g1:< A B F > = 0362.g1:< C B G >>`.

Add to each the triangle :g:`FBC <0362.g1:< F B C >>`; therefore the triangles :g:`AFC <0362.g1:< A F C >>`, :g:`GFC <0362.g1:< G F C >>` are :eq:`equal <0362.g1:< A F C > = 0362.g1:< G F C >>`, and hence they must be in the same parallels.

Therefore :g:`AG <0362.g1:/ A G />`, :g:`CF <0362.g1:/ C F />` are parallel.

Also, since each of the alternate angles :g:`AFG <0362.g1:< A F G )>`, :g:`FGC <0362.g1:< F G C )>` is half a right angle, :g:`AF <0362.g1:/ A F />`, :g:`CG <0362.g1:/ C G />` are parallel.

Hence :g:`AFCG <0362.g1:< A F C G >>` is a parallelogram; and :g:`AF <0362.g1:/ A F />`, :g:`CG <0362.g1:/ C G />` are :eq:`equal <0362.g1:/ A F / = 0362.g1:/ C G />`.

Thus the triangles :g:`ABF <0362.g1:< A B F >>`, :g:`CBG <0362.g1:< C B G >>` have two angles and one side respectively equal; therefore :g:`AB <0362.g1:/ A B />` is :eq:`equal <0362.g1:/ A B / = 0362.g1:/ B C /; 0362.g1:/ B F / = 0362.g1:/ B G />` to :g:`BC <0362.g1:/ B C />`, and :g:`BF <0362.g1:/ B F />` to :g:`BG <0362.g1:/ B G />`.
