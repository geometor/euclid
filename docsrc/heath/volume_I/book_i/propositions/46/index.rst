PROPOSITION 46.
===============

.. enunciation::

   On a given straight line to describe\ [#f1]_ a square.

Let :g:`$AB$ <0361.g1:/ A B />` be the given straight line;

thus it is required to describe a square on the straight line :g:`$AB$ <0361.g1:/ A B />`.

.. picture:: 0361.g1.svg
   :alt: Folio 0361 Figure 1 - Proposition 46

Let :g:`$AC$ <0361.g1:/ A C />` be drawn at right angles to the straight line :g:`$AB$ <0361.g1:/ A B />` from the point :g:`$A$ <0361.g1:A>` on it :just:`[I. 11] <I.11>`, and let :g:`$AD$ <0361.g1:/ A D />` be made equal to :g:`$AB$ <0361.g1:/ A B />`;

through the point :g:`$D$ <0361.g1:D>` let :g:`$DE$ <0361.g1:/ D E />` be drawn parallel to :g:`$AB$ <0361.g1:/ A B />`, and through the point :g:`$B$ <0361.g1:B>` let :g:`$BE$ <0361.g1:/ B E />` be drawn parallel to :g:`$AD$ <0361.g1:/ A D />`. :just:`[I. 31] <I.31>`
Therefore :g:`$ADEB$ <0361.g1:< A D E B >>` is a parallelogram;

.. _folio-i-0362:

.. folio:: 0362
   :page: 348

.. assertion:: [I. 34] <I.34>

   therefore :g:`$AB$ <0361.g1:/ A B />` is equal to :g:`$DE$ <0361.g1:/ D E />`, and :g:`$AD$ <0361.g1:/ A D />` to :g:`$BE$ <0361.g1:/ B E />`.

But :g:`$AB$ <0361.g1:/ A B />` is equal to :g:`$AD$ <0361.g1:/ A D />`;

.. subclause::

   therefore the four straight lines :g:`$BA$ <0361.g1:/ B A />`, :g:`$AD$ <0361.g1:/ A D />`, :g:`$DE$ <0361.g1:/ D E />`, :g:`$EB$ <0361.g1:/ E B />` are equal to one another;

.. assertion::

   therefore the parallelogram :g:`$ADEB$ <0361.g1:< A D E B >>` is equilateral.

I say next that it is also right-angled. For, since the straight line :g:`$AD$ <0361.g1:/ A D />` falls upon the parallels :g:`$AB$ <0361.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$DE$ <0361.g1:/ D E />`,

.. assertion:: [I. 29] <I.29>

   the angles :g:`$BAD$ <0361.g1:< B A D )>`, :g:`$ADE$ <0361.g1:< A D E )>` are equal to two right angles.

But the angle :g:`$BAD$ <0361.g1:< B A D )>` is right;

.. assertion::

   therefore the angle :g:`$ADE$ <0361.g1:< A D E )>` is also right.

And in parallelogrammic areas the opposite sides and angles are equal to one another; :just:`[I. 34] <I.34>`

.. assertion::

   therefore each of the opposite angles :g:`$ABE$ <0361.g1:< A B E )>`, :g:`$BED$ <0361.g1:< B E D )>` is also right.

.. assertion::

   Therefore :g:`$ADEB$ <0361.g1:< A D E B >>` is right-angled.

And it was also proved equilateral.

.. assertion::

   Therefore it is a square; and it is described on the straight line :g:`$AB$ <0361.g1:/ A B />`.

.. qed::

   Q. E. F.

.. [#f1] :person:`Proclus <proclus>` (:cite:`p. 423, 18 <proclus:in-euclid:423.18>` sqq.) notes the difference between the word *construct* (συστήσασθαι) applied by :person:`Euclid <euclid>` to the construction of a triangle (and, he might have added, of an angle) and the words *describe on* (ἀναγράφειν ἀπό) used of drawing a square on a given straight line as one side. The triangle (or angle) is, so to say, pieced together, while the describing of a square on a given straight line is the making of a figure “from” one side, and corresponds to the multiplication of the number representing the side by itself.
