Commentary on Proposition II.9
==============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition II.9 <II.9>`.

.. folio:: 0408
   :page: 394

It is noteworthy that, while the first eight propositions of Book II. are proved independently of the Pythagorean theorem I. 47, all the remaining propositions beginning with the 9th are proved by means of it. Also the 9th and 10th propositions mark a new departure in another respect; the method of demonstration by showing in the figures the various rectangles and squares to which the theorems relate is here abandoned.

The 9th and 10th propositions are related to one another in the same way as the 5th and 6th; they really prove the same result which can, as in the earlier case, be comprised in a single enunciation thus: *The sum of the squares on the sum and difference of two given straight lines is equal to twice the sum of the squares on the lines*.

The semi-algebraical proof of Prop. 9 is that suggested by the remark on the algebraical formulae given at the end of the note on II. 7. It applies with a very slight modification to both II. 9 and II. 10. We will put in brackets the variations belonging to II. 10. The first of the annexed lines is the figure for II. 9 and the second for II. 10.

.. picture:: 0408.g1.svg
   :alt: Diagram

By II. 4, the square on :g:`$AD$ <0408.g1:/ A D />` is equal to the squares on :g:`$AC$ <0408.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$CD$ <0408.g1:/ C D />` and twice the rectangle :g:`$AC$ <0408.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$CD$ <0408.g1:/ C D />`.

By II. 7, the squares on :g:`$CB$ <0408.g1:/ C B />`, :g:`$CD$ <0408.g1:/ C D />` (:g:`$CD$ <0408.g1:/ C' D' />`, :g:`$CB$ <0408.g1:/ C' B' />`) are equal to twice the rectangle :g:`$CB$ <0408.g1:/ C B />`, :g:`$CD$ <0408.g1:/ C D />` together with the square on :g:`$BD$ <0408.g1:/ B D />`.

By addition of these equals crosswise, the squares on :g:`$AD$ <0408.g1:/ A D />`, :g:`$DB$ <0408.g1:/ D B />` together with twice the rectangle :g:`$CB$ <0408.g1:/ C B />`, :g:`$CD$ <0408.g1:/ C D />` are equal to the squares on :g:`$AC$ <0408.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$CD$ <0408.g1:/ C D />`, :g:`$CB$ <0408.g1:/ C B />`, :g:`$CD$ <0408.g1:/ C D />` together with twice the rectangle :g:`$AC$ <0408.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$CD$ <0408.g1:/ C D />`.

But :g:`$AC$ <0408.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$CB$ <0408.g1:/ C B />` are equal, and therefore the rectangles :g:`$AC$ <0408.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$CD$ <0408.g1:/ C D />` and :g:`$CB$ <0408.g1:/ C B />`, :g:`$CD$ <0408.g1:/ C D />` are equal.

Taking away the equals, we see that the squares on :g:`$AD$ <0408.g1:/ A D />`, :g:`$DB$ <0408.g1:/ D B />` are equal to the squares on :g:`$AC$ <0408.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$CD$ <0408.g1:/ C D />`, :g:`$CB$ <0408.g1:/ C B />`, :g:`$CD$ <0408.g1:/ C D />`, i.e. to twice the squares on :g:`$AC$ <0408.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$CD$ <0408.g1:/ C D />`.

To show also that the method of geometrical algebra illustrated by II. 1—8 is still effective for the purpose of proving II. 9, 10, we will now prove II. 9 in that manner.

Now, since :g:`$AD$ <0408.g2:/ A D />`, :g:`$CD$ <0408.g2:/ C D />` are respectively equal to :g:`$DE$ <0408.g2:/ D E />`, :g:`$DH$ <0408.g2:/ D H />`,

.. assertion::

   :g:`$HE$ <0408.g2:/ H E />` is equal to :g:`$AC$ <0408.g2:/ A C />` or :g:`$CB$ <0408.g2:/ C B />`;

and, since :g:`$HL$ <0408.g2:/ H L />` is equal to :g:`$CD$ <0408.g2:/ C D />`, :g:`$LE$ <0408.g2:/ L E />` is equal to :g:`$DB$ <0408.g2:/ D B />`.

Similarly, since each of the segments :g:`$EM$ <0408.g2:/ E M />`, :g:`$MQ$ <0408.g2:/ M Q />` is equal to :g:`$CD$ <0408.g2:/ C D />`,

.. assertion::

   :g:`$FQ$ <0408.g2:/ F Q />` is equal to :g:`$EL$ <0408.g2:/ E L />` or :g:`$BD$ <0408.g2:/ B D />`.

Therefore :g:`$OQ$ <0408.g2:< O Q >>` is equal to the square on :g:`$DB$ <0408.g2:/ D B />`.

We have to prove that the squares on :g:`$AD$ <0408.g2:/ A D />`, :g:`$DB$ <0408.g2:/ D B />` are equal to twice the squares on :g:`$AC$ <0408.g2:/ A C />`, :g:`$CD$ <0408.g2:/ C D />`.

Now the square on :g:`$AD$ <0408.g2:/ A D />` includes :g:`$KM$ <0408.g2:< K M >>` (the square on :g:`$AC$ <0408.g2:/ A C />`) and :g:`$CH$ <0408.g2:< C H >>`, :g:`$HN$ <0408.g2:< H N >>` (that is, twice the square on :g:`$CD$ <0408.g2:/ C D />`).

Therefore we have to prove that what is left over of the square on :g:`$AD$ <0408.g2:/ A D />` together with the square on :g:`$DB$ <0408.g2:/ D B />` is equal to the square on :g:`$AC$ <0408.g2:/ A C />`.

The parts left over are the rectangles :g:`$CK$ <0408.g2:< C K >>` and :g:`$NE$ <0408.g2:< N E >>`, which are equal to :g:`$KN$ <0408.g2:< K N >>`, :g:`$PM$ <0408.g2:< P M >>` respectively.

But the latter with the square on :g:`$DB$ <0408.g2:/ D B />` are equal to the rectangles :g:`$KN$ <0408.g2:< K N >>`, :g:`$PM$ <0408.g2:< P M >>` and the square :g:`$OQ$ <0408.g2:< O Q >>`,

.. assertion::

   i.e. to the square :g:`$KM$ <0408.g2:< K M >>`, or the square on :g:`$AC$ <0408.g2:/ A C />`.

Hence the required result follows.
