Commentary on Proposition II.11
===============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition II.11 <II.11>`.

.. folio:: 0417
   :page: 403

As the solution of this problem is necessary to that of inscribing a regular pentagon in a circle (Eucl. IV. 10, 11), we must necessarily conclude that it was solved by the Pythagoreans, or, in other words, that they discovered the geometrical solution of the quadratic equation
\[
a(a - x) = x^2,
\]
or
\[
x^2 + ax = a^2.
\]

The solution in II. 11, too, exactly corresponds to the solution of the more general equation
\[
x^2 + ax = b^2,
\]
which, as shown above (pp. 387—8), :person:`Simson <simson>` based upon II. 6. Only Simson's solution, if applied here, gives us the point :g:`F <0416.g1:F>` on :g:`CA <0416.g1:[ C A ]>` produced and does not directly find the point :g:`H <0416.g1:H>`. It takes :g:`E <0416.g1:E>` the middle point of :g:`CA <0416.g1:/ C A />`, draws :g:`AB <0416.g1:/ A B />` at right angles to :g:`CA <0416.g1:/ C A />` and of length :eq:`equal <0416.g1:/ A B / = 0416.g1:/ C A />` to :g:`CA <0416.g1:/ C A />`, and then describes a circle with :g:`EB <0416.g1:/ E B />` as radius cutting :g:`EA <0416.g1:[ E A ]>` produced in :g:`F <0416.g1:F>`. The only difference between the solution in this case and in the more general case is that :g:`AB <0416.g1:/ A B />` is here :eq:`equal <0416.g1:/ A B / = 0416.g1:/ C A />` to :g:`CA <0416.g1:/ C A />` instead of being equal to another given straight line $b$.

As in the more general case, there is, from Euclid's point of view, only one solution.

The construction shows that :g:`CF <0416.g1:/ C F />` is also divided at :g:`A <0416.g1:A>` in the manner described in the enunciation, since the rectangle :g:`CF <0416.g1:/ C F />`, :g:`FA <0416.g1:/ F A />` is :eq:`equal <0416.g1:< C F G K > = 0416.g1:< A B D C >>` to the square on :g:`CA <0416.g1:/ C A />`.

The problem in II. 11 reappears in VI. 30 in the form of *cutting a given straight line in extreme and mean ratio*.
