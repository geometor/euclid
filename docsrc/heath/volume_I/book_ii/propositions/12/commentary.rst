Commentary on Proposition II.12
===============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition II.12 <II.12>`.

.. folio:: 0418
   :page: 404

Since in this proposition and the next we have to do with the squares on the sides of triangles, the particular form of graphic representation of areas which we have had in Book II. up to this point does not help us to visualise the results of the propositions in the same way, and only two lines of proof are possible, (1) by means of the *results* of certain earlier propositions in Book II. combined with the *result* of I. 47 and (2) by means of the procedure in Euclid's proof of I. 47 itself. The alternative proofs of II. 12, 13 after the manner of Euclid's proof of I. 47 are therefore alone worth giving.

These proofs appear in certain modern text-books (e.g. Mehler, :person:`Henrici <henrici>` and Treutlein, H. M. Taylor, Smith and Bryant). Smith and Bryant are not correct in saying (p. 142) that they cannot be traced further back than Lardner's :person:`Euclid <euclid>` (1828); they are to be found in Gregory of St. Vincent's work (published in 1647) *Opus geometricum quadraturae circuli et sectionum coni*, Book I. Pt. 2, Props. 44, 45 (pp. 31, 32).

To prove II. 12, take an obtuse-angled triangle :g:`ABC <0419.g1:< A B C >>` in which the angle at :g:`A <0419.g1:< B A C )>` is the obtuse angle
Describe squares on :g:`BC <0419.g1:/ B C />`, :g:`CA <0419.g1:/ C A />`, :g:`AB <0419.g1:/ A B />`, as :g:`BCED <0419.g1:< B C E D >>`, :g:`CAGF <0419.g1:< C A G F >>`, :g:`ABKH <0419.g1:< A B K H >>`.

.. _folio-i-0419:

.. folio:: 0419
   :page: 405

Draw :g:`AL <0419.g1:/ A L />`, :g:`BM <0419.g1:/ B M />`, :g:`CN <0419.g1:/ C N />`, perpendicular to :g:`BC <0419.g1:/ B C />`, :g:`CA <0419.g1:[ C A ]>`, :g:`AB <0419.g1:[ A B ]>` (produced if necessary), and produce them to meet the further sides of the squares on them in :g:`P <0419.g1:P>`, :g:`Q <0419.g1:Q>`, :g:`R <0419.g1:R>` respectively.

.. picture:: 0419.g1.svg
   :alt: Diagram

Join :g:`AD <0419.g1:/ A D />`, :g:`CK <0419.g1:/ C K />`.

Then, as in I. 47, the triangles :g:`KBC <0419.g1:< K B C >>`, :g:`ABD <0419.g1:< A B D >>` are :eq:`equal <0419.g1:< K B C > = 0419.g1:< A B D >>` in all respects;

therefore their doubles, the parallelograms in the same parallels respectively, are equal;

that is, the rectangle :g:`BP <0419.g1:< B L P D >>` is :eq:`equal <0419.g1:< B L P D > = 0419.g1:< B N R K >>` to the rectangle :g:`BR <0419.g1:< B N R K >>`.

Similarly the rectangle :g:`CP <0419.g1:< L C E P >>` is :eq:`equal <0419.g1:< L C E P > = 0419.g1:< C M Q F >>` to the rectangle :g:`CQ <0419.g1:< C M Q F >>`.

Also, if :g:`BG <0419.g1:/ B G />`, :g:`CH <0419.g1:/ C H />` be joined, we see that

the triangles :g:`BAG <0419.g1:< B A G >>`, :g:`HAC <0419.g1:< H A C >>` are :eq:`equal <0419.g1:< B A G > = 0419.g1:< H A C >>` in all respects;

therefore their doubles, the rectangles :g:`AQ <0419.g1:< A M Q G >>`, :g:`AR <0419.g1:< A N R H >>`, are :eq:`equal <0419.g1:< A M Q G > = 0419.g1:< A N R H >>`.

Now the square on :g:`BC <0419.g1:/ B C />` is equal to the sum of the rectangles :g:`BP <0419.g1:< B L P D >>`, :g:`CP <0419.g1:< L C E P >>`,

i.e. to the sum of the rectangles :g:`BR <0419.g1:< B N R K >>`, :g:`CQ <0419.g1:< C M Q F >>`,

i.e. to the sum of the squares :g:`BH <0419.g1:< A B K H >>`, :g:`CG <0419.g1:< C A G F >>` and the rectangles :g:`AR <0419.g1:< A N R H >>`, :g:`AQ <0419.g1:< A M Q G >>`.

But the rectangles :g:`AR <0419.g1:< A N R H >>`, :g:`AQ <0419.g1:< A M Q G >>` are :eq:`equal <0419.g1:< A N R H > = 0419.g1:< A M Q G >>`, and they are respectively the rectangle contained by :g:`BA <0419.g1:/ B A />`, :g:`AN <0419.g1:/ A N />` and the rectangle contained by :g:`CA <0419.g1:/ C A />`, :g:`AM <0419.g1:/ A M />`.

Therefore the square on :g:`BC <0419.g1:/ B C />` is equal to the squares on :g:`BA <0419.g1:/ B A />`, :g:`AC <0419.g1:/ A C />` together with twice the rectangle :g:`BA <0419.g1:/ B A />`, :g:`AN <0419.g1:/ A N />` or :g:`CA <0419.g1:/ C A />`, :g:`AM <0419.g1:/ A M />`.

Incidentally this proof shows that the rectangle :g:`BA <0419.g1:/ B A />`, :g:`AN <0419.g1:/ A N />` is equal to the rectangle :g:`CA <0419.g1:/ C A />`, :g:`AM <0419.g1:/ A M />`: a result which will be seen later on to be a particular case of the theorem in III. 35.

:person:`Heron <heron>` (in :person:`an-Nairīzī <an-nairizi>`, ed. :person:`Curtze <curtze>`, p. 109) gives a "converse" of II. 12 related to it as I. 48 is related to I. 47.

*In any triangle, if the square on one of the sides is greater than the squares on the other two sides, the angle contained by the latter is obtuse.*

Let :g:`ABC <0419.g2:< A B C >>` be a triangle such that the square on :g:`BC <0419.g2:/ B C />` is greater than the squares on :g:`BA <0419.g2:/ B A />`, :g:`AC <0419.g2:/ A C />`.

Draw :g:`AD <0419.g2:/ A D />` at right angles to :g:`AC <0419.g2:/ A C />` and of length :eq:`equal <0419.g2:/ A D / = 0419.g2:/ A B />` to :g:`AB <0419.g2:/ A B />`.

.. picture:: 0419.g2.svg
   :alt: Diagram

Join :g:`DC <0419.g2:/ D C />`.

Then, since :g:`DAC <0419.g2:< D A C )>` is a right angle, the square on :g:`DC <0419.g2:/ D C />` is equal to the squares on :g:`DA <0419.g2:/ D A />`, :g:`AC <0419.g2:/ A C />`, [I. 47]

i.e. to the squares on :g:`BA <0419.g2:/ B A />`, :g:`AC <0419.g2:/ A C />`.

But the square on :g:`BC <0419.g2:/ B C />` is greater than the squares on :g:`BA <0419.g2:/ B A />`, :g:`AC <0419.g2:/ A C />`; therefore the square on :g:`BC <0419.g2:/ B C />` is greater than the square on :g:`DC <0419.g2:/ D C />`.

Therefore :g:`BC <0419.g2:/ B C />` is greater than :g:`DC <0419.g2:/ D C />`.

Thus, in the triangles :g:`BAC <0419.g2:< B A C >>`, :g:`DAC <0419.g2:< D A C >>`, the two sides :g:`BA <0419.g2:/ B A />`, :g:`AC <0419.g2:/ A C />` are :eq:`equal <0419.g2:/ B A / = 0419.g2:/ D A />` to the two sides :g:`DA <0419.g2:/ D A />`, :g:`AC <0419.g2:/ A C />` respectively,

but the base :g:`BC <0419.g2:/ B C />` is greater than the base :g:`DC <0419.g2:/ D C />`.

.. folio:: 0420
   :page: 406

Therefore the angle :g:`BAC <0419.g2:< B A C )>` is greater than the angle :g:`DAC <0419.g2:< D A C )>`; :just:`[I. 25] <I.25>`

that is, the angle :g:`BAC <0419.g2:< B A C )>` is obtuse.
