Commentary on Proposition II.13
===============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition II.13 <II.13>`.

.. folio:: 0420
   :page: 406

As the text stands, this proposition is unequivocally enunciated of *acute-angled* triangles; and, as if to obviate any doubt as to whether the restriction was fully intended, the enunciation speaks of the rectangle contained by one of the sides containing the acute angle and the straight line intercepted *within* by the perpendicular towards the acute angle. On the other hand, it
is curious that it speaks of the square on the side subtending *the* acute angle; and again the *setting-out* begins "let :g:`ABC <0420.g1:< A B C >>` be an acute-angled triangle *having the angle at* :g:`B <0420.g1:< A B C )>` *acute*," though the last words have no point if all the angles of the triangle are necessarily acute.

.. _folio-i-0421:

.. folio:: 0421
   :page: 407

It was however very early noticed, not only by Isaacus Monachus, :person:`Campanus <campanus>`, Peletarius, :person:`Clavius <clavius>`, Commandinus and the rest, but by the Greek scholiast (:person:`Heiberg <heiberg>`, Vol. V. p. 253), that the relation between the sides of a triangle established by this theorem is true of the side opposite to, and the sides about, an acute angle respectively in any sort of triangle whether acute-angled, right-angled or obtuse-angled. The scholiast tries to explain away the word "acute-angled" in the enunciation: "Since in the definitions he calls acute-angled the triangle which has three acute angles, you must know that he does not mean that here, but calls all triangles acute-angled because all have an acute angle, one at least, if not all. The enunciation therefore is: 'In any triangle the square on the side subtending the acute angle is less than the squares on the sides containing the acute angle by twice the rectangle, etc.'"

We may judge too by Heron's enunciation of his "converse" of the proposition that he would have left the word "acute-angled" out of the enunciation. His converse is: *In any triangle in which the square on one of the sides is less than the squares on the other two sides, the angle contained by the latter sides is acute.*

If the triangle that we take is a right-angled triangle, and the perpendicular is drawn, not from the right angle, but from the acute angle *not* referred to in the enunciation, the proposition reduces to I. 47, and this case need not detain us.

.. picture:: 0421.g1.svg
   :alt: Diagram

The other cases can be proved, like II. 12, after the manner of I. 47.

Let us take first the case where all the angles of the triangle are acute.

.. picture:: 0421.g2.svg
   :alt: Diagram

As before, if we draw :g:`ALP <0421.g2:/ A L P />`, :g:`BMQ <0421.g2:/ B M Q />`, :g:`CNR <0421.g2:/ C N R />` perpendicular to :g:`BC <0421.g2:/ B C />`, :g:`CA <0421.g2:/ C A />`, :g:`AB <0421.g2:/ A B />` and meeting the further sides of the squares on :g:`BC <0421.g2:/ B C />`, :g:`CA <0421.g2:/ C A />`, :g:`AB <0421.g2:/ A B />` in :g:`P <0421.g2:P>`, :g:`Q <0421.g2:Q>`, :g:`R <0421.g2:R>`, and if we join :g:`KC <0421.g2:/ K C />`, :g:`AD <0421.g2:/ A D />`, we have

the triangles :g:`KBC <0421.g2:< K B C >>`, :g:`ABD <0421.g2:< A B D >>` :eq:`equal <0421.g2:< K B C > = 0421.g2:< A B D >>` in all respects,

and consequently the rectangles :g:`BP <0421.g2:< B L P D >>`, :g:`BR <0421.g2:< B N R K >>` :eq:`equal <0421.g2:< B L P D > = 0421.g2:< B N R K >>` to one another.

Similarly the rectangles :g:`CP <0421.g2:< L C E P >>`, :g:`CQ <0421.g2:< C M Q F >>` are :eq:`equal <0421.g2:< L C E P > = 0421.g2:< C M Q F >>` to one another.

.. _folio-i-0422:

.. folio:: 0422
   :page: 408

Next, by joining :g:`BG <0421.g2:/ B G />`, :g:`CH <0421.g2:/ C H />`, we prove in like manner that the rectangles :g:`AR <0421.g2:< A N R H >>`, :g:`AQ <0421.g2:< A M Q G >>` are :eq:`equal <0421.g2:< A N R H > = 0421.g2:< A M Q G >>`.

Now the square on :g:`BC <0421.g2:/ B C />` is equal to the sum of the rectangles :g:`BP <0421.g2:< B L P D >>`, :g:`CP <0421.g2:< L C E P >>`,

i.e. to the sum of the rectangles :g:`BR <0421.g2:< B N R K >>`, :g:`CQ <0421.g2:< C M Q F >>`,

i.e. to the sum of the squares :g:`BH <0421.g2:< A B K H >>`, :g:`CG <0421.g2:< C A G F >>` diminished by the rectangles :g:`AR <0421.g2:< A N R H >>`, :g:`AQ <0421.g2:< A M Q G >>`.

But the rectangles :g:`AR <0421.g2:< A N R H >>`, :g:`AQ <0421.g2:< A M Q G >>` are :eq:`equal <0421.g2:< A N R H > = 0421.g2:< A M Q G >>`, and they are respectively the rectangles contained by :g:`BA <0421.g2:/ B A />`, :g:`AN <0421.g2:/ A N />` and by :g:`CA <0421.g2:/ C A />`, :g:`AM <0421.g2:/ A M />`.

Therefore the square on :g:`BC <0421.g2:/ B C />` is less than the squares on :g:`BA <0421.g2:/ B A />`, :g:`AC <0421.g2:/ A C />` by twice the rectangle :g:`BA <0421.g2:/ B A />`, :g:`AN <0421.g2:/ A N />` or :g:`CA <0421.g2:/ C A />`, :g:`AM <0421.g2:/ A M />`.

Next suppose that we have to prove the theorem in the case where the triangle has an obtuse angle at :g:`A <0422.g1:< B A C )>`.

Take :g:`B <0422.g1:< A B C )>` as the acute angle under consideration, so that :g:`AC <0422.g1:/ A C />` is the side opposite to it.

.. picture:: 0422.g1.svg
   :alt: Diagram

Now the square on :g:`CA <0422.g1:/ C A />` is equal to the difference of the rectangles :g:`CQ <0422.g1:< C M Q F >>`, :g:`AQ <0422.g1:< A M Q G >>`,

i.e. to the difference between :g:`CP <0422.g1:< L C E P >>` and :g:`AQ <0422.g1:< A M Q G >>`,

i.e. to the difference between the square :g:`BE <0422.g1:< B C E D >>` and the sum of the rectangles :g:`BP <0422.g1:< B L P D >>`, :g:`AQ <0422.g1:< A M Q G >>`,

i.e. to the difference between the square :g:`BE <0422.g1:< B C E D >>` and the sum of the rectangles :g:`BP <0422.g1:< B L P D >>`, :g:`AR <0422.g1:< A N R H >>`,

i.e. to the difference between the sum of the squares :g:`BE <0422.g1:< B C E D >>`, :g:`BH <0422.g1:< A B K H >>` and the sum of the rectangles :g:`BP <0422.g1:< B L P D >>`, :g:`BR <0422.g1:< B N R K >>`

(since :g:`AR <0422.g1:< A N R H >>` is the difference between :g:`BR <0422.g1:< B N R K >>` and :g:`BH <0422.g1:< A B K H >>`).

But :g:`BP <0422.g1:< B L P D >>`, :g:`BR <0422.g1:< B N R K >>` are :eq:`equal <0422.g1:< B L P D > = 0422.g1:< B N R K >>`, and they are respectively the rectangles :g:`CB <0422.g1:/ C B />`, :g:`BL <0422.g1:/ B L />` and :g:`AB <0422.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`BN <0422.g1:/ B N />`.

Therefore the square on :g:`CA <0422.g1:/ C A />` is less than the squares on :g:`AB <0422.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`BC <0422.g1:/ B C />` by twice the rectangle :g:`CB <0422.g1:/ C B />`, :g:`BL <0422.g1:/ B L />` or :g:`AB <0422.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`BN <0422.g1:/ B N />`.

Heron's proof of his converse proposition (:person:`an-Nairīzī <an-nairizi>`, ed. :person:`Curtze <curtze>`, p. 110), which is also given by the Greek scholiast above quoted, is of course simple. For let :g:`ABC <0422.g2:< A B C >>` be a triangle in which the square on :g:`AC <0422.g2:/ A C />` is less than the squares on :g:`AB <0422.g2:/ A B />`, :g:`BC <0422.g2:/ B C />`.

.. picture:: 0422.g2.svg
   :alt: Diagram

Draw :g:`BD <0422.g2:/ B D />` at right angles to :g:`BC <0422.g2:/ B C />` and of length :eq:`equal <0422.g2:/ B D / = 0422.g2:/ B A />` to :g:`BA <0422.g2:/ B A />`.

Join :g:`DC <0422.g2:/ D C />`.

Then, since the angle :g:`CBD <0422.g2:< C B D )>` is right,
the square on :g:`DC <0422.g2:/ D C />` is equal to the squares on :g:`DB <0422.g2:/ D B />`, :g:`BC <0422.g2:/ B C />`,
i.e. to the squares on :g:`AB <0422.g2:/ A B />`, :g:`BC <0422.g2:/ B C />`. [I. 47]

But the square on :g:`AC <0422.g2:/ A C />` is less than the squares on :g:`AB <0422.g2:/ A B />`, :g:`BC <0422.g2:/ B C />`.

Therefore the square on :g:`AC <0422.g2:/ A C />` is less than the square on :g:`DC <0422.g2:/ D C />`.

Therefore :g:`AC <0422.g2:/ A C />` is less than :g:`DC <0422.g2:/ D C />`.

Hence in the two triangles :g:`DBC <0422.g2:< D B C >>`, :g:`ABC <0422.g2:< A B C >>` the sides about the angles :g:`DBC <0422.g2:< D B C )>`, :g:`ABC <0422.g2:< A B C )>` are respectively :eq:`equal <0422.g2:/ B D / = 0422.g2:/ B A />`, but the base :g:`DC <0422.g2:/ D C />` is greater than the base :g:`AC <0422.g2:/ A C />`.

.. _folio-i-0423:

.. folio:: 0423
   :page: 409

Therefore the angle :g:`DBC <0422.g2:< D B C )>` (a right angle) is greater than the angle :g:`ABC <0422.g2:< A B C )>` :just:`[I. 25] <I.25>`, which latter is therefore *acute*.

It may be noted, lastly, that II. 12, 13 are supplementary to I. 47 and complete the theory of the relations between the squares on the sides of *any* triangle, whether right-angled or not.
