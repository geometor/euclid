# Chapter II Summary and Outline: Euclid's Other Works

## Executive Summary

Sir Thomas L. Heath provides a critical catalogue and mathematical evaluation of all Euclidean treatises outside the *Elements*, drawing on ancient testimonies from Proclus, Pappus of Alexandria, Archimedes, Eutocius, and Arabic bio-bibliographies (notably Ibn an-Nadīm's *Kitāb al-Fihrist*).

Heath divides these works into lost treatises, extant mathematical and physical treatises, and spurious/apocryphal attributions:

1. **Lost Advanced Geometric Treatises**:
   - **The *Pseudaria* (Ψευδάρια, Book of Fallacies)**: An introductory pedagogical work designed as a "cathartic and exercise" to train students in recognizing geometric fallacies and paralogisms.
   - **The *Porisms* (Πορίσματα, 3 Books)**: A masterpiece of higher geometry contained in Pappus' *Treasury of Analysis* (τόπος ἀναλυόμενος), comprising 171 theorems and 38 lemmas. Heath explores the ancient definitions (distinguishing a porism from a theorem and a problem), Simson's celebrated restoration, Chasles' link to projective geometry and anharmonic ratios, and Zeuthen's conic hypothesis.
   - **The *Surface-Loci* (τόποι πρὸς ἐπιφανείᾳ, 2 Books)**: Loci consisting of surfaces (cylinders, cones, and surfaces of revolution of the second degree). Preserves Pappus' lemma containing the first complete ancient proof of the general **focus-and-directrix property of conics**.
   - **The *Conics* (4 Books)**: Euclid's systematization of conic sections connecting the work of Aristaeus (*Solid Loci*) with the subsequent monumental synthesis of Apollonius of Perga.

2. **Extant Works**:
   - **The *Data* (Δεδομένα)**: Supplementary to the planimetric books of the *Elements*, serving as an elementary manual of geometric analysis and the geometric solution of quadratic equations.
   - **The *On Divisions (of Figures)* (περὶ διαιρέσεων βιβλίον)**: Lost in Greek; recovered via Woepcke's 1851 translation of a Paris Arabic manuscript (emended by Thābit ibn Qurra) and John Dee / Commandino's 1570 Latin edition of Muḥammad al-Baghdādī; comprehensively reconstructed by R. C. Archibald.
   - **The *Phaenomena* (Φαινόμενα)**: Astronomical application of spherical geometry (*Sphaerica*), resting on Autolycus of Pitane and Eudoxus of Cnidus.
   - **The *Optics* (Ὀπτικά)**: Genuine Euclidean treatise on direct vision and perspective (distinguished from the spurious *Catoptrica* likely due to Theon of Alexandria).
   - **The *Elements of Music* (αἱ κατὰ μουσικὴν στοιχειώσεις)**: Containing the mathematical *Sectio Canonis* (κατατομὴ κανόνος, Pythagorean intervals), while the *Harmonic Introduction* (εἰσαγωγὴ ἁρμονική) is identified as the work of Aristoxenus' pupil Cleonides.

3. **Arabic Bibliographical Records**:
   - The *Fihrist* catalogue analyzed by H. Suter, documenting Arabic knowledge of the Euclidean corpus and rejecting mechanical tracts such as *De levi et ponderoso* as Hellenistic interpolations postdating Archimedes.

## Detailed Outline

### § 1. The *Pseudaria* (Book of Fallacies) (pp. 7–8)
- Proclus' characterization (*ed. Friedlein*, p. 70): cathartic training in the discovery of paralogisms.
- Elementary scope and pedagogical function alongside the *Elements*.
- Alexander of Aphrodisias' reference in his commentary on Aristotle's *Sophistici Elenchi*.

### § 2. The *Data* (pp. 8)
- Role in the *Treasury of Analysis* (Pappus VII, p. 638).
- Conceptual framework: proving what properties become *given* (in magnitude, species, or position) under given conditions.
- Connection with the geometric solution of quadratic equations.
- Modern editions: Heiberg & Menge (Teubner Vol. VI), Marinus' commentary.

### § 3. *On Divisions of Figures* (pp. 8–10)
- Proclus' notice: dividing figures into like (e.g. triangles into triangles) and unlike figures (e.g. triangle into triangle and quadrilateral).
- Transmission history:
  - John Dee's discovery, Commandino's 1570 edition (*Machometus Bagdadinus* / Abū Bakr Muḥammad ibn 'Abd al-Bāqī al-Baghdādī).
  - Franz Woepcke's discovery of the Paris Arabic MS (1851), containing 36 propositions.
  - Restoration and commentary by R. C. Archibald (1915) incorporating Leonardo of Pisa's *Practica geometriae* (1220).

### § 4. The *Porisms* (pp. 10–15)
- Significance and extensive historiography (Robert Simson, Michel Chasles, J. L. Heiberg, Gino Loria).
- Complete extract of Pappus' preface to the *Porisms* (*Collection* VII, pp. 648–660):
  - Nature intermediate between theorems and problems.
  - Ancient vs. later definitions: "that which falls short of a locus-theorem in respect of its hypothesis."
  - Enunciation of the grand ten-proposition porism (the complete quadrangle / collinearity theorem).
  - Generalization to $n$ straight lines (Simson's theorem).
  - List of 29 classes of things sought across 3 books (171 theorems, 38 lemmas).
- Proclus' distinction: *corollary* (incidental bonus) vs. *Euclidean porism* (seeking and exhibiting an existing entity, e.g. Prop. III.1).
- Modern mathematical interpretations: projective geometry, transversals, anharmonic ratios (Chasles), and Zeuthen's conic-locus hypothesis.

### § 5. The *Surface-Loci* (pp. 15–16)
- Placement in the *Treasury of Analysis* (2 books).
- Conjectured contents: cones, cylinders, and quadric surfaces of revolution (Chasles).
- Pappus' two lemmas:
  - Lemma 1: Elliptical parallel sections of cylinders and cones (Tannery's restoration).
  - Lemma 2: First complete ancient enunciation and demonstration of the **focus-and-directrix property of conics**.

### § 6. The *Conics* (pp. 16)
- Four books of Euclid completed and extended to eight by Apollonius.
- Relation to Aristaeus' *Solid Loci*.
- Archimedes' references to the "Elements of Conics."
- Early terminology: sections of right-angled, acute-angled, and obtuse-angled cones.

### § 7. The *Phaenomena* (pp. 16–17)
- Extant astronomical work on spherical geometry.
- Textual recensions: MS Vindobonensis philos. Gr. 103 vs. Gregory's text; Menge's edition (Teubner Vol. VIII).
- Historical dependence on Autolycus (*On the Moving Sphere*) and Eudoxus.

### § 8. The *Optics* and *Catoptrica* (pp. 17)
- Euclid's genuine *Optics* (Heiberg Teubner Vol. VII) vs Theon's recension.
- Spuriousness of the *Catoptrica*.

### § 9. The *Elements of Music* and the Arabic Corpus (pp. 17–18)
- *Sectio Canonis* (κατατομὴ κανόνος): Pythagorean interval theory, quoted by Porphyry.
- *Harmonic Introduction* (εἰσαγωγὴ ἁρμονική): attributed correctly to Cleonides.
- Analysis of Ibn an-Nadīm's *Fihrist* (Suter's edition):
  - *Data*, *Phaenomena*, *Divisions* (emended by Thābit), *Porisms*, *Canon*.
  - Spurious mechanical tracts: *De levi et ponderoso* (Basel 1537).
