Commentary on Proposition III.2
===============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition III.2 <III.2>`.

.. folio:: 0015
   :page: 9

The :term:`*reductio ad absurdum* <reductio-ad-absurdum>` form of proof is not really necessary in this case, and it has the additional disadvantage that it requires the destruction of two hypotheses, namely that the chord is (1) outside, (2) on the circle. To prove the proposition directly, we have only to show that, if $E$ be any point on the straight line :g:`$AB$ <0015.g1:/ A B />` between :g:`$A$ <0015.g1:A>` and :g:`$B$ <0015.g1:B>`, :g:`$DE$ <0015.g1:/ D E />` is less than the radius of the circle. This may be done by the method shown above, under I. 24, for proving what is assumed in that proposition, namely that, in the figure of the proposition, :g:`$F$ <0015.g1:F>` falls below :g:`$EG$ <0015.g1:/ E G />` if :g:`$DE$ <0015.g1:/ D E />` is not greater than :g:`$DF$ <0015.g1:/ D F />`. The assumption amounts to the following proposition, which :person:`De Morgan <de-morgan>` would make to precede I. 24: "Every straight line drawn from the vertex of a triangle to the base is less than the greater of the two sides, or than either if they be equal." The case here is that in which the two sides are equal; and, since the angle :g:`$DAB$ <0015.g1:< D A B )>` is equal to the angle :g:`$DBA$ <0015.g1:< D B A )>`, while the exterior angle :g:`$DEA$ <0015.g1:< D E A )>` is greater than the interior and opposite angle :g:`$DBA$ <0015.g1:< D B A )>`, it follows that the angle :g:`$DEA$ <0015.g1:< D E A )>` is greater than the angle :g:`$DAE$ <0015.g1:< D A E )>`, whence :g:`$DE$ <0015.g1:/ D E />` must be less than :g:`$DA$ <0015.g1:/ D A />` or :g:`$DB$ <0015.g1:/ D B />`.

.. picture:: 0015.g1.svg
   :alt: Direct proof of Proposition 2

:person:`Camerer <camerer>` points out that we may add to this proposition the further statement that all points on :g:`$AB$ <0015.g1:[ A B ]>` *produced* in either direction are outside the circle. This follows from the proposition (also proved by means of the theorems that the exterior angle of a triangle is greater than either of the interior and opposite angles and that the greater angle is subtended by the greater side) which :person:`De Morgan <de-morgan>` proposes to introduce after I. 21, namely,
"The perpendicular is the shortest straight line that can be drawn from a
given point to a given straight line, and of others that which is nearer to the perpendicular is less than the more remote, and the converse; also not more than two equal straight lines can be drawn from the point to the line, one on each side of the perpendicular."

.. _folio-ii-0016:

.. folio:: 0016
   :page: 10

The fact that not more than two equal straight lines can be drawn from a given point to a given straight line not passing through it is proved by :person:`Proclus <proclus>` on I. 16 (see the note to that proposition) and can alternatively be proved by means of I. 7, as shown above in the note on I. 12. It follows that

.. assertion::

   *A straight line cannot cut a circle in more than two points*

a proposition which :person:`De Morgan <de-morgan>` would introduce here after III. 2. The proof given does not apply to a straight line *passing through the centre*; but that such a line only cuts the circle in two points is self-evident.
