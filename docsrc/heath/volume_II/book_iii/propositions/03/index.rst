PROPOSITION 3.
==============

.. enunciation::

   If in a circle a straight line through the centre bisect a straight line not through the centre, it also cuts it at right angles; and if it cut it at right angles, it also bisects it.

Let :g:`$ABC$ <0016.g1:( A B C )>` be a circle, and in it let a straight line :g:`$CD$ <0016.g1:/ C D />` through the centre :eq:`bisect <0016.g1:/ A F / = 0016.g1:/ F B />` a straight line :g:`$AB$ <0016.g1:/ A B />` not through the centre at the point :g:`$F$ <0016.g1:F>`;

I say that it also cuts it at :eq:`right angles <0016.g1:< A F E ) = 0016.g1:< B F E )>`.

.. picture:: 0016.g1.svg
   :alt: Proposition 3

For let the centre of the circle :g:`$ABC$ <0016.g1:( A B C )>` be taken, and let it be :g:`$E$ <0016.g1:E>`; let :g:`$EA$ <0016.g1:/ E A />`, :g:`$EB$ <0016.g1:/ E B />` be joined.

Then, since :g:`$AF$ <0016.g1:/ A F />` is :eq:`equal <0016.g1:/ A F / = 0016.g1:/ F B />` to :g:`$FB$ <0016.g1:/ F B />`, and :g:`$FE$ <0016.g1:/ F E />` is common,

.. subclause::

   two sides are :eq:`equal <0016.g1:/ A F / = 0016.g1:/ F B />` to two sides;

and the base :g:`$EA$ <0016.g1:/ E A />` is :eq:`equal <0016.g1:/ E A / = 0016.g1:/ E B />` to the base :g:`$EB$ <0016.g1:/ E B />`;

.. assertion:: [I. 8] <I.8>

   therefore the angle :g:`$AFE$ <0016.g1:< A F E )>` is :eq:`equal <0016.g1:< A F E ) = 0016.g1:< B F E )>` to the angle :g:`$BFE$ <0016.g1:< B F E )>`.

But, when a straight line set up on a straight line makes the adjacent angles equal to one another, each of the equal angles is right; :just:`[I. Def. 10] <I.def.10>`

.. assertion::

   therefore each of the angles :g:`$AFE$ <0016.g1:< A F E )>`, :g:`$BFE$ <0016.g1:< B F E )>` is :eq:`right <0016.g1:< A F E ) = 0016.g1:< B F E )>`.

.. assertion::

   Therefore :g:`$CD$ <0016.g1:/ C D />`, which is through the centre, and :eq:`bisects <0016.g1:/ A F / = 0016.g1:/ F B />` :g:`$AB$ <0016.g1:/ A B />` which is not through the centre, also cuts it at :eq:`right angles <0016.g1:< A F E ) = 0016.g1:< B F E )>`.

Again, let :g:`$CD$ <0016.g1:/ C D />` cut :g:`$AB$ <0016.g1:/ A B />` at :eq:`right angles <0016.g1:< A F E ) = 0016.g1:< B F E )>`;

I say that it also :eq:`bisects <0016.g1:/ A F / = 0016.g1:/ F B />` it, that is, that :g:`$AF$ <0016.g1:/ A F />` is :eq:`equal <0016.g1:/ A F / = 0016.g1:/ F B />` to :g:`$FB$ <0016.g1:/ F B />`.

.. _folio-ii-0017:

.. folio:: 0017
   :page: 11

For, with the same construction,\ [#f1]_

.. subclause::

   since :g:`$EA$ <0016.g1:/ E A />` is :eq:`equal <0016.g1:/ E A / = 0016.g1:/ E B />` to :g:`$EB$ <0016.g1:/ E B />`,

the angle :g:`$EAF$ <0016.g1:< E A F )>` is also :eq:`equal <0016.g1:< E A F ) = 0016.g1:< E B F )>` to the angle :g:`$EBF$ <0016.g1:< E B F )>`. :just:`[I. 5] <I.5>`

But the right angle :g:`$AFE$ <0016.g1:< A F E )>` is :eq:`equal <0016.g1:< A F E ) = 0016.g1:< B F E )>` to the right angle :g:`$BFE$ <0016.g1:< B F E )>`, therefore :g:`$EAF$ <0016.g1:< E A F >>`, :g:`$EBF$ <0016.g1:< E B F >>` are two triangles having two angles :eq:`equal <0016.g1:< E A F ) = 0016.g1:< E B F ); 0016.g1:< A F E ) = 0016.g1:< B F E )>` to two angles and one side equal to one side, namely :g:`$EF$ <0016.g1:/ E F />`, which is common to them, and subtends one of the :eq:`equal <0016.g1:< E A F ) = 0016.g1:< E B F )>` angles; therefore they will also have the remaining sides :eq:`equal <0016.g1:/ A F / = 0016.g1:/ F B /; 0016.g1:/ E A / = 0016.g1:/ E B />` to the remaining sides; :just:`[I. 26] <I.26>`

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$AF$ <0016.g1:/ A F />` is :eq:`equal <0016.g1:/ A F / = 0016.g1:/ F B />` to :g:`$FB$ <0016.g1:/ F B />`.

.. qed::

   Therefore etc. :just:`Q. E. D.`

.. [#f1] **with the same construction**, τῶν αὐτῶν κατασκευασθέντων.
