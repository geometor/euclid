PROPOSITION 14.
===============

.. enunciation::

   In a circle equal straight lines are equally distant from the centre, and those which are equally distant from the centre are equal to one another.

Let :g:`$ABDC$ <0041.g1:( A B D C )>` be a circle, and let :g:`$AB$ <0041.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$CD$ <0041.g1:/ C D />` be equal straight
lines in it;
I say that :g:`$AB$ <0041.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$CD$ <0041.g1:/ C D />` are equally distant from the centre.

For let the centre of the circle :g:`$ABDC$ <0041.g1:( A B D C )>` be taken :just:`[III. 1] <III.1>`,
and let it be :g:`$E$ <0041.g1:E>`; from :g:`$E$ <0041.g1:E>` let :g:`$EF$ <0041.g1:/ E F />`, :g:`$EG$ <0041.g1:/ E G />` be drawn perpendicular
to :g:`$AB$ <0041.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$CD$ <0041.g1:/ C D />`, and let :g:`$AE$ <0041.g1:/ A E />`, :g:`$EC$ <0041.g1:/ E C />` be joined.

.. _folio-ii-0041:

.. folio:: 0041
   :page: 35

.. picture:: 0041.g1.svg
   :alt: Proposition 14 diagram

Then, since a straight line :g:`$EF$ <0041.g1:/ E F />` through
the centre cuts a straight line :g:`$AB$ <0041.g1:/ A B />` not through
the centre at right angles, it also bisects it. :just:`[III. 3] <III.3>`

.. assertion::

   Therefore :g:`$AF$ <0041.g1:/ A F />` is equal to :g:`$FB$ <0041.g1:/ F B />`; therefore :g:`$AB$ <0041.g1:/ A B />` is double of :g:`$AF$ <0041.g1:/ A F />`.

For the same reason

.. assertion::

   :g:`$CD$ <0041.g1:/ C D />` is also double of :g:`$CG$ <0041.g1:/ C G />`;

and :g:`$AB$ <0041.g1:/ A B />` is equal to :g:`$CD$ <0041.g1:/ C D />`;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$AF$ <0041.g1:/ A F />` is also equal to :g:`$CG$ <0041.g1:/ C G />`.

And, since :g:`$AE$ <0041.g1:/ A E />` is equal to :g:`$EC$ <0041.g1:/ E C />`,

.. assertion::

   the square on :g:`$AE$ <0041.g1:/ A E />` is also equal to the square on :g:`$EC$ <0041.g1:/ E C />`.

But the squares on :g:`$AF$ <0041.g1:/ A F />`, :g:`$EF$ <0041.g1:/ E F />` are equal to the square on :g:`$AE$ <0041.g1:/ A E />`,
for the angle at :g:`$F$ <0041.g1:< E F A )>` is right;
and the squares on :g:`$EG$ <0041.g1:/ E G />`, :g:`$GC$ <0041.g1:/ G C />` are equal to the square on :g:`$EC$ <0041.g1:/ E C />`,
for the angle at :g:`$G$ <0041.g1:< E G C )>` is right; :just:`[I. 47] <I.47>`

.. assertion::

   therefore the squares on :g:`$AF$ <0041.g1:/ A F />`, :g:`$FE$ <0041.g1:/ F E />` are equal to the squares on :g:`$CG$ <0041.g1:/ C G />`, :g:`$GE$ <0041.g1:/ G E />`,

of which the square on :g:`$AF$ <0041.g1:/ A F />` is equal to the square on :g:`$CG$ <0041.g1:/ C G />`,
for :g:`$AF$ <0041.g1:/ A F />` is equal to :g:`$CG$ <0041.g1:/ C G />`;

.. assertion::

   therefore the square on :g:`$FE$ <0041.g1:/ F E />` which remains is equal to the square on :g:`$EG$ <0041.g1:/ E G />`,

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$EF$ <0041.g1:/ E F />` is equal to :g:`$EG$ <0041.g1:/ E G />`.

But in a circle straight lines are said to be equally distant
from the centre when the perpendiculars drawn to them from
the centre are equal; :just:`[III. Def. 4] <III.def.4>`

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$AB$ <0041.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$CD$ <0041.g1:/ C D />` are equally distant from the centre.

Next, let the straight lines :g:`$AB$ <0041.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$CD$ <0041.g1:/ C D />` be equally distant
from the centre; that is, let :g:`$EF$ <0041.g1:/ E F />` be equal to :g:`$EG$ <0041.g1:/ E G />`.

I say that :g:`$AB$ <0041.g1:/ A B />` is also equal to :g:`$CD$ <0041.g1:/ C D />`.

For, with the same construction, we can prove, similarly,
that :g:`$AB$ <0041.g1:/ A B />` is double of :g:`$AF$ <0041.g1:/ A F />`, and :g:`$CD$ <0041.g1:/ C D />` of :g:`$CG$ <0041.g1:/ C G />`.

And, since :g:`$AE$ <0041.g1:/ A E />` is equal to :g:`$CE$ <0041.g1:/ C E />`,

.. assertion::

   the square on :g:`$AE$ <0041.g1:/ A E />` is equal to the square on :g:`$CE$ <0041.g1:/ C E />`.

But the squares on :g:`$EF$ <0041.g1:/ E F />`, :g:`$FA$ <0041.g1:/ F A />` are equal to the square on :g:`$AE$ <0041.g1:/ A E />`,
and the squares on :g:`$EG$ <0041.g1:/ E G />`, :g:`$GC$ <0041.g1:/ G C />` equal to the square on :g:`$CE$ <0041.g1:/ C E />`. :just:`[I. 47] <I.47>`
Therefore the squares on :g:`$EF$ <0041.g1:/ E F />`, :g:`$FA$ <0041.g1:/ F A />` are equal to the
squares on :g:`$EG$ <0041.g1:/ E G />`, :g:`$GC$ <0041.g1:/ G C />`,
of which the square on :g:`$EF$ <0041.g1:/ E F />` is equal to the square on :g:`$EG$ <0041.g1:/ E G />`,
for :g:`$EF$ <0041.g1:/ E F />` is equal to :g:`$EG$ <0041.g1:/ E G />`;
therefore the square on :g:`$AF$ <0041.g1:/ A F />` which remains is equal to the
square on :g:`$CG$ <0041.g1:/ C G />`;

.. _folio-ii-0042:

.. folio:: 0042
   :page: 36

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$AF$ <0041.g1:/ A F />` is equal to :g:`$CG$ <0041.g1:/ C G />`.

And :g:`$AB$ <0041.g1:/ A B />` is double of :g:`$AF$ <0041.g1:/ A F />`, and :g:`$CD$ <0041.g1:/ C D />` double of :g:`$CG$ <0041.g1:/ C G />`;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$AB$ <0041.g1:/ A B />` is equal to :g:`$CD$ <0041.g1:/ C D />`.

.. qed::

   Therefore etc. :just:`Q. E. D.`
