PROPOSITION 21.
===============

.. enunciation::

   In a circle the angles in the same segment are equal to one another.

Let :g:`$ABCD$ <0055.g1:( A B C D )>` be a circle, and let the angles :g:`$BAD$ <0055.g1:< B A D )>`, :g:`$BED$ <0055.g1:< B E D )>`
be angles in the same segment :g:`$BAED$ <0055.g1:< B A E D )>`;
I say that the angles :g:`$BAD$ <0055.g1:< B A D )>`, :g:`$BED$ <0055.g1:< B E D )>` are
:eq:`equal <0055.g1:< B A D ) = 0055.g1:< B E D )>` to one another.

.. picture:: 0055.g1.svg
   :alt: Diagram for Proposition 21

For let the centre of the circle
:g:`$ABCD$ <0055.g1:( A B C D )>` be taken, and let it be :g:`$F$ <0055.g1:F>`; let
:g:`$BF$ <0055.g1:/ B F />`, :g:`$FD$ <0055.g1:/ F D />` be joined.

Now, since the angle :g:`$BFD$ <0055.g1:< B F D )>` is at
the centre,
and the angle :g:`$BAD$ <0055.g1:< B A D )>` at the circumference
,

.. subclause::

   and they have the same circumference :g:`$BCD$ <0055.g1:( B C D )>` as base,

therefore the angle :g:`$BFD$ <0055.g1:< B F D )>` is double of the angle :g:`$BAD$ <0055.g1:< B A D )>`. :just:`[III. 20] <III.20>`

For the same reason

.. assertion::

   the angle :g:`$BFD$ <0055.g1:< B F D )>` is also double of the angle :g:`$BED$ <0055.g1:< B E D )>`; therefore the angle :g:`$BAD$ <0055.g1:< B A D )>` is :eq:`equal <0055.g1:< B A D ) = 0055.g1:< B E D )>` to the angle :g:`$BED$ <0055.g1:< B E D )>`.

.. qed::

   Therefore etc. :just:`Q. E. D.`
