PROPOSITION 24.
===============

.. enunciation::

   Similar segments of circles on equal straight lines are equal to one another.

For let :g:`$AEB$ <0060.g1:( A E B )>`, :g:`$CFD$ <0060.g1:( C F D )>` be similar segments of circles on
equal straight lines :g:`$AB$ <0060.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$CD$ <0060.g1:/ C D />`;
I say that the segment :g:`$AEB$ <0060.g1:( A E B )>` is equal to the segment :g:`$CFD$ <0060.g1:( C F D )>`.

For, if the segment :g:`$AEB$ <0060.g1:( A E B )>` be applied to :g:`$CFD$ <0060.g1:( C F D )>`, and if the
point :g:`$A$ <0060.g1:A>` be placed on :g:`$C$ <0060.g1:C>` and the straight line :g:`$AB$ <0060.g1:/ A B />` on :g:`$CD$ <0060.g1:/ C D />`,
the point :g:`$B$ <0060.g1:B>` will also coincide with the point :g:`$D$ <0060.g1:D>`, because
:g:`$AB$ <0060.g1:/ A B />` is equal to :g:`$CD$ <0060.g1:/ C D />`;

.. _folio-ii-0060:

.. folio:: 0060
   :page: 54

.. subclause::

   and, :g:`$AB$ <0060.g1:/ A B />` coinciding with :g:`$CD$ <0060.g1:/ C D />`,

the segment :g:`$AEB$ <0060.g1:( A E B )>` will also coincide with :g:`$CFD$ <0060.g1:( C F D )>`.

.. picture:: 0060.g1.svg
   :alt: Diagram for Proposition 24

For, if the straight line :g:`$AB$ <0060.g1:/ A B />` coincide with :g:`$CD$ <0060.g1:/ C D />` but the
segment :g:`$AEB$ <0060.g1:( A E B )>` do not coincide with :g:`$CFD$ <0060.g1:( C F D )>`,
it will either fall within it, or outside it;
or it will fall awry,\ [#f1]_ as :g:`$CGD$ <0060.g1:( C G D )>`, and a circle cuts a circle at more
points than two: which is impossible. :just:`[III. 10] <III.10>`

Therefore, if the straight line :g:`$AB$ <0060.g1:/ A B />` be applied to :g:`$CD$ <0060.g1:/ C D />`, the
segment :g:`$AEB$ <0060.g1:( A E B )>` will not fail to coincide with :g:`$CFD$ <0060.g1:( C F D )>` also;

.. assertion::

   therefore it will coincide with it and will be equal to it.

.. qed::

   Therefore etc. :just:`Q. E. D.`

.. [#f1] **fall awry**, $\text{παραλλάξει}$, the same word as used in the like case in I. 8. The word implies that the applied figure will partly fall short of, and partly overlap, the figure to which it is applied.
