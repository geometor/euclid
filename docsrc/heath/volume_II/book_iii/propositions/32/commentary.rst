Commentary on Proposition III.32
================================

Back to :ref:`Proposition III.32 <III.32>`.

.. folio:: 0072
   :page: 66

The converse of this theorem is true, namely that, *If a straight line drawn through one extremity of a chord of a circle make with that chord angles equal respectively to the angles in the alternate segments of the circle, the straight line so drawn touches the circle.*
This can, as :person:`Camerer <camerer>` and :person:`Todhunter <todhunter>` remark, be proved indirectly; or we may prove it, with :person:`Clavius <clavius>`, directly. Let :g:`$BD$ <0072.g1:/ B D />` be the given chord, and let :g:`$EF$ <0072.g1:/ E F />` be drawn through :g:`$B$ <0072.g1:B>` so that it makes with :g:`$BD$ <0072.g1:/ B D />` angles equal to the angles in the alternate segments of the circle respectively.

.. _folio-ii-0073:

.. folio:: 0073
   :page: 67

Let :g:`$BA$ <0072.g1:/ B A />` be the diameter through :g:`$B$ <0072.g1:B>`, and let :g:`$C$ <0072.g1:C>` be any point on the circumference of the segment :g:`$DCB$ <0072.g1:( D C B )>` which does not contain :g:`$A$ <0072.g1:A>`. Join :g:`$AD$ <0072.g1:/ A D />`, :g:`$DC$ <0072.g1:/ D C />`, :g:`$CB$ <0072.g1:/ C B />`.

Then, since, by hypothesis, the angle :g:`$FBD$ <0072.g1:< F B D )>` is equal to the angle :g:`$BAD$ <0072.g1:< B A D )>`,
let the angle :g:`$ABD$ <0072.g1:< A B D )>` be added to both;

.. assertion::

   therefore the angle :g:`$ABF$ <0072.g1:< A B F )>` is equal to the angles :g:`$ABD$ <0072.g1:< A B D )>`, :g:`$BAD$ <0072.g1:< B A D )>`.

But the angle :g:`$BDA$ <0072.g1:< B D A )>`, being the angle in a semicircle, is a right angle;
therefore the remaining angles :g:`$ABD$ <0072.g1:< A B D )>`, :g:`$BAD$ <0072.g1:< B A D )>` in the triangle :g:`$ABD$ <0072.g1:< A B D >>` are
equal to a right angle.

Therefore the angle :g:`$ABF$ <0072.g1:< A B F )>` is right;
hence, since :g:`$BA$ <0072.g1:/ B A />` is the diameter through :g:`$B$ <0072.g1:B>`,

.. assertion:: [III. 16, Por.]

   :g:`$EF$ <0072.g1:/ E F />` touches the circle at :g:`$B$ <0072.g1:B>`.

:person:`Pappus <pappus>` assumes in one place (IV. p. 196) the consequence of this proposition that, *If two circles touch, any straight line drawn through the point of contact and terminated by both circles cuts off segments in each which are respectively similar.* :person:`Pappus <pappus>` also shows how to prove this (VII. p. 826) by drawing the common tangent at the point of contact and using this proposition, III. 32.
