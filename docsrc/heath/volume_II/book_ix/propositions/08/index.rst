PROPOSITION 8.
==============

.. enunciation::

   If as many numbers as we please beginning from an unit be in continued proportion, the third from the unit will be square, as will also those which successively leave out one; the fourth will be cube, as will also all those which leave out two; and the seventh will be at once cube and square, as will also those which leave out five.

Let there be as many numbers as we please, :g:`$A$ <0396.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0396.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0396.g1:C>`, :g:`$D$ <0396.g1:D>`, :g:`$E$ <0396.g1:E>`, :g:`$F$ <0396.g1:F>`, beginning from an unit and in continued proportion;

I say that :g:`$B$ <0396.g1:B>`, the third from the unit, is square, as are also all those which leave out one; :g:`$C$ <0396.g1:C>`, the fourth, is cube, as are also all those which leave out two; and :g:`$F$ <0396.g1:F>`, the seventh, is at once cube and square, as are also all those which leave out five.

.. picture:: 0396.g1.svg
   :alt: Proposition 8 Diagram

.. subclause::

   For since, as the unit is to :g:`$A$ <0396.g1:A>`, so is :g:`$A$ <0396.g1:A>` to :g:`$B$ <0396.g1:B>`,

.. assertion::

   therefore the unit measures the number :g:`$A$ <0396.g1:A>` the same number of times that :g:`$A$ <0396.g1:A>` measures :g:`$B$ <0396.g1:B>`. :just:`[VII. Def. 20] <VII.def.20>`

.. subclause::

   But the unit measures the number :g:`$A$ <0396.g1:A>` according to the units in it;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$A$ <0396.g1:A>` also measures :g:`$B$ <0396.g1:B>` according to the units in :g:`$A$ <0396.g1:A>`.

.. assertion::

   Therefore :g:`$A$ <0396.g1:A>` by multiplying itself has made :g:`$B$ <0396.g1:B>`;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$B$ <0396.g1:B>` is square.

.. subclause::

   And, since :g:`$B$ <0396.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0396.g1:C>`, :g:`$D$ <0396.g1:D>` are in continued proportion, and :g:`$B$ <0396.g1:B>` is square,

.. assertion:: [VIII. 22] <VIII.22>

   therefore :g:`$D$ <0396.g1:D>` is also square.

.. _folio-ii-0397:

.. folio:: 0397
   :page: 391

.. subclause::

   For the same reason

.. assertion::

   :g:`$F$ <0396.g1:F>` is also square.

.. assertion::

   Similarly we can prove that all those which leave out one are square.

I say next that :g:`$C$ <0396.g1:C>`, the fourth from the unit, is cube, as are also all those which leave out two.

.. subclause::

   For since, as the unit is to :g:`$A$ <0396.g1:A>`, so is :g:`$B$ <0396.g1:B>` to :g:`$C$ <0396.g1:C>`,

.. assertion::

   therefore the unit measures the number :g:`$A$ <0396.g1:A>` the same number of times that :g:`$B$ <0396.g1:B>` measures :g:`$C$ <0396.g1:C>`.

.. subclause::

   But the unit measures the number :g:`$A$ <0396.g1:A>` according to the units in :g:`$A$ <0396.g1:A>`;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$B$ <0396.g1:B>` also measures :g:`$C$ <0396.g1:C>` according to the units in :g:`$A$ <0396.g1:A>`.

.. assertion::

   Therefore :g:`$A$ <0396.g1:A>` by multiplying :g:`$B$ <0396.g1:B>` has made :g:`$C$ <0396.g1:C>`.

.. subclause::

   Since then :g:`$A$ <0396.g1:A>` by multiplying itself has made :g:`$B$ <0396.g1:B>`, and by multiplying :g:`$B$ <0396.g1:B>` has made :g:`$C$ <0396.g1:C>`,

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$C$ <0396.g1:C>` is cube.

.. subclause::

   And, since :g:`$C$ <0396.g1:C>`, :g:`$D$ <0396.g1:D>`, :g:`$E$ <0396.g1:E>`, :g:`$F$ <0396.g1:F>` are in continued proportion, and :g:`$C$ <0396.g1:C>` is cube,

.. assertion:: [VIII. 23] <VIII.23>

   therefore :g:`$F$ <0396.g1:F>` is also cube.

.. subclause::

   But it was also proved square;

.. assertion::

   therefore the seventh from the unit is both cube and square.

.. assertion::

   Similarly we can prove that all the numbers which leave out five are also both cube and square.

.. qed::

   :just:`Q. E. D.`
