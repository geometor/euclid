Commentary on Proposition IX.11
===============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition IX.11 <IX.11>`.

.. folio:: 0402
   :page: 396

The proposition and the porism together assert that, if :math:`1, a, a_2, \dots a_n` be a geometrical progression, :math:`a_r` measures :math:`a_n` and gives the quotient :math:`a_{n-r}\ (r < n)`.

Euclid only proves that :math:`a_n = a \cdot a_{n-1}`, as follows.

.. math::

   1 : a = a_{n-1} : a_n.

Therefore :math:`1` measures :math:`a` the same number of times as :math:`a_{n-1}` measures :math:`a_n.` Hence :math:`1` measures :math:`a_{n-1}` the same number of times as :math:`a` measures :math:`a_n;` :just:`[VII. 15] <VII.15>`

that is,

.. math::

   a_n = a \cdot a_{n-1}.

We can supply the proof of the porism as follows.

.. math::

   \begin{aligned}
   1 : a &= a_r : a_{r+1}, \\
   a : a_2 &= a_{r+1} : a_{r+2}, \\
   &\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots \\
   a_{n-r-1} : a_{n-r} &= a_{n-1} : a_n,
   \end{aligned}

whence, :term:`*ex aequali* <ex-aequali>`,

.. math::

   1 : a_{n-r} = a_r : a_n.\text{ \hfill [VII. 14]}

It follows, by the same argument as before, that

.. math::

   a_n = a_r \cdot a_{n-r}.

With our notation, we have the theorem of indices that

.. math::

   a^{m+n} = a^m \cdot a^n.
