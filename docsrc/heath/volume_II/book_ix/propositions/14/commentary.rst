Commentary on Proposition IX.14
===============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition IX.14 <IX.14>`.

.. folio:: 0409
   :page: 403

In other words, a number can be resolved into prime factors in only one way.

Let :math:`a` be the least number measured by each of the prime numbers :math:`b, c, d, \dots k`.

If possible, suppose that :math:`a` has a prime factor :math:`p` different from :math:`b, c, d, \dots k`.

Let

.. math::

   a = p \cdot m.

Now :math:`b, c, d, \dots k`, measuring :math:`a`, must measure one of the two factors :math:`p, m`. :just:`[VII. 30] <VII.30>`

They do not, by hypothesis, measure :math:`p` ; therefore they must measure :math:`m`, a number less than :math:`a` : which is contrary to the hypothesis.

Therefore :math:`a` has no prime factors except :math:`b, c, d, \dots k`.
