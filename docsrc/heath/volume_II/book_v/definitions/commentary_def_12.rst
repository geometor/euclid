Commentary on Book V Definition 12
==================================

Back to :ref:`Book V Definitions <V.def>`.

**Ἐναλλὰξ λόγος ἐστὶ λῆψις τοῦ ἡγουμένου πρὸς τὸ ἡγούμενον καὶ τοῦ ἑπομένου πρὸς τὸ ἑπόμενον.**

We now come to a number of expressions for the transformation of ratios or proportions. The first is :term:`ἐναλλάξ <alternando>`, *alternately*, which would be better described with reference to a proportion of four terms than with reference to a ratio. But probably Euclid defined all the terms in Deff. 12—16 with reference to *ratios* because to define them with reference to proportions would look like assuming what ought to be proved, namely the legitimacy of the various transformations of proportions (cf. V. 16, 7 Por., 18, 17, 19 Por.). The word :term:`ἐναλλάξ <alternando>` is of course a common term which has no exclusive reference to mathematics. But this same use of it with reference to proportions already occurs in :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>`: :cite:`*Anal. post.* I. 5, 74 a 18 <aristotle:anal-post:i-5-74-a-18>`, καὶ τὸ :term:`ἀνάλογον <proportional>` ὅτι :term:`ἐναλλάξ <alternando>`, "and that a proportion (is true) alternately, or :term:`*alternando* <alternando>`." Used with :term:`λόγος <ratio>`, as here, the adverb :term:`ἐναλλάξ <alternando>` has the sense of an adjective, "alternate"; we have already had it similarly used of "alternate angles" (αἱ ἐναλλὰξ γωνίαι) in the theory of parallels.
