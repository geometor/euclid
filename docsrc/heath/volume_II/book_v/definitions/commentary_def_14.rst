Commentary on Book V Definition 14
==================================

Back to :ref:`Book V Definitions <V.def>`.

**Σύνθεσις λόγου ἐστὶ λῆψις τοῦ ἡγουμένου μετὰ τοῦ ἑπομένου ὡς ἑνὸς πρὸς αὐτὸ τὸ ἑπόμενον.**

The *composition* of a ratio is to be distinguished from the *compounding* of ratios and *compounded ratio* (:term:`συγκείμενος λόγος <compound-ratio>`) as explained above in the note
on Deff. 9, 10. The fact is that συντίθημι and what serves for the passive of it (σύγκειμαι) are used for *adding* as well as *compounding* in the sense of compounding ratios. In order to distinguish the two senses, I have always used the word :term:`*componendo* <componendo>` where the sense is that of this definition, though this requires a slight departure from the literal rendering of some passages. Thus the enunciation of V. 17 says, literally, "if magnitudes compounded be in proportion they will also be in proportion separated" (ἐὰν συγκείμενα :term:`μεγέθη <megethos>` :term:`ἀνάλογον <proportional>` ᾖ, καὶ διαιρεθέντα :term:`ἀνάλογον <proportional>` ἔσται). This practically means that, if :math:`A + B` is to :math:`B` as :math:`C + D` is to :math:`D`, then :math:`A` is to :math:`B` as :math:`C` is to :math:`D`. I have accordingly translated as follows: "if magnitudes be proportional :term:`*componendo* <componendo>`, they will also be proportional :term:`*separando* <separando>`." (It will be observed that :term:`*separando* <separando>`, a term explained in the next note, is here used, not relatively to the proportion :math:`A` is to :math:`B` as :math:`C` is to :math:`D`, but relatively to the proportion :term:`*componendo* <componendo>`, viz. :math:`A + B` is to :math:`B` as :math:`C + D` is to :math:`D`.) The corresponding term for :term:`*componendo* <componendo>` in the Greek mathematicians is :term:`συνθέντι <componendo>`, literally "to one who has compounded," i.e. "if we compound." (For this absolute use of the dative of the participle cf. :person:`Nicomachus <nicomachus>` I. 8, 9 ἀπὸ :term:`μονάδος <unit>`...κατὰ τὸν διπλάσιον :term:`λόγον <ratio>` προχωροῦντι μέχρις ἀπείρου, ὅσοι καὶ ἂν γένωνται, οὗτοι πάντες ἀρτιάκις ἄρτιοί εἰσιν. A very good instance from :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>` is :cite:`*Eth. Nic.* I. 5, 1097 b 12 <aristotle:eth-nic:i-5-1097-b-12>` ἐπεκτείνοντι γὰρ ἐπὶ τοὺς γονεῖς καὶ τοὺς ἀπογόνους καὶ τῶν φίλων τοὺς φίλους εἰς ἄπειρον πρόεισιν.) A variation for :term:`συνθέντι <componendo>` found in :person:`Archimedes <archimedes>` is :term:`κατὰ σύνθεσιν <componendo>`. Perhaps the more exclusive use of the form :term:`συνθέντι <componendo>` by geometers later than Euclid to denote the :term:`*composition of a ratio* <componendo>`, as compared with Euclid's more general use of :term:`σύνθεσις <synthesis>` and other parts of the verb συντίθημι or σύγκειμαι, may point to a desire to get rid of ambiguity of terms and to make the terminology of geometry more exact.
