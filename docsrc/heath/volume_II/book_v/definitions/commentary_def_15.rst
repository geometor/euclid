==================================

----


**Διαίρεσις λόγου ἐστὶ λῆψις τῆς ὑπεροχῆς, ᾗ ὑπερέχει τὸ ἡγούμενον τοῦ ἑπομένου, πρὸς αὐτὸ τὸ ἑπόμενον.**

As :term:`*composition of a ratio* <componendo>` means the transformation, e.g., of the ratio of :math:`A` to :math:`B` into the ratio of :math:`A + B` to :math:`B`, so the :term:`*separation of a ratio* <separando>` indicates the transformation of it into the ratio of :math:`A - B` to :math:`B`. Thus, as the new antecedent is in one case got by *adding* the original antecedent to the original consequent, so the antecedent in the other case is obtained by *subtracting* the original consequent from the original antecedent (it being assumed that the latter is greater than the former). Hence the literal translations of :term:`διαίρεσις λόγου <separando>`, "division of a ratio," and of :term:`διελόντι <separando>` (the corresponding term to :term:`συνθέντι <componendo>`) as :term:`*dividendo* <separando>`, scarcely give a sufficiently obvious explanation of the meaning. :person:`Heiberg <heiberg>` accordingly translates by "subtractio rationis," which again may be thought to depart too far from the Greek. Perhaps "separation" and :term:`*separando* <separando>` may serve as a compromise.
