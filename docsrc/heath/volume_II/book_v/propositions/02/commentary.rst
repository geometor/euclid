Commentary on Proposition V.2
=============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition V.2 <V.2>`.

.. folio:: 0146
   :page: 140

To find the corresponding formula for the result of this proposition, we may suppose :math:`a` to be the "second" magnitude and :math:`b` the "fourth." If now the "first" magnitude is :math:`ma`, the "third" is, by hypothesis, :math:`mb`; and, if the "fifth" magnitude is :math:`na`, the "sixth" is :math:`nb`. The proposition then asserts that :math:`ma + na` is the same multiple of :math:`a` that :math:`mb + nb` is of :math:`b`.

More generally, if :math:`pa`, :math:`qa \dots` and :math:`pb`, :math:`qb \dots` be any further equimultiples of :math:`a`, :math:`b` respectively, :math:`ma + na + pa + qa + \dots` is the same multiple of :math:`a` that :math:`mb + nb + pb + qb + \dots` is of :math:`b`. This extension is stated in :person:`Simson <simson>`'s corollary to V. 2 thus:

"From this it is plain that, if any number of magnitudes :g:`AB <0145.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`BG <0145.g1:/ B G />`, :math:`GH` be multiples of another :g:`C <0145.g1:C>`; and as many :g:`DE <0145.g1:/ D E />`, :math:`EK`, :math:`KL` be the same multiples of :g:`F <0145.g1:F>`, each of each; the whole of the first, viz. :math:`AH`, is the same multiple of :g:`C <0145.g1:C>` that the whole of the last, viz. :math:`DL`, is of :g:`F <0145.g1:F>`."

The course of the proof, which separates :math:`m` into its units and also :math:`n` into its units, practically tells us that the multiple of :math:`a` arrived at by adding the *two* multiples is the :math:`(m + n)`\ th multiple; or practically we are shown that

.. math::

   ma + na = (m + n)a,

or, more generally, that

.. math::

   ma + na + pa + \dots = (m + n + p + \dots)a.
