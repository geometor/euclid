Commentary on Proposition V.3
=============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition V.3 <V.3>`.

.. folio:: 0147
   :page: 141

:person:`Heiberg <heiberg>` remarks of the use of :term:`*ex aequali* <ex-aequali>` in the enunciation of this proposition that, strictly speaking, it has no reference to the definition (17) of a *ratio ex aequali*. But the uses of the expression here and in the definition are, I think, sufficiently parallel, as may be seen thus. The proposition asserts that, if

.. math::

   na, nb \text{ are equimultiples of } a, b,

and if

.. math::

   m.na, m.nb \text{ are equimultiples of } na, nb,

then :math:`m.na` is the same multiple of :math:`a` that :math:`m.nb` is of :math:`b`. Clearly the proposition can be extended by taking further equimultiples of the last equimultiples and so on; and we can prove that

.. math::

   p.q\dots m.na \text{ is the same multiple of } a \text{ that } p.q\dots m.nb \text{ is of } b,

where the series of numbers :math:`p.q\dots m.n` is exactly the same in both expressions;
and :term:`*ex aequali* <ex-aequali>` (:math:`\delta\iota\text{'} \text{ }\ἴ\sigma\omicron\upsilon`) expresses the fact that the equimultiples are *at the same distance* from :math:`a`, :math:`b` in the series :math:`na`, :math:`m.na \dots` and :math:`nb`, :math:`m.nb \dots` respectively.

.. _folio-ii-0148:

.. folio:: 0148
   :page: 142

Here again the proof breaks :math:`m` into its units, and then breaks :math:`n` into its units; and we are practically shown that the multiple of :math:`a` arrived at, viz. :math:`m.na`, is the multiple denoted by the product of the numbers :math:`m`, :math:`n`, i.e. the :math:`(mn)`\ th multiple, or in other words that

.. math::

   m.na = mn.a.
