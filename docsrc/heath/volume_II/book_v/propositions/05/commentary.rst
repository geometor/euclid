Commentary on Proposition V.5
=============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition V.5 <V.5>`.

.. folio:: 0152
   :page: 146

This proposition corresponds to V. 1, with subtraction taking the place of addition. It proves the formula

$$ma - mb = m(a - b).$$

Euclid's construction assumes that, if :g:`$AE$ <0151.g1:/ A E />` is any multiple of :g:`$CF$ <0151.g1:/ C F />`, and :g:`$EB$ <0151.g1:/ E B />` is any other magnitude, a fourth straight line can be found such that :g:`$EB$ <0151.g1:/ E B />` is the same multiple of it that :g:`$AE$ <0151.g1:/ A E />` is of :g:`$CF$ <0151.g1:/ C F />`, or in other words that, given any magnitude, we can divide it into any number of equal parts. This is however not proved, even of straight lines, much less other magnitudes, until VI. 9. :person:`Peletarius <peletier>` had already seen this objection to the construction. The difficulty is not got over by regarding it merely as a *hypothetical* construction ; for hypothetical constructions are not in Euclid's manner. The remedy is to substitute the alternative construction given by :person:`Simson <simson>`, after :person:`Peletarius <peletier>` and :person:`Campanus <campanus>`' translation from the Arabic, which only requires us to add a magnitude to itself a certain number of times. The demonstration follows Euclid's line exactly.

Euclid's proof amounts to this.

Suppose a magnitude $x$ taken such that

$$ma - mb = mx, \text{ say.}$$

Add $mb$ to each side, whence (by V. 1)

$$ma = m(x + b).$$

Therefore

$$a = x + b, \text{ or } x = a - b,$$

so that

$$ma - mb = m(a - b).$$

:person:`Simson <simson>`'s proof, on the other hand, argues thus.

Take $x = m(a - b)$, the same multiple of $(a - b)$ that $mb$ is of $b$.

Then, by addition of $mb$ to both sides, we have :just:`[V. 1] <V.1>`

$$x + mb = ma,$$

or

$$x = ma - mb.$$

That is,

$$ma - mb = m(a - b).$$
