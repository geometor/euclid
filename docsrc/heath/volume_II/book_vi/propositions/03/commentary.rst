Commentary on Proposition VI.3
==============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition VI.3 <VI.3>`.

.. folio:: 0203
   :page: 197

The demonstration assumes that :g:`$CE$ <0202.g1:/ C E />` will meet :g:`$BA$ <0202.g1:/ B A />` produced in some point :g:`$E$ <0202.g1:E>`. This is proved in the same way as it is proved in **VI. 4** that :g:`$BA$ <0207.g1:/ B A />`, :g:`$ED$ <0207.g1:/ E D />` will meet if produced. The angles :g:`$ABD$ <0202.g1:< A B D )>`, :g:`$BDA$ <0202.g1:< B D A )>` in the figure of **VI. 3** are together less than two right angles, and the angle :g:`$BDA$ <0202.g1:< B D A )>` is equal to the angle :g:`$BCE$ <0202.g1:< B C E )>`, since :g:`$DA$ <0202.g1:/ D A />`, :g:`$CE$ <0202.g1:/ C E />` are parallel. Therefore the angles :g:`$ABC$ <0202.g1:< A B C )>`, :g:`$BCE$ <0202.g1:< B C E )>` are together less than two right angles; and :g:`$BA$ <0202.g1:/ B A />`, :g:`$CE$ <0202.g1:/ C E />` must meet, by **I. Post. 5**.

The corresponding proposition about the segments into which :g:`$BC$ <0203.g1:/ B C />` is divided *externally* by the bisector of the *external* angle at :g:`$A$ <0203.g1:A>` when that bisector meets :g:`$BC$ <0203.g1:/ B C />` produced (i.e. when the sides :g:`$AB$ <0203.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$AC$ <0203.g1:/ A C />` are not equal) is important. :person:`Simson <simson>` gives it as a separate proposition, A, noting the fact that :person:`Pappus <pappus>` assumes the result without proof (:person:`Pappus <pappus>`, **VII.** p. 730, 24).

The best plan is however, as :person:`De Morgan <de-morgan>` says, to combine Props. 3 and A in one proposition, which may be enunciated thus: *If an angle of a triangle be bisected internally or externally by a straight line which cuts the opposite side or the opposite side produced, the segments of that side will have the same ratio as the other sides of the triangle; and, if a side of a triangle be divided internally or externally so that its segments have the same ratio as the other sides of the triangle, the straight line drawn from the point of section to the angular point which is opposite to the first mentioned side will bisect the interior or exterior angle at that angular point.*

.. picture:: 0203.g1.svg
   :alt: Proposition 3 and Proposition A

Let :g:`$AC$ <0203.g1:/ A C />` be the smaller of the two sides :g:`$AB$ <0203.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$AC$ <0203.g1:/ A C />`, so that the bisector :g:`$AD$ <0203.g1:/ A D />` of the exterior angle at :g:`$A$ <0203.g1:A>` may meet :g:`$BC$ <0203.g1:/ B C />` produced beyond :g:`$C$ <0203.g1:C>`. Draw :g:`$CE$ <0203.g1:/ C E />` through :g:`$C$ <0203.g1:C>` parallel to :g:`$DA$ <0203.g1:/ D A />`, meeting :g:`$BA$ <0203.g1:/ B A />` in :g:`$E$ <0203.g1:E>`.

Then, if :g:`$FAC$ <0203.g1:< F A C )>` is the exterior angle bisected by :g:`$AD$ <0203.g1:/ A D />` in the case of external bisection, and if a point :g:`$F$ <0203.g1:F>` is taken on :g:`$AB$ <0203.g1:/ A B />` in the figure of **VI. 3**, the proof of
**VI. 3** can be used almost word for word for the other case. We have only to speak of the angle “:g:`$FAC$ <0203.g1:< F A C )>`” for the angle “:g:`$BAC$ <0203.g1:< B A C )>`,” and of the angle “:g:`$FAD$ <0203.g1:< F A D )>`” for the angle “:g:`$BAD$ <0203.g1:< B A D )>`” wherever they occur, to say “let :g:`$BA$ <0203.g1:/ B A />`, or :g:`$BA$ <0203.g1:/ B A />` produced, meet :g:`$CE$ <0203.g1:/ C E />` in :g:`$E$ <0203.g1:E>`,” and to substitute “:g:`$BA$ <0203.g1:/ B A />` or :g:`$BA$ <0203.g1:/ B A />` produced” for “:g:`$BAE$ <0203.g1:/ B A E />`” lower down.

.. _folio-ii-0204:

.. folio:: 0204
   :page: 198

.. picture:: 0204.g1.svg
   :alt: Apollonius Circle (Internal and External Bisectors)

If :g:`$AD$ <0204.g1:/ A D />`, :g:`$AE$ <0204.g1:/ A E />` be the internal and external bisectors of the angle :g:`$A$ <0204.g1:< A )>` in a triangle of which the sides :g:`$AB$ <0204.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$AC$ <0204.g1:/ A C />` are unequal, :g:`$AC$ <0204.g1:/ A C />` being the smaller, and if :g:`$AD$ <0204.g1:/ A D />`, :g:`$AE$ <0204.g1:/ A E />` meet :g:`$BC$ <0204.g1:/ B C />` and :g:`$BC$ <0204.g1:/ B C />` produced in :g:`$D$ <0204.g1:D>`, :g:`$E$ <0204.g1:E>` respectively, the ratios of :g:`$BD$ <0204.g1:/ B D />` to :g:`$DC$ <0204.g1:/ D C />` and of :g:`$BE$ <0204.g1:/ B E />` to :g:`$EC$ <0204.g1:/ E C />` are alike equal to the ratio of :g:`$BA$ <0204.g1:/ B A />` to :g:`$AC$ <0204.g1:/ A C />`.

.. assertion::

   Therefore :g:`$BE$ <0204.g1:/ B E />` is to :g:`$EC$ <0204.g1:/ E C />` as :g:`$BD$ <0204.g1:/ B D />` to :g:`$DC$ <0204.g1:/ D C />`,

that is, :g:`$BE$ <0204.g1:/ B E />` is to :g:`$EC$ <0204.g1:/ E C />` as the difference between :g:`$BE$ <0204.g1:/ B E />` and :g:`$ED$ <0204.g1:/ E D />` is to the difference between :g:`$ED$ <0204.g1:/ E D />` and :g:`$EC$ <0204.g1:/ E C />`, whence :g:`$BE$ <0204.g1:/ B E />`, :g:`$ED$ <0204.g1:/ E D />`, :g:`$EC$ <0204.g1:/ E C />` are in *harmonic progression*, or :g:`$DE$ <0204.g1:/ D E />` is a *harmonic mean* between :g:`$BE$ <0204.g1:/ B E />` and :g:`$EC$ <0204.g1:/ E C />`, or again :g:`$B$ <0204.g1:B>`, :g:`$D$ <0204.g1:D>`, :g:`$C$ <0204.g1:C>`, :g:`$E$ <0204.g1:E>` is a *harmonic range*.

Since the angle :g:`$DAC$ <0204.g1:< D A C )>` is half of the angle :g:`$BAC$ <0204.g1:< B A C )>`, and the angle :g:`$CAE$ <0204.g1:< C A E )>` half of the angle :g:`$CAF$ <0204.g1:< C A F )>`, while the angles :g:`$BAC$ <0204.g1:< B A C )>`, :g:`$CAF$ <0204.g1:< C A F )>` are equal to two right angles,

.. assertion::

   the angle :g:`$DAE$ <0204.g1:< D A E )>` is a right angle.

Hence the circle described on :g:`$DE$ <0204.g1:/ D E />` as diameter passes through :g:`$A$ <0204.g1:A>`.

Now, if the ratio of :g:`$BA$ <0204.g1:/ B A />` to :g:`$AC$ <0204.g1:/ A C />` is given, and if :g:`$BC$ <0204.g1:/ B C />` is given, the points :g:`$D$ <0204.g1:D>`, :g:`$E$ <0204.g1:E>` on :g:`$BC$ <0204.g1:/ B C />` and :g:`$BC$ <0204.g1:/ B C />` produced are given, and therefore so is the circle on :g:`$D$ <0204.g1:D>`, :g:`$E$ <0204.g1:E>` as diameter. Hence *the locus of a point such that its distances from two given points are in a given ratio (not being a ratio of equality) is a circle*.

This locus was discussed by :person:`Apollonius <apollonius>` in his :cite:`*Plane Loci* <apollonius:plane-loci>`, Book **II.**, as we know from :person:`Pappus <pappus>` (**VII.** p. 666), who says that the book contained the theorem that, if from two given points straight lines inflected to another point are in a given ratio, the point in which they meet will lie on either a straight line or a circumference of a circle. The straight line is of course the locus when the ratio is one of equality. The other case is quoted in the following form by :person:`Eutocius <eutocius>` (:person:`Apollonius <apollonius>`, ed. :person:`Heiberg <heiberg>`, **II.** pp. 180—4).

*Given two points in a plane and a proportion between unequal straight lines, it is possible to describe a circle in the plane so that the straight lines inflected from the given points to the circumference of the circle shall have a ratio the same as the given one.*

:person:`Apollonius <apollonius>`' construction, as given by :person:`Eutocius <eutocius>`, is remarkable because he makes no use of either of the points :g:`$D$ <0205.g1:D>`, :g:`$E$ <0205.g1:E>`. He finds :g:`$O$ <0205.g1:O>`, the centre of the required circle, and the length of its radius directly from the data :g:`$BC$ <0205.g1:/ B C />` and the given ratio which we will call $h : k$. But the construction was not discovered by :person:`Apollonius <apollonius>`; it belongs to a much earlier date, since it appears in exactly
the same form in :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>`, :cite:`*Meteorologica* III. 5, 376 a 3 <aristotle:meteorologica:iii-5-376-a-3>` sqq. The analysis leading up to the construction is, as usual, not given either by :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>` or :person:`Eutocius <eutocius>`. We are told to take three straight lines $x$, :g:`$CO$ <0205.g1:/ C O />` (a length measured along :g:`$BC$ <0205.g1:/ B C />` produced beyond :g:`$C$ <0205.g1:C>`, where :g:`$B$ <0205.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0205.g1:C>` are the points at which the greater and smaller of the inflected lines respectively terminate), and $r$, such that, if $h : k$ be the given ratio and $h > k$,

.. _folio-ii-0205:

.. folio:: 0205
   :page: 199

.. container:: center

   $k : h = h : k + x$, $(\alpha)$ $x :$ :g:`$BC$ <0205.g1:/ B C />` $= k :$ :g:`$CO$ <0205.g1:/ C O />` $= h : r$. $(\beta)$

.. picture:: 0205.g1.svg
   :alt: Apollonius Circle Construction Diagram

This determines the position of :g:`$O$ <0205.g1:O>`, and the length of $r$, the radius of the required circle. The circle is then drawn, any point :g:`$P$ <0205.g1:P>` is taken on it and joined to :g:`$B$ <0205.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0205.g1:C>` respectively, and it is proved that

.. container:: center

   :g:`$PB$ <0205.g1:/ P B />` $:$ :g:`$PC$ <0205.g1:/ P C />` $= h : k$.

We may conjecture that the analysis proceeded somewhat as follows.

It would be seen that :g:`$B$ <0205.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0205.g1:C>` are "conjugate points" with reference to the circle on :g:`$DE$ <0205.g1:/ D E />` as diameter. (*Cf.* :person:`Apollonius <apollonius>`, :cite:`*Conics* <apollonius:conics>`, I. 36, where it is proved, in terms, for a circle as well as for an ellipse and a hyperbola, that, if the polar of :g:`$B$ <0205.g1:B>` meets the diameter :g:`$DE$ <0205.g1:/ D E />` in :g:`$C$ <0205.g1:C>`, then :g:`$EC$ <0205.g1:/ E C />` $:$ :g:`$CD$ <0205.g1:/ C D />` $=$ :g:`$EB$ <0205.g1:/ E B />` $:$ :g:`$BD$ <0205.g1:/ B D />`.)

If :g:`$O$ <0205.g1:O>` be the middle point of :g:`$DE$ <0205.g1:/ D E />`, and therefore the centre of the circle, :g:`$D$ <0205.g1:D>`, :g:`$E$ <0205.g1:E>` may be eliminated, as in the :cite:`*Conics* <apollonius:conics>`, I. 37, thus.

Since

.. container:: center

   :g:`$EC$ <0205.g1:/ E C />` $:$ :g:`$CD$ <0205.g1:/ C D />` $=$ :g:`$EB$ <0205.g1:/ E B />` $:$ :g:`$BD$ <0205.g1:/ B D />`,

it follows that :g:`$EC$ <0205.g1:/ E C />` $+$ :g:`$CD$ <0205.g1:/ C D />` $:$ :g:`$EC$ <0205.g1:/ E C />` $\sim$ :g:`$CD$ <0205.g1:/ C D />` $=$ :g:`$EB$ <0205.g1:/ E B />` $+$ :g:`$BD$ <0205.g1:/ B D />` $:$ :g:`$EB$ <0205.g1:/ E B />` $\sim$ :g:`$BD$ <0205.g1:/ B D />`,
or

.. container:: center

   $2$ :g:`$OD$ <0205.g1:/ O D />` $: 2$ :g:`$OC$ <0205.g1:/ O C />` $= 2$ :g:`$OB$ <0205.g1:/ O B />` $: 2$ :g:`$OD$ <0205.g1:/ O D />`,

that is,

.. container:: center

   :g:`$BO$ <0205.g1:/ B O />` $\cdot$ :g:`$OC$ <0205.g1:/ O C />` $=$ :g:`$OD^2$ <0205.g1:/ O D />` $= r^2$, say.

If therefore :g:`$P$ <0205.g1:P>` be any point on the circle with centre :g:`$O$ <0205.g1:O>` and radius $r$,

.. container:: center

   :g:`$BO$ <0205.g1:/ B O />` $:$ :g:`$OP$ <0205.g1:/ O P />` $=$ :g:`$OP$ <0205.g1:/ O P />` $:$ :g:`$OC$ <0205.g1:/ O C />`,

so that :g:`$BOP$ <0205.g1:< B O P >>`, :g:`$POC$ <0205.g1:< P O C >>` are similar triangles.

In addition, $h : k =$ :g:`$BD$ <0205.g1:/ B D />` $:$ :g:`$DC$ <0205.g1:/ D C />` $=$ :g:`$BE$ <0205.g1:/ B E />` $:$ :g:`$EC$ <0205.g1:/ E C />`

.. container:: center

   $=$ :g:`$BD$ <0205.g1:/ B D />` $+$ :g:`$BE$ <0205.g1:/ B E />` $:$ :g:`$DE$ <0205.g1:/ D E />` $=$ :g:`$BO$ <0205.g1:/ B O />` $: r$.

Hence we require that

.. container:: center

   :g:`$BO$ <0205.g1:/ B O />` $: r = r :$ :g:`$OC$ <0205.g1:/ O C />` $=$ :g:`$BP$ <0205.g1:/ B P />` $:$ :g:`$PC$ <0205.g1:/ P C />` $= h : k$. $(\delta)$

Therefore, alternately,

.. container:: center

   $k :$ :g:`$CO$ <0205.g1:/ C O />` $= h : r$,

which is the second relation in $(\beta)$ above.

Now assume a length $x$ such that each of the last ratios is equal to $x :$ :g:`$BC$ <0205.g1:/ B C />`, as in $(\beta)$.

.. _folio-ii-0206:

.. folio:: 0206
   :page: 200

Then

.. container:: center

   x : :g:`$BC$ <0205.g1:/ B C />` = k : :g:`$CO$ <0205.g1:/ C O />` = h : r.

Therefore

.. container:: center

   x + k : :g:`$BO$ <0205.g1:/ B O />` = h : r,

and, alternately,

.. container:: center

   x + k : h = :g:`$BO$ <0205.g1:/ B O />` : r = h : k, from (\delta) above;

and this is the relation $(\alpha)$ which remained to be found.

:person:`Apollonius <apollonius>`' proof of the construction is given by :person:`Eutocius <eutocius>`, who begins by saying that it is manifest that $r$ is a mean proportional between :g:`$BO$ <0205.g1:/ B O />` and :g:`$OC$ <0205.g1:/ O C />`. This is seen as follows.

From $(\beta)$ we derive

.. container:: center

   x : :g:`$BC$ <0205.g1:/ B C />` = k : :g:`$CO$ <0205.g1:/ C O />` = h : r = (k + x) : :g:`$BO$ <0205.g1:/ B O />`,

whence

.. container:: center

   :g:`$BO$ <0205.g1:/ B O />` : r = (k + x) : h = h : k, by (\alpha), = r : :g:`$CO$ <0205.g1:/ C O />`, by (\beta),

and therefore

.. container:: center

   r^2 = :g:`$BO$ <0205.g1:/ B O />` \,.\, :g:`$CO$ <0205.g1:/ C O />`.

But the triangles :g:`$BOP$ <0205.g1:< B O P >>`, :g:`$POC$ <0205.g1:< P O C >>` have the angle at :g:`$O$ <0205.g1:< O )>` common, and, since :g:`$BO$ <0205.g1:/ B O />` : :g:`$OP$ <0205.g1:/ O P />` = :g:`$OP$ <0205.g1:/ O P />` : :g:`$OC$ <0205.g1:/ O C />`, the triangles are similar and the angles :g:`$OPC$ <0205.g1:< O P C )>`, :g:`$OBP$ <0205.g1:< O B P )>` are equal.

[Up to this point :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>`'s proof is exactly the same; from this point it diverges slightly.]

If now :g:`$CL$ <0205.g1:/ C L />` be drawn parallel to :g:`$BP$ <0205.g1:/ B P />` meeting :g:`$OP$ <0205.g1:/ O P />` in :g:`$L$ <0205.g1:L>`, the angles :g:`$BPC$ <0205.g1:< B P C )>`, :g:`$LCP$ <0205.g1:< L C P )>` are equal also.

Therefore the triangles :g:`$BPC$ <0205.g1:< B P C >>`, :g:`$PCL$ <0205.g1:< P C L >>` are similar, and

.. container:: center

   :g:`$BP$ <0205.g1:/ B P />` : :g:`$PC$ <0205.g1:/ P C />` = :g:`$PC$ <0205.g1:/ P C />` : :g:`$CL$ <0205.g1:/ C L />`,

whence

.. container:: center

   :g:`$BP^2$ <0205.g1:/ B P />` : :g:`$PC^2$ <0205.g1:/ P C />` = :g:`$BP$ <0205.g1:/ B P />` : :g:`$CL$ <0205.g1:/ C L />` = :g:`$BO$ <0205.g1:/ B O />` : :g:`$OC$ <0205.g1:/ O C />`, by parallels, = :g:`$BO^2$ <0205.g1:/ B O />` : :g:`$OP^2$ <0205.g1:/ O P />` (since :g:`$BO$ <0205.g1:/ B O />` : :g:`$OP$ <0205.g1:/ O P />` = :g:`$OP$ <0205.g1:/ O P />` : :g:`$OC$ <0205.g1:/ O C />`).

Therefore

.. container:: center

   :g:`$BP$ <0205.g1:/ B P />` : :g:`$PC$ <0205.g1:/ P C />` = :g:`$BO$ <0205.g1:/ B O />` : :g:`$OP$ <0205.g1:/ O P />` = h : k (for :g:`$OP$ <0205.g1:/ O P />` = r).

[:person:`Aristotle <aristotle>` infers this more directly from the similar triangles :g:`$POB$ <0205.g1:< P O B >>`, :g:`$COP$ <0205.g1:< C O P >>`. Since these triangles are similar,

.. container:: center

   :g:`$OP$ <0205.g1:/ O P />` : :g:`$CP$ <0205.g1:/ C P />` = :g:`$OB$ <0205.g1:/ O B />` : :g:`$BP$ <0205.g1:/ B P />`,

whence

.. container:: center

   :g:`$BP$ <0205.g1:/ B P />` : :g:`$PC$ <0205.g1:/ P C />` = :g:`$BO$ <0205.g1:/ B O />` : :g:`$OP$ <0205.g1:/ O P />` = h : k.]

:person:`Apollonius <apollonius>` proves lastly, by :term:`*reductio ad absurdum* <reductio-ad-absurdum>`, that the last equation cannot be true with reference to any point :g:`$P$ <0205.g1:P>` which is not on the circle so described.
