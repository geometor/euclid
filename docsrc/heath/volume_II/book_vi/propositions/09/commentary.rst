Commentary on Proposition VI.9
==============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition VI.9 <VI.9>`.

.. folio:: 0218
   :page: 212

:person:`Simson <simson>` observes that "this is demonstrated in a particular case, viz. that in which the third part of a straight line is required to be cut off; which is not at all like Euclid's manner. Besides, the author of that demonstration, from four magnitudes being proportionals, concludes that the third of them is the same multiple of the fourth which the first is of the second; now this is nowhere demonstrated in the 5th book, as we now have it; but the editor assumes it from the confused notion which the vulgar have of proportionals."

The truth of the assumption referred to is proved by :person:`Simson <simson>` in his proposition D given above (p. 128); hence he is able to supply a general and legitimate proof of the present proposition.

"Let :g:`AB <0218.g2:/ A B />` be the given straight line; it is required to cut off any part from it.

From the point :g:`A <0218.g2:A>` draw a straight line :g:`AC <0218.g2:/ A C />` making any angle with :g:`AB <0218.g2:/ A B />`; in :g:`AC <0218.g2:/ A C />` take any point :g:`D <0218.g2:D>`, and take :g:`AC <0218.g2:/ A C />` the same multiple of :g:`AD <0218.g2:/ A D />` that :g:`AB <0218.g2:/ A B />` is of the part which is to be cut off from it;

.. picture:: 0218.g2.svg
   :alt: Proposition 9, Simson's Alternative

join :g:`BC <0218.g2:/ B C />`, and draw :g:`DE <0218.g2:/ D E />` parallel to it: then :g:`AE <0218.g2:/ A E />` is the part required to be cut off.

.. _folio-ii-0219:

.. folio:: 0219
   :page: 213

Because :g:`ED <0218.g2:/ E D />` is parallel to one of the sides of the triangle :g:`ABC <0218.g2:< A B C >>`, viz. to :g:`BC <0218.g2:/ B C />`,

.. assertion:: [VI. 2] <VI.2>

   as :g:`CD <0218.g2:/ C D />` is to :g:`DA <0218.g2:/ D A />`, so is :g:`BE <0218.g2:/ B E />` to :g:`EA <0218.g2:/ E A />`,

and, :term:`*componendo* <componendo>`,

.. assertion:: [V. 18] <V.18>

   :g:`CA <0218.g2:/ C A />` is to :g:`AD <0218.g2:/ A D />`, as :g:`BA <0218.g2:/ B A />` to :g:`AE <0218.g2:/ A E />`.

But :g:`CA <0218.g2:/ C A />` is a multiple of :g:`AD <0218.g2:/ A D />`;

.. assertion:: [Prop. D]

   therefore :g:`BA <0218.g2:/ B A />` is the same multiple of :g:`AE <0218.g2:/ A E />`.

.. assertion::

   Whatever part therefore :g:`AD <0218.g2:/ A D />` is of :g:`AC <0218.g2:/ A C />`, :g:`AE <0218.g2:/ A E />` is the same part of :g:`AB <0218.g2:/ A B />`; wherefore from the straight line :g:`AB <0218.g2:/ A B />` the part required is cut off."

The use of :person:`Simson <simson>`'s Prop. D can be avoided, as noted by :person:`Camerer <camerer>` after Baermann, in the following way. We first prove, as above, that

.. assertion::

   :g:`CA <0218.g2:/ C A />` is to :g:`AD <0218.g2:/ A D />` as :g:`BA <0218.g2:/ B A />` is to :g:`AE <0218.g2:/ A E />`.

Then we infer that, alternately,

.. assertion:: [V. 16] <V.16>

   :g:`CA <0218.g2:/ C A />` is to :g:`BA <0218.g2:/ B A />` as :g:`AD <0218.g2:/ A D />` to :g:`AE <0218.g2:/ A E />`.

But :g:`AD <0218.g2:/ A D />` is to :g:`AE <0218.g2:/ A E />` as n · :g:`AD <0218.g2:/ A D />` to n · :g:`AE <0218.g2:/ A E />` (where $n$ is the number of times that :g:`AD <0218.g2:/ A D />` is contained in :g:`AC <0218.g2:/ A C />`); :just:`[V. 15] <V.15>`

.. assertion:: [V. 11] <V.11>

   whence :g:`AC <0218.g2:/ A C />` is to :g:`AB <0218.g2:/ A B />` as n · :g:`AD <0218.g2:/ A D />` is to n · :g:`AE <0218.g2:/ A E />`.

In this proportion the first term is equal to the third; therefore :just:`[V. 14] <V.14>`

the second is equal to the fourth,

.. assertion::

   so that :g:`AB <0218.g2:/ A B />` is equal to $n$ times :g:`AE <0218.g2:/ A E />`.

Prop. 9 is of course only a particular case of Prop. 10.
