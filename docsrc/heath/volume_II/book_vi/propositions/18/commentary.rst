Commentary on Proposition VI.18
===============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition VI.18 <VI.18>`.

.. folio:: 0236
   :page: 230

:person:`Simson <simson>` thinks the proof of this proposition has been vitiated, his grounds for this view being (1) that it is demonstrated only with reference to quadrilaterals, and does not show how it may be extended to figures of five or more sides, (2) that Euclid infers, from the fact of two triangles being equiangular, that a side of the one is to the corresponding side of the other as another side of the first is to the side corresponding to it in the other, *i.e.* he permutes, without mentioning the fact that he does so, the proportions obtained in VI. 4, whereas the proof of the very next proposition gives, in a similar case, the intermediate step of permutation. I think this is hypercriticism. As regards (2) it should be noted that the permuted form of the proportion is arrived at first in the *proof* of VI. 4; and the omission of the
intermediate step of :term:`*alternando* <alternando>`, whether accidental or not, is of no importance. On the other hand, the use of this form of the proportion certainly simplifies the proof of the proposition, since it makes unnecessary the subsequent :term:`*ex aequali* <ex-aequali>` steps of :person:`Simson <simson>`'s proof, their place being taken by the inference :just:`[v. 11]` that ratios which are the same with a third ratio are the same with one another.

.. folio:: 0237

Nor is the first objection of any importance. We have only to take as the

.. picture:: 0237.g1.svg
   :alt: Extension to polygon

given polygon a polygon of five sides at least, as :g:`$CDEFG$ <0237.g1:< C D E F G >>`, join one extremity of :g:`$CD$ <0237.g1:/ C D />`, say :g:`$D$ <0237.g1:D>`, to each of the angular points other than :g:`$C$ <0237.g1:C>` and :g:`$E$ <0237.g1:E>`, and then use the same mode of construction as Euclid's for any number of successive triangles as :g:`$ABL$ <0237.g1:< A B L >>`, :g:`$LBK$ <0237.g1:< L B K >>`, etc., that may have to be made. Euclid's construction and proof for a quadrilateral are quite sufficient to show how to deal with the case of a figure of five or any greater number of sides.

:person:`Clavius <clavius>` has a construction which, given the power of moving a figure

.. picture:: 0237.g2.svg
   :alt: Clavius's construction

bodily from one position to any other, is easier. :g:`$CDEFG$ <0237.g2:< C D E F G >>` being the given polygon, join :g:`$CE$ <0237.g2:/ C E />`, :g:`$CF$ <0237.g2:/ C F />`. Place :g:`$AB$ <0237.g2:/ A B />` on :g:`$CD$ <0237.g2:/ C D />` so that :g:`$A$ <0237.g2:A>` falls on :g:`$C$ <0237.g2:C>`, and let :g:`$B$ <0237.g2:B>` fall on :g:`$D'$ <0237.g2:D'>`, which may either lie on :g:`$CD$ <0237.g2:/ C D />` or on :g:`$CD$ <0237.g2:[ C D ]>` produced.

Now draw :g:`$D'E'$ <0237.g2:/ D' E' />` parallel to :g:`$DE$ <0237.g2:/ D E />`, meeting :g:`$CE$ <0237.g2:/ C E />`, produced if necessary, in :g:`$E'$ <0237.g2:E'>`, :g:`$E'F'$ <0237.g2:/ E' F' />` parallel to :g:`$EF$ <0237.g2:/ E F />`, meeting :g:`$CF$ <0237.g2:/ C F />`, produced if necessary, in :g:`$F'$ <0237.g2:F'>`, and so on.

Let the parallel to the last side but one, :g:`$FG$ <0237.g2:/ F G />`, meet :g:`$CG$ <0237.g2:/ C G />`, produced if necessary, in :g:`$G'$ <0237.g2:G'>`.

Then :g:`$CD'E'F'G'$ <0237.g2:< C D' E' F' G' >>` is similar and similarly situated to :g:`$CDEFG$ <0237.g2:< C D E F G >>`, and it is constructed on :g:`$CD'$ <0237.g2:/ C D' />`, a straight line :eq:`equal <0237.g2:/ C D' / = 0237.g2:/ A B />` to :g:`$AB$ <0237.g2:/ A B />`.

The proof of this is obvious.

A more general construction is indicated in the subjoined figure. If :g:`$CDEFG$ <0238.g1:< C D E F G >>` be the given polygon, suppose its angular points all joined to any point :g:`$O$ <0238.g1:O>` and the connecting straight lines produced both ways. Then, if :g:`$C'D'$ <0238.g1:/ C' D' />`, a straight line :eq:`equal <0238.g1:/ C' D' / = 0237.g2:/ A B />` to :g:`$AB$ <0237.g2:/ A B />`, be placed so that it is parallel to :g:`$CD$ <0238.g1:/ C D />`, and :g:`$C'$ <0238.g1:C'>`, :g:`$D'$ <0238.g1:D'>` lie respectively on :g:`$OC$ <0238.g1:/ O C />`, :g:`$OD$ <0238.g1:/ O D />` (this can of course be done by finding fourth proportionals), we have only to draw :g:`$D'E'$ <0238.g1:/ D' E' />`, :g:`$E'F'$ <0238.g1:/ E' F' />`, etc., parallel to the corresponding sides of the original polygon in the manner shown.

:person:`De Morgan <de-morgan>` would rearrange Props. 18 and 20 in the following manner. He would combine Prop. 18 and the first part of Prop. 20 into one, with the enunciation :

.. picture:: 0238.g1.svg
   :alt: General construction for similar polygons

*Pairs of similar triangles, similarly put together, give similar figures ; and every pair of similar figures is composed of pairs of similar triangles similarly put together.*

He would then make the *problem* of VI. 18 an application of the first part. In form this would certainly appear to be an improvement; but, provided that the relation of the propositions is understood, the matter of form is perhaps not of great importance.
