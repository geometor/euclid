Commentary on Proposition VI.27
===============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition VI.27 <VI.27>`.

.. _folio-ii-0264:

.. folio:: 0264
   :page: 258

We have already (note on I. 44) seen the significance, in Greek geometry, of the theory of "the application of areas, their exceeding and their falling-short." In I. 44 it was a question of "applying to a given straight line (exactly, without 'excess' or 'defect') a parallelogram equal to a given rectilineal figure, in a given angle." Here, in VI. 27—29, it is a question of parallelograms applied to a straight line but "*deficient (or exceeding) by* *parallelograms similar and similarly* *situated to a given parallelogram.*"

Apart from size, it is easy to construct any number of parallelograms "deficient" or "exceeding" in the manner described. Given the straight line :g:`$AB$ <0264.g1:/ A B />` to which the parallelogram has to be applied, we describe on the base :g:`$CB$ <0264.g1:/ C B />`, where :g:`$C$ <0264.g1:C>` is on :g:`$AB$ <0264.g1:/ A B />`, or on :g:`$BA$ <0264.g1:[ B A ]>` produced beyond :g:`$A$ <0264.g1:A>`, any parallelogram "similarly situated" and either equal or similar to the given parallelogram (Euclid takes the similar and similarly situated parallelogram on half the line), draw the diagonal :g:`$BD$ <0264.g1:/ B D />`, take on it (produced if necessary) any points as :g:`$E$ <0264.g1:E>`, :g:`$K$ <0264.g1:K>`, draw :g:`$EF$ <0264.g1:/ E F />`, or :g:`$KL$ <0264.g1:/ K L />`, parallel to :g:`$CD$ <0264.g1:/ C D />` to meet :g:`$AB$ <0264.g1:/ A B />` or :g:`$AB$ <0264.g1:[ A B ]>` produced and complete the parallelograms, as :g:`$AH$ <0264.g1:< A H >>`, :g:`$ML$ <0264.g1:< M L >>`.

.. picture:: 0264.g1.svg
   :alt: Application of areas locus

If the point :g:`$E$ <0264.g1:E>` is taken on :g:`$BD$ <0264.g1:/ B D />` or :g:`$BD$ <0264.g1:[ B D ]>` produced beyond :g:`$D$ <0264.g1:D>`, it must be so taken that :g:`$EF$ <0264.g1:/ E F />` meets :g:`$AB$ <0264.g1:/ A B />` between :g:`$A$ <0264.g1:A>` and :g:`$B$ <0264.g1:B>`. Otherwise the parallelogram :g:`$AE$ <0264.g1:< A E >>` would not be applied to :g:`$AB$ <0264.g1:/ A B />` itself, as it is required to be.

The parallelograms :g:`$BD$ <0264.g1:< B D >>`, :g:`$BE$ <0264.g1:< B E >>`, being about the same diameter, are similar :just:`[VI. 24] <VI.24>`, and :g:`$BE$ <0264.g1:< B E >>` is the defect of the parallelogram :g:`$AE$ <0264.g1:< A E >>` relatively to :g:`$AB$ <0264.g1:/ A B />`. :g:`$AE$ <0264.g1:< A E >>` is then a parallelogram applied to :g:`$AB$ <0264.g1:/ A B />` but deficient by a parallelogram similar and similarly situated to :g:`$BD$ <0264.g1:< B D >>`.

If :g:`$K$ <0264.g1:K>` is on :g:`$DB$ <0264.g1:[ D B ]>` produced, the parallelogram :g:`$BK$ <0264.g1:< B K >>` is similar to :g:`$BD$ <0264.g1:< B D >>`, but it is the *excess* of the parallelogram :g:`$AK$ <0264.g1:< A K >>` relatively to the base :g:`$AB$ <0264.g1:/ A B />`. :g:`$AK$ <0264.g1:< A K >>` is a parallelogram applied to :g:`$AB$ <0264.g1:/ A B />` but exceeding by a parallelogram similar and similarly situated to :g:`$BD$ <0264.g1:< B D >>`.

Thus it is seen that :g:`$BD$ <0264.g1:[ B D ]>` produced both ways is the *locus* of points, such as :g:`$E$ <0264.g1:E>` or :g:`$K$ <0264.g1:K>`, which determine, with the direction of :g:`$CD$ <0264.g1:/ C D />`, the position of :g:`$A$ <0264.g1:A>`, and the direction of :g:`$AB$ <0264.g1:/ A B />`, parallelograms applied to :g:`$AB$ <0264.g1:/ A B />` and *deficient* or *exceeding* by parallelograms similar and similarly situated to the given parallelogram.

The importance of VI. 27—29 from a historical point of view cannot be overrated. They give the geometrical equivalent of the algebraical solution of the most general form of quadratic equation when that equation has a real and positive root. It will also enable us to find a real *negative* root of a quadratic equation ; for such an equation can, by altering the sign of *x*, be turned into another with a real *positive* root, when the geometrical method again becomes applicable. It will also, as we shall see, enable us to represent *both* roots when both are real and positive, and therefore to represent both roots when both are real but either positive or negative.

The method of these propositions was constantly used by the Greek geometers in the solution of problems, and they constitute the foundation of Book X. of the $Elements$ and of :person:`Apollonius <apollonius>`' treatment of the conic sections. :person:`Simson <simson>`'s observation on the subject is entirely justified. He says namely on VI. 28, 29 : "These two problems, to the first of which the 27th Prop. is necessary, are the most general and useful of all in the Elements, and are most frequently made use of by the ancient geometers in the solution of other problems ; and therefore are very ignorantly left out by :person:`Tacquet <tacquet>` and
Dechales in their editions of the Elements, who pretend that they are scarce of any use."

It is strange that, with this observation before him, even :person:`Todhunter <todhunter>` should have written as follows. "We have omitted in the sixth Book Propositions 27, 28, 29 and the first solution which Euclid gives of Proposition 30, as they appear now to be never required, and have been condemned as useless by various modern commentators; see Austin, Walker and Lardner."

VI. 27 contains the :term:`διορισμός <diorismos>`, the condition for a real solution, of the problem contained in the proposition following it. The maximum of all the parallelograms having the given property which can be applied to a given straight line is that which is described upon half the line (τὸ ἀπὸ τῆς ἡμισείας ἀναγραφόμενον). This corresponds to the condition that an equation of the form

$$ax - px^2 = A$$

may have a real root. The correctness of the result may be seen by taking the case in which the parallelograms are rectangles, which enables us to leave out of account the sine of the angle of the parallelograms without any real loss of generality. Suppose the sides of the rectangle to which the defect is to be similar to be as $b$ to $c$, $b$ corresponding to the side of the defect which lies along :g:`$AB$ <0265.g1:/ A B />`. Suppose that :g:`$AKFG$ <0265.g1:< A K F G >>` is any parallelogram applied to :g:`$AB$ <0265.g1:/ A B />` having the given property, that :g:`$AB$ <0265.g1:/ A B />` $= a$, and that :g:`$FK$ <0265.g1:/ F K />` $= x$.

.. picture:: 0265.g1.svg
   :alt: Proposition 27 algebraic interpretation

Then

.. container:: center

   :g:`$KB$ <0265.g1:/ K B />` $= \frac{b}{c}x$, and therefore :g:`$AK$ <0265.g1:/ A K />` $= a - \frac{b}{c}x$.

Hence $(a - \frac{b}{c}x)x = S$, where $S$ is the area of the rectangle :g:`$AKFG$ <0265.g1:< A K F G >>`.

Thus, given the equation

$$ax - \frac{b}{c}x^2 = S,$$

where $S$ is undetermined, VI. 27 tells us that, if $x$ is to have a real value, $S$ cannot be greater than the rectangle :g:`$CE$ <0265.g1:< C E >>`.

Now :g:`$CB$ <0265.g1:/ C B />` $= \frac{a}{2}$, and therefore :g:`$CD$ <0265.g1:/ C D />` $= \frac{c}{b}\cdot\frac{a}{2}$; whence

$$S \ngtr \frac{c}{b}\cdot\frac{a^2}{4},$$

which is just the same result as we obtain by the algebraical method.

In the particular case where *the defect of the parallelogram is to be a square*, the condition becomes the statement of the fact that, *if a straight line be divided into two parts, the rectangle contained by the parts cannot exceed the square on half the line*.

Now suppose that, instead of taking :g:`$F$ <0265.g1:F>` on :g:`$BD$ <0265.g1:/ B D />` as in the figure of the proposition, we take :g:`$F$ <0266.g1:F>` on :g:`$BD$ <0266.g1:[ B D ]>` produced beyond :g:`$D$ <0266.g1:D>` but so that :g:`$DF$ <0266.g1:/ D F />` is less than :g:`$BD$ <0266.g1:/ B D />`.

Complete the figure, as shown, after the manner of the construction in the proposition.

Then the parallelogram :g:`$FKBH$ <0266.g1:< F K B H >>` is similar to the given parallelogram to which the defect is to be similar. Hence the parallelogram :g:`$GAKF$ <0266.g1:< G A K F >>` is also a parallelogram applied to :g:`$AB$ <0266.g1:/ A B />` and satisfying the given condition.

We can now prove that :g:`$GAKF$ <0266.g1:< G A K F >>` is less than :g:`$CE$ <0266.g1:< C E >>` or :g:`$AD$ <0266.g1:< A D >>`.

Let :g:`$ED$ <0266.g1:[ E D ]>` produced meet :g:`$AG$ <0266.g1:/ A G />` in :g:`$O$ <0266.g1:O>`.

.. picture:: 0266.g1.svg
   :alt: Proposition 27 second case

Now, since :g:`$BF$ <0266.g1:/ B F />` is the diagonal of the parallelogram :g:`$KH$ <0266.g1:< K H >>`, the complements :g:`$KD$ <0266.g1:< K D >>`, :g:`$DH$ <0266.g1:< D H >>` are :eq:`equal <0266.g1:< K D > = 0266.g1:< D H >>`.

But

.. container:: center

   :g:`$DH$ <0266.g1:< D H >>` = :g:`$DG$ <0266.g1:< D G >>`, and :g:`$DG$ <0266.g1:< D G >>` is greater than :g:`$OF$ <0266.g1:< O F >>`.

Therefore :g:`$KD$ <0266.g1:< K D >>` > :g:`$OF$ <0266.g1:< O F >>`.

Add :g:`$OK$ <0266.g1:< O K >>` to each; and

.. container:: center

   :g:`$AD$ <0266.g1:< A D >>`, or :g:`$CE$ <0266.g1:< C E >>`, > :g:`$AF$ <0266.g1:< A F >>`.

This other "case" of the proposition is found in all the mss., but :person:`Heiberg <heiberg>` relegates it to the Appendix as being very obviously interpolated. The reasons for this course are that it is not in Euclid's manner to give a separate demonstration of such a "case"; it is rather his habit to give one case only and to leave the student to satisfy himself about any others (cf. I. 7). Internal evidence is also against the genuineness of the separate proof. It is put after the *conclusion* of the proposition instead of before it, and, if Euclid had intended to discuss two cases, he would have distinguished them at the beginning of the proposition, as it was his invariable practice to do. Moreover the second "case" is the less worth giving because it can be so easily reduced to the first. For suppose :g:`$F'$ <0266.g1:F'>` to be taken on :g:`$BD$ <0266.g1:/ B D />` so that :g:`$FD$ <0266.g1:/ F D />` = :g:`$F'D$ <0266.g1:/ F' D />`. Produce :g:`$BF$ <0266.g1:[ B F ]>` to meet :g:`$AG$ <0266.g1:[ A G ]>` produced in :g:`$P$ <0266.g1:P>`. Complete the parallelogram :g:`$BAPQ$ <0266.g1:< B A P Q >>`, and draw through :g:`$F'$ <0266.g1:F'>` straight lines parallel to and meeting its opposite sides.

Then the complement :g:`$F'Q$ <0266.g1:< F' Q >>` is :eq:`equal <0266.g1:< F' Q > = 0266.g1:< A F' >>` to the complement :g:`$AF'$ <0266.g1:< A F' >>`.

And it is at once seen that :g:`$AF$ <0266.g1:< A F >>`, :g:`$F'Q$ <0266.g1:< F' Q >>` are :eq:`equal <0266.g1:< A F > = 0266.g1:< F' Q >>` and similar. Hence the solution of the problem represented by :g:`$AF$ <0266.g1:< A F >>` or :g:`$F'Q$ <0266.g1:< F' Q >>` gives a parallelogram of the same size as :g:`$AF'$ <0266.g1:< A F' >>` arrived at as in the first "case."

It is worth noting that the actual difference between the parallelogram :g:`$AF$ <0266.g1:< A F >>` and the maximum area :g:`$AD$ <0266.g1:< A D >>` that it can possibly have is represented in the figure. The difference is the small parallelogram :g:`$DF$ <0266.g1:< D F >>`.
