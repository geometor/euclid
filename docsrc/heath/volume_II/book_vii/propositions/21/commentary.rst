Commentary on Proposition VII.21
================================

Back to :ref:`Proposition VII.21 <VII.21>`.

.. folio:: 0329
   :page: 323

In other words, if :math:`a`, :math:`b` are prime to one another, the ratio :math:`a : b` is "in its lowest terms."

The proof is equivalent to the following.

If not, suppose that :math:`c`, :math:`d` are the *least* numbers for which

.. math::

   a : b = c : d.

[Euclid only supposes *some* numbers :math:`c`, :math:`d` in the ratio of :math:`a` to :math:`b` such that :math:`c < a`, and (consequently) :math:`d < b`. It is however necessary to suppose that :math:`c`, :math:`d` are the *least* numbers in that ratio in order to enable VII. 20 to be used in the proof.]

Then [VII. 20] :math:`a = mc`, and :math:`b = md`, where :math:`m` is some integer.

Therefore

.. math::

   a = cm, \quad b = dm, \qquad \text{[VII. 16]}

and :math:`m` is a common measure of :math:`a`, :math:`b`, though these are prime to one another : which is impossible. :just:`[VII. Def. 12] <VII.def.12>`

Thus the least numbers in the ratio of :math:`a` to :math:`b` cannot be less than :math:`a`, :math:`b` themselves.

Where I have quoted VII. 16 :person:`Heiberg <heiberg>` regards the reference as being to VII. 15. I think the phraseology of the text combined with that of Def. 15 suggests the former rather than the latter.
