Commentary on Proposition VII.24
================================

Back to :ref:`Proposition VII.24 <VII.24>`.

.. folio:: 0332
   :page: 326

If :math:`a`, :math:`b` are both prime to :math:`c`, then :math:`ab`, :math:`c` are prime to one another. The proof is again by reductio ad absurdum. If :math:`ab`, :math:`c` are not prime to one another, let them be measured by :math:`d` and be equal to :math:`md`, :math:`nd`, say, respectively.

Now, since :math:`a`, :math:`c` are prime to one another and :math:`d` measures :math:`c`,

.. math::

   a,\ d\text{ are prime to one another. \qquad [VII. 23]}

But, since :math:`ab = md`,

.. math::

   d : a = b : m. \qquad\text{[VII. 19]}

Therefore [VII. 20] :math:`d` measures :math:`b`, or :math:`b = pd`, say. But :math:`c = nd`.

Therefore :math:`d` measures both :math:`b` and :math:`c`, which are therefore not prime to one another : which is impossible.

Therefore etc.
