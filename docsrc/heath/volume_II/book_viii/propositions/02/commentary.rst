Commentary on Proposition VIII.2
================================

Back to :ref:`Proposition VIII.2 <VIII.2>`.

.. folio:: 0354
   :page: 348

To find a series of numbers in geometrical progression and in the least terms which have a given common ratio (understanding by that term *the ratio of one term to the next*).

Reduce the given ratio to its lowest terms, say, $a : b$. (This can be done by VII. 33.)

Then

$$a^n,\, a^{n-1}b,\, a^{n-2}b^2,\, \dots a^2 b^{n-2},\, a b^{n-1},\, b^n$$

is the required series of numbers if $(n + 1)$ terms are required.

That this is a series of terms with the given common ratio is clear from VII. 17, 18.

That the G.P. is in the smallest terms possible is proved thus.

$a, b$ are prime to one another, since the ratio $a : b$ is in its lowest terms. :just:`[VII. 22] <VII.22>`

Therefore $a^2, b^2$ are prime to one another; so are $a^3, b^3$ and, generally, $a^n, b^n$. :just:`[VII. 27] <VII.27>`

Whence the G.P. is in the smallest possible terms, by VIII. 1.

The Porism observes that, if there are $n$ terms in the series, the extremes are $(n - 1)\text{th}$ powers.
