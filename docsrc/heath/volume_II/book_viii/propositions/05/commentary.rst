Commentary on Proposition VIII.5
================================

Back to :ref:`Proposition VIII.5 <VIII.5>`.

.. folio:: 0360
   :page: 354

If

$$a = cd,\quad b = ef,$$

then $a$ has to $b$ the ratio compounded of $c : e$ and $d : f$.

Take three numbers the least which are continuously in the given ratios.

.. _folio-ii-0361:

.. folio:: 0361
   :page: 355

If $l$ is the L.C.M. of $e$, $d$ and $l = me = nd$, the three numbers are

$$\left. \begin{array}{r} mc,\, me \\ = nd \end{array} \right\},\, nf.\hfill\text{[VIII. 4]}$$

Now

$$\begin{aligned}
dc : de &= c : e\hfill\text{[VII. 17]}\\
&= mc : me = mc : nd.
\end{aligned}$$

Also

$$\begin{aligned}
ed : ef &= d : f\hfill\text{[VII. 17]}\\
&= nd : nf.
\end{aligned}$$

Therefore, :term:`*ex aequali* <ex-aequali>`,

$$\begin{aligned}
cd : ef &= mc : nf\\
&= (\text{ratio compounded of } c : e \text{ and } d : f).
\end{aligned}$$

It will be seen that this proof follows exactly the method of VI. 23 for parallelograms.
