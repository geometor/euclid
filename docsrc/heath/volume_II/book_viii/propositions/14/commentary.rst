Commentary on Proposition VIII.14
=================================

Back to :ref:`Proposition VIII.14 <VIII.14>`.

.. folio:: 0374
   :page: 368

If :math:`a^2` measures :math:`b^2`, :math:`a` measures :math:`b`; and, if :math:`a` measures :math:`b`, :math:`a^2` measures :math:`b^2`.

(1) :math:`a^2`, :math:`ab`, :math:`b^2` are in continued proportion in the ratio of :math:`a` to :math:`b`.

.. math::

   \begin{array}{ll}
   \text{Therefore, since} & a^2 \text{ measures } b^2\text{,} \\
   & a^2 \text{ measures } ab\text{.} & \text{[VIII. 7]} \\
   \text{But} & a^2 : ab = a : b\text{.} \\
   \text{Therefore} & a \text{ measures } b\text{.}
   \end{array}

(2) Since :math:`a` measures :math:`b`, :math:`a^2` measures :math:`ab`.

And :math:`a^2`, :math:`ab`, :math:`b^2` are continuously proportional.

.. math::

   \begin{array}{ll}
   \text{Thus} & ab \text{ measures } b^2\text{.} \\
   \text{And} & a^2 \text{ measures } ab\text{.} \\
   \text{Therefore} & a^2 \text{ measures } b^2\text{.}
   \end{array}

It will be seen that Euclid puts the last step shortly, saying that, since :math:`a^2` measures :math:`ab`, and :math:`a^2`, :math:`ab`, :math:`b^2` are in continued proportion, :math:`a^2` measures :math:`b^2`. The same thing happens in VIII. 15, where the series of terms is one more than here.
