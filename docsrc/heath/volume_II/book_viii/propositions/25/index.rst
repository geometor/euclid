PROPOSITION 25.
===============

.. enunciation::

   If two numbers have to one another the ratio which a cube number has to a cube number, and the first be cube, the second will also be cube.

For let the two numbers :g:`$A$ <0387.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0387.g1:B>` have to one another the ratio which the cube number :g:`$C$ <0387.g1:C>` has to the cube number :g:`$D$ <0387.g1:D>`, and let :g:`$A$ <0387.g1:A>` be cube;

I say that :g:`$B$ <0387.g1:B>` is also cube.

.. _folio-ii-0387:

.. folio:: 0387
   :page: 381

.. subclause::

   For, since :g:`$C$ <0387.g1:C>`, :g:`$D$ <0387.g1:D>` are cube, :g:`$C$ <0387.g1:C>`, :g:`$D$ <0387.g1:D>` are similar solid numbers.

.. assertion::

   Therefore two mean proportional numbers fall between :g:`$C$ <0387.g1:C>`, :g:`$D$ <0387.g1:D>`. :just:`[VIII. 19] <VIII.19>`

.. picture:: 0387.g1.svg
   :alt: Proposition 25

.. subclause::

   And, as many numbers as fall between :g:`$C$ <0387.g1:C>`, :g:`$D$ <0387.g1:D>` in continued proportion, so many will also fall between those which have the same ratio with them; :just:`[VIII. 8] <VIII.8>`

.. assertion::

   so that two mean proportional numbers fall between :g:`$A$ <0387.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0387.g1:B>` also.

Let :g:`$E$ <0387.g1:E>`, :g:`$F$ <0387.g1:F>` so fall.

.. subclause::

   Since, then, the four numbers :g:`$A$ <0387.g1:A>`, :g:`$E$ <0387.g1:E>`, :g:`$F$ <0387.g1:F>`, :g:`$B$ <0387.g1:B>` are in continued proportion, and :g:`$A$ <0387.g1:A>` is cube,

.. assertion:: [VIII. 23] <VIII.23>

   therefore :g:`$B$ <0387.g1:B>` is also cube.

.. qed::

   :just:`Q. E. D.`
