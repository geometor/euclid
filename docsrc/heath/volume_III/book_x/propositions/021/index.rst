PROPOSITION 21.
===============

.. enunciation::

   The rectangle contained by rational straight lines commensurable in square only is irrational, and the side of the square equal to it is irrational. Let the latter be called medial.

For let the rectangle :g:`$AC$ <0054.g1:< A C >>` be contained by the rational straight lines :g:`$AB$ <0054.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$BC$ <0054.g1:/ B C />` commensurable in square only;

.. _folio-iii-0054:

.. folio:: 0054
   :page: 50

I say that :g:`$AC$ <0054.g1:< A C >>` is irrational, and the side of the square equal to it is irrational; and let the latter be called :term:`**medial** <medial>`.

.. picture:: 0054.g1.svg
   :alt: Proposition 21

For on :g:`$AB$ <0054.g1:/ A B />` let the square :g:`$AD$ <0054.g1:< A D >>` be described;

.. assertion:: [X. Def. 4] <X.def.4>

   therefore :g:`$AD$ <0054.g1:< A D >>` is rational.

.. subclause::

   And, since :g:`$AB$ <0054.g1:/ A B />` is incommensurable in length with :g:`$BC$ <0054.g1:/ B C />`, for by hypothesis they are commensurable in square only, while :g:`$AB$ <0054.g1:/ A B />` is equal to :g:`$BD$ <0054.g1:/ B D />`,

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$DB$ <0054.g1:/ D B />` is also incommensurable in length with :g:`$BC$ <0054.g1:/ B C />`.

.. subclause:: [VI. 1] <VI.1>

   And, as :g:`$DB$ <0054.g1:/ D B />` is to :g:`$BC$ <0054.g1:/ B C />`, so is :g:`$AD$ <0054.g1:< A D >>` to :g:`$AC$ <0054.g1:< A C >>`;

.. assertion:: [X. 11] <X.11>

   therefore :g:`$DA$ <0054.g1:< D A >>` is incommensurable with :g:`$AC$ <0054.g1:< A C >>`.

.. subclause::

   But :g:`$DA$ <0054.g1:< D A >>` is rational;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$AC$ <0054.g1:< A C >>` is irrational, so that the side of the square equal to :g:`$AC$ <0054.g1:< A C >>` is also irrational. :just:`[X. Def. 4] <X.def.4>`

.. qed::

   And let the latter be called :term:`**medial** <medial>`. :just:`Q. E. D.`
