PROPOSITION 22.
===============

.. enunciation::

   The square on a medial straight line, if applied to a rational straight line, produces as breadth a straight line rational and incommensurable in length with that to which it is applied.

.. picture:: 0055.g1.svg
   :alt: Proposition 22

Let :g:`$A$ <0055.g1:A>` be medial and :g:`$CB$ <0055.g1:/ C B />` rational, and let a rectangular area :g:`$BD$ <0055.g1:< B D >>` equal to the square on :g:`$A$ <0055.g1:A>` be applied to :g:`$BC$ <0055.g1:/ B C />`, producing :g:`$CD$ <0055.g1:/ C D />` as breadth;

I say that :g:`$CD$ <0055.g1:/ C D />` is rational and incommensurable in length with :g:`$CB$ <0055.g1:/ C B />`.

.. subclause::

   For, since :g:`$A$ <0055.g1:A>` is medial, the square on it is equal to a rectangular area contained by rational straight lines commensurable in square only. :just:`[X. 21] <X.21>`

Let the square on it be equal to :g:`$GF$ <0055.g1:< G F >>`.

.. subclause::

   But the square on it is also equal to :g:`$BD$ <0055.g1:< B D >>`;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$BD$ <0055.g1:< B D >>` is equal to :g:`$GF$ <0055.g1:< G F >>`.

.. subclause::

   But it is also equiangular with it; and in equal and equiangular parallelograms the sides about the equal angles are reciprocally proportional; :just:`[VI. 14] <VI.14>`

.. assertion::

   therefore, proportionally, as :g:`$BC$ <0055.g1:/ B C />` is to :g:`$EG$ <0055.g1:/ E G />`, so is :g:`$EF$ <0055.g1:/ E F />` to :g:`$CD$ <0055.g1:/ C D />`.

.. assertion::

   Therefore also, as the square on :g:`$BC$ <0055.g1:/ B C />` is to the square on :g:`$EG$ <0055.g1:/ E G />`, so is the square on :g:`$EF$ <0055.g1:/ E F />` to the square on :g:`$CD$ <0055.g1:/ C D />`. :just:`[VI. 22] <VI.22>`

.. _folio-iii-0056:

.. folio:: 0056
   :page: 52

.. subclause::

   But the square on :g:`$CB$ <0055.g1:/ C B />` is commensurable with the square on :g:`$EG$ <0055.g1:/ E G />`, for each of these straight lines is rational;

.. assertion::

   therefore the square on :g:`$EF$ <0055.g1:/ E F />` is also commensurable with the square on :g:`$CD$ <0055.g1:/ C D />`. :just:`[X. 11] <X.11>`

.. subclause::

   But the square on :g:`$EF$ <0055.g1:/ E F />` is rational;

.. assertion:: [X. Def. 4] <X.def.4>

   therefore the square on :g:`$CD$ <0055.g1:/ C D />` is also rational;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$CD$ <0055.g1:/ C D />` is rational.

.. subclause::

   And, since :g:`$EF$ <0055.g1:/ E F />` is incommensurable in length with :g:`$EG$ <0055.g1:/ E G />`, for they are commensurable in square only, and, as :g:`$EF$ <0055.g1:/ E F />` is to :g:`$EG$ <0055.g1:/ E G />`, so is the square on :g:`$EF$ <0055.g1:/ E F />` to the rectangle :g:`$FE$ <0055.g1:/ F E />`, :g:`$EG$ <0055.g1:/ E G />`, :just:`[Lemma]`

.. assertion::

   therefore the square on :g:`$EF$ <0055.g1:/ E F />` is incommensurable with the rectangle :g:`$FE$ <0055.g1:/ F E />`, :g:`$EG$ <0055.g1:/ E G />`. :just:`[X. 11] <X.11>`

.. subclause::

   But the square on :g:`$CD$ <0055.g1:/ C D />` is commensurable with the square on :g:`$EF$ <0055.g1:/ E F />`, for the straight lines are rational in square; and the rectangle :g:`$DC$ <0055.g1:/ D C />`, :g:`$CB$ <0055.g1:/ C B />` is commensurable with the rectangle :g:`$FE$ <0055.g1:/ F E />`, :g:`$EG$ <0055.g1:/ E G />`, for they are equal to the square on :g:`$A$ <0055.g1:A>`;

.. assertion::

   therefore the square on :g:`$CD$ <0055.g1:/ C D />` is also incommensurable with the rectangle :g:`$DC$ <0055.g1:/ D C />`, :g:`$CB$ <0055.g1:/ C B />`. :just:`[X. 13] <X.13>`

.. subclause::

   But, as the square on :g:`$CD$ <0055.g1:/ C D />` is to the rectangle :g:`$DC$ <0055.g1:/ D C />`, :g:`$CB$ <0055.g1:/ C B />`, so is :g:`$DC$ <0055.g1:/ D C />` to :g:`$CB$ <0055.g1:/ C B />`; :just:`[Lemma]`

.. assertion:: [X. 11] <X.11>

   therefore :g:`$DC$ <0055.g1:/ D C />` is incommensurable in length with :g:`$CB$ <0055.g1:/ C B />`.

.. qed::

   Therefore :g:`$CD$ <0055.g1:/ C D />` is rational and incommensurable in length with :g:`$CB$ <0055.g1:/ C B />`. :just:`Q. E. D.`
