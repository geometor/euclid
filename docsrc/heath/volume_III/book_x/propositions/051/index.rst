PROPOSITION 51.
===============

.. enunciation::

   To find the fourth binomial straight line.

Let two numbers :g:`$AC$ <0113.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$CB$ <0113.g1:/ C B />` be set out such that :g:`$AB$ <0113.g1:/ A B />` neither has to :g:`$BC$ <0113.g1:/ C B />`, nor yet to :g:`$AC$ <0113.g1:/ A C />`, the ratio which a square number has to a square number.

.. picture:: 0113.g1.svg
   :alt: Proposition 51

Let a rational straight line :g:`$D$ <0113.g1:D>` be set out, and let :g:`$EF$ <0113.g1:/ E F />` be commensurable in length with :g:`$D$ <0113.g1:D>`;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$EF$ <0113.g1:/ E F />` is also rational.

Let it be contrived that, as the number :g:`$BA$ <0113.g1:/ A B />` is to :g:`$AC$ <0113.g1:/ A C />`, so is the square on :g:`$EF$ <0113.g1:/ E F />` to the square on :g:`$FG$ <0113.g1:/ F G />`; :just:`[x. 6, Por.]`

.. assertion::

   therefore the square on :g:`$EF$ <0113.g1:/ E F />` is commensurable with the square on :g:`$FG$ <0113.g1:/ F G />`; :just:`[x. 6]` therefore :g:`$FG$ <0113.g1:/ F G />` is also rational.

.. subclause::

   Now, since :g:`$BA$ <0113.g1:/ A B />` has not to :g:`$AC$ <0113.g1:/ A C />` the ratio which a square number has to a square number,

.. assertion::

   neither has the square on :g:`$EF$ <0113.g1:/ E F />` to the square on :g:`$FG$ <0113.g1:/ F G />` the ratio which a square number has to a square number; therefore :g:`$EF$ <0113.g1:/ E F />` is incommensurable in length with :g:`$FG$ <0113.g1:/ F G />`. :just:`[x. 9]`

.. assertion::

   Therefore :g:`$EF$ <0113.g1:/ E F />`, :g:`$FG$ <0113.g1:/ F G />` are rational straight lines commensurable in square only; so that :g:`$EG$ <0113.g1:/ E G />` is binomial.

I say next that it is also a fourth binomial straight line.

.. subclause::

   For since, as :g:`$BA$ <0113.g1:/ A B />` is to :g:`$AC$ <0113.g1:/ A C />`, so is the square on :g:`$EF$ <0113.g1:/ E F />` to the square on :g:`$FG$ <0113.g1:/ F G />`,

.. assertion::

   therefore the square on :g:`$EF$ <0113.g1:/ E F />` is greater than the square on :g:`$FG$ <0113.g1:/ F G />`.

Let then the squares on :g:`$FG$ <0113.g1:/ F G />`, :g:`$H$ <0113.g1:H>` be equal to the square on :g:`$EF$ <0113.g1:/ E F />`;

.. _folio-iii-0114:

.. folio:: 0114
   :page: 110

.. assertion::

   therefore, :term:`*convertendo* <convertendo>`, as the number :g:`$AB$ <0113.g1:/ A B />` is to :g:`$BC$ <0113.g1:/ C B />`, so is the square on :g:`$EF$ <0113.g1:/ E F />` to the square on :g:`$H$ <0113.g1:H>`. :just:`[v. 19, Por.]`

.. subclause::

   But :g:`$AB$ <0113.g1:/ A B />` has not to :g:`$BC$ <0113.g1:/ C B />` the ratio which a square number has to a square number;

.. assertion::

   therefore neither has the square on :g:`$EF$ <0113.g1:/ E F />` to the square on :g:`$H$ <0113.g1:H>` the ratio which a square number has to a square number.

.. assertion::

   Therefore :g:`$EF$ <0113.g1:/ E F />` is incommensurable in length with :g:`$H$ <0113.g1:H>`; :just:`[x. 9]` therefore the square on :g:`$EF$ <0113.g1:/ E F />` is greater than the square on :g:`$GF$ <0113.g1:/ F G />` by the square on a straight line incommensurable with :g:`$EF$ <0113.g1:/ E F />`.

.. subclause::

   And :g:`$EF$ <0113.g1:/ E F />`, :g:`$FG$ <0113.g1:/ F G />` are rational straight lines commensurable in square only, and :g:`$EF$ <0113.g1:/ E F />` is commensurable in length with :g:`$D$ <0113.g1:D>`.

.. qed::

   Therefore :g:`$EG$ <0113.g1:/ E G />` is a fourth binomial straight line. :just:`Q. E. D.`
