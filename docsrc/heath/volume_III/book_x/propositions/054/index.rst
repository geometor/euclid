PROPOSITION 54.
===============

.. enunciation::

   If an area be contained by a rational straight line and the first binomial, the "side"\ [#f1]_ of the area is the irrational straight line which is called binomial.

For let the area :g:`$AC$ <0121.g1:< A C >>` be contained by the rational straight line :g:`$AB$ <0121.g1:/ A B />` and the first binomial :g:`$AD$ <0121.g1:/ A D />`; I say that the "side" of the area :g:`$AC$ <0121.g1:< A C >>` is the irrational straight line which is called binomial.

For, since :g:`$AD$ <0121.g1:/ A D />` is a first binomial straight line, let it be divided into its terms at :g:`$E$ <0121.g1:E>`, and let :g:`$AE$ <0121.g1:/ A E />` be the greater term.

.. _folio-iii-0121:

.. folio:: 0121
   :page: 117

It is then manifest that :g:`$AE$ <0121.g1:/ A E />`, :g:`$ED$ <0121.g1:/ E D />` are rational straight lines commensurable in square only, the square on :g:`$AE$ <0121.g1:/ A E />` is greater than the square on :g:`$ED$ <0121.g1:/ E D />` by the square on a straight line commensurable with :g:`$AE$ <0121.g1:/ A E />`, and :g:`$AE$ <0121.g1:/ A E />` is commensurable in length with the rational straight line :g:`$AB$ <0121.g1:/ A B />` set out. :just:`[X. Deff. II. 1]`

Let :g:`$ED$ <0121.g1:/ E D />` be :eq:`bisected <0121.g1:/ E F / = 0121.g1:/ F D />` at the point :g:`$F$ <0121.g1:F>`.

.. picture:: 0121.g1.svg
   :alt: Proposition 54

.. subclause::

   Then, since the square on :g:`$AE$ <0121.g1:/ A E />` is greater than the square on :g:`$ED$ <0121.g1:/ E D />` by the square on a straight line commensurable with :g:`$AE$ <0121.g1:/ A E />`,

.. assertion::

   therefore, if there be applied to the greater :g:`$AE$ <0121.g1:/ A E />` a parallelogram equal to the fourth part of the square on the less, that is, to the square on :g:`$EF$ <0121.g1:/ E F />`, and deficient by a square figure, it divides it into commensurable parts. :just:`[X. 17] <X.17>`

Let then the rectangle :g:`$AG$ <0121.g1:/ A G />`, :g:`$GE$ <0121.g1:/ G E />` equal to the square on :g:`$EF$ <0121.g1:/ E F />` be applied to :g:`$AE$ <0121.g1:/ A E />`;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$AG$ <0121.g1:/ A G />` is commensurable in length with :g:`$EG$ <0121.g1:/ G E />`.

Let :g:`$GH$ <0121.g1:/ G H />`, :g:`$EK$ <0121.g1:/ E K />`, :g:`$FL$ <0121.g1:/ F L />` be drawn from :g:`$G$ <0121.g1:G>`, :g:`$E$ <0121.g1:E>`, :g:`$F$ <0121.g1:F>` parallel to either of the straight lines :g:`$AB$ <0121.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$CD$ <0121.g1:/ D C />`; let the square :g:`$SN$ <0121.g1:< S N >>` be constructed :eq:`equal <0121.g1:< S N > = 0121.g1:< A H >>` to the parallelogram :g:`$AH$ <0121.g1:< A H >>`, and the square :g:`$NQ$ <0121.g1:< N Q >>` :eq:`equal <0121.g1:< N Q > = 0121.g1:< G K >>` to :g:`$GK$ <0121.g1:< G K >>`, :just:`[II. 14] <II.14>` and let them be placed so that :g:`$MN$ <0121.g1:/ M N />` is in a straight line with :g:`$NO$ <0121.g1:/ N O />`;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$RN$ <0121.g1:/ R N />` is also in a straight line with :g:`$NP$ <0121.g1:/ N P />`.

And let the parallelogram :g:`$SQ$ <0121.g1:< S Q >>` be completed;

.. assertion:: [Lemma]

   therefore :g:`$SQ$ <0121.g1:< S Q >>` is a square.

.. subclause::

   Now, since the rectangle :g:`$AG$ <0121.g1:/ A G />`, :g:`$GE$ <0121.g1:/ G E />` is equal to the square on :g:`$EF$ <0121.g1:/ E F />`,

.. assertion:: [VI. 17] <VI.17>

   therefore, as :g:`$AG$ <0121.g1:/ A G />` is to :g:`$EF$ <0121.g1:/ E F />`, so is :g:`$FE$ <0121.g1:/ E F />` to :g:`$EG$ <0121.g1:/ G E />`;

.. assertion:: [VI. 1] <VI.1>

   therefore also, as :g:`$AH$ <0121.g1:< A H >>` is to :g:`$EL$ <0121.g1:< E L >>`, so is :g:`$EL$ <0121.g1:< E L >>` to :g:`$KG$ <0121.g1:< G K >>`;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$EL$ <0121.g1:< E L >>` is a mean proportional between :g:`$AH$ <0121.g1:< A H >>`, :g:`$GK$ <0121.g1:< G K >>`.

.. subclause::

   But :g:`$AH$ <0121.g1:< A H >>` is :eq:`equal <0121.g1:< A H > = 0121.g1:< S N >; 0121.g1:< G K > = 0121.g1:< N Q >>` to :g:`$SN$ <0121.g1:< S N >>`, and :g:`$GK$ <0121.g1:< G K >>` to :g:`$NQ$ <0121.g1:< N Q >>`;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$EL$ <0121.g1:< E L >>` is a mean proportional between :g:`$SN$ <0121.g1:< S N >>`, :g:`$NQ$ <0121.g1:< N Q >>`.

.. _folio-iii-0122:

.. folio:: 0122
   :page: 118

.. subclause::

   But :g:`$MR$ <0121.g1:< M R >>` is also a mean proportional between the same :g:`$SN$ <0121.g1:< S N >>`, :g:`$NQ$ <0121.g1:< N Q >>`; :just:`[Lemma]`

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$EL$ <0121.g1:< E L >>` is :eq:`equal <0121.g1:< E L > = 0121.g1:< M R >>` to :g:`$MR$ <0121.g1:< M R >>`,

.. assertion::

   so that it is also :eq:`equal <0121.g1:< E L > = 0121.g1:< P O >>` to :g:`$PO$ <0121.g1:< P O >>`.

.. subclause::

   But :g:`$AH$ <0121.g1:< A H >>`, :g:`$GK$ <0121.g1:< G K >>` are also :eq:`equal <0121.g1:< A H > = 0121.g1:< S N >; 0121.g1:< G K > = 0121.g1:< N Q >>` to :g:`$SN$ <0121.g1:< S N >>`, :g:`$NQ$ <0121.g1:< N Q >>`;

.. assertion::

   therefore the whole :g:`$AC$ <0121.g1:< A C >>` is :eq:`equal <0121.g1:< A C > = 0121.g1:< S Q >>` to the whole :g:`$SQ$ <0121.g1:< S Q >>`, that is, to the square on :g:`$MO$ <0121.g1:/ M O />`;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$MO$ <0121.g1:/ M O />` is the "side" of :g:`$AC$ <0121.g1:< A C >>`.

I say next that :g:`$MO$ <0121.g1:/ M O />` is binomial.

.. subclause::

   For, since :g:`$AG$ <0121.g1:/ A G />` is commensurable with :g:`$GE$ <0121.g1:/ G E />`,

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$AE$ <0121.g1:/ A E />` is also commensurable with each of the straight lines :g:`$AG$ <0121.g1:/ A G />`, :g:`$GE$ <0121.g1:/ G E />`. :just:`[X. 15] <X.15>`

.. subclause::

   But :g:`$AE$ <0121.g1:/ A E />` is also, by hypothesis, commensurable with :g:`$AB$ <0121.g1:/ A B />`;

.. assertion:: [X. 12] <X.12>

   therefore :g:`$AG$ <0121.g1:/ A G />`, :g:`$GE$ <0121.g1:/ G E />` are also commensurable with :g:`$AB$ <0121.g1:/ A B />`.

.. subclause::

   And :g:`$AB$ <0121.g1:/ A B />` is rational;

.. assertion::

   therefore each of the straight lines :g:`$AG$ <0121.g1:/ A G />`, :g:`$GE$ <0121.g1:/ G E />` is also rational;

.. assertion::

   therefore each of the rectangles :g:`$AH$ <0121.g1:< A H >>`, :g:`$GK$ <0121.g1:< G K >>` is rational, :just:`[X. 19] <X.19>` and :g:`$AH$ <0121.g1:< A H >>` is commensurable with :g:`$GK$ <0121.g1:< G K >>`.

.. subclause::

   But :g:`$AH$ <0121.g1:< A H >>` is :eq:`equal <0121.g1:< A H > = 0121.g1:< S N >; 0121.g1:< G K > = 0121.g1:< N Q >>` to :g:`$SN$ <0121.g1:< S N >>`, and :g:`$GK$ <0121.g1:< G K >>` to :g:`$NQ$ <0121.g1:< N Q >>`;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$SN$ <0121.g1:< S N >>`, :g:`$NQ$ <0121.g1:< N Q >>`, that is, the squares on :g:`$MN$ <0121.g1:/ M N />`, :g:`$NO$ <0121.g1:/ N O />`, are rational and commensurable.

.. subclause::

   And, since :g:`$AE$ <0121.g1:/ A E />` is incommensurable in length with :g:`$ED$ <0121.g1:/ E D />`, while :g:`$AE$ <0121.g1:/ A E />` is commensurable with :g:`$AG$ <0121.g1:/ A G />`, and :g:`$DE$ <0121.g1:/ E D />` is commensurable with :g:`$EF$ <0121.g1:/ E F />`,

.. assertion:: [X. 13] <X.13>

   therefore :g:`$AG$ <0121.g1:/ A G />` is also incommensurable with :g:`$EF$ <0121.g1:/ E F />`,

.. assertion:: [VI. 1, X. 11] <VI.1, X.11>

   so that :g:`$AH$ <0121.g1:< A H >>` is also incommensurable with :g:`$EL$ <0121.g1:< E L >>`.

.. subclause::

   But :g:`$AH$ <0121.g1:< A H >>` is :eq:`equal <0121.g1:< A H > = 0121.g1:< S N >; 0121.g1:< E L > = 0121.g1:< M R >>` to :g:`$SN$ <0121.g1:< S N >>`, and :g:`$EL$ <0121.g1:< E L >>` to :g:`$MR$ <0121.g1:< M R >>`;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$SN$ <0121.g1:< S N >>` is also incommensurable with :g:`$MR$ <0121.g1:< M R >>`.

.. subclause:: [VI. 1] <VI.1>

   But, as :g:`$SN$ <0121.g1:< S N >>` is to :g:`$MR$ <0121.g1:< M R >>`, so is :g:`$PN$ <0121.g1:/ N P />` to :g:`$NR$ <0121.g1:/ R N />`;

.. assertion:: [X. 11] <X.11>

   therefore :g:`$PN$ <0121.g1:/ N P />` is incommensurable with :g:`$NR$ <0121.g1:/ R N />`.

.. subclause::

   But :g:`$PN$ <0121.g1:/ N P />` is :eq:`equal <0121.g1:/ N P / = 0121.g1:/ M N /; 0121.g1:/ R N / = 0121.g1:/ N O />` to :g:`$MN$ <0121.g1:/ M N />`, and :g:`$NR$ <0121.g1:/ R N />` to :g:`$NO$ <0121.g1:/ N O />`;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$MN$ <0121.g1:/ M N />` is incommensurable with :g:`$NO$ <0121.g1:/ N O />`.

.. subclause::

   And the square on :g:`$MN$ <0121.g1:/ M N />` is commensurable with the square on :g:`$NO$ <0121.g1:/ N O />`, and each is rational;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$MN$ <0121.g1:/ M N />`, :g:`$NO$ <0121.g1:/ N O />` are rational straight lines commensurable in square only.

.. _folio-iii-0123:

.. folio:: 0123
   :page: 119

.. qed::

   Therefore :g:`$MO$ <0121.g1:/ M O />` is binomial :just:`[X. 36] <X.36>` and the "side" of :g:`$AC$ <0121.g1:< A C >>`. :just:`Q. E. D.`

.. [#f1] **"side."** I use the word "side" in the sense explained in the note on x. Def. 4 (p. 13 above), i.e. as short for "side of a square equal to." The Greek is ἡ τὸ χωρίον :term:`δυναμένη <dynamene>`.
