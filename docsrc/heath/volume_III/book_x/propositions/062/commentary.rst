Commentary on Proposition X.62
==============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition X.62 <X.62>`.

.. _folio-iii-0143:

.. folio:: 0143
   :page: 139

We have to prove that [cf. x. 38]

.. math::

   \frac{1}{\sigma}\left(k^{\frac{1}{4}}\rho + \frac{\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho}{k^{\frac{1}{4}}}\right)^2

is a :term:`*third binomial* <third-binomial>` straight line.
Take :math:`x, y, z` such that

.. math::

   \begin{aligned}
   \sigma x &= k^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho^2,\\
   \sigma y &= \frac{\lambda\rho^2}{k^{\frac{1}{2}}},\\
   \sigma \cdot 2z &= 2\sqrt{\lambda}\cdot \rho^2.
   \end{aligned}

(:math:`\alpha`) Now :math:`k^{\frac{1}{4}}\rho, \frac{\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho}{k^{\frac{1}{4}}}` are medial straight lines commensurable in square only and containing a medial rectangle. [x. 38]
The sum of the squares on them, or :math:`\sigma(x + y)`, is medial;
therefore

.. math::

   (x + y)\text{ is rational and } \smile \sigma \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(1).

And :math:`\sigma \cdot 2z` being medial also,

.. math::

   2z\text{ is rational and } \smile \sigma \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(2).

Now

.. math::

   k^{\frac{1}{4}}\rho : \frac{\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho}{k^{\frac{1}{4}}} = (k^{\frac{1}{4}}\rho)^2 : k^{\frac{1}{4}}\rho \cdot \frac{\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho}{k^{\frac{1}{4}}}

.. math::

   = \sigma x : \sigma z,

whence :math:`\sigma x \smile \sigma z.`
But :math:`(k^{\frac{1}{4}}\rho)^2 \sim \left\{(k^{\frac{1}{4}}\rho)^2 + \left(\frac{\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho}{k^{\frac{1}{4}}}\right)^2\right\},` or :math:`\sigma x \sim \sigma(x + y),` and :math:`\sigma z \sim \sigma \cdot 2z;`
therefore

.. math::

   \sigma(x + y) \smile \sigma \cdot 2z,

or

.. math::

   (x + y) \smile 2z \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(3).

Hence [(1), (2), (3)] :math:`(x + y) + 2z` is a binomial straight line\ :math:`\dots\dots\dots\dots(4).`

(:math:`\beta`) As before,

.. math::

   (x + y) > 2z,

and

.. math::

   x \sim y.

Also

.. math::

   xy = z^2.

Therefore [x. 17]

.. math::

   \sqrt{(x + y)^2 - (2z)^2} \sim (x + y).

And [(1), (2)] neither :math:`(x + y)` nor :math:`2z` is :math:`\sim \sigma.`
Therefore :math:`(x + y) + 2z` is a :term:`*third binomial* <third-binomial>` straight line.
Obviously

.. math::

   (x + y) + 2z = \frac{\rho^2}{\sigma}\left\{\frac{k + \lambda}{\sqrt{k}} + 2\sqrt{\lambda}\right\}.
