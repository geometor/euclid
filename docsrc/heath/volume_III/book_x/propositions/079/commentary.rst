Commentary on Proposition X.79
==============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition X.79 <X.79>`.

.. folio:: 0172
   :page: 168

This proposition proves the equivalent of the well-known theorem of surds that, if :math:`a - \sqrt b = x - \sqrt y`, then :math:`a = x, b = y`; and, if :math:`\sqrt a - \sqrt b = \sqrt x - \sqrt y`, then :math:`a = x, b = y`.

The method of proof corresponds to that of x. 42 for positive signs.

Suppose, if possible, that an *apotome* can be expressed as :math:`(x - y)` and also as :math:`(x' - y')`, where :math:`x, y` are rational straight lines commensurable in square only, and :math:`x', y'` are so also.

Of :math:`x, x'`, let :math:`x` be the greater.

Now, since

.. math::

   x - y = x' - y',

.. math::

   x^2 + y^2 - (x'^2 + y'^2) = 2xy - 2x'y'.

But :math:`(x^2 + y^2), (x'^2 + y'^2)` are both rational, so that their difference is a rational area.

On the other hand, :math:`2xy, 2x'y'` are both *medial* areas, being of the form :math:`\sqrt k \cdot \rho^2`; therefore the difference between two medial areas is rational: which is impossible [x. 26].
