Commentary on Proposition X.81
==============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition X.81 <X.81>`.

.. folio:: 0176
   :page: 172

As the irrationality of the :term:`*second apotome of a medial* <second-apotome-of-a-medial-straight-line>` straight line was deduced [x. 75] from the irrationality of an apotome, so the present theorem is reduced to x. 79.

Suppose, if possible, that :math:`(x - y)`, :math:`(x' - y')` are the *same* second apotome of a medial straight line; and let (say) :math:`x` be greater than :math:`x'`.

Apply :math:`(x^2 + y^2)`, :math:`2xy` and also :math:`(x'^2 + y'^2)`, :math:`2x'y'` to a rational straight line :math:`\sigma`, i.e. put

.. math::

   \left. \begin{aligned} x^2 + y^2 &= \sigma u \\ 2xy &= \sigma v \end{aligned} \right\} \quad\text{and}\quad \left. \begin{aligned} x'^2 + y'^2 &= \sigma u' \\ 2x'y' &= \sigma v' \end{aligned} \right\}.

Dealing with :math:`(x - y)` first, we have: :math:`(x^2 + y^2)` is a medial area, and :math:`2xy` is also a medial area. Therefore :math:`u`, :math:`v` are both rational and :math:`\smile \sigma` ............................................(1).

.. math::

   \begin{aligned}
   &\text{Also, since } x \underset{-}{\sim} y, & x &\smile y, \\
   &\text{so that } & x^2 &\smile xy, \\
   &\text{whence, as usual, } & x^2 + y^2 &\smile 2xy, \\
   &\text{that is, } & \sigma u &\smile \sigma v, \\
   &\text{and therefore } & u &\smile v \text{ .....................................................(2).}
   \end{aligned}

Thus [(1) and (2)] :math:`u`, :math:`v` are rational and :math:`\underset{-}{\sim}`, so that :math:`(u - v)` is an *apotome*. Similarly :math:`(u' - v')` is proved to be the same apotome. Hence this apotome is formed in two ways: which contradicts x. 79. Therefore the original hypothesis is false, and *a second apotome of a medial straight line is uniquely formed*.
