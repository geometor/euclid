PROPOSITION 1.
==============

.. enunciation::

   A part of a straight line cannot be in the plane of reference\ [#f1]_ and a part in a plane more elevated.\ [#f2]_

For, if possible, let a part :g:`$AB$ <0276.g1:/ A B />` of the straight line :g:`$ABC$ <0276.g1:/ A B C />` be in the plane of reference, and a part :g:`$BC$ <0276.g1:/ B C />` in a plane more elevated.

.. picture:: 0276.g1.svg
   :alt: Book XI, Proposition 1

There will then be in the plane of reference some straight line continuous with :g:`$AB$ <0276.g1:/ A B />` in a straight line.

Let it be :g:`$BD$ <0276.g1:/ B D />`;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$AB$ <0276.g1:/ A B />` is a common segment of the two straight lines :g:`$ABC$ <0276.g1:/ A B C />`, :g:`$ABD$ <0276.g1:/ A B D />`:

.. assertion::

   which is impossible, inasmuch as, if we describe a circle with centre :g:`$B$ <0276.g1:B>` and distance :g:`$AB$ <0276.g1:/ A B />`, the diameters will cut off unequal circumferences of the circle.

.. assertion::

   Therefore a part of a straight line cannot be in the plane of reference, and a part in a plane more elevated.

.. qed::

   :just:`Q. E. D.`

.. [#f1] **the plane of reference,** :term:`τὸ ὑποκείμενον ἐπίπεδον <plane-of-reference>`, the plane laid down or assumed.

.. [#f2] **more elevated,** μετεωροτέρῳ.
