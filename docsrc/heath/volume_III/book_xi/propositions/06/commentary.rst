Commentary on Proposition XI.6
==============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition XI.6 <XI.6>`.

.. folio:: 0288
   :page: 284

If anyone wishes to convince himself of the real necessity for some general agreement as to the order in which propositions in elementary geometry should be taken, let him contemplate the hopeless result of too much independence on the part of editors in the matter of this proposition and its converse, XI. 8.

:person:`Legendre <legendre>` adopts a different, and elegant, method of proof; but he applies it to XI. 8, which he gives first, and then deduces XI. 6 from it by *reductio ad absurdum*. Dr :person:`Mehler <mehler>` uses :person:`Legendre <legendre>`'s method of proof but applies it to

XI. 6, and then gives XI. 8 as a deduction from it. Lardner follows :person:`Legendre <legendre>`. :person:`Holgate <holgate>`, the editor of a recent American book, gives Euclid's proof of XI. 6 and deduces XI. 8 by :term:`*reductio ad absurdum* <reductio-ad-absurdum>`. His countrymen, :person:`Schultze <schultze>` and :person:`Sevenoak <sevenoak>`, give XI. 8 first, but put it after, and deduce it from, Eucl. XI. 10; they then give XI. 6, practically as a deduction from XI. 8 by *reductio ad absurdum*, after a proposition corresponding to Eucl. XI. 11 and 12, and a corollary to the effect that through a given point one and only one perpendicular can be drawn to a given plane.

We will now give the proof of XI. 6 by :person:`Legendre <legendre>`'s method (adopted by Smith and Bryant as well as by :person:`Mehler <mehler>`).

Let :g:`$AB$ <0288.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$CD$ <0288.g1:/ C D />` be both perpendicular to the same plane :g:`$MN$ <0288.g1:< M N >>`.

Join :g:`$BD$ <0288.g1:/ B D />`.

Now, since :g:`$BD$ <0288.g1:/ B D />` meets :g:`$AB$ <0288.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$CD$ <0288.g1:/ C D />`, both of which are perpendicular to the plane :g:`$MN$ <0288.g1:< M N >>` in which :g:`$BD$ <0288.g1:/ B D />` is, the angles :g:`$ABD$ <0288.g1:< A B D )>`, :g:`$CDB$ <0288.g1:< C D B )>` are :eq:`right angles <0288.g1:< A B D ) = 0288.g1:< C D B )>`.

:g:`$AB$ <0288.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$CD$ <0288.g1:/ C D />` will therefore be parallel *provided that they are in the same plane*.

   :alt: Book XI - Proposition 6, Legendre's proof

Through :g:`$D$ <0288.g1:D>` draw :g:`$EDF$ <0288.g1:/ E D F />`, in the plane :g:`$MN$ <0288.g1:< M N >>`, at right angles to :g:`$BD$ <0288.g1:/ B D />`, and make :g:`$ED$ <0288.g1:/ E D />` :eq:`equal <0288.g1:/ E D / = 0288.g1:/ D F />` to :g:`$DF$ <0288.g1:/ D F />`.

Join :g:`$BE$ <0288.g1:/ B E />`, :g:`$BF$ <0288.g1:/ B F />`, :g:`$AE$ <0288.g1:/ A E />`, :g:`$AD$ <0288.g1:/ A D />`, :g:`$AF$ <0288.g1:/ A F />`. Then the triangles :g:`$BDE$ <0288.g1:< B D E >>`, :g:`$BDF$ <0288.g1:< B D F >>` are :eq:`equal <0288.g1:< B D E > = 0288.g1:< B D F >>` in all respects (by I. 4), so that

.. container:: center

   :g:`$BE$ <0288.g1:/ B E />` is :eq:`equal <0288.g1:/ B E / = 0288.g1:/ B F />` to :g:`$BF$ <0288.g1:/ B F />`.

It follows, since the angles :g:`$ABE$ <0288.g1:< A B E )>`, :g:`$ABF$ <0288.g1:< A B F )>` are :eq:`right <0288.g1:< A B E ) = 0288.g1:< A B F )>`, that the triangles :g:`$ABE$ <0288.g1:< A B E >>`, :g:`$ABF$ <0288.g1:< A B F >>` are :eq:`equal <0288.g1:< A B E > = 0288.g1:< A B F >>` in all respects, and

.. container:: center

   :g:`$AE$ <0288.g1:/ A E />` is :eq:`equal <0288.g1:/ A E / = 0288.g1:/ A F />` to :g:`$AF$ <0288.g1:/ A F />`.

[:person:`Mehler <mehler>` now argues elegantly thus. If :g:`$CE$ <0288.g1:/ C E />`, :g:`$CF$ <0288.g1:/ C F />` be also joined, it is clear that

.. container:: center

   :g:`$CE$ <0288.g1:/ C E />` is :eq:`equal <0288.g1:/ C E / = 0288.g1:/ C F />` to :g:`$CF$ <0288.g1:/ C F />`.

Hence each of the four points :g:`$A$ <0288.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0288.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0288.g1:C>`, :g:`$D$ <0288.g1:D>` is equidistant from the two points :g:`$E$ <0288.g1:E>`, :g:`$F$ <0288.g1:F>`. *Therefore the points* :g:`$A$ <0288.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0288.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0288.g1:C>`, :g:`$D$ <0288.g1:D>` *are in one plane*, so that :g:`$AB$ <0288.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$CD$ <0288.g1:/ C D />` are parallel. If, however, we do not use the locus of points equidistant from two fixed points, we proceed as follows.]

The triangles :g:`$AED$ <0288.g1:< A E D >>`, :g:`$AFD$ <0288.g1:< A F D >>` have their sides :eq:`equal <0288.g1:/ A E / = 0288.g1:/ A F /; 0288.g1:/ E D / = 0288.g1:/ D F />` respectively; hence [I. 8] the angles :g:`$ADE$ <0288.g1:< A D E )>`, :g:`$ADF$ <0288.g1:< A D F )>` are :eq:`equal <0288.g1:< A D E ) = 0288.g1:< A D F )>`, so that :g:`$ED$ <0288.g1:/ E D />` is at :eq:`right angles <0288.g1:< A D E ) = 0288.g1:< A D F )>` to :g:`$AD$ <0288.g1:/ A D />`.

Thus :g:`$ED$ <0288.g1:/ E D />` is at right angles to :g:`$BD$ <0288.g1:/ B D />`, :g:`$AD$ <0288.g1:/ A D />`, :g:`$CD$ <0288.g1:/ C D />`; therefore :g:`$CD$ <0288.g1:/ C D />` is in the plane through :g:`$AD$ <0288.g1:/ A D />`, :g:`$BD$ <0288.g1:/ B D />`. :just:`[XI. 5] <XI.5>`

But :g:`$AB$ <0288.g1:/ A B />` is in that same plane; :just:`[XI. 2] <XI.2>` therefore :g:`$AB$ <0288.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$CD$ <0288.g1:/ C D />` are in the same plane.

And the angles :g:`$ABD$ <0288.g1:< A B D )>`, :g:`$CDB$ <0288.g1:< C D B )>` are :eq:`right <0288.g1:< A B D ) = 0288.g1:< C D B )>`; therefore :g:`$AB$ <0288.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$CD$ <0288.g1:/ C D />` are parallel.
