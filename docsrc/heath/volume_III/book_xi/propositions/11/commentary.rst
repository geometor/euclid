Commentary on Proposition XI.11
===============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition XI.11 <XI.11>`.

.. folio:: 0297
   :page: 293

The text-books differ in the *form* which they give to this proposition rather than in substance. They commonly assume the construction of a *plane* through the point :g:`$A$ <0297.g1:A>` at right angles to any straight line :g:`$BC$ <0297.g1:/ B C />` in the given plane (the construction being effected in the manner shown at the end of the note on XI. 5 above). The advantage of this method is that it enables a perpendicular to be drawn from a point *in* the plane also, by the same construction. (Where the letters for the two figures differ, those referring to the second figure are put in brackets.)

.. picture:: 0297.g1.svg
   :alt: Proposition 11, Alternative Method

We can include the construction of the plane through :g:`$A$ <0297.g1:A>` perpendicular to :g:`$BC$ <0297.g1:/ B C />`, and make the whole into one proposition, thus.

:g:`$BC$ <0297.g1:/ B C />` being any straight line in the given plane :g:`$MN$ <0297.g1:< M N >>`, draw :g:`$AD$ <0297.g1:/ A D />` perpendicular to :g:`$BC$ <0297.g1:/ B C />`. In any plane passing through :g:`$BC$ <0297.g1:/ B C />` but not through :g:`$A$ <0297.g1:A>` draw :g:`$DE$ <0297.g1:/ D E />` at right angles to :g:`$BC$ <0297.g1:/ B C />`. Through :g:`$DA$ <0297.g1:/ D A />`, :g:`$DE$ <0297.g1:/ D E />` draw a plane; this will intersect the given plane :g:`$MN$ <0297.g1:< M N >>` in a straight line, as :g:`$FD$ <0297.g1:/ F D />` (:g:`$AD$ <0297.g1:/ A D />`). In the plane :g:`$AG$ <0297.g1:< A G >>` draw :g:`$AH$ <0297.g1:/ A H />` perpendicular to :g:`$FG$ <0297.g1:/ F G />` (:g:`$AD$ <0297.g1:/ A D />`). Then :g:`$AH$ <0297.g1:/ A H />` is the perpendicular required.

.. folio:: 0298
   :page: 294

In the plane :g:`$MN$ <0297.g1:< M N >>`, through :g:`$H$ <0297.g1:H>` in the first figure and :g:`$A$ <0297.g1:A>` in the second, draw :g:`$KL$ <0297.g1:/ K L />` parallel to :g:`$BC$ <0297.g1:/ B C />`. Now, since :g:`$BC$ <0297.g1:/ B C />` is perpendicular to both :g:`$DA$ <0297.g1:/ D A />` and :g:`$DE$ <0297.g1:/ D E />`, :g:`$BC$ <0297.g1:/ B C />` is perpendicular to the plane :g:`$AG$ <0297.g1:< A G >>`. :just:`[XI. 4] <XI.4>` Therefore :g:`$KL$ <0297.g1:/ K L />`, being parallel to :g:`$BC$ <0297.g1:/ B C />`, is also perpendicular to the plane :g:`$AG$ <0297.g1:< A G >>` :just:`[XI. 8] <XI.8>`, and therefore to :g:`$AH$ <0297.g1:/ A H />` which meets it and is in that plane. Therefore :g:`$AH$ <0297.g1:/ A H />` is perpendicular to both :g:`$FD$ <0297.g1:/ F D />` (:g:`$AD$ <0297.g1:/ A D />`) and :g:`$KL$ <0297.g1:/ K L />` at their point of intersection. Therefore :g:`$AH$ <0297.g1:/ A H />` is perpendicular to the plane :g:`$MN$ <0297.g1:< M N >>`.

Thus we have solved the problem in XI. 12 as well as that in XI. 11; and this direct method of drawing a perpendicular to a plane from a point *in* it is obviously preferable to Euclid's method by which the construction of a perpendicular to a plane from a point *without* it is assumed, and a line is merely drawn from a point in the plane parallel to the perpendicular obtained in XI. 11.
