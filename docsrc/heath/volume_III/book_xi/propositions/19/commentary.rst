Commentary on Proposition XI.19
===============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition XI.19 <XI.19>`.

.. folio:: 0309
   :page: 305

:person:`Legendre <legendre>`, followed by other writers already quoted, uses a preliminary proposition equivalent to Euclid's definition of planes at right angles to one another.

*If two planes are perpendicular to one another, a straight line drawn in one of them perpendicular to their common section will be perpendicular to the other.*

Let the perpendicular planes :g:`$CE$ <0308.g1:< C E >>`, :g:`$MN$ <0308.g1:< M N >>` (figure of last note) intersect in :g:`$CD$ <0308.g1:/ C D />`, and let :g:`$AB$ <0308.g1:/ A B />` be drawn in :g:`$CE$ <0308.g1:< C E >>` perpendicular to :g:`$CD$ <0308.g1:/ C D />`.

In the plane :g:`$MN$ <0308.g1:< M N >>` draw :g:`$BF$ <0308.g1:/ B F />` at right angles to :g:`$CD$ <0308.g1:/ C D />`.

Then, since the planes are perpendicular, the angle :g:`$ABF$ <0308.g1:< A B F )>` (their *inclination*) is a right angle.

Therefore :g:`$AB$ <0308.g1:/ A B />` is perpendicular to both :g:`$CD$ <0308.g1:/ C D />` and :g:`$BF$ <0308.g1:/ B F />`, and therefore to the plane :g:`$MN$ <0308.g1:< M N >>`.

We are now in a position to prove XI. 19, viz. *If two planes be perpendicular to a third, their intersection is also perpendicular to that third plane.*

Let each of the two planes :g:`$AC$ <0309.g1:< A C >>`, :g:`$AD$ <0309.g1:< A D >>` intersecting in :g:`$AB$ <0309.g1:/ A B />` be perpendicular to the plane :g:`$MN$ <0309.g1:< M N >>`. Let :g:`$AC$ <0309.g1:< A C >>`, :g:`$AD$ <0309.g1:< A D >>` intersect :g:`$MN$ <0309.g1:< M N >>` in :g:`$BC$ <0309.g1:/ B C />`, :g:`$BD$ <0309.g1:/ B D />` respectively. In the plane :g:`$MN$ <0309.g1:< M N >>` draw :g:`$BE$ <0309.g1:/ B E />` at right angles to :g:`$BC$ <0309.g1:/ B C />` and :g:`$BF$ <0309.g1:/ B F />` at right angles to :g:`$BD$ <0309.g1:/ B D />`.

.. picture:: 0309.g1.svg
   :alt: Book XI, Proposition 19, Heath Commentary Diagram

Now, since the planes :g:`$AC$ <0309.g1:< A C >>`, :g:`$MN$ <0309.g1:< M N >>` are at right angles, and :g:`$BE$ <0309.g1:/ B E />` is drawn in the latter perpendicular to :g:`$BC$ <0309.g1:/ B C />`, :g:`$BE$ <0309.g1:/ B E />` is perpendicular to the plane :g:`$AC$ <0309.g1:< A C >>`. Hence :g:`$AB$ <0309.g1:/ A B />` is perpendicular to :g:`$BE$ <0309.g1:/ B E />`. :just:`[XI. 4] <XI.4>` Similarly :g:`$AB$ <0309.g1:/ A B />` is perpendicular to :g:`$BF$ <0309.g1:/ B F />`. Therefore :g:`$AB$ <0309.g1:/ A B />` is perpendicular to the plane through :g:`$BE$ <0309.g1:/ B E />`, :g:`$BF$ <0309.g1:/ B F />`, i.e. to the plane :g:`$MN$ <0309.g1:< M N >>`.

An useful problem is that of drawing a common perpendicular to two straight lines not in one plane, and in connexion with this the following proposition may be given.

.. _folio-iii-0310:

.. folio:: 0310
   :page: 306

*Given a plane and a straight line not perpendicular to it, one plane, and only one, can be drawn through the straight line perpendicular to the plane.*

Let :g:`$AB$ <0310.g1:/ A B />` be the given straight line, :g:`$MN$ <0310.g1:< M N >>` the given plane. From any point :g:`$C$ <0310.g1:C>` in :g:`$AB$ <0310.g1:/ A B />` draw :g:`$CD$ <0310.g1:/ C D />` perpendicular to the plane :g:`$MN$ <0310.g1:< M N >>`. Through :g:`$AB$ <0310.g1:/ A B />` and :g:`$CD$ <0310.g1:/ C D />` draw a plane :g:`$AE$ <0310.g1:< A E >>`. Then the plane :g:`$AE$ <0310.g1:< A E >>` is perpendicular to the plane :g:`$MN$ <0310.g1:< M N >>`. :just:`[XI. 18] <XI.18>`

.. picture:: 0310.g1.svg
   :alt: Book XI, Proposition 19, Lemma 1 Diagram

If any other plane could be drawn through :g:`$AB$ <0310.g1:/ A B />` perpendicular to :g:`$MN$ <0310.g1:< M N >>`, the intersection :g:`$AB$ <0310.g1:/ A B />` of the two planes perpendicular to :g:`$MN$ <0310.g1:< M N >>` would itself be perpendicular to :g:`$MN$ <0310.g1:< M N >>`: :just:`[XI. 19] <XI.19>` which contradicts the hypothesis.

*To draw a common perpendicular to two straight lines not in the same plane.*

Let :g:`$AB$ <0310.g2:/ A B />`, :g:`$CD$ <0310.g2:/ C D />` be the given straight lines. Through :g:`$CD$ <0310.g2:/ C D />` draw the plane :g:`$MN$ <0310.g2:< M N >>` parallel to :g:`$AB$ <0310.g2:/ A B />` (Prop. 2 in note to XI. 15). Through :g:`$AB$ <0310.g2:/ A B />` draw the plane :g:`$AF$ <0310.g2:< A F >>` perpendicular to the plane :g:`$MN$ <0310.g2:< M N >>` (see the last preceding proposition).

.. picture:: 0310.g2.svg
   :alt: Book XI, Proposition 19, Lemma 2 Diagram

Let the planes :g:`$AF$ <0310.g2:< A F >>`, :g:`$MN$ <0310.g2:< M N >>` intersect in :g:`$EF$ <0310.g2:/ E F />`, and let :g:`$EF$ <0310.g2:/ E F />` meet :g:`$CD$ <0310.g2:/ C D />` in :g:`$G$ <0310.g2:G>`. From :g:`$G$ <0310.g2:G>`, in the plane :g:`$AF$ <0310.g2:< A F >>`, draw :g:`$GH$ <0310.g2:/ G H />` at right angles to :g:`$EF$ <0310.g2:/ E F />`, meeting :g:`$AB$ <0310.g2:/ A B />` in :g:`$H$ <0310.g2:H>`. :g:`$GH$ <0310.g2:/ G H />` is then the required perpendicular. For :g:`$AB$ <0310.g2:/ A B />` is parallel to :g:`$EF$ <0310.g2:/ E F />` (Prop. 4 in note to XI. 15); therefore :g:`$GH$ <0310.g2:/ G H />`, being perpendicular to :g:`$EF$ <0310.g2:/ E F />`, is also perpendicular to :g:`$AB$ <0310.g2:/ A B />`. But, the plane :g:`$AF$ <0310.g2:< A F >>` being perpendicular to the plane :g:`$MN$ <0310.g2:< M N >>`, and :g:`$GH$ <0310.g2:/ G H />` being perpendicular to :g:`$EF$ <0310.g2:/ E F />`, their intersection, :g:`$GH$ <0310.g2:/ G H />` is perpendicular to the plane :g:`$MN$ <0310.g2:< M N >>`, and therefore to :g:`$CD$ <0310.g2:/ C D />`. Therefore :g:`$GH$ <0310.g2:/ G H />` is perpendicular to both :g:`$AB$ <0310.g2:/ A B />` and :g:`$CD$ <0310.g2:/ C D />`.

*Only one common perpendicular can be drawn to two straight lines not in one plane.* For, if possible, let :g:`$KL$ <0310.g2:/ K L />` also be perpendicular to both :g:`$AB$ <0310.g2:/ A B />` and :g:`$CD$ <0310.g2:/ C D />`. Let the plane through :g:`$KL$ <0310.g2:/ K L />`, :g:`$AB$ <0310.g2:/ A B />` meet the plane :g:`$MN$ <0310.g2:< M N >>` in :g:`$LQ$ <0310.g2:/ L Q />`. Then :g:`$AB$ <0310.g2:/ A B />` is parallel to :g:`$LQ$ <0310.g2:/ L Q />` (Prop. 4 in note to XI. 15), so that :g:`$KL$ <0310.g2:/ K L />`, being perpendicular to :g:`$AB$ <0310.g2:/ A B />`, is also perpendicular to :g:`$LQ$ <0310.g2:/ L Q />`. Therefore :g:`$KL$ <0310.g2:/ K L />` is perpendicular to both :g:`$CL$ <0310.g2:/ C L />` and :g:`$LQ$ <0310.g2:/ L Q />`, and consequently to the plane :g:`$MN$ <0310.g2:< M N >>`. But, if :g:`$KP$ <0310.g2:/ K P />` be drawn in the plane :g:`$AF$ <0310.g2:< A F >>` perpendicular to :g:`$EF$ <0310.g2:/ E F />`, :g:`$KP$ <0310.g2:/ K P />` is also perpendicular to the plane :g:`$MN$ <0310.g2:< M N >>`.

.. _folio-iii-0311:

.. folio:: 0311
   :page: 307

Thus there are two perpendiculars from the point :g:`$K$ <0310.g2:K>` to the plane :g:`$MN$ <0310.g2:< M N >>`: which is impossible.

:person:`Rausenberger <rausenberger>`’s construction for the same problem is more elegant. Draw, he says, through each straight line a plane parallel to the other. Then draw through each straight line a plane perpendicular to the plane through the other. The two planes last drawn will intersect in a straight line, and this straight line is the common perpendicular required.

.. picture:: 0311.g1.svg
   :alt: Book XI, Proposition 19, Rausenberger's Construction Diagram

The form of the construction best suited for examination purposes, because the most self-contained, is doubtless that given by Smith and Bryant.

Let :g:`$AB$ <0311.g2:/ A B />`, :g:`$CD$ <0311.g2:/ C D />` be the two given straight lines.

Through any point :g:`$E$ <0311.g2:E>` in :g:`$CD$ <0311.g2:/ C D />` draw :g:`$EF$ <0311.g2:/ E F />` parallel to :g:`$AB$ <0311.g2:/ A B />`.

From any point :g:`$G$ <0311.g2:G>` in :g:`$AB$ <0311.g2:/ A B />` draw :g:`$GH$ <0311.g2:/ G H />` perpendicular to the plane :g:`$CDF$ <0311.g2:< C D F >>`, meeting the plane in :g:`$H$ <0311.g2:H>`.

Through :g:`$H$ <0311.g2:H>` in the plane :g:`$CDF$ <0311.g2:< C D F >>` draw :g:`$HK$ <0311.g2:/ H K />` parallel to :g:`$FE$ <0311.g2:/ F E />` or :g:`$AB$ <0311.g2:/ A B />`, to cut :g:`$CD$ <0311.g2:/ C D />` in :g:`$K$ <0311.g2:K>`.

Then, since :g:`$AB$ <0311.g2:/ A B />`, :g:`$HK$ <0311.g2:/ H K />` are parallel, :g:`$AGHK$ <0311.g2:< A G H K >>` is a plane.

Complete the parallelogram :g:`$GHKL$ <0311.g2:< G H K L >>`.

Now, since :g:`$LK$ <0311.g2:/ L K />`, :g:`$GH$ <0311.g2:/ G H />` are parallel, and :g:`$GH$ <0311.g2:/ G H />` is perpendicular to the plane :g:`$CDF$ <0311.g2:< C D F >>`, :g:`$LK$ <0311.g2:/ L K />` is perpendicular to the plane :g:`$CDF$ <0311.g2:< C D F >>`.

Therefore :g:`$LK$ <0311.g2:/ L K />` is perpendicular to :g:`$CD$ <0311.g2:/ C D />` and :g:`$KH$ <0311.g2:/ K H />`, and therefore to :g:`$AB$ <0311.g2:/ A B />` which is parallel to :g:`$KH$ <0311.g2:/ K H />`.
