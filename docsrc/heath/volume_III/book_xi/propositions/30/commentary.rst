Commentary on Proposition XI.30
===============================

Back to :ref:`Proposition XI.30 <XI.30>`.

.. folio:: 0339
   :page: 335

This proposition completes the proof of the theorem that *Two parallelepipeds on the same base and of the same height are equivalent.*

:person:`Legendre <legendre>` deduced the useful theorem that *Every parallelepiped can be changed into an equivalent rectangular parallelepiped having the same height and an equivalent base.*

For suppose we have a parallelepiped on the base :g:`$ABCD$ <0340.g1:< A B C D >>` with :g:`$EFGH$ <0340.g1:< E F G H >>` for the opposite face.

.. folio:: 0340
   :page: 336

Draw :g:`$AI$ <0340.g1:/ A I />`, :g:`$BK$ <0340.g1:/ B K />`, :g:`$CL$ <0340.g1:/ C L />`, :g:`$DM$ <0340.g1:/ D M />` :eq:`perpendicular <0340.g1:/ A I / = 0340.g1:< E F G H >; 0340.g1:/ B K / = 0340.g1:< E F G H >; 0340.g1:/ C L / = 0340.g1:< E F G H >; 0340.g1:/ D M / = 0340.g1:< E F G H >>` to the plane through :g:`$EFGH$ <0340.g1:< E F G H >>` and all :eq:`equal <0340.g1:/ A I / = 0340.g1:/ B K / = 0340.g1:/ C L / = 0340.g1:/ D M />` to the height of the parallelepiped :g:`$AG$ <0340.g1:< A G >>`. Then, on joining :g:`$IK$ <0340.g1:/ I K />`, :g:`$KL$ <0340.g1:/ K L />`, :g:`$LM$ <0340.g1:/ L M />`, :g:`$MI$ <0340.g1:/ M I />`, we have a parallelepiped equivalent to the original one and having its lateral faces :g:`$AK$ <0340.g1:< A K >>`, :g:`$BL$ <0340.g1:< B L >>`, :g:`$CM$ <0340.g1:< C M >>`, :g:`$DI$ <0340.g1:< D I >>` rectangles.

.. picture:: 0340.g1.svg
   :alt: Book XI, Proposition 30, Commentary Diagram 1

If :g:`$ABCD$ <0340.g1:< A B C D >>` is not a rectangle, draw :g:`$AO$ <0340.g1:/ A O />`, :g:`$DN$ <0340.g1:/ D N />` in the plane :g:`$AC$ <0340.g1:< A C >>` :eq:`perpendicular <0340.g1:/ A O / = 0340.g1:/ B C /; 0340.g1:/ D N / = 0340.g1:/ B C />` to :g:`$BC$ <0340.g1:/ B C />`, and :g:`$IP$ <0340.g1:/ I P />`, :g:`$MQ$ <0340.g1:/ M Q />` in the plane :g:`$IL$ <0340.g1:< I L >>` :eq:`perpendicular <0340.g1:/ I P / = 0340.g1:/ K L /; 0340.g1:/ M Q / = 0340.g1:/ K L />` to :g:`$KL$ <0340.g1:/ K L />`.

Joining :g:`$OP$ <0340.g1:/ O P />`, :g:`$NQ$ <0340.g1:/ N Q />`, we have a rectangular parallelepiped on :g:`$AOND$ <0340.g1:< A O N D >>` as base which is equivalent to the parallelepiped with :g:`$ABCD$ <0340.g1:< A B C D >>` as base and :g:`$IKLM$ <0340.g1:< I K L M >>` as opposite face, since we may regard these parallelepipeds as being on the same base :g:`$ADMI$ <0340.g1:< A D M I >>` and of the same height (:g:`$AO$ <0340.g1:/ A O />`).

That is, a rectangular parallelepiped has been constructed which is equivalent to the given parallelepiped and has (1) the same height, (2) an equivalent base.

The American text-books which I have quoted adopt a somewhat different construction shown in the subjoined figure.

.. picture:: 0340.g2.svg
   :alt: Book XI, Proposition 30, Commentary Diagram 2

The edges :g:`$AB$ <0340.g2:/ A B />`, :g:`$DC$ <0340.g2:/ D C />`, :g:`$EF$ <0340.g2:/ E F />`, :g:`$HG$ <0340.g2:/ H G />` of the original parallelepiped are produced and cut at right angles by two parallel planes at a distance apart :g:`$A'B'$ <0340.g2:/ A' B' />` :eq:`equal <0340.g2:/ A' B' / = 0340.g2:/ A B />` to :g:`$AB$ <0340.g2:/ A B />`.

Thus a parallelepiped is formed in which all the faces are rectangles except :g:`$A'H'$ <0340.g2:< A' H' >>`, :g:`$B'G'$ <0340.g2:< B' G' >>`.

Next produce :g:`$D'A'$ <0340.g2:/ D' A' />`, :g:`$C'B'$ <0340.g2:/ C' B' />`, :g:`$G'F'$ <0340.g2:/ G' F' />`, :g:`$H'E'$ <0340.g2:/ H' E' />` and cut them perpendicularly by two parallel planes at a distance apart :g:`$B''C''$ <0340.g2:/ B'' C'' />` :eq:`equal <0340.g2:/ B'' C'' / = 0340.g2:/ B' C' />` to :g:`$B'C'$ <0340.g2:/ B' C' />`.

The points of section determine a *rectangular parallelepiped*.

The equivalence of the three parallelepipeds is proved, not by Eucl. XI. 29, 30, but by the proposition about a right section of a prism given above in the note to XI. 28 (3 in that note).
