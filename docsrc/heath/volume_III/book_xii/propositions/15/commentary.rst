Commentary on Proposition XII.15
================================

Back to :ref:`Proposition XII.15 <XII.15>`.

.. folio:: 0426
   :page: 422

**I.** If the heights of the two cylinders are equal, and their volumes are equal, the bases are equal, since the latter are proportional to the volumes. :just:`[XII. 11] <XII.11>` If the heights are *not* equal, cut off from the higher cylinder a cylinder of the same height as the lower. Then, if :g:`$LK$ <0424.g1:/ L K />`, :g:`$QN$ <0424.g1:/ Q N />` be the :eq:`equal <0424.g1:/ L K / = 0424.g1:/ Q N />` heights, we have, by XII. 11, (base :g:`$ABCD$ <0424.g1:( A B C D )>`) : (base :g:`$EFGH$ <0424.g1:( E F G H )>`) = (cylinder :g:`$AO$ <0424.g1:< A O >>`) : (cylinder :g:`$ES$ <0424.g1:< E S >>`) = (cylinder :g:`$EP$ <0424.g1:< E P >>`) : (cylinder :g:`$ES$ <0424.g1:< E S >>`), by hypothesis, = :g:`$MN$ <0424.g1:/ M N />` : :g:`$QN$ <0424.g1:/ Q N />` :just:`[XII. 13] <XII.13>` = :g:`$MN$ <0424.g1:/ M N />` : :g:`$KL$ <0424.g1:/ K L />`.

**II.** In the converse part of the proposition, Euclid omits the case where the cylinders have equal heights. In this case of course the reciprocal ratios are both ratios of equality; the bases are therefore equal, and consequently the cylinders. If the heights are *not* equal, we have, with the same construction as before, (base :g:`$ABCD$ <0424.g1:( A B C D )>`) : (base :g:`$EFGH$ <0424.g1:( E F G H )>`) = :g:`$MN$ <0424.g1:/ M N />` : :g:`$KL$ <0424.g1:/ K L />`. But :just:`[XII. 11] <XII.11>` (base :g:`$ABCD$ <0424.g1:( A B C D )>`) : (base :g:`$EFGH$ <0424.g1:( E F G H )>`) = (cylinder :g:`$AO$ <0424.g1:< A O >>`) : (cylinder :g:`$ES$ <0424.g1:< E S >>`), and :g:`$MN$ <0424.g1:/ M N />` : :g:`$KL$ <0424.g1:/ K L />` = :g:`$MN$ <0424.g1:/ M N />` : :g:`$QN$ <0424.g1:/ Q N />` = (cylinder :g:`$EP$ <0424.g1:< E P >>`) : (cylinder :g:`$ES$ <0424.g1:< E S >>`). :just:`[XII. 13] <XII.13>` Therefore (cylinder :g:`$AO$ <0424.g1:< A O >>`) : (cylinder :g:`$ES$ <0424.g1:< E S >>`) = (cylinder :g:`$EP$ <0424.g1:< E P >>`) : (cylinder :g:`$ES$ <0424.g1:< E S >>`), and consequently (cylinder :g:`$AO$ <0424.g1:< A O >>`) = (cylinder :g:`$EP$ <0424.g1:< E P >>`).

Similarly for the cones, which are equal to one-third of the cylinders respectively.

:person:`Legendre <legendre>` deduces these propositions about cones and cylinders from two others which he establishes by a method similar to that adopted by him for the theorem of XII. 2 (see note on that proposition). The first (for the cylinder) is as follows. *The volume of a cylinder is equal to the product of its base by its height.* Suppose :g:`$CA$ <0426.g1:/ C A />` to be the radius of the base of the given cylinder, $h$ its height. For brevity let us denote by (surf. :g:`$CA$ <0426.g1:( C A )>`) the area of the circle of which :g:`$CA$ <0426.g1:/ C A />` is the radius. If (surf. :g:`$CA$ <0426.g1:( C A )>`) $\times h$ is not the measure of the given cylinder, it will be the measure of a cylinder greater or less than it.

.. picture:: 0426.g1.svg
   :alt: Legendre's Cylinder Theorem Diagram

**I.** First let it be the measure of a less cylinder, that, for example, of which the circle with radius :g:`$CD$ <0426.g1:/ C D />` is the base, and $h$ is the height.

.. folio:: 0427
   :page: 423

Circumscribe about the circle with radius :g:`$CD$ <0426.g1:/ C D />` a regular polygon :g:`$GHI\dots$ <0426.g1:< G H I >>` such that its sides do not anywhere meet the circle with radius :g:`$CA$ <0426.g1:/ C A />`. [See note on XII. 2, p. 393 above, for :person:`Legendre <legendre>`'s lemma relating to this construction.] Imagine a prism erected on the polygon as base and with height $h$. Then (volume of prism) = (polygon :g:`$GHI\dots$ <0426.g1:< G H I >>`) $\times h$. [:person:`Legendre <legendre>` has previously proved this proposition, first for a parallelepiped (by transforming it into a rectangular one), then for a triangular prism (half of a parallelepiped of the same height), and lastly for a prism with a polygonal base.] But (polygon :g:`$GHI\dots$ <0426.g1:< G H I >>`) $<$ (surf. :g:`$CA$ <0426.g1:( C A )>`). Therefore (volume of prism) $<$ (surf. :g:`$CA$ <0426.g1:( C A )>`) $\times h$ $<$ (cylinder on circle of rad. :g:`$CD$ <0426.g1:/ C D />`), by hypothesis. But the prism is *greater* than the latter cylinder, since it includes it: which is impossible.

**II.** In order not to multiply figures let us, in this second case, suppose that :g:`$CD$ <0426.g1:/ C D />` is the radius of the base of the given cylinder, and that (surf. :g:`$CD$ <0426.g1:( C D )>`) $\times h$ is the measure of a cylinder greater than it, e.g. a cylinder on the circle with radius :g:`$CA$ <0426.g1:/ C A />` as base and of height $h$. Then, with the same construction, (volume of prism) = (polygon :g:`$GHI\dots$ <0426.g1:< G H I >>`) $\times h$. And (polygon :g:`$GHI\dots$ <0426.g1:< G H I >>`) $>$ (surf. :g:`$CD$ <0426.g1:( C D )>`). Therefore (volume of prism) $>$ (surf. :g:`$CD$ <0426.g1:( C D )>`) $\times h$ $>$ (cylinder on surf. :g:`$CA$ <0426.g1:( C A )>`), by hypothesis. But the volume of the prism is also *less* than that cylinder, being included by it: which is impossible.

Therefore (volume of cylinder) = (its base) $\times$ (its height).

It follows as a corollary that *Cylinders of the same height are to one another as their bases* [XII. 13], *and cylinders on the same base are to one another as their heights* [XII. 14].

Also *Similar cylinders are as the cubes of their heights, or as the cubes of the diameters of their bases* [Eucl. XII. 12].

For the bases are as the squares on their diameters; and, since the cylinders are similar, the diameters of the bases are as their heights. Therefore the bases are as the squares on the heights, and the bases multiplied by the heights, or the cylinders themselves, are as the cubes of the heights.

I need not reproduce :person:`Legendre <legendre>`'s proofs of the corresponding propositions for the cone.
