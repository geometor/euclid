Commentary on Proposition XII.17
================================

Back to :ref:`Proposition XII.17 <XII.17>`.

.. folio:: 0435
   :page: 431

This proposition is of great length and therefore requires summarising in order to make it easier to grasp. Moreover there are some assumptions in it which require to be proved, and some omissions to be supplied. The figure also is one of some complexity, and, in addition, the text and the figure treat two points :g:`$Z$ <0435.g1:Z>` and :g:`$V$ <0435.g1:V>`, which are really one and the same, as different.

The first thing needed is to know that all sections of a sphere by planes through the centre are circles and equal to one another (great circles or "greatest circles" as Euclid calls them, more appropriately). Euclid uses his definition of a sphere as the figure described by a semicircle revolving about its diameter. This of course establishes that all planes through the particular diameter make equal circular sections; but it is also assumed that the same sphere is generated by *any other* semicircle of the same size and with its centre at the same point.

.. picture:: 0435.g1.svg
   :alt: Book XII, Proposition 17, Heath's Diagram

The construction and argument of the proposition may be shortly given as follows.

A plane through the centre of two concentric spheres cuts them in great circles of which :g:`$BE$ <0435.g1:( B E )>`, :g:`$GF$ <0435.g1:( G F )>` are quadrants.

A regular polygon with an even number of sides is inscribed (*exactly as in Prop. 16*) to the outer circle such that its sides do not touch the inner circle. :g:`$BK$ <0435.g1:/ B K />`, :g:`$KL$ <0435.g1:/ K L />`, :g:`$LM$ <0435.g1:/ L M />`, :g:`$ME$ <0435.g1:/ M E />` are the sides in the quadrant :g:`$BE$ <0435.g1:( B E )>`.

.. _folio-iii-0436:

.. folio:: 0436
   :page: 432

:g:`$AO$ <0435.g1:/ A O />` is drawn at right angles to the plane :g:`$ABE$ <0435.g1:< A B E >>`, and through :g:`$AO$ <0435.g1:/ A O />` are drawn planes passing through :g:`$B$ <0435.g1:B>`, :g:`$K$ <0435.g1:K>`, :g:`$L$ <0435.g1:L>`, :g:`$M$ <0435.g1:M>`, :g:`$E$ <0435.g1:E>`, etc., cutting the sphere in great circles.

:g:`$OB$ <0435.g1:( O B )>`, :g:`$OK$ <0435.g1:( O K )>` are quadrants of two of these great circles.

As these quadrants are :eq:`equal <0435.g1:( O B ) = 0435.g1:( O K ) = 0435.g1:( B E )>` to the quadrant :g:`$BE$ <0435.g1:( B E )>`, they will be divisible into arcs :eq:`equal <0435.g1:/ B P / = 0435.g1:/ P Q / = 0435.g1:/ Q R / = 0435.g1:/ R O / = 0435.g1:/ K S / = 0435.g1:/ S T / = 0435.g1:/ T U / = 0435.g1:/ U O / = 0435.g1:/ B K / = 0435.g1:/ K L / = 0435.g1:/ L M / = 0435.g1:/ M E />` in number and magnitude to the arcs :g:`$BK$ <0435.g1:/ B K />`, :g:`$KL$ <0435.g1:/ K L />`, :g:`$LM$ <0435.g1:/ L M />`, :g:`$ME$ <0435.g1:/ M E />`.

Dividing the other quadrants of these circles, and also all the quadrants of the other circles through :g:`$OA$ <0435.g1:/ O A />`, in this way we shall have in all the circles a polygon equal to that in the circle of which :g:`$BE$ <0435.g1:( B E )>` is a quadrant.

:g:`$BP$ <0435.g1:/ B P />`, :g:`$PQ$ <0435.g1:/ P Q />`, :g:`$QR$ <0435.g1:/ Q R />`, :g:`$RO$ <0435.g1:/ R O />` and :g:`$KS$ <0435.g1:/ K S />`, :g:`$ST$ <0435.g1:/ S T />`, :g:`$TU$ <0435.g1:/ T U />`, :g:`$UO$ <0435.g1:/ U O />` are the sides of these polygons in the quadrants :g:`$BO$ <0435.g1:( B O )>`, :g:`$KO$ <0435.g1:( K O )>`.

Joining :g:`$PS$ <0435.g1:/ P S />`, :g:`$QT$ <0435.g1:/ Q T />`, :g:`$RU$ <0435.g1:/ R U />`, and making the same construction all round the circles through :g:`$AO$ <0435.g1:/ A O />`, we have a certain polyhedron inscribed in the outer sphere.

Draw :g:`$PV$ <0435.g1:/ P V />` perpendicular to :g:`$AB$ <0435.g1:/ A B />` and therefore (since the planes :g:`$OAB$ <0435.g1:< O A B >>`, :g:`$BAE$ <0435.g1:< B A E >>` are at right angles) perpendicular to the plane :g:`$BAE$ <0435.g1:< B A E >>` ; :just:`[XI. Def. 4] <XI.def.4>` draw :g:`$SW$ <0435.g1:/ S W />` perpendicular to :g:`$AK$ <0435.g1:/ A K />` and therefore (for a like reason) perpendicular to the plane :g:`$BAE$ <0435.g1:< B A E >>`.

Draw :g:`$KZ$ <0435.g1:/ K Z />` perpendicular to :g:`$BA$ <0435.g1:/ B A />`. (Since :g:`$BK$ <0435.g1:/ B K />` = :g:`$BP$ <0435.g1:/ B P />`, and :g:`$DB$ <0435.g1:/ D B />` . :g:`$BV$ <0435.g1:/ B V />` = :g:`$BP^2$ <0435.g1:/ B P />`, :g:`$DB$ <0435.g1:/ D B />` . :g:`$BZ$ <0435.g1:/ B Z />` = :g:`$BK^2$ <0435.g1:/ B K />`, it follows that :g:`$BV$ <0435.g1:/ B V />` = :g:`$BZ$ <0435.g1:/ B Z />`, and :g:`$Z$ <0435.g1:Z>`, :g:`$V$ <0435.g1:V>` coincide.)

Now, since $\angle s$ :g:`$PAV$ <0435.g1:< P A V )>`, :g:`$SAW$ <0435.g1:< S A W )>`, being angles subtended at the centre by :eq:`equal <0435.g1:/ B P / = 0435.g1:/ K S />` arcs of :eq:`equal <0435.g1:( O B ) = 0435.g1:( O K )>` circles, are :eq:`equal <0435.g1:< P A V ) = 0435.g1:< S A W )>`,

and since             $\angle s$ :g:`$PVA$ <0435.g1:< P V A )>`, :g:`$SWA$ <0435.g1:< S W A )>` are :eq:`right <0435.g1:< P V A ) = 0435.g1:< S W A )>`,

while :g:`$AS$ <0435.g1:/ A S />` = :g:`$AP$ <0435.g1:/ A P />`, $\Delta s$ :g:`$PAV$ <0435.g1:< P A V >>`, :g:`$SAW$ <0435.g1:< S A W >>` are :eq:`equal <0435.g1:< P A V > = 0435.g1:< S A W >>` in all respects, :just:`[I. 26] <I.26>`

and                     :g:`$AV$ <0435.g1:/ A V />` = :g:`$AW$ <0435.g1:/ A W />`.

Consequently         :g:`$AB$ <0435.g1:/ A B />` : :g:`$AV$ <0435.g1:/ A V />` = :g:`$AK$ <0435.g1:/ A K />` : :g:`$AW$ <0435.g1:/ A W />` ;

and :g:`$VW$ <0435.g1:/ V W />`, :g:`$BK$ <0435.g1:/ B K />` are parallel.

But :g:`$PV$ <0435.g1:/ P V />`, :g:`$SW$ <0435.g1:/ S W />` are parallel (being both perpendicular to one plane) and :eq:`equal <0435.g1:/ P V / = 0435.g1:/ S W />` (by the :eq:`equal <0435.g1:< P A V > = 0435.g1:< S A W >>` $\Delta s$ :g:`$PAV$ <0435.g1:< P A V >>`, :g:`$SAW$ <0435.g1:< S A W >>`),

therefore             :g:`$VW$ <0435.g1:/ V W />`, :g:`$PS$ <0435.g1:/ P S />` are :eq:`equal <0435.g1:/ V W / = 0435.g1:/ P S />` and parallel.

Therefore :g:`$BK$ <0435.g1:/ B K />` (being parallel to :g:`$VW$ <0435.g1:/ V W />`) is parallel to :g:`$PS$ <0435.g1:/ P S />`.

Consequently (1) :g:`$BPSK$ <0435.g1:< B P S K >>` is a quadrilateral *in one plane*.

Similarly the other quadrilaterals :g:`$PQTS$ <0435.g1:< P Q T S >>`, :g:`$QRUT$ <0435.g1:< Q R U T >>` are in one plane ; and the triangle :g:`$ORU$ <0435.g1:< O R U >>` is in one plane.

*In order now to prove that the plane :g:`$BPSK$ <0435.g1:< B P S K >>` does not anywhere touch the inner sphere we have to prove that the shortest distance from :g:`$A$ <0435.g1:A>` to the plane is greater than :g:`$AZ$ <0435.g1:/ A Z />`, which by the construction in XII. 16 is greater than :g:`$AG$ <0435.g1:/ A G />`.*

Draw :g:`$AX$ <0435.g1:/ A X />` perpendicular to the plane :g:`$BPSK$ <0435.g1:< B P S K >>`.

Then   :g:`$AX^2$ <0435.g1:/ A X />` + :g:`$XB^2$ <0435.g1:/ X B />` = :g:`$AX^2$ <0435.g1:/ A X />` + :g:`$XK^2$ <0435.g1:/ X K />` = :g:`$AX^2$ <0435.g1:/ A X />` + :g:`$XS^2$ <0435.g1:/ X S />` = :g:`$AX^2$ <0435.g1:/ A X />` + :g:`$XP^2$ <0435.g1:/ X P />` = :g:`$AB^2$ <0435.g1:/ A B />`,

whence                     :g:`$XB$ <0435.g1:/ X B />` = :g:`$XK$ <0435.g1:/ X K />` = :g:`$XS$ <0435.g1:/ X S />` = :g:`$XP$ <0435.g1:/ X P />`,

or (2) the quadrilateral :g:`$BPSK$ <0435.g1:< B P S K >>` is inscribable in a circle with :g:`$X$ <0435.g1:X>` as centre and radius :g:`$XB$ <0435.g1:/ X B />`.

Now                         :g:`$BK$ <0435.g1:/ B K />` $>$ :g:`$VW$ <0435.g1:/ V W />`

$>$ :g:`$PS$ <0435.g1:/ P S />` ; therefore in the quadrilateral :g:`$BPSK$ <0435.g1:< B P S K >>` three sides :g:`$BK$ <0435.g1:/ B K />`, :g:`$BP$ <0435.g1:/ B P />`, :g:`$KS$ <0435.g1:/ K S />` are :eq:`equal <0435.g1:/ B K / = 0435.g1:/ B P / = 0435.g1:/ K S />`, but :g:`$PS$ <0435.g1:/ P S />` is less.

Consequently the angles about :g:`$X$ <0435.g1:X>` are three equal angles and one smaller angle ;

.. folio:: 0437
   :page: 433

therefore any one of the equal angles is greater than a right angle, i.e. $\angle$ :g:`$BXK$ <0435.g1:< B X K )>` is obtuse.

Therefore (3)                                 :g:`$BK^2$ <0435.g1:/ B K />` $>$ 2 :g:`$BX^2$ <0435.g1:/ B X />`. :just:`[II. 12] <II.12>`

Next, consider the semicircle :g:`$BKD$ <0435.g1:( B K D )>` with :g:`$KZ$ <0435.g1:/ K Z />` drawn perpendicular to :g:`$BD$ <0435.g1:/ B D />`.

We have                                 :g:`$BD$ <0435.g1:/ B D />` $<$ 2 :g:`$DZ$ <0435.g1:/ D Z />`,

so that                               :g:`$DB$ <0435.g1:/ D B />` . :g:`$BZ$ <0435.g1:/ B Z />` $<$ 2 :g:`$DZ$ <0435.g1:/ D Z />` . :g:`$ZB$ <0435.g1:/ Z B />`,

or                                         :g:`$BK^2$ <0435.g1:/ B K />` $<$ 2 :g:`$KZ^2$ <0435.g1:/ K Z />` ;

therefore, :term:`*a fortiori* <a-fortiori>`, [by (3) above]

(4)                                         :g:`$BX^2$ <0435.g1:/ B X />` $<$ :g:`$KZ^2$ <0435.g1:/ K Z />`.

Now                                     :g:`$AK^2$ <0435.g1:/ A K />` = :g:`$AB^2$ <0435.g1:/ A B />` ;

therefore                     :g:`$AZ^2$ <0435.g1:/ A Z />` + :g:`$ZK^2$ <0435.g1:/ Z K />` = :g:`$AX^2$ <0435.g1:/ A X />` + :g:`$XB^2$ <0435.g1:/ X B />`.

And                                     :g:`$BX^2$ <0435.g1:/ B X />` $<$ :g:`$KZ^2$ <0435.g1:/ K Z />` ;

therefore                                 :g:`$AX^2$ <0435.g1:/ A X />` $>$ :g:`$AZ^2$ <0435.g1:/ A Z />`,

or (5)                                       :g:`$AX$ <0435.g1:/ A X />` $>$ :g:`$AZ$ <0435.g1:/ A Z />`.

But, by the construction in XII. 16, :g:`$AZ$ <0435.g1:/ A Z />` $>$ :g:`$AG$ <0435.g1:/ A G />` ; therefore, :term:`*a fortiori* <a-fortiori>`, :g:`$AX$ <0435.g1:/ A X />` $>$ :g:`$AG$ <0435.g1:/ A G />`.

And, since the perpendicular :g:`$AX$ <0435.g1:/ A X />` is the shortest distance from :g:`$A$ <0435.g1:A>` to the plane :g:`$BPSK$ <0435.g1:< B P S K >>`,

(6) the plane :g:`$BPSK$ <0435.g1:< B P S K >>` does not anywhere meet the inner sphere.

Euclid omits to prove that, :term:`*a fortiori* <a-fortiori>`, the other quadrilaterals :g:`$PQTS$ <0435.g1:< P Q T S >>`, :g:`$QRUT$ <0435.g1:< Q R U T >>`, and the triangle :g:`$ROU$ <0435.g1:< R O U >>`, do not anywhere meet the inner sphere.

For this purpose it is only necessary to show that the radii of the circles circumscribing :g:`$BPSK$ <0435.g1:< B P S K >>`, :g:`$PQTS$ <0435.g1:< P Q T S >>`, :g:`$QRUT$ <0435.g1:< Q R U T >>` and :g:`$ROU$ <0435.g1:< R O U >>` are in descending order of magnitude.

.. picture:: 0437.g1.svg
   :alt: Proposition 17, Lemma figure

We have therefore to prove that, if :g:`$ABCD$ <0437.g1:< A B C D >>`, :g:`$A'B'C'D'$ <0437.g1:< A' B' C' D' >>` are two quadrilaterals inscribable in circles, and :g:`$AD$ <0437.g1:/ A D />` = :g:`$BC$ <0437.g1:/ B C />` = :g:`$A'D'$ <0437.g1:/ A' D' />` = :g:`$B'C'$ <0437.g1:/ B' C' />`, while :g:`$AB$ <0437.g1:/ A B />` is not greater than :g:`$AD$ <0437.g1:/ A D />`, :g:`$A'B'$ <0437.g1:/ A' B' />` = :g:`$CD$ <0437.g1:/ C D />`, and :g:`$AB$ <0437.g1:/ A B />` $>$ :g:`$CD$ <0437.g1:/ C D />` $>$ :g:`$C'D'$ <0437.g1:/ C' D' />`, then the radius :g:`$OA$ <0437.g1:/ O A />` of the circle circumscribing the first quadrilateral is greater than the radius :g:`$O'A'$ <0437.g1:/ O' A' />` of the circle circumscribing the second.

:person:`Clavius <clavius>`, and :person:`Simson <simson>` after him, prove this by :term:`*reductio ad absurdum* <reductio-ad-absurdum>`.

(1)   *If* :g:`$OA$ <0437.g1:/ O A />` *=* :g:`$O'A'$ <0437.g1:/ O' A' />`,

it follows that $\angle s$ :g:`$AOD$ <0437.g1:< A O D )>`, :g:`$BOC$ <0437.g1:< B O C )>`, :g:`$A'O'D'$ <0437.g1:< A' O' D' )>`, :g:`$B'O'C'$ <0437.g1:< B' O' C' )>` are all :eq:`equal <0437.g1:< A O D ) = 0437.g1:< B O C ) = 0437.g1:< A' O' D' ) = 0437.g1:< B' O' C' )>`.

Also                         $\angle$ :g:`$AOB$ <0437.g1:< A O B )>` $>$ $\angle$ :g:`$A'O'B'$ <0437.g1:< A' O' B' )>`,

$\angle$ :g:`$COD$ <0437.g1:< C O D )>` $>$ $\angle$ :g:`$C'O'D'$ <0437.g1:< C' O' D' )>`, whence the four angles about :g:`$O$ <0437.g1:O>` are together greater than the four angles about :g:`$O'$ <0437.g1:O'>`, i.e. greater than four right angles ; which is impossible.

(2) *If* :g:`$O'A'$ <0437.g1:/ O' A' />` *$>$* :g:`$OA$ <0437.g1:/ O A />`, cut off from :g:`$O'A'$ <0437.g1:/ O' A' />`, :g:`$O'B'$ <0437.g1:/ O' B' />`, :g:`$O'C'$ <0437.g1:/ O' C' />`, :g:`$O'D'$ <0437.g1:/ O' D' />` lengths equal to :g:`$OA$ <0437.g1:/ O A />`, and draw the inner quadrilateral as shown in the figure (:g:`$XYZW$ <0437.g1:< X Y Z W >>`). Then :g:`$AB$ <0437.g1:/ A B />` $>$ :g:`$A'B'$ <0437.g1:/ A' B' />` $>$ :g:`$XY$ <0437.g1:/ X Y />`, :g:`$CD$ <0437.g1:/ C D />` $>$ :g:`$C'D'$ <0437.g1:/ C' D' />` $>$ :g:`$ZW$ <0437.g1:/ Z W />`, :g:`$AD$ <0437.g1:/ A D />` = :g:`$A'D'$ <0437.g1:/ A' D' />` $>$ :g:`$WX$ <0437.g1:/ W X />`, :g:`$BC$ <0437.g1:/ B C />` = :g:`$B'C'$ <0437.g1:/ B' C' />` $>$ :g:`$YZ$ <0437.g1:/ Y Z />`.

Consequently the same absurdity as in (1) follows :term:`*a fortiori* <a-fortiori>`.

Therefore, since :g:`$OA$ <0437.g1:/ O A />` is neither equal to nor less than :g:`$O'A'$ <0437.g1:/ O' A' />`, :g:`$OA$ <0437.g1:/ O A />` $>$ :g:`$O'A'$ <0437.g1:/ O' A' />`.

The fact is also sufficiently clear if we draw :g:`$MO$ <0437.g1:/ M O />`, :g:`$NO$ <0437.g1:/ N O />` bisecting :g:`$DA$ <0437.g1:/ D A />`, :g:`$DC$ <0437.g1:/ D C />` perpendicularly and therefore meeting in :g:`$O$ <0437.g1:O>`, the centre of the circumscribed circle, and then suppose the side :g:`$DA$ <0437.g1:/ D A />` with the perpendicular :g:`$MO$ <0437.g1:/ M O />` to turn inwards about :g:`$D$ <0437.g1:D>` as centre. Then the intersection of :g:`$MO$ <0437.g1:/ M O />` and :g:`$NO$ <0437.g1:/ N O />`, as :g:`$P$ <0437.g1:P>`, will gradually move towards :g:`$N$ <0437.g1:N>`.

:person:`Simson <simson>` gives his proof as “Lemma II.” immediately before XII. 17. He adds to the Porism some words explaining how we may construct a similar polyhedron in another sphere and how we may prove that the polyhedra are similar.

The Porism is of course of the essence of the matter because it is the porism which as much as the construction is wanted in the next proposition. It would therefore not have been amiss to include the Porism in the enunciation of XII. 17 so as to call attention to it.
