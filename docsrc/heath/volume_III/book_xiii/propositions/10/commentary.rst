Commentary on Proposition XIII.10
=================================

Back to :ref:`Proposition XIII.10 <XIII.10>`.

.. folio:: 0464
   :page: 460

:g:`ABCDE <0462.g1:< A B C D E >>` being a regular pentagon inscribed in a circle, and :g:`AG <0462.g1:/ A G />` the diameter through :g:`A <0462.g1:A>`, it follows that $(\text{arc }$ :g:`CG <0462.g1:( C G )>` $) = (\text{arc }$ :g:`GD <0462.g1:( G D )>` $), and :g:`CG <0462.g1:( C G )>`, :g:`GD <0462.g1:( G D )>` are sides of an inscribed regular decagon.

:g:`FHK <0462.g1:/ F H K />` being drawn perpendicular to :g:`AB <0462.g1:/ A B />`, it follows, by I. 26, that $\angle\text{s}$ :g:`AFK <0462.g1:< A F K )>`, :g:`BFK <0462.g1:< B F K )>` are :eq:`equal <0462.g1:< A F K ) = 0462.g1:< B F K )>`, and :g:`BK <0462.g1:/ B K />`, :g:`KA <0462.g1:/ K A />` are sides of the regular decagon.

Similarly it may be proved that, :g:`FLM <0462.g1:/ F L M />` being perpendicular to :g:`AK <0462.g1:/ A K />`, :g:`AL <0462.g1:/ A L />` $= $ :g:`LK <0462.g1:/ L K />`, and $(\text{arc }$ :g:`AM <0462.g1:( A M )>` $) = (\text{arc }$ :g:`MK <0462.g1:( M K )>` $).

The main facts to prove are that (1) the triangles :g:`ABF <0462.g1:< A B F >>`, :g:`FBN <0462.g1:< F B N >>` are similar, and (2) the triangles :g:`ABK <0462.g1:< A B K >>`, :g:`AKN <0462.g1:< A K N >>` are similar.

(1) $2\,(\text{arc }$ :g:`CG <0462.g1:( C G )>` $) = (\text{arc }$ :g:`CD <0462.g1:( C D )>` $)$ $= (\text{arc }$ :g:`AB <0462.g1:( A B )>` $)$ $= 2\,(\text{arc }$ :g:`BK <0462.g1:( B K )>` $), or $(\text{arc }$ :g:`CG <0462.g1:( C G )>` $) = (\text{arc }$ :g:`BK <0462.g1:( B K )>` $) = (\text{arc }$ :g:`AK <0462.g1:( A K )>` $)$ $= 2\,(\text{arc }$ :g:`KM <0462.g1:( K M )>` $). And $(\text{arc }$ :g:`CB <0462.g1:( C B )>` $) = 2\,(\text{arc }$ :g:`BK <0462.g1:( B K )>` $). Therefore, by addition, $(\text{arc }$ :g:`BCG <0462.g1:( B C G )>` $) = 2\,(\text{arc }$ :g:`BKM <0462.g1:( B K M )>` $). Therefore $\angle$ :g:`BFG <0462.g1:< B F G )>` $= 2\,\angle$ :g:`BFN <0462.g1:< B F N )>`. But $\angle$ :g:`BFG <0462.g1:< B F G )>` $= 2\,\angle$ :g:`FAB <0462.g1:< F A B )>`, so that $\angle$ :g:`FAB <0462.g1:< F A B )>` $= \angle$ :g:`BFN <0462.g1:< B F N )>`. Hence, in the $\Delta\text{s}$ :g:`ABF <0462.g1:< A B F >>`, :g:`FBN <0462.g1:< F B N >>`, $\angle$ :g:`FAB <0462.g1:< F A B )>` $= \angle$ :g:`BFN <0462.g1:< B F N )>`, and $\angle$ :g:`ABF <0462.g1:< A B F )>` is common; therefore $\angle$ :g:`AFB <0462.g1:< A F B )>` $= \angle$ :g:`BNF <0462.g1:< B N F )>`, and $\Delta\text{s}$ :g:`ABF <0462.g1:< A B F >>`, :g:`FBN <0462.g1:< F B N >>` are similar.

(2) Since :g:`AL <0462.g1:/ A L />` $= $ :g:`LK <0462.g1:/ L K />`, and the angles at :g:`L <0462.g1:L>` are right, :g:`AN <0462.g1:/ A N />` $= $ :g:`NK <0462.g1:/ N K />`, and $\angle$ :g:`NKA <0462.g1:< N K A )>` $= \angle$ :g:`NAK <0462.g1:< N A K )>` $= \angle$ :g:`KBA <0462.g1:< K B A )>`. Hence, in the $\Delta\text{s}$ :g:`ABK <0462.g1:< A B K >>`, :g:`AKN <0462.g1:< A K N >>`, $\angle$ :g:`ABK <0462.g1:< A B K )>` $= \angle$ :g:`AKN <0462.g1:< A K N )>`, and $\angle$ :g:`KAN <0462.g1:< K A N )>` is common; whence the third angles are :eq:`equal <0462.g1:< A K B ) = 0462.g1:< K N A )>`; therefore the triangles :g:`ABK <0462.g1:< A B K >>`, :g:`AKN <0462.g1:< A K N >>` are similar.

Now from the similarity of $\Delta\text{s}$ :g:`ABF <0462.g1:< A B F >>`, :g:`FBN <0462.g1:< F B N >>` it follows that :g:`AB <0462.g1:/ A B />` $:$ :g:`BF <0462.g1:/ B F />` $= $ :g:`BF <0462.g1:/ B F />` $:$ :g:`BN <0462.g1:/ B N />`, or $(\text{rect. }$ :g:`AB <0462.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`BN <0462.g1:/ B N />` $) = (\text{sq. on }$ :g:`BF <0462.g1:/ B F />` $)$.

And, from the similarity of :g:`ABK <0462.g1:< A B K >>`, :g:`AKN <0462.g1:< A K N >>`, :g:`BA <0462.g1:/ B A />` $:$ :g:`AK <0462.g1:/ A K />` $= $ :g:`AK <0462.g1:/ A K />` $:$ :g:`AN <0462.g1:/ A N />`, or $(\text{rect. }$ :g:`BA <0462.g1:/ B A />`, :g:`AN <0462.g1:/ A N />` $) = (\text{sq. on }$ :g:`AK <0462.g1:/ A K />` $)$.

Therefore, by addition, $(\text{rect. }$ :g:`AB <0462.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`BN <0462.g1:/ B N />` $) + (\text{rect. }$ :g:`BA <0462.g1:/ B A />`, :g:`AN <0462.g1:/ A N />` $) = (\text{sq. on }$ :g:`BF <0462.g1:/ B F />` $) + (\text{sq. on }$ :g:`AK <0462.g1:/ A K />` $), that is, $(\text{sq. on }$ :g:`AB <0462.g1:/ A B />` $) = (\text{sq. on }$ :g:`BF <0462.g1:/ B F />` $) + (\text{sq. on }$ :g:`AK <0462.g1:/ A K />` $)$.

If $r$ be the radius of the circle, we have seen (XIII. 9, note) that :g:`AK <0462.g1:/ A K />` $= \frac{r}{2}(\sqrt{5} - 1)$.

Therefore

$$\begin{aligned}
(\text{side of pentagon})^2 &= r^2 + \frac{r^2}{4}(6 - 2\sqrt{5}) \\
&= \frac{r^2}{4}(10 - 2\sqrt{5}),
\end{aligned}$$

so that

$$(\text{side of pentagon}) = \frac{r}{2}\sqrt{10 - 2\sqrt{5}}.$$
