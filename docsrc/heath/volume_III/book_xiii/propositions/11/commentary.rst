Commentary on Proposition XIII.11
=================================

Back to :ref:`Proposition XIII.11 <XIII.11>`.

.. folio:: 0469
   :page: 465

Here we require certain definitions and propositions of Book X. First we require the definition of an *apotome* :just:`[see X. 73]`, which is a straight line of the form :math:`(\rho \sim \sqrt{k} \cdot \rho)`, where :math:`\rho` is a "rational" straight line and :math:`k` is any integer or numerical fraction, the square root of which is not *integral* or expressible in integers. The lesser of the straight lines :math:`\rho`, :math:`\sqrt{k} \cdot \rho` is the *annex*.

Next we require the definition of the :term:`*fourth apotome* <fourth-apotome>` :just:`[X. Deff. III. (after X. 84)]`, which is a straight line of the form :math:`(x - y)`, where :math:`x`, :math:`y` are (being both rational and commensurable in square only) are also such that :math:`\sqrt{x^2 - y^2}` is incommensurable with :math:`x`, while :math:`x` is commensurable with a given rational straight line :math:`\rho`. As shown on X. 88 (note), the :term:`*fourth apotome* <fourth-apotome>` is of the form

.. math::

   (k\rho - \frac{k\rho}{\sqrt{1 + \lambda}}).

Lastly the *minor* (straight line) is the irrational straight line defined in

X. 76. It is of the form :math:`(x - y)`, where :math:`x`, :math:`y` are incommensurable in square, and :math:`(x^2 + y^2)` is 'rational,' while :math:`xy` is 'medial.' As shown in the note on

X. 76, the *minor irrational straight line* is of the form

.. math::

   \frac{\rho}{\sqrt{2}} \sqrt{1 + \frac{k}{\sqrt{1 + k^2}}} - \frac{\rho}{\sqrt{2}} \sqrt{1 - \frac{k}{\sqrt{1 + k^2}}}.

The proposition may be put as follows. :g:`ABCDE <0466.g1:< A B C D E >>` being a regular pentagon inscribed in a circle, :g:`AG <0466.g1:/ A G />`, :g:`BH <0466.g1:/ B H />` the diameters through :g:`A <0466.g1:A>`, :g:`B <0466.g1:B>` meeting :g:`CD <0466.g1:/ C D />` in :g:`L <0466.g1:L>` and :g:`AC <0466.g1:/ A C />` in :g:`M <0466.g1:M>` respectively, :g:`FK <0466.g1:/ F K />` is made equal to :math:`\frac{1}{4}` :g:`AF <0466.g1:/ A F />`. Now, the radius :g:`AF <0466.g1:/ A F />` (:math:`r`) being rational, so are :g:`FK <0466.g1:/ F K />`, :g:`BK <0466.g1:/ B K />`. The arcs :g:`CG <0466.g1:( C G )>`, :g:`GD <0466.g1:( G D )>` are :eq:`equal <0466.g1:( C G ) = 0466.g1:( G D )>`; hence :math:`\angle\text{s}` at :g:`L <0466.g1:L>` are right, and :g:`CD <0466.g1:/ C D />` :math:`= 2` :g:`CL <0466.g1:/ C L />`. Similarly :math:`\angle\text{s}` at :g:`M <0466.g1:M>` are right, and :g:`AC <0466.g1:/ A C />` :math:`= 2` :g:`CM <0466.g1:/ C M />`.

We have to prove

(1) that :g:`BM <0466.g1:/ B M />` is an apotome,

(2) that :g:`BM <0466.g1:/ B M />` is a fourth apotome,

(3) that :g:`BA <0466.g1:/ B A />` is a minor irrational straight line.

Remembering that, if :g:`CA <0466.g1:/ C A />` is divided in extreme and mean ratio, the greater segment is equal to the side of the pentagon :just:`[XIII. 8] <XIII.8>`, and that accordingly :just:`[XIII. 1] <XIII.1>` :math:`(` :g:`CD <0466.g1:/ C D />` :math:`+ \frac{1}{2}` :g:`CA <0466.g1:/ C A />` :math:`)^2 = 5(\frac{1}{2}` :g:`CA <0466.g1:/ C A />` :math:`)^2`, we work towards a proportion containing the ratio :math:`(` :g:`CD <0466.g1:/ C D />` :math:`+` :g:`CM <0466.g1:/ C M />` :math:`)^2 :` :g:`CM <0466.g1:/ C M />` :math:`^2`, thus.

The :math:`\Delta\text{s}` :g:`ACL <0466.g1:< A C L >>`, :g:`AFM <0466.g1:< A F M >>` are equiangular and therefore similar. Therefore :g:`LC <0466.g1:/ L C />` :math:`:` :g:`CA <0466.g1:/ C A />` :math:`=` :g:`MF <0466.g1:/ M F />` :math:`:` :g:`FA <0466.g1:/ F A />`, and accordingly :math:`2` :g:`LC <0466.g1:/ L C />` :math:`:` :g:`CA <0466.g1:/ C A />` :math:`=` :g:`MF <0466.g1:/ M F />` :math:`: \frac{1}{2}` :g:`FA <0466.g1:/ F A />`; thus :math:`2` :g:`LC <0466.g1:/ L C />` :math:`: \frac{1}{2}` :g:`CA <0466.g1:/ C A />` :math:`=` :g:`MF <0466.g1:/ M F />` :math:`: \frac{1}{4}` :g:`FA <0466.g1:/ F A />`, or :g:`DC <0466.g1:/ D C />` :math:`:` :g:`CM <0466.g1:/ C M />` :math:`=` :g:`MF <0466.g1:/ M F />` :math:`:` :g:`FK <0466.g1:/ F K />`; whence, :term:`*componendo* <componendo>`, and squaring, :math:`(` :g:`DC <0466.g1:/ D C />` :math:`+` :g:`CM <0466.g1:/ C M />` :math:`)^2 :` :g:`CM <0466.g1:/ C M />` :math:`^2 =` :g:`MK <0466.g1:/ M K />` :math:`^2 :` :g:`KF <0466.g1:/ K F />` :math:`^2`. But :math:`(` :g:`DC <0466.g1:/ D C />` :math:`+` :g:`CM <0466.g1:/ C M />` :math:`)^2 = 5` :g:`CM <0466.g1:/ C M />` :math:`^2`; therefore :g:`MK <0466.g1:/ M K />` :math:`^2 = 5` :g:`KF <0466.g1:/ K F />` :math:`^2`.

(1) To prove that :g:`BM <0466.g1:/ B M />` is an apotome and :g:`MK <0466.g1:/ M K />` its annex. We have :g:`BF <0466.g1:/ B F />` :math:`= 4` :g:`FK <0466.g1:/ F K />`; therefore :g:`BK <0466.g1:/ B K />` :math:`= 5` :g:`FK <0466.g1:/ F K />`, :g:`BK <0466.g1:/ B K />` :math:`^2 = 25` :g:`FK <0466.g1:/ F K />` :math:`^2` :math:`= 5` :g:`MK <0466.g1:/ M K />` :math:`^2`, from above; therefore :g:`BK <0466.g1:/ B K />` :math:`^2` has not to :g:`MK <0466.g1:/ M K />` :math:`^2` the ratio of a square number to a square number; therefore :g:`BK <0466.g1:/ B K />`, :g:`MK <0466.g1:/ M K />` are incommensurable in length. They are therefore rational and commensurable in square only; accordingly :g:`BM <0466.g1:/ B M />` is an apotome. [:g:`BK <0466.g1:/ B K />` :math:`^2 = 5` :g:`MK <0466.g1:/ M K />` :math:`^2 = \frac{25}{16}r^2`, and :g:`BK <0466.g1:/ B K />` :math:`= \frac{5}{4}r`. Consequently :g:`BK <0466.g1:/ B K />` :math:`-` :g:`MK <0466.g1:/ M K />` :math:`= (\frac{5}{4}r - \frac{\sqrt{5}}{4}r)`.]

(2) To prove that :g:`BM <0466.g1:/ B M />` is a fourth apotome. First, since :g:`KF <0466.g1:/ K F />`, :g:`FB <0466.g1:/ F B />` are commensurable, :g:`BK <0466.g1:/ B K />`, :g:`BF <0466.g1:/ B F />` are commensurable, i.e. :g:`BK <0466.g1:/ B K />` is commensurable with :g:`BH <0466.g1:/ B H />`, a given rational straight line. Secondly, if :g:`N <0466.g1:/ N />` :math:`^2 =` :g:`BK <0466.g1:/ B K />` :math:`^2 -` :g:`KM <0466.g1:/ K M />` :math:`^2`, since :g:`BK <0466.g1:/ B K />` :math:`^2 :` :g:`KM <0466.g1:/ K M />` :math:`^2 = 5 : 1`, it follows that :g:`BK <0466.g1:/ B K />` :math:`^2 :` :g:`N <0466.g1:/ N />` :math:`^2 = 5 : 4`, whence :g:`BK <0466.g1:/ B K />`, :g:`N <0466.g1:/ N />` are incommensurable. Therefore :g:`BM <0466.g1:/ B M />` is a fourth apotome.

(3) To prove that :g:`BA <0466.g1:/ B A />` is a minor irrational straight line. If a fourth apotome form a rectangle with a rational straight line, the side of the square equivalent to the rectangle is *minor* :just:`[X. 94] <X.94>`. Now :g:`BA <0466.g1:/ B A />` :math:`^2 =` :g:`HB <0466.g1:/ H B />` :math:`\cdot` :g:`BM <0466.g1:/ B M />`, :g:`HB <0466.g1:/ H B />` is rational, and :g:`BM <0466.g1:/ B M />` is a fourth apotome; therefore :g:`BA <0466.g1:/ B A />` is a minor irrational straight line. [:g:`BA <0466.g1:/ B A />` :math:`= r\sqrt{2} \cdot \sqrt{\frac{5}{4} - \frac{\sqrt{5}}{4}} = \frac{r}{2}\sqrt{10 - 2\sqrt{5}}`. If this is separated into the difference between two straight lines, we have :g:`BA <0466.g1:/ B A />` :math:`= \frac{r}{2}\sqrt{5 + 2\sqrt{5}} - \frac{r}{2}\sqrt{5 - 2\sqrt{5}}`.]
