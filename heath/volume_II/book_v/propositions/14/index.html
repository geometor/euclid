PROPOSITION 14.¶

If a first magnitude have to a second the same ratio as a third has to a fourth, and the first be greater than the third, the second will also be greater than the fourth ; if equal, equal ; and if less, less.

For let a first magnitude $A$ have the same ratio to a second $B$ as a third $C$ has to a fourth $D$ ; and let $A$ be greater than $C$ ;

I say that $B$ is also greater than $D$.

For, since $A$ is greater than $C$, and $B$ is another, chance, magnitude,

[V. 8]therefore $A$ has to $B$ a greater ratio than $C$ has to $B$.

But, as $A$ is to $B$, so is $C$ to $D$ ;

[V. 13]therefore $C$ has also to $D$ a greater ratio than $C$ has to $B$.
[V. 10]But that to which the same has a greater ratio is less ;
therefore $D$ is less than $B$ ; so that $B$ is greater than $D$.

Similarly we can prove that, if $A$ be equal to $C$, $B$ will also be equal to $D$ ; and, if $A$ be less than $C$, $B$ will also be less than $D$.

Therefore etc.

See Commentary on Proposition V.14.
