Commentary on Proposition V.21¶

Back to Proposition V.21.

[Folio 0185 | p. 179]

Algebraically, if

$$\begin{aligned} & a : b = e : f, \\ \text{and} \quad & b : c = d : e, \end{aligned}$$

$$\text{then, according as } a > = < c, \quad d > = < f.$$

Simson’s alterations correspond to those which he makes in Prop. 20. After the first case he proceeds thus.

“Secondly, let $A$ be equal to $C$; then shall $D$ be equal to $F$. Because $A$ and $C$ are equal,

[V. 7]$A$ is to $B$ as $C$ is to $B$.

But $A$ is to $B$ as $E$ is to $F$, and $C$ is to $B$ as $E$ is to $D$:

[V. 11]wherefore $E$ is to $F$ as $E$ to $D$,
[V. 9]and therefore $D$ is equal to $F$.

Next, let $A$ be less than $C$; then shall $D$ be less than $F$. For $C$ is greater than $A$, and, as was shown,

$C$ is to $B$ as $E$ to $D$,

and, in like manner,

$B$ is to $A$ as $F$ to $E$;

therefore $F$ is greater than $D$, by the first case, and therefore $D$ is less than $F$.”

The proof may be shown thus.

$$\text{According as } a > = < c, \quad a : b > = < c : b.$$

But $a : b = e : f$, and, by inversion, $c : b = e : d$.

$$\begin{aligned} \text{Therefore, according as } a > = < c, \quad & e : f > = < e : d, \\ \text{and therefore } & d > = < f. \end{aligned}$$
