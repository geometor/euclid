PROPOSITION 23.¶

If two numbers be prime to one another, the number which measures the one of them will be prime to the remaining number.

Let $A$, $B$ be two numbers prime to one another, and let any number $C$ measure $A$;

I say that $C$, $B$ are also prime to one another.

For, if $C$, $B$ are not prime to one another, some number will measure $C$, $B$.

Let a number measure them, and let it be $D$.

Since $D$ measures $C$, and $C$ measures $A$,
therefore $D$ also measures $A$.
But it also measures $B$ ;
[Folio 0331 | p. 325]
therefore $D$ measures $A$, $B$ which are prime to one another : which is impossible. [VII. Def. 12]
Therefore no number will measure the numbers $C$, $B$.
Therefore $C$, $B$ are prime to one another.
Q. E. D.

See Commentary on Proposition VII.23.
