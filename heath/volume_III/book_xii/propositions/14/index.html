PROPOSITION 14.¶

Cones and cylinders which are on equal bases are to one another as their heights.

For let $EB$, $FD$ be cylinders on equal bases, the circles $AB$, $CD$; I say that, as the cylinder $EB$ is to the cylinder $FD$, so is the axis $GH$ to the axis $KL$.

For let the axis $KL$ be produced to the point $N$, let $LN$ be made equal to the axis $GH$, and let the cylinder $CM$ be conceived about $LN$ as axis.

Since then the cylinders $EB$, $CM$ are of the same height, they are to one another as their bases. [XII. 11]
But the bases are equal to one another:
therefore the cylinders $EB$, $CM$ are also equal.
And, since the cylinder $FM$ has been cut by the plane $CD$ which is parallel to its opposite planes,
therefore, as the cylinder $CM$ is to the cylinder $FD$, so is the axis $LN$ to the axis $KL$. [XII. 13]
But the cylinder $CM$ is equal to the cylinder $EB$, and the axis $LN$ to the axis $GH$;
therefore, as the cylinder $EB$ is to the cylinder $FD$, so is the axis $GH$ to the axis $KL$.
But, as the cylinder $EB$ is to the cylinder $FD$, so is the cone $ABG$ to the cone $CDK$. [XII. 10]
Therefore also, as the axis $GH$ is to the axis $KL$, so is the cone $ABG$ to the cone $CDK$ and the cylinder $EB$ to the cylinder $FD$.
Q. E. D.
