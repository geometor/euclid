# Transcription Notes: Folio 0192 (Page 178)

## Summary & Folio Continuity
- **Folio**: `0192` (Page 178)
- **Header**: `178 BOOK I [I. DEFF. 8, 9`
- **Preceding Folio (`0191`, Page 177)**: Ends mid-quotation in item 2 (Eudemus on the angle as *quality*) quoting Aristotle's *Categories* (`"figure and the shape subsisting in each thing, and,`).
- **Current Folio (`0192`, Page 178)**: Completes the analysis of the three Aristotelian categories for the angle:
  - Concludes item 2 (*quality*, `ποιότης`): Eudemus's derivation of angle from `κλάσις` (deflection), objections from divisibility and equality vs. similarity (`ἀνόμοιοι`), and Simplicius's testimony (*in Arist. De caelo* 538, 21) that proponents of *quale* called equal angles *similar* angles (as Aristotle himself does in *De caelo* 296 b 20, 311 b 34).
  - Item 3 (*relation*, `πρός τι`): Syrianus's view of Euclid's `κλίσις` (inclination) vs. Euclid's treatment of angles as magnitudes contained (`περιεχομένη`) by two lines.
  - Syrianus/Proclus's synthesis: the angle partakes of all three categories (quantity in magnitude, quality in form, relation between bounding lines/planes).
  - Opens **"Ancient classification of 'angles'"** (from Proclus pp. 126, 7—127, 16, attributed to Geminus) and presents the full taxonomic tree of angles on surfaces and in solids.
- **Following Folio (`0193`, Page 179)**: Continues the sentence begun just above the taxonomic tree (`...In order to show it by a diagram it` -> `will be necessary to make a convention about terms.`).

## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Greek Terminology in Geminus's Classification**:
  - `ἐν στερεοῖς` — in solids (solid angles).
  - `κερατοειδής` — horn-like (line-concave angle between a straight line and a concave arc).
  - `ἀμφίκυρτοι` — convex-convex (biconvex curvilineal angle).
  - `ἀμφίκοιλοι` / `ξυστροειδεῖς` — concave-concave / "scraper-like" (shaped like a strigil, `ξύστρα`).
- **Typographic Note**: Under `made by "mixed" lines`, the printed text reads `e.g. the angle made by a curve, such as the cissoid and hippopede, with itself)` with a closing parenthesis `)` at the end of the gloss but no opening parenthesis before `e.g.`; preserved verbatim.
