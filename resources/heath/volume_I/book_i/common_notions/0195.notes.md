## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Veronese's Cluster of Rays**: Concludes the discussion of Def. 9 (rectilineal angle) by presenting Veronese's duality between a linear system of points on a line (segments) and a cluster of rays through a vertex $V$ (angles), establishing the equality of all flat angles.
- **Definitions 10, 11, 12 (Right, Obtuse, and Acute Angles; Perpendicular)**:
  - Gives the Greek text and English translation of Definitions 10–12.
  - **ἐφεξῆς** (*adjacent*): Literally "(next) in order," illustrated via Aristotle, *Physics* VI. 1, 231b8 (things having nothing of the same kind, συγγενές, between them).
  - **κάθετος** (*perpendicular*): Literally "let fall" (sc. ἡ κάθετος εὐθεῖα, a plumb-line let fall to the earth); Proclus (p. 283, 9) notes the older astronomical term κατὰ γνώμονα (*gnomon-wise*), from the upright stick set perpendicular to the horizon.
  - **Philosophical Priority of the Right Angle**: Cites Plato (*Republic* VI. 510C) on the three kinds of angles as hypotheses assumed by geometers, and Aristotle (*Metaphysics* 1084b7, 1035b6) on the priority of the right angle in definition (ὅτι ὥρισται), notion (τῷ λόγῳ), and form versus the priority of the acute angle as part and matter.
