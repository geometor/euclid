## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Conclusion of Notes on Definitions 10–12**:
  - Proclus (p. 133, 15) on the perpendicular as the measure of heights of figures and the right angle as the standard distinguishing rectilineal angles.
  - Pseudo-Aristotle, *Problems* (16, 4, 913b36) on rebound angles and the right angle as the *limit* (ὅρος) of opposite angles.
  - Proclus's qualification regarding mixed angles: the *horn-like* (κερατοειδής) angle (between circumference and tangent, cf. III. 16) and the "angle of a semicircle" are both less than a right angle, yet neither is an acute rectilineal angle. Note a minor original print ligature/type defect in line 18 ("less tnan an *acute* angle" -> transcribed as "less than an *acute* angle").
  - Notes that the *existence* of the right angle is proved by construction in Proposition I. 11.
- **Definition 13 (Ὅρος / Boundary)**:
  - Greek text: `Ὅρος ἐστίν, ὅ τινός ἐστι πέρας.` (*A boundary is that which is an extremity of anything.*)
  - Notes Aristotle's synonymous use of ὅρος and πέρας in *De gen. animal.* II. 6, 745a6, 9, and Proclus's remark (p. 136, 8) linking ὅρος to land measurement and boundary markers.
- **Definition 14 (Σχῆμα / Figure)**:
  - Greek text: `Σχῆμά ἐστι τὸ ὑπό τινος ἤ τινων ὅρων περιεχόμενον.` (*A figure is that which is contained by any boundary or boundaries.*)
  - Surveys Plato (*Meno* 76A: figure as the extremity of a solid, τοῦτο, εἰς ὃ τὸ στερεὸν περαίνει) and Aristotle (*Physics* I. 5, 188a25; *Categories* 8, 10a11 on σχῆμα as shape/μορφή in the category of quality; *De anima* III. 1, 425a18 on figure as a sort of magnitude; *De caelo* II. 4, 286b13 on plane figures as rectilineal or περιφερόγραμμον).
