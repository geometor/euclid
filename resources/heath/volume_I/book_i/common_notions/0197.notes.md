## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Aristotle on General Definitions of Figure**: Quotes *De anima* II. 3, 414b20–28, where Aristotle parallels the ordered series of figures (triangle, quadrilateral, etc.) with the species of soul, arguing against seeking an abstract general definition detached from irreducible species.
- **Originality of Euclid's Definition 14**: Heath contrasts Euclid's definition of figure with Plato's and Aristotle's:
  1. By requiring enclosure by a *boundary* (ὅρος), Euclid excludes the straight line and the angle (supported by Heron, the alternative definition of a straight line, and Geminus's distinction between a line that *forms a figure*, σχηματοποιοῦσα, and one that *extends indefinitely*, ἐπ’ ἄπειρον ἐκβαλλομένη, such as a hyperbola or parabola).
  2. Unlike Plato (*Meno* 76A) and Posidonius (via Proclus, p. 143, 8), who identify the figure with the *confining limit* (πέρας συγκλεῖον) or boundary itself, Euclid defines the figure as the *enclosed content* together with its boundary (so that a circle is the entire plane surface bounded by the circumference, not merely the circumference).
