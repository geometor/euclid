## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Textual Criticism of Def. 15 (Heiberg's Brackets & Papyrus Herculanensis No. 1061)**:
  - Explains why Heiberg brackets `ἣ καλεῖται περιφέρεια` and `πρὸς τὴν τοῦ κύκλου περιφέρειαν`: although present in the extant medieval MSS., both glosses are absent in Proclus, Taurus, Sextus Empiricus, Boethius, Papyrus Herculanensis No. 1061 (cf. Heiberg, *Hermes* XXXVIII, 1903, p. 47), and an-Nairīzī's Arabic text (while Heron omits the second gloss).
  - Notes that περιφέρεια was already used by Aristotle both generally (*contour*) and mathematically (rainbow, circumference, or arc of a circle), so Euclid could use it in Deff. 17–18 as a common term without formal definition.
- **Pre-Euclidean Antecedents**:
  - Plato, *Parmenides* 137E (στρογγύλον defined by extremes every way equidistant from the middle).
  - Aristotle, *De caelo* II. 4, 286b13–16 (περιφερόγραμμον plane figure bounded by one line), *Rhetoric* III. 6, 1407b27 (ἐπίπεδον τὸ ἐκ τοῦ μέσου ἴσον), and *De caelo* II. 4, 287a19 (contrasted with an egg-shaped figure). Note a minor print defect in line 25 where the 'b' in "bounded" is slightly nicked ("Dounded" -> transcribed as "bounded").
  - κέντρον (*centre*): Regularly used in Greek geometry and quoted by Proclus from the "oracles" (λόγια).
- **Nominal vs. Genetic Definition & Postulate 3**:
  - Emphasizes that Euclid's Def. 15 is not *genetic*—it defines what the word "circle" means, whereas the existence and constructibility of the circle are postulated in Postulate 3 (contrasted with Heron's genetic Def. 27 via a revolving straight line, and the commentary of Simplicius and an-Nairīzī on compasses and the circle as a plane surface).
