## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Centre vs. Pole (τῶν ἐντὸς τοῦ σχήματος κειμένων)**:
  - Notes that while Euclid generally distinguishes "circle" (the plane figure) from "circumference of a circle," he occasionally uses "circle" for the circumference (as in III. 10).
  - Cites Heron, Proclus (p. 153, 3), and Simplicius on the necessity of the qualification "lying within the figure" (i.e., in the plane of the circle) in Def. 15: any point outside the plane of the circle equidistant from all points of the circumference is a *pole* (lying on the perpendicular or "gnomon" erected at the centre).
- **Definition 17 (Διάμετρος / Diameter of a Circle)**:
  - Greek text: `Διάμετρος δὲ τοῦ κύκλου ἐστὶν εὐθεῖά τις διὰ τοῦ κέντρου ἠγμένη καὶ περατουμένη ἐφ’ ἑκάτερα τὰ μέρη ὑπὸ τῆς τοῦ κύκλου περιφερείας, ἥτις καὶ δίχα τέμνει τὸν κύκλον.`
  - Defends the authenticity and necessity of the final clause (`ἥτις καὶ δίχα τέμνει τὸν κύκλον`, omitted by Simson) as the required justification for Definition 18 (*semicircle*).
  - **διάμετρος vs. διαγώνιος**: Notes Simplicius's etymology of *diameter* (passing through and measuring/dividing equally), Aristotle's phrase τὰ κατὰ διάμετρον κείμενα ("things diametrically situated"), and Euclid's use of διάμετρος for the diagonal of a square or parallelogram (whereas διαγώνιος, *diagonal*, is a later term defined by Heron, Def. 67).
  - **Proofs of Bisection by a Diameter (Thales, Proclus, Saccheri)**:
    - Cites Proclus (p. 157, 10) attributing the theorem to Thales, along with Proclus's superposition proof.
    - Introduces Saccheri's proof (from *Euclides ab omni naevo vindicatus*) based on three preceding Lemmas regarding straight lines.
