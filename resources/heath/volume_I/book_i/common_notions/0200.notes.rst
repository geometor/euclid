Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
--------------------------------------------

- **Saccheri's Proof & Simson's Deduction (Def. 17)**:

  - Completes the four steps (i)–(iv) of Saccheri's proof that diameter $MAN$ bisects both the circle $MDHNKM$ and its circumference by folding portion $MNKM$ about $M, N$ onto $MNHDM$ and applying his three Lemmas on straight lines.
  - Notes Robert Simson's observation that bisection of the circle by its diameter also follows from III. 31 (angles in a semicircle are right angles, hence equal, making the two parts "similar segments" by III. Def. 11) and III. 24 (similar segments of circles on equal straight lines are equal to one another)—though that deduction comes much later than Book I, Def. 18.

- **Definition 18 (Ἡμικύκλιον / Semicircle)**:

  - Greek text: ``Ἡμικύκλιον δέ ἐστι τὸ περιεχόμενον σχῆμα ὑπό τε τῆς διαμέτρου καὶ τῆς ἀπολαμβανομένης ὑπ’ αὐτῆς περιφερείας. κέντρον δὲ τοῦ ἡμικυκλίου τὸ αὐτό, ὃ καὶ τοῦ κύκλου ἐστίν.``
  - Discusses the second sentence ("And the centre of the semicircle is the same as that of the circle"), which Heiberg restored from Proclus though omitted in the medieval MSS. Heath argues against Scarburgh's view that it is a gloss by Proclus, pointing out that Proclus's own fanciful comment—that the centre of a figure can lie inside (circle), on the perimeter (semicircle), or outside (single-branch hyperbola)—presupposes the sentence as part of Euclid's text.
  - Notes Proclus and Simplicius's observation on Euclid's ordering of plane figures by the number of bounding lines: 1 line (circle), 2 lines (semicircle), 3 lines (triangle), 4 lines (quadrilateral), and more than 4 lines (multilateral).
