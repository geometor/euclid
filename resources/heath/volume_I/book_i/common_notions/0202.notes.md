## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Etymology of Scalene (`σκαληνός`)**:
  - Connected by Proclus (p. 168, 24) with `σκάζω` ("to limp") and by others with `σκολιός` ("crooked, aslant"); used by Plato for odd numbers and by Apollonius for oblique circular cones.
- **Seven Species of Triangles & the "Four-Sided Triangle" Paradox**:
  - Proclus enumerates 7 species of triangles (1 equilateral, 3 isosceles, 3 scalene).
  - Discusses the paradox of the *four-sided triangle* (`ἀκιδοειδής`, *barb-like*, or `κοιλογώνιος`, *hollow-angled* in Zenodorus): a non-convex quadrilateral with a re-entrant angle, which ancients did not recognize as a fourth interior angle because it exceeds two right angles.
- **Definition 22 (Quadrilaterals)**:
  - Defines five classes of `τετράπλευρα`:
    1. `τετράγωνον` (*square*): equilateral and right-angled (`ἰσόπλευρόν τέ ἐστι καὶ ὀρθογώνιον`).
    2. `ἑτερόμηκες` (*oblong*): right-angled but not equilateral.
    3. `ῥόμβος` (*rhombus*): equilateral but not right-angled.
    4. `ῥομβοειδὲς` (*rhomboid*): opposite sides and angles equal, neither equilateral nor right-angled.
    5. `τραπέζια` (*trapezia*): all other quadrilaterals.
  - Notes Pythagorean origins of `τετράγωνον` and `ἑτερόμηκες` (*Metaph.* 986 a 26), and later broader uses of `τετράγωνον` for any four-angled figure in Aristotle (*De anima* II. 3, 414 b 31; *Metaph.* 1054 b 2), Heron (Def. 100), and Proclus (p. 166, 10).
