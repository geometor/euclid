## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Logical Redundancy in Definitions (Todhunter's Objection)**:
  - Addresses Todhunter's critique that Euclid's definitions of the square and rhomboid contain logically redundant properties (e.g., an equilateral quadrilateral with one right angle is already a square; opposite sides equal already implies opposite angles equal).
  - Explains Euclid's foundational principle: definitions may include multiple essential attributes so long as existence and possession of all attributes are proved by construction prior to use (as Euclid does for the square in Book I Prop. 46).
- **Etymology & Solid Geometry of the Rhombus (`ῥόμβος`)**:
  - Derived from `ῥέμβω` ("to turn round and round", a spinning-top).
  - Compares Archimedes' *solid rhombus* (two right circular cones sharing a base with vertices in opposite directions) and Proclus' description of a rhombus as a "spinning square" (`τετράγωνον ῥομβούμενον`) or a deformed/shaken square, citing J. H. T. Müller (*Beiträge zur Terminologie der griechischen Mathematiker*, 1860, p. 20).
- **Posidonius' & Heron's Seven-Species Classification of Quadrilaterals**:
  - Because Euclid has not yet defined parallels, he groups all non-parallelograms (and non-rhomboid/rhombus/oblong/square figures) under `τραπέζια`.
  - Posidonius (via Proclus) and Heron divide Quadrilaterals into `parallelograms` (`rectangular`: *square*, *oblong*; `non-rectangular`: *rhombus*, *rhomboid*) and `non-parallelograms` (`two sides parallel (trapezium)`: *isosceles trapezium*, *scalene trapezium*; `no sides parallel (trapezoid)`).
