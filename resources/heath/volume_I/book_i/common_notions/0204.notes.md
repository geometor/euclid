## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Restricted Sense of `τραπέζιον`**:
  - Notes that in Euclid's *On Divisions of Figures* (`περὶ διαιρέσεων`) and in Archimedes, `τραπέζιον` is used in the restricted sense of a quadrilateral with two parallel sides (as in Posidonius/Heron), whereas in Book I Def. 22 it encompasses all quadrilaterals other than squares, oblongs, rhombi, and rhomboids.
- **Definition 23 (Parallel Straight Lines)**:
  - Greek text: `Παράλληλοί εἰσιν εὐθεῖαι, αἵτινες ἐν τῷ αὐτῷ ἐπιπέδῳ οὖσαι καὶ ἐκβαλλόμεναι εἰς ἄπειρον ἐφ᾽ ἑκάτερα τὰ μέρη ἐπὶ μηδέτερα συμπίπτουσιν ἀλλήλαις.`
  - Lexical analysis of `Παράλληλος` (absent as one word in Plato, familiar in Aristotle), `εἰς ἄπειρον` / `ἐπ᾽ ἄπειρον` (adverbial "indefinitely" or "without limit", not "to a region at infinity"), and `ἐφ᾽ ἑκάτερα τὰ μέρη` ("in both directions", cf. Thucydides II. 96).
- **Aristotle on Non-Secant Parallels & Non-Euclidean Hypotheses**:
  - Cites *Analytica Posteriora* I. 12, 77 b 22 and *Analytica Priora* II. 17, 66 a 11, where Aristotle notes that one might conclude that parallels meet starting either from ($a$) the interior angle being greater than the exterior, or ($b$) the angles of a triangle exceeding two right angles.
- **Posidonius' & Simplicius' Equidistance Definitions**:
  - Quotes Proclus (p. 176, 6–17) on Posidonius' definition of parallels via equal perpendiculars (`ὁρίζειν δύναται`) and Simplicius (via an-Nairīzī, p. 25, ed. Curtze) on equidistant straight lines.
