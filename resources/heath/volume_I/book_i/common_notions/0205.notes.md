## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Simplicius & "Aganis" on Equidistance (an-Nairīzī, ed. Besthorn-Heiberg, pp. 9–10)**:
  - Quotes the definition of parallels by "the philosopher Aganis" based on constant shortest distance everywhere between the lines, which Aganis later uses to attempt a proof of the Parallel Postulate.
- **Geminus on Asymptotic vs. Equidistant Non-Meeting Lines (Proclus p. 177, 11)**:
  - Distinguishes non-meeting lines in a plane into those that remain at a constant distance and those that continually lessen their distance without meeting—specifically the hyperbola and its asymptote, and the conchoid and its asymptote ("the most paradoxical theorem in geometry, since it shows that the convergence of some lines is non-convergent").
  - Note: In line 20 of the printed text, Heath's translation of Geminus reads `"Of those which meet and are in one plane..."` (a slight slip in Heath's printed text for `"Of those which do not meet and are in one plane..."`, preserved verbatim per Zero-OCR fidelity).
- **Aristotle's *Petitio Principii* (*Anal. prior.* II. 16, 65 a 4) and Euclid's Postulate 5**:
  - Examines Aristotle's critique of circular reasoning among "those who think that they draw parallels" (`τὰς παραλλήλους γράφειν`), demonstrating that pre-Euclidean geometers tacitly assumed parallel properties, and that Euclid himself eliminated this *petitio principii* by formulating Postulate 5 prior to Book I Prop. 29.
  - Introduces Philoponus' commentary on this passage as an early trace of the *direction*-theory of parallels.
