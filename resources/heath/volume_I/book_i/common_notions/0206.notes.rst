Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
--------------------------------------------

- **Philoponus on the Meridian Plane & the Direction-Theory of Parallels**:

  - Quotes and interprets Philoponus' explanation of the pre-Euclidean *petitio principii*: attempting to draw parallel lines by placing them in the plane of the meridian circle (or aiming at a distant point on the meridian circle), which tacitly assumes what is sought.
  - Compares Heinrich Schotten's critique (*Inhalt und Methode des planimetrischen Unterrichts*, Vol. II, p. 188 sqq.) of the "north-to-south" meridian argument as a nominal rather than real identity of direction.

- **Schotten's Threefold Classification of Definitions of Parallels**:

  1. **Non-secant / No common point**: ($a$) do not cut one another; ($b$) meet at infinity; ($c$) have a common point at infinity (projective geometry).
  2. **Same or like direction**: including definitions via equal angles made with a transversal.
  3. **Constant distance (Equidistance)**: including the locus of points equidistant from a straight line.
  - Explains the conceptual bridges linking non-secancy, points at infinity, common direction, and equidistance.
