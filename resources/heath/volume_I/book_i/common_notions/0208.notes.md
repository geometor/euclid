## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Veronese's Reflex Definition & Postulate of Parallels**:
  - Completes the quotation of Veronese's definition of parallel lines via central symmetry about the midpoint of a common transversal, noting that Veronese's corresponding "Postulate of Parallels" (that parallel lines are reflex figures with respect to the midpoints of *all* their transversal segments) is less self-evident than Euclid's.
- **Group (2) — The Direction-Theory**:
  - Cites **C. L. Dodgson** (*Euclid and his modern Rivals*, 1879, p. 101), **Killing** (*Einführung in die Grundlagen der Geometrie*, I. p. 5, attributing the origin to **Leibniz**), and **Gauss** (*Werke*, IV. p. 365).
  - Exposes the *petitio principii* of defining parallels via "sameness of direction": recognizing identical direction via equal angles with *one* transversal requires an unstated axiom that the lines make equal corresponding angles with *any* fourth transversal.
- **Group (3) — Equidistant Straight Lines**:
  - Discusses **Clavius** (born 1537) and **Borelli** (*Euclides restitutus*, 1658), along with **Saccheri's** critique that defining parallels as equidistant straight lines requires a prior proof (impossible without a postulate) that the locus of points equidistant from a straight line is itself a straight line. Exposes the fallacy in Clavius' appeal to Euclid's Definition 4 ("lying evenly").
