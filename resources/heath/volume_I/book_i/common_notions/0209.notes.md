## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Greek Text & Idiom**:
  - `Ἠιτήσθω ἀπὸ παντὸς σημείου ἐπὶ πᾶν σημεῖον εὐθεῖαν γραμμὴν ἀγαγεῖν.` (Note the capital initial `Ἠ` with smooth breathing and iota adscript `Ἠιτήσθω` at the opening of the Postulates).
  - Explains the Greek universal quantifier idiom `ἀπὸ παντὸς σημείου ἐπὶ πᾶν σημεῖον` ("from every point to every point") rendered in English as "from any point to any point."
- **Threefold Import of Postulate 1**:
  1. **Existence**: Satisfaction of Aristotle's requirement that existence of primary lines (straight lines and circles) be postulated (contrasted with modern explicit point-existence axioms in Veronese, Ingrami, and Enriques).
  2. **Ideal vs. Instrumental Construction**: Refutes empirical objections (Aristotle, *Anal. post.* I. 10, 76b41) regarding imperfect drawing instruments, citing **an-Nairīzī** (ed. Curtze, p. 30, via Gherard of Cremona) and **Simplicius** ("drawing of a straight line from Aries to Libra").
  3. **Uniqueness**: Implies that the straight line joining two points is unique (*"two straight lines cannot enclose a space"*, often interpolated in MSS/editions as a Common Notion or Postulate, and explicitly linked to Postulate 1 by **Proclus**, p. 239, 16, on I. 4).
