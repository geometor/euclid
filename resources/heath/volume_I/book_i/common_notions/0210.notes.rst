- **Continuity**: Continues the discussion of Postulate 1 from Folio ``0209`` (Page 195), examining Proclus' and Saccheri's attempted proofs that two straight lines cannot enclose a space, before introducing **Postulate 2** and its implied uniqueness (that two straight lines cannot have a common segment). Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
--------------------------------------------

- **Postulate 1 Uniqueness Proofs**:

  - **Proclus (p. 239)**: Attempts to prove that two lines $ACB, ADB$ cannot enclose a space by describing a circle with centre $B$ and radius $BA$ cutting the lines produced in $F, E$. Heath points out the fallacy: it assumes $E$ and $F$ are distinct rather than coinciding (giving three common points).
  - **Saccheri**: Appeals to rotational invariance around fixed endpoints $A$ and $X$ derived from Euclid's Definition 4 ("lying evenly").

- **Postulate 2**:

  - **Greek Text**: ``Καὶ πεπερασμένην εὐθεῖαν κατὰ τὸ συνεχὲς ἐπ’ εὐθείας ἐκβαλεῖν.``
  - **Lexical Note**: ``πεπερασμένην`` ("finite", corresponding to the modern Italian *"rectilineal segment"* with two extremities).
  - **Zeno of Sidon's Objection (Proclus, p. 214, 18)**: The Epicurean Zeno argued that Proposition I. 1 tacitly assumes two straight lines cannot share a common segment, else the sides $AC, BC$ might meet before $C$ and share a segment up to $C$. Heath notes that Euclid does not explicitly state this until XI. 1, though I. 4 requires it.
