# Transcription Notes: Folio 0211 (Page 197)

## Summary & Folio Continuity
- **Folio**: `0211` (Page 197)
- **Section**: Book I, First Principles — Commentary on Postulate 2 (continued)
- **Continuity**: Continues mid-sentence from Folio `0210` (Page 196) ("triangle which is equal to it..."). Examines Proclus' and Simplicius' proof that two straight lines cannot share a common segment, Zeno of Sidon's and Posidonius' debate over the bisection of a circle by its diameter, and begins Saccheri's five-stage proof (Stage (i)), which continues onto Folio `0212` (Page 198).

## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Proclus (p. 215, 24) & Simplicius on Common Segments**:
  - Attempts to prove that $AC, AD$ cannot share common segment $AB$ by describing a circle with centre $B$ and radius $BA$ and appealing to the bisection of a circle by diameters $ABC$ and $ABD$.
  - **Zeno of Sidon vs. Posidonius**: Zeno objected that the superposition proof of circle bisection assumes two circumferences cannot share a common arc; Posidonius replied that the proof holds even if they fail to coincide in part only. Heath observes the deeper circularity: constructing a diameter through a given point on the circumference already requires producing the radius uniquely via Postulate 2.
- **Saccheri's Five-Stage Proof (Stage i)**:
  - Sets up the fan of rays $XB, XM, XD, XF, XC$ from the bifurcation point $X$ of a putative common segment $AX$, arguing that if $AXB$ and $AXC$ are both straight continuations of $AX$, then every intermediate ray $XM$ must also form a straight line $AXM$.
