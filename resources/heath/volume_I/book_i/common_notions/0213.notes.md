## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Zeno of Sidon & Posidonius on Hypothetical Construction**: Zeno criticized the proof that two straight lines $AC, AD$ cannot share a common segment $AB$ on the ground that erecting a perpendicular $BE$ assumes Book I, Prop. 11. Posidonius defended the proof via *hypothetical construction* ("suppose then it happens to be the straight line we have set up"), noting that the existence of a perpendicular is already implied in the definition of right angles.
- **Postulate 3**:
  - Greek text: `Καὶ παντὶ κέντρῳ καὶ διαστήματι κύκλον γράφεσθαι.`
  - Grammatical note: Euclid's text uses the passive infinitive (`γράφεσθαι`, "a circle can be drawn"), whereas Proclus quotes the active (`γράψαι`), matching Postulates 1 and 2.
  - **διάστημα (*distance* / *radius*)**: Heath notes that Greek mathematics had no single noun for *radius*, using `διάστημα` ("distance", or "dimension" in Aristotle's *Physics* IV. 1, 209 a 4) for the radius parameter when describing a circle, and `αἱ ἐκ τοῦ κέντρου [ἀγόμεναι γραμμαί]` ("straight lines drawn from the centre") for radial segments (e.g., Arist. *Meteorologica* III. 5, 376 b 8 with distance `ΜΠ`, and II. 5, 362 b 1; Eucl. III. Def. 1 and Prop. 26).
  - **Continuity and Infinitude of Space**: Citing W. B. Frankland, Heath notes that allowing a circle of *any* distance removes restrictions on size: (1) indefinitely small circles imply the *continuity* of space (not discrete with an irreducible minimum distance).
