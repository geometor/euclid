## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Infinitude vs. Unboundedness in I. 16**: Heath notes that Postulate 3 (allowing circles of arbitrarily large radius) implies the *infinitude* of space, which is required for the validity of the exterior angle theorem (I. 16)—a theorem that fails in spherical/elliptic spaces that are unbounded in extent but finite in size.
- **Postulate 4**:
  - Greek text: `Καὶ πάσας τὰς ὀρθὰς γωνίας ἴσας ἀλλήλαις εἶναι.`
  - **Homogeneity of Space**: Heath emphasizes that proving the equality of right angles by superposition requires assuming the *invariability of figures* (or *homogeneity of space*), so Euclid's Postulate 4 serves as the direct geometric postulate of spatial homogeneity.
  - **Proclus' Proof**: Applies right angle $DEF$ to $ABC$ with $EF$ falling along $BG$ inside $\angle ABC$, and produces $CB$ to $H$ and $GB$ to $K$, deriving the contradiction $\angle ABH > \angle ABG$ and $\angle ABH < \angle ABG$. Heath notes the hidden assumption that $CB$ and $GB$ have unique continuations and that $BK$ falls outside $\angle ABH$.
  - **Saccheri's Third Lemma**: *If two straight lines* $\text{AB}, \text{CXD}$ *meet one another at an intermediate point* $\text{X}$, *they do not touch at that point, but cut one another.* (Note: `AB`, `CXD`, and `X` are printed in Roman type inside the italicized lemma statement.)
