Transcription Notes: Folio 0217 (Page 203)
==========================================

Summary & Folio Continuity
--------------------------

- **Folio 0217 (Page 203)** continues the historical commentary on **Postulate 5**, completing Proclus' report of Geminus' critique (appealing to asymptotic curves, ``ἀσύμπτωτοι``), and discussing Simplicius' testimony (preserved in an-Nairīzī) regarding earlier proofs by "Abthiniathus" (or "Anthisathus"), Diodorus, and Ptolemy, together with Heath's conjecture connecting "Abthiniathus/Anthisathus" to Peithon, friend of Serenus of Antinoeia (*De sectione cylindri*, ed. Heiberg, p. 96).
- **Preceding Folio** (``0216.rst``, **p. 202**): Ends mid-sentence at the beginning of Proclus' critique of Postulate 5 ("It may be that some would be").
- **Following Folio** (``0218.rst``, **p. 204**): Continues the discussion of Simplicius ("Simplicius adds in full and word for word the attempt of his 'friend' or his 'master Aganis'...") and begins the section on Ptolemy's attempt to prove Postulate 5.

Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
--------------------------------------------

- **Geminus on Converging vs. Meeting Lines** (``ἀσύμπτωτοι``): Geminus (quoted by Proclus) argues that convergence of two lines does not logically entail intersection without proof, pointing to hyperbolas/conchoids that approach indefinitely yet remain non-secant (``ἀσύμπτωτοι``). He cites Aristotle (*Eth. Nic.* I. 3, 1094 b 25, asking scientific proofs of a rhetorician vs. plausibilities from a geometer) and Plato (*Phaedo* 92 D, Simmias on proofs from probabilities).
- **Simplicius (via an-Nairīzī) & "Abthiniathus"**:

  - Simplicius cites proofs of Postulate 5 by "Abthiniathus" (written "Anthisathus" in one Arabic passage, noted by H. Suter), Diodorus (possibly author of the *Analemma*), and Ptolemy (using I. 13, 15, and 16 or 18).
  - Heath conjectures that "Abthiniathus"/"Anthisathus" might represent a corruption of an epithet ("of Antinoeia" or "of Antissa") associated with **Peithon the geometer**, friend of Serenus of Antinoeia (Antinoupolis), who illustrated parallel lines via shadows cast by pillars with a torch or lamp burning behind them.
