## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Ptolemy's Alternative Proof of I. 28**:
  - Given lines $AB, CD$ cut by transversal $EFGH$ such that $\angle BFG + \angle FGD = 2\text{ right angles}$, the supplementary interior angles on the other side $\angle AFG + \angle FGC$ also equal two right angles.
  - Ptolemy argues by symmetry that if $FB, GD$ meet at $K$ on one side, then $FA, GC$ must also meet at $L$ on the other side, so that the two straight lines $LABK$ and $LCDK$ enclose a space (impossible).
  - Note: Inside the italicized sentence (`"*If therefore* FB, GD, *when the interior angles are equal to two right angles, meet at* K, *the straight lines* FA, GC *will also meet if produced*"`), the vertex labels `FB, GD`, `K`, and `FA, GC` are printed in Roman type for contrast.
- **Heath's Bracketed Clarification**: Points out that Ptolemy's symmetry step is made rigorous if one assumed the alternate interior angles were *severally* equal ($\angle BFG = \angle CGF$ and $\angle FGD = \angle AFG$), allowing triangle $KFG$ to be rotated in the plane about the midpoint $O$ of $FG$ to coincide with $LFG$, or by drawing a perpendicular $MN$ through $O$ (following Frankland).
