II. Ptolemy now tries to prove I. 29 without using our Postulate, and then deduces the Postulate from it (Proclus, pp. 365, 14—367, 27).

The argument to prove I. 29 is as follows.

The straight line which cuts the parallels must make the sum of the interior angles on the same side equal to, greater than, or less than, two right angles.

"Let <span data-ref="0219.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0219.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> be parallel, and let <span data-ref="0219.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span> meet them. I say (1) that <span data-ref="0219.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span> does not make the interior angles on the same side greater than two right angles.

![Ptolemy's diagram for parallel lines AB, CD cut by transversal FG](0219.g1.svg)

"For, if the angles <span data-ref="0219.g1:< A F G )">$AFG$</span>, <span data-ref="0219.g1:< C G F )">$CGF$</span> are greater than two right angles, the remaining angles <span data-ref="0219.g1:< B F G )">$BFG$</span>, <span data-ref="0219.g1:< D G F )">$DGF$</span> are less than two right angles.

"But the same two angles are also greater than two right angles; *for <span data-ref="0219.g1:/ A F /">$\text{AF}$</span>, <span data-ref="0219.g1:/ C G /">$\text{CG}$</span> are no more parallel than <span data-ref="0219.g1:/ F B /">$\text{FB}$</span>, <span data-ref="0219.g1:/ G D /">$\text{GD}$</span>, so that, if the straight line falling on <span data-ref="0219.g1:/ A F /">$\text{AF}$</span>, <span data-ref="0219.g1:/ C G /">$\text{CG}$</span> makes the interior angles greater than two right angles, the straight line falling on <span data-ref="0219.g1:/ F B /">$\text{FB}$</span>, <span data-ref="0219.g1:/ G D /">$\text{GD}$</span> will also make the interior angles greater than two right angles.*

"But the same angles are also less than two right angles; for the four angles <span data-ref="0219.g1:< A F G )">$AFG$</span>, <span data-ref="0219.g1:< C G F )">$CGF$</span>, <span data-ref="0219.g1:< B F G )">$BFG$</span>, <span data-ref="0219.g1:< D G F )">$DGF$</span> are equal to four right angles:

which is impossible.

"Similarly (2) we can show that the straight line falling on the parallels does not make the interior angles on the same side less than two right angles.

"But (3), if it makes them neither greater nor less than two right angles, it can only make the interior angles on the same side *equal* to two right angles."

III. Ptolemy deduces Post. 5 thus:

Suppose that the straight lines making angles with a transversal less than two right angles do not meet on the side on which those angles are.

Then, *a fortiori*, they will not meet on the other side on which are the angles *greater* than two right angles.

Hence the straight lines will not meet in either direction; they are therefore *parallel*.

But, if so, the angles made by them with the transversal are equal to two right angles, by the preceding proposition ($=$ I. 29).

Therefore the same angles will be both equal to and less than two right angles:

which is impossible.

Hence the straight lines will meet.
