# Transcription Notes: Folio 0220 (Page 206)

## Summary & Folio Continuity
- **Folio**: `0220` (Page 206)
- **Section**: Book I, Note on Postulate 5 (continued from Folio `0219` / Page 205 and continuing onto Folio `0221` / Page 207).
- **Content**:
  1. **Part IV of Ptolemy's attempt**: Proof that the straight lines must meet on the side where the interior angles are less than two right angles (using Euclid I.16, exterior angle theorem, on triangle $KFG$, though $FBK$ is drawn bent at $B$ and $D$).
  2. Critique of Ptolemy's fallacy (assuming Playfair's Axiom in disguise).
  3. **Proclus**: Introduction of the Zeno-like ("Achilles and the tortoise") bisection paradox (Proclus, *In Eucl.* p. 368, 26 sqq.) purporting to prove that the lines $AB, CD$ can never meet.

## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Original Typographical Slip in (2)**: Under step `(2)`, Heath prints `therefore they meet towards A, B, that is, on the side where are the angles less than two right angles.` Geometrically and by symmetry with `either towards A, C, or towards B, D` ten lines earlier, `A, B` is a slip in the 1908 print for `A, C`. We preserve `A, B` verbatim in `0220.md`.
- **Exterior Angle Application**: Note that in `0220.g1.png`, to depict the impossible meeting towards $B, D$ at $K$, the lines $AB$ and $CD$ bend at $B$ and $D$ to meet at $K$, forming what the text calls "the triangle $KFG$".
