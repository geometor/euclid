II. *If <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ A C /">$\text{AC}$</span>, <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ B D /">$\text{BD}$</span> be drawn from the extremities of <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ A B /">$\text{AB}$</span> at right angles to it and on the same side, and if <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ A C /">$\text{AC}$</span>, <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ B D /">$\text{BD}$</span> be made equal to one another and <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ C D /">$\text{CD}$</span> be joined, each of the angles <span data-ref="0223.g1:< A C D )">$\text{ACD}$</span>, <span data-ref="0223.g1:< B D C )">$\text{BDC}$</span> will be right, and <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ C D /">$\text{CD}$</span> will be equal to <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ A B /">$\text{AB}$</span>.*

The first part of this lemma is proved by *reductio ad absurdum* from the preceding lemma. If, e.g., the angle <span data-ref="0223.g1:< A C D )">$ACD$</span> is not right, it must either be acute or obtuse.

Suppose it is acute; then, by lemma 1, <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is greater than <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>, which is contrary to the hypothesis. And so on.

The angles <span data-ref="0223.g1:< A C D )">$ACD$</span>, <span data-ref="0223.g1:< B D C )">$BDC$</span> being proved to be right angles, it is easy to prove that <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> are equal.

[It is of course assumed in this "proof" that, if the angle <span data-ref="0223.g1:< A C D )">$ACD$</span> is acute, the angle <span data-ref="0223.g1:< B D C )">$BDC$</span> is obtuse, and *vice versa*.]

III. *In any triangle the three angles are together equal to two right angles.*

This is proved for a *right-angled* triangle by means of the foregoing lemma, the four angles of the quadrilateral <span data-ref="0223.g1:< A B C D >">$ABCD$</span> of that lemma being all right angles. The proposition is then true for *any* triangle, since any triangle can be divided into two right-angled triangles.

IV. Here we have the final "proof" of Post. 5. Three cases are distinguished, but it is enough to show the case where one of the interior angles is right and the other acute.

Suppose <span data-ref="0223.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0223.g2:/ C D /">$CD$</span> to be two straight lines met by <span data-ref="0223.g2:/ F E /">$FCE$</span> making the angle <span data-ref="0223.g2:< E C D )">$ECD$</span> a right angle and the angle <span data-ref="0223.g2:< C E B )">$CEB$</span> an acute angle.

Take any point <span data-ref="0223.g2:G">$G$</span> on <span data-ref="0223.g2:/ E B /">$EB$</span>, and draw <span data-ref="0223.g2:/ G H /">$GH$</span> perpendicular to <span data-ref="0223.g2:/ E C /">$EC$</span>.

Since the angle <span data-ref="0223.g2:< C E B )">$CEG$</span> is acute, the perpendicular <span data-ref="0223.g2:/ G H /">$GH$</span> will fall on the side of <span data-ref="0223.g2:E">$E$</span> towards <span data-ref="0223.g2:D">$D$</span>, and will either coincide with <span data-ref="0223.g2:/ C D /">$CD$</span> or not coincide with it. In the former case the proposition is proved.
