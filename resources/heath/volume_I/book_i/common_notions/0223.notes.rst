Transcription Notes: Folio 0223 (Page 209)
==========================================

Summary & Folio Continuity
--------------------------

- **Folio**: ``0223`` (Page 209)
- **Section**: Book I, Note on Postulate 5 (continued from Folio ``0222`` / Page 208 and continuing onto Folio ``0224`` / Page 210).
- **Content**:

  1. Completion of Naṣīraddīn aṭ-Ṭūsī's Lemma I (*b*) and Heath's bracketed summary of Saccheri's critique (which points out the possibility of an asymptotic common perpendicular—precisely what happens for ultraparallel lines in hyperbolic geometry).
  2. **Lemma II**: The birectangular isosceles quadrilateral $ABCD$ (later famous as the "Saccheri quadrilateral"), proving that the summit angles $ACD, BDC$ are right and $CD = AB$ by appealing to Lemma I.
  3. **Lemma III**: Proof that the angle sum of any triangle is equal to two right angles (Euclid I.32), deduced from the rectangle $ABCD$ of Lemma II.
  4. **Part IV**: Beginning of the final deduction of Postulate 5 (where $\angle ECD$ is right and $\angle CEB$ is acute), using Pasch-like triangle exit reasoning and an Archimedean step construction along $EC$ and $EB$ (continued on page 210).

Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
--------------------------------------------

- **Italicized Enunciations**: In Lemmas II and III, the enunciations are italicized with upright roman capitals for the geometric labels (``AC``, ``BD``, ``AB``, ``CD``, ``ACD``, ``BDC``).
- **Implicit Assumption in Lemma II**: Heath's bracketed note (``[It is of course assumed in this "proof" that, if the angle ACD is acute, the angle BDC is obtuse, and vice versa.]``) pinpoints why Lemma I cannot legitimately rule out the Hypothesis of the Acute Angle or the Hypothesis of the Obtuse Angle, since by symmetry $\angle ACD = \angle BDC$.
