## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Wallis's Proof & Similar Figures**: Heath concludes Wallis's proof using triangles $PCA$ and $\pi C \alpha$, noting in brackets that the proof rests on the postulate of the existence of similar figures of arbitrary magnitude, which Saccheri showed to be equivalent to assuming the "hypothesis of the right angle."
- **Gerolamo Saccheri (1667–1733)**: Discusses *Euclides ab omni naevo vindicatus* (1733), citing Engel & Stäckel (*Die Theorie der Parallellinien von Euklid bis auf Gauss*, 1895, pp. 41–136) and G. Boccardini (*L'Euclide emendato del P. Gerolamo Saccheri*, Milan, 1904), as well as Beltrami (1889) and Veronese (*Fondamenti di geometria*, p. 570).
- **Saccheri's Quadrilateral**: Introduces the birectangular isosceles quadrilateral $ABDC$ ($AC = BD$, with $AC \perp AB$ and $BD \perp AB$) and the three hypotheses regarding the equal summit angles at $C$ and $D$:
  1. *The hypothesis of the right angle* (Euclidean),
  2. *The hypothesis of the obtuse angle* (Riemannian),
  3. *The hypothesis of the acute angle* (Lobachevskian/Bolyaian).
