## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Lambert's Area Formulas & Imaginary Sphere**: Gives $\Delta = k(\pi - A - B - C)$ for the third hypothesis and $\Delta = k(A + B + C - \pi)$ for the second hypothesis, quoting § 82 of Lambert's tract on spherical triangles and the famous conjecture that the third hypothesis holds on an "imaginary spherical surface" (substituting $r\sqrt{-1}$ for $r$ in $k = r^2$, anticipating Lobachevsky's *Géométrie imaginaire*, 1837).
- **Adrien Marie Legendre (1752–1833)**: Surveys Legendre's attempts across the editions of his *Éléments de Géométrie* (1st ed. 1794 to 12th ed. 1823) and his final memoir *Réflexions sur différentes manières de démontrer la théorie des parallèles* (*Mémoires de l'Académie Royale des Sciences*, XII, 1833, p. 367 sqq.):
  - 1st ed. (1794) & 12th ed. notes: Dimensional/analytical argument assuming invariance under change of unit of length (equivalent to Wallis's similar figures postulate).
  - 2nd ed.: Assumption that a circle passes through any three non-collinear points.
  - 3rd ed. (1800) onwards: Geometric proofs of the Saccheri–Legendre theorem that the angle sum of a triangle cannot exceed two right angles.
