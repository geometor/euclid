Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
--------------------------------------------

- **Legendre's Angle Deficit Doubling Proof**: If $\triangle ABC$ has angle sum $2R - \delta$ with $A$ the smallest angle ($A < \frac{2}{3}R$), constructing $\triangle DBC \cong \triangle ACB$ on $BC$ and drawing a transversal $EDF$ across $AB$ and $AC$ produced yields $\triangle AEF$ composed of four sub-triangles ($ABC, DBC, DEB, FDC$). Since the angle sums of $\triangle DEB$ and $\triangle FDC$ cannot exceed $2R$, subtracting the straight angles ($2R$ each) at $B, C, D$ shows that $\triangle AEF$ has angle sum at most $2R - 2\delta$. Iterating $n$ times gives a deficit of at least $2^n \delta$, which eventually exceeds $2R$.
- **Hidden Postulate**: As Heath highlights in italics, the construction requires assuming that *through a given point $D$ within a given angle $A$ less than two-thirds of a right angle we can always draw a straight line which shall meet both sides of the angle*—an assumption that fails in hyperbolic geometry (where $D$ can lie outside the horocycle/asymptotic region of lines intersecting both rays of angle $A$) and is equivalent to Postulate 5.
