But again, in the twelfth, he returned to the plan of constructing any number of successive triangles such that the sum of the three angles in all of them remains equal to the sum of the three angles of the original triangle, but two of the angles of the new triangles become smaller and smaller, while the third becomes larger and larger; and this time he claims to prove in one proposition that the sum of the three angles of the original triangle is *equal* to two right angles by continuing the construction of new triangles *indefinitely* and compressing the two smaller angles of the ultimate triangle into nothing, while the third angle is made to become a *flat* angle at the same time. The construction and attempted proof are as follows.

Let <span data-ref="0229.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> be the given triangle; let <span data-ref="0229.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> be the greatest side and <span data-ref="0229.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> the least; therefore <span data-ref="0229.g1:< A C B )">$C$</span> is the greatest angle and <span data-ref="0229.g1:< C A B )">$A$</span> the least.

From <span data-ref="0229.g1:A">$A$</span> draw <span data-ref="0229.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> to the middle point of <span data-ref="0229.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, and produce <span data-ref="0229.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> to <span data-ref="0229.g1:C'">$C'$</span>, making <span data-ref="0229.g1:/ A C' /">$AC'$</span> equal to <span data-ref="0229.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>.

Produce <span data-ref="0229.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> to <span data-ref="0229.g1:B'">$B'$</span>, making <span data-ref="0229.g1:/ A B' /">$AB'$</span> equal to twice <span data-ref="0229.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>.

The triangle <span data-ref="0229.g1:< A B' C' >">$AB'C'$</span> is then such that the sum of its three angles is equal to the sum of the three angles of the triangle <span data-ref="0229.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span>.

For take <span data-ref="0229.g1:/ A K /">$AK$</span> along <span data-ref="0229.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> equal to <span data-ref="0229.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, and join <span data-ref="0229.g1:/ C' K /">$C'K$</span>.

Then the triangles <span data-ref="0229.g1:< A B D >">$ABD$</span>, <span data-ref="0229.g1:< A C' K >">$AC'K$</span> have two sides and the included angles respectively equal, and are therefore equal in all respects; and <span data-ref="0229.g1:/ C' K /">$C'K$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0229.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> or <span data-ref="0229.g1:/ D C /">$DC$</span>.

Next, in the triangles <span data-ref="0229.g1:< B' C' K >">$B'C'K$</span>, <span data-ref="0229.g1:< A C D >">$ACD$</span>, the angles <span data-ref="0229.g1:< B' K C' )">$B'KC'$</span>, <span data-ref="0229.g1:< A D C )">$ADC$</span> are equal, being respectively supplementary to the equal angles <span data-ref="0229.g1:< A K C' )">$AKC'$</span>, <span data-ref="0229.g1:< A D B )">$ADB$</span>; and the two sides about the equal angles are respectively equal;

therefore the triangles <span data-ref="0229.g1:< B' C' K >">$B'C'K$</span>, <span data-ref="0229.g1:< A C D >">$ACD$</span> are equal in all respects.

Thus the angle <span data-ref="0229.g1:< A C' B' )">$AC'B'$</span> is the sum of two angles respectively equal to the angles <span data-ref="0229.g1:< A B C )">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0229.g1:< A C B )">$C$</span> of the original triangle; and the angle <span data-ref="0229.g1:< C A B )">$A$</span> in the original triangle is the sum of two angles respectively equal to the angles at <span data-ref="0229.g1:A">$A$</span> and <span data-ref="0229.g1:B'">$B'$</span> in the triangle <span data-ref="0229.g1:< A B' C' >">$AB'C'$</span>.

It follows that the sum of the three angles of the new triangle <span data-ref="0229.g1:< A B' C' >">$AB'C'$</span> is equal to the sum of the angles of the triangle <span data-ref="0229.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span>.

Moreover, the side <span data-ref="0229.g1:/ A C' /">$AC'$</span>, being equal to <span data-ref="0229.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, and therefore greater than <span data-ref="0229.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, is greater than <span data-ref="0229.g1:/ B' C' /">$B'C'$</span> which is equal to <span data-ref="0229.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>.

Hence the angle <span data-ref="0229.g1:< C' A B' )">$C'AB'$</span> is less than the angle <span data-ref="0229.g1:< A B' C' )">$AB'C'$</span>; so that the angle <span data-ref="0229.g1:< C' A B' )">$C'AB'$</span> is less than $\frac{1}{2}A$, where <span data-ref="0229.g1:< C A B )">$A$</span> denotes the angle <span data-ref="0229.g1:< C A B )">$CAB$</span> of the original triangle.
