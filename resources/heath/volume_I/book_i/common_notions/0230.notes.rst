Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
--------------------------------------------

- **Typographical Detail in (3)**: On line 3 of p. 216, Heath prints ``the angle C''AB' is less than half the angle C'AB'`` (using $B'$ rather than $B''$, which is geometrically identical since $A, B, B', B''$ are collinear). This has been transcribed verbatim as ``$C''AB'$``.
- **Stein's Critique (1824)**: Heath cites J. P. W. Stein's refutation of Legendre's limiting argument in Gergonne's *Annales de Mathématiques* XV (1824), pp. 77–79 (in hyperbolic geometry, as $n \to \infty$, the side $Ab$ grows without bound and the angle at $c$ does not approach a straight angle $\pi$, but rather $\pi - \delta$).
- **Legendre's First Theorem (Saccheri–Legendre Theorem), First Proof**: Reproduces the proof from the 3rd edition of the *Éléments* (1800) using a chain of congruent triangles ($ABC, DCE, FEG, HGI, KIJ \dots$) along a baseline $AC E G J$, comparing the base segments $AC = CE = EG \dots$ with the upper segments $BD = DF = FH \dots$ via Euclid I. 24 (the Hinge Theorem).
