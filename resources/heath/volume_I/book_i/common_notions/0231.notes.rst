Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
--------------------------------------------

- **Legendre's Second Proof of Theorem I**: Uses the standard Euclidean construction of Eucl. I. 16 (bisecting side $BC$ at $H$ and doubling median $AH$ to $D$) to construct $\triangle ABD$ having the same angle sum $2R + \alpha$ as $\triangle ABC$, with one angle at most $\frac{1}{2}\angle CAB$. Iterating $n$ times yields an angle $\le (\angle CAB)/2^n < \alpha$, forcing the remaining two angles to exceed $2R$.
- **Riemann Hypothesis Note**: Heath (following Bonola) explicitly notes that both of Legendre's proofs, like Eucl. I. 16, assume that a straight line is infinite in length and does not return into itself, failing under elliptic (Riemannian) geometry.
- **Angular Deficit (Theorem II)**: Shows that if two component triangles have angle sums $2R - \alpha$ and $2R - \beta$, their composite triangle has angle sum $(2R - \alpha) + (2R - \beta) - 2R = 2R - (\alpha + \beta)$.
