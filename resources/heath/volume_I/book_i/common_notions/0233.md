For the proof of this we require the following

LEMMA. *It is always possible, through a point <span data-ref="0233.g1:P">$P$</span>, to draw a straight line which shall make, with a given straight line (<span data-ref="0233.g1:[ Q R' ]">$r$</span>), an angle less than any assigned angle.*

Let <span data-ref="0233.g1:Q">$Q$</span> be the foot of the perpendicular from <span data-ref="0233.g1:P">$P$</span> upon <span data-ref="0233.g1:[ Q R' ]">$r$</span>.

Let a segment <span data-ref="0233.g1:/ Q R /">$QR$</span> be taken on <span data-ref="0233.g1:[ Q R' ]">$r$</span>, on either side of <span data-ref="0233.g1:Q">$Q$</span>, such that <span data-ref="0233.g1:/ Q R /">$QR$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0233.g1:/ P Q /">$PQ$</span>.

![Diagram for Lemma to Proposition VII](0233.g1.svg)

Join <span data-ref="0233.g1:/ P R /">$PR$</span>, and mark off the segment <span data-ref="0233.g1:/ R R' /">$RR'$</span> equal to <span data-ref="0233.g1:/ P R /">$PR$</span>; join <span data-ref="0233.g1:/ P R' /">$PR'$</span>.

If <span data-ref="0233.g1:< Q P R )">$\omega$</span> represents the angle <span data-ref="0233.g1:< Q P R )">$QPR$</span> or the angle <span data-ref="0233.g1:< Q R P )">$QRP$</span>, each of the equal angles <span data-ref="0233.g1:< R P R' )">$RPR'$</span>, <span data-ref="0233.g1:< R R' P )">$RR'P$</span> is not greater than <span data-ref="0233.g1:< R P R' )">$\omega/2$</span>.

Continuing the construction, we obtain, after the requisite number of operations, a triangle $PR_{n-1}R_n$ in which each of the equal angles is equal to or less than $\omega/2^n$.

Hence we shall arrive at a straight line $PR_n$ which, starting from <span data-ref="0233.g1:P">$P$</span> and meeting <span data-ref="0233.g1:[ Q R' ]">$r$</span>, makes with <span data-ref="0233.g1:[ Q R' ]">$r$</span> an angle as small as we please.

To return now to the Proposition. Draw from <span data-ref="0233.g1:P">$P$</span> the straight line <span data-ref="0233.g1:[ P S ]">$s$</span> perpendicular to <span data-ref="0233.g1:/ P Q /">$PQ$</span>.

Then any straight line drawn from <span data-ref="0233.g1:P">$P$</span> which meets <span data-ref="0233.g1:[ Q R' ]">$r$</span> in <span data-ref="0233.g1:R">$R$</span> will form equal angles with <span data-ref="0233.g1:[ Q R' ]">$r$</span> and <span data-ref="0233.g1:[ P S ]">$s$</span>, since, by hypothesis, the sum of the angles of the triangle <span data-ref="0233.g1:< P Q R >">$PQR$</span> is equal to two right angles.

And since, by the Lemma, it is always possible to draw through <span data-ref="0233.g1:P">$P$</span> straight lines which form with <span data-ref="0233.g1:[ Q R' ]">$r$</span> angles as small as we please, it follows that all the straight lines through <span data-ref="0233.g1:P">$P$</span>, except <span data-ref="0233.g1:[ P S ]">$s$</span>, will meet <span data-ref="0233.g1:[ Q R' ]">$r$</span>. Hence <span data-ref="0233.g1:[ P S ]">$s$</span> is the only parallel to <span data-ref="0233.g1:[ Q R' ]">$r$</span> that can be drawn through <span data-ref="0233.g1:P">$P$</span>.

The history of the attempts to prove Postulate 5 or something equivalent has now been brought down to the parting of the ways. The further developments on lines independent of the Postulate, beginning with Schweikart (1780—1857), Taurinus (1794—1874), Gauss (1777—1855), Lobachewsky (1793—1856), J. Bolyai (1802—1860), Riemann (1826—1866), belong to the history of non-Euclidean geometry, which is outside the scope of this work. I may refer the reader to the full article *Sulla teoria delle parallele e sulle geometrie non-euclidee* by R. Bonola in *Questioni riguardanti le matematiche elementari*, I., of which I have made considerable use in the above, to the same author's *La geometria non-euclidea*, Bologna, 1906, to the first volume of Killing's *Einführung in die Grundlagen der Geometrie*, Paderborn, 1893, to P. Mansion's *Premiers principes de métagéométrie*, and P. Barbarin's *La géométrie non-Euclidienne*, Paris, 1902, to the historical summary in Veronese's *Fondamenti di geometria*, 1891, p. 565 sqq., and (for original sources) to Engel and Stäckel, *Die Theorie der Parallellinien von Euklid bis auf Gauss*, 1895, and *Urkunden zur Geschichte der nicht-Euklidischen Geometrie*, I. (Lobachewsky), 1899, and II. (Wolfgang und Johann Bolyai). I will only add that it was Gauss who first expressed a conviction that the Postulate could never be proved; he indicated this in reviews in the *Göttingische gelehrte Anzeigen*, 20 Apr. 1816 and 28 Oct. 1822, and affirmed it in a letter to Bessel of 27 January, 1829. The actual indemonstrability of the Postulate was proved by Beltrami (1868) and by Hoüel (*Note sur l'impossibilité de démontrer par une construction plane le principe de la théorie des parallèles dit Postulatum d'Euclide* in Battaglini's *Giornale di matematiche*, VIII., 1870, pp. 84—89).
