Alternatives for Postulate 5.
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

It may be convenient to collect here a few of the more noteworthy substitutes which have from time to time been formally suggested or tacitly assumed.

(1) *Through a given point only one parallel can be drawn to a given straight line* or, *Two straight lines which intersect one another cannot both be parallel to one and the same straight line.*

This is commonly known as "Playfair's Axiom," but it was of course not a new discovery. It is distinctly stated in Proclus' note to Eucl. I. 31.

(1 *a*) *If a straight line intersect one of two parallels, it will intersect the other also* (:person:`Proclus <proclus>`).

(1 *b*) *Straight lines parallel to the same straight line are parallel to one another.*

The forms (1 *a*) and (1 *b*) are exactly equivalent to (1).

(2) *There exist straight lines everywhere equidistant from one another* (:person:`Posidonius <posidonius>` and :person:`Geminus <geminus>`); with which may be compared Proclus' tacit assumption that *Parallels remain, throughout their length, at a finite distance from one another.*

(3) *There exists a triangle in which the sum of the three angles is equal to two right angles* (:person:`Legendre <legendre>`).

(4) *Given any figure, there exists a figure similar to it of any size we please* (:person:`Wallis <wallis>`, :person:`Carnot <carnot>`, :person:`Laplace <laplace>`).

:person:`Saccheri <saccheri>` points out that it is not necessary to assume so much, and that it is enough to postulate that *there exist two unequal triangles with equal angles*.

(5) *Through any point within an angle less than two-thirds of a right angle a straight line can always be drawn which meets both sides of the angle* (:person:`Legendre <legendre>`).

With this may be compared the similar axiom of Lorenz (*Grundriss der reinen und angewandten Mathematik*, 1791): *Every straight line through a point within an angle must meet one of the sides of the angle.*

(6) *Given any three points not in a straight line, there exists a circle passing through them* (:person:`Legendre <legendre>`, :person:`W. Bolyai <farkas-bolyai>`).

(7) "*If I could prove that a rectilineal triangle is possible the content of which is greater than any given area, I am in a position to prove perfectly rigorously the whole of geometry*" (:person:`Gauss <gauss>`, in a letter to :person:`W. Bolyai <farkas-bolyai>`, 1799).

Cf. the proposition of :person:`Legendre <legendre>` numbered IV. above, and the axiom of Worpitzky: *There exists no triangle in which every angle is as small as we please.*

(8) *If in a quadrilateral three angles are right angles, the fourth angle is a right angle also* (Clairaut, 1741).

(9) *If two straight lines are parallel, they are figures opposite to (or the reflex of) one another with respect to the middle points of all their transversal segments* (:person:`Veronese <veronese>`, *Elementi*, 1904).

Or, *Two parallel straight lines intercept, on every transversal which passes through the middle point of a segment included between them, another segment the middle point of which is the middle point of the first* (:person:`Ingrami <ingrami>`, *Elementi*, 1904).

:person:`Veronese <veronese>` and :person:`Ingrami <ingrami>` deduce immediately Playfair's Axiom.
