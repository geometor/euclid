## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Tannery's Thesis & Heath's Rebuttal**:
  1. *Order of Postulates and Common Notions*: Tannery argued Euclid would not have placed special geometrical principles (Postulates) before general principles (Common Notions). Heath counters that placing Common Notions between the Definitions and the Postulates would have awkwardly separated the Definitions of straight lines and circles from the Postulates asserting their existence.
  2. *Lexical history of ἔννοια*: Against Tannery's claim that ἔννοια only meant a notion of an *object* rather than a *proposition*, Heath cites Aristotle, *Eth. Nic.* IX. 11, 1171a32 (`ἡ ἔννοια τοῦ συναλγεῖν τοὺς φίλους`, "the notion that friends sympathise") and 1171b14, alongside Aristotle's fluid terminology for axioms (`τὰ κοινά`, `κοιναὶ δόξαι`).
  3. *Apollonius fragment in Proclus (p. 100, 6)*: Heath points out that Apollonius's use of `ἔννοιαν` of a line when measuring lengths of roads or walls is actually the older non-propositional sense ("notion of an object"), undermining Tannery's claim that Apollonius coined *Common Notions* for axioms.
