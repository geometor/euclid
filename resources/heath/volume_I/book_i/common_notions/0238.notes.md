## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Tannery vs. Heiberg on Axiom (*c*)**:
  - Heath endorses Tannery's observation against Heiberg that "halves of equals are equal" in I. 37, 38 cannot be deduced directly from Common Notion 3 (which subtracts equals from equals, without establishing that the remainder equals the subtracted part unless halves are already assumed equal).
- **Pappus's Axioms (*e*) and (*f*) & Simplicius's Proof**:
  - `συναναγράφεσθαι` ("to be on record with the others", Proclus pp. 197, 6—198, 5).
  - Simplicius's proof (preserved in an-Nairīzī) demonstrates (*e*) using linear segments $AB = CD$ with unequal additions $EB > FD$, cutting off $BG = DF$ so that $AG = CF$ (by C. N. 2) and the excess of $AE$ over $CF$ is $GE$, the excess of $BE$ over $DF$.
- **Common Notion 4 & `ἐφαρμόζειν`**:
  - **Greek Text**: `Καὶ τὰ ἐφαρμόζοντα ἐπ᾽ ἄλληλα ἴσα ἀλλήλοις ἐστίν.` (*Things which coincide with one another are equal to one another.*)
  - Heath distinguishes passive `ἐφαρμόζεσθαι` ("to be applied to", as in placing one figure upon another) from intransitive active `ἐφαρμόζειν` ("to fit exactly / coincide with").
