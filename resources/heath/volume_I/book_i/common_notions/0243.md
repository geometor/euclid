**Hilbert's system.**

The following are substantially the Postulates laid down.

(1) *If one segment is congruent with another, the second is also congruent with the first.*

(2) *If an angle is congruent with another angle, the second angle is also congruent with the first.*

(3) *Two segments congruent with a third are congruent with one another.*

(4) *Two angles congruent with a third are congruent with one another.*

(5) *Any segment $\text{AB}$ is congruent with itself, independently of its sense.*

This we may express symbolically thus:
$$AB \equiv AB \equiv BA.$$

(6) *Any angle $(\text{ab})$ is congruent with itself, independently of its sense.*

This we may express symbolically thus:
$$(ab) \equiv (ab) \equiv (ba).$$

(7) *On any straight line $\text{r}'$, starting from any one of its points $\text{A}'$, and on each side of it respectively, there exists one and only one segment congruent with a segment $\text{AB}$ belonging to the straight line $\text{r}$.*

(8) *Given a ray $\text{a}$, issuing from a point $\text{O}$, in any plane which contains it and on each of the two sides of it, there exists one and only one ray $\text{b}$ issuing from $\text{O}$ such that the angle $(\text{ab})$ is congruent with a given angle $(\text{a}'\text{b}')$.*

(9) *If $\text{AB}$, $\text{BC}$ are two consecutive segments of the same straight line $\text{r}$ (segments, that is, having an extremity and no other point common), and $\text{A}'\text{B}'$, $\text{B}'\text{C}'$ two consecutive segments on another straight line $\text{r}'$, and if $\text{AB} \equiv \text{A}'\text{B}'$, $\text{BC} \equiv \text{B}'\text{C}'$, then*
$$AC \equiv A'C'.$$

(10) *If $(\text{ab})$, $(\text{bc})$ are two consecutive angles in the same plane $\pi$ (angles, that is, having the vertex and one side common), and $(\text{a}'\text{b}')$, $(\text{b}'\text{c}')$ two consecutive angles in another plane $\pi'$, and if $(\text{ab}) \equiv (\text{a}'\text{b}')$, $(\text{bc}) = (\text{b}'\text{c}')$, then*
$$(ac) = (a'c').$$

(11) *If two triangles have two sides and the included angles respectively congruent, they have also their third sides congruent as well as the angles opposite to the congruent sides respectively.*

As a matter of fact, Hilbert's postulate corresponding to (11) does not assert the equality of the third sides in each, but only the equality of the two remaining angles in one triangle to the two remaining angles in the other respectively. He proves the equality of the third sides (thereby completing the theorem of Eucl. I. 4) by *reductio ad absurdum* thus. Let <span data-ref="0243.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span>, <span data-ref="0243.g1:< A' B' C' >">$A'B'C'$</span> be the two triangles which have the sides <span data-ref="0243.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0243.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> respectively congruent with the sides <span data-ref="0243.g1:/ A' B' /">$A'B'$</span>, <span data-ref="0243.g1:/ A' C' /">$A'C'$</span> and the included angle at <span data-ref="0243.g1:< B A C )">$A$</span> congruent with the included angle at <span data-ref="0243.g1:< B' A' C' )">$A'$</span>.

![Diagram for Hilbert's proof of SAS congruence](0243.g1.svg)

Then, by Hilbert's own postulate, the angles <span data-ref="0243.g1:< A B C )">$ABC$</span>, <span data-ref="0243.g1:< A' B' C' )">$A'B'C'$</span> are congruent, as also the angles <span data-ref="0243.g1:< A C B )">$ACB$</span>, <span data-ref="0243.g1:< A' C' B' )">$A'C'B'$</span>.

If <span data-ref="0243.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is not congruent with <span data-ref="0243.g1:/ B' C' /">$B'C'$</span>, let <span data-ref="0243.g1:D">$D$</span> be taken on <span data-ref="0243.g1:/ B' C' /">$B'C'$</span> such that <span data-ref="0243.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, <span data-ref="0243.g1:/ B' D /">$B'D$</span> are congruent, and join <span data-ref="0243.g1:/ A' D /">$A'D$</span>.
