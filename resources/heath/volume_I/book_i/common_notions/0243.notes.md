## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Hilbert's Congruence Axioms**: Heath summarizes Hilbert's Group III axioms of congruence as 11 numbered postulates (combining symmetric/transitive properties of segment and angle congruence, uniqueness of segment/angle laying-off, additivity of segments/angles, and the triangle SAS congruence postulate).
- **Typographical Variant in Postulate (10)**: In postulate (10), Heath prints `(ab) \equiv (a'b')`, followed by `(bc) = (b'c')` and `(ac) = (a'c')` using the standard equals sign `=` instead of the three-bar congruence symbol `\equiv` used in (5), (6), and (9). This has been preserved verbatim.
- **Upright vs. Italic Symbols in Italic Enunciations**: Inside the italicized postulate enunciations (5)–(10), geometric labels (`AB`, `(ab)`, `r'`, `A'`, `r`, `a`, `O`, `b`, `(a'b')`, `BC`, `A'B'`, `B'C'`, `(bc)`, `(b'c')`) are set in upright Roman type to contrast with the surrounding italic text, whereas the displayed equations (`AB \equiv AB \equiv BA`, etc.) are set in math italics.
