Hilbert's next proposition is that

*Given that the angle <span data-ref="0244.g2:< A O B )">$(\text{h}, \text{k})$</span> in the plane $\alpha$ is congruent with the angle <span data-ref="0244.g2:< A' O' B' )">$(\text{h}', \text{k}')$</span> in the plane $\alpha'$, and that <span data-ref="0244.g2:[ O C ]">$\text{l}$</span> is a half-ray in the plane $\alpha$ starting from the vertex of the angle <span data-ref="0244.g2:< A O B )">$(\text{h}, \text{k})$</span> and lying within that angle, there always exists a half-ray <span data-ref="0244.g2:[ O' C' ]">$\text{l}'$</span> in the second plane $\alpha'$, starting from the vertex of the angle <span data-ref="0244.g2:< A' O' B' )">$(\text{h}', \text{k}')$</span> and lying within that angle, such that*
$$(\text{h}, \text{l}) \equiv (\text{h}', \text{l}'), \text{ and } (\text{k}, \text{l}) \equiv (\text{k}', \text{l}').$$

If <span data-ref="0244.g2:O">$O$</span>, <span data-ref="0244.g2:O'">$O'$</span> are the vertices, we choose points <span data-ref="0244.g2:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0244.g2:B">$B$</span> on <span data-ref="0244.g2:[ O A ]">$h$</span>, <span data-ref="0244.g2:[ O B ]">$k$</span>, and points <span data-ref="0244.g2:A'">$A'$</span>, <span data-ref="0244.g2:B'">$B'$</span> on <span data-ref="0244.g2:[ O' A' ]">$h'$</span>, <span data-ref="0244.g2:[ O' B' ]">$k'$</span> respectively, such that <span data-ref="0244.g2:/ O A /">$OA$</span>, <span data-ref="0244.g2:/ O' A' /">$O'A'$</span> are congruent and also <span data-ref="0244.g2:/ O B /">$OB$</span>, <span data-ref="0244.g2:/ O' B' /">$O'B'$</span>.
