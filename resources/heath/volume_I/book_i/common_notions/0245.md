The congruence of the angles <span data-ref="0244.g2:< A O C )">$(h, l)$</span>, <span data-ref="0244.g2:< A' O' C' )">$(h', l')$</span> follows from (11) directly, and that of <span data-ref="0244.g2:< B O C )">$(k, l)$</span> and <span data-ref="0244.g2:< B' O' C' )">$(k', l')$</span> follows in the same way after we have inferred by means of (9) that, <span data-ref="0244.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0244.g2:/ A C /">$AC$</span> being respectively congruent with <span data-ref="0244.g2:/ A' B' /">$A'B'$</span>, <span data-ref="0244.g2:/ A' C' /">$A'C'$</span>, the difference <span data-ref="0244.g2:/ C B /">$BC$</span> is congruent with the difference <span data-ref="0244.g2:/ C' B' /">$B'C'$</span>.

It is by means of the two propositions just given that Hilbert proves that

*All right angles are congruent with one another.*

Let the angle <span data-ref="0245.g1:< B A D )">$BAD$</span> be congruent with its adjacent angle <span data-ref="0245.g1:< C A D )">$CAD$</span>, and likewise the angle <span data-ref="0245.g1:< B' A' D' )">$B'A'D'$</span> congruent with its adjacent angle <span data-ref="0245.g1:< C' A' D' )">$C'A'D'$</span>. All four angles are then right angles.

![Diagram for congruence of all right angles](0245.g1.svg)

If the angle <span data-ref="0245.g1:< B' A' D' )">$B'A'D'$</span> is not congruent with the angle <span data-ref="0245.g1:< B A D )">$BAD$</span>, let the angle with <span data-ref="0245.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> for one side and congruent with the angle <span data-ref="0245.g1:< B' A' D' )">$B'A'D'$</span> be the angle <span data-ref="0245.g1:< B A D'' )">$BAD''$</span>, so that <span data-ref="0245.g1:/ A D'' /">$AD''$</span> falls either within the angle <span data-ref="0245.g1:< B A D )">$BAD$</span> or within the angle <span data-ref="0245.g1:< C A D )">$DAC$</span>. Suppose the former.

By the last proposition but one (about adjacent angles), the angles <span data-ref="0245.g1:< B' A' D' )">$B'A'D'$</span>, <span data-ref="0245.g1:< B A D'' )">$BAD''$</span> being congruent, the angles <span data-ref="0245.g1:< C' A' D' )">$C'A'D'$</span>, <span data-ref="0245.g1:< C A D'' )">$CAD''$</span> are congruent.

Hence, by the hypothesis and postulate (4) above, the angles <span data-ref="0245.g1:< B A D'' )">$BAD''$</span>, <span data-ref="0245.g1:< C A D'' )">$CAD''$</span> are also congruent.

And, since the angles <span data-ref="0245.g1:< B A D )">$BAD$</span>, <span data-ref="0245.g1:< C A D )">$CAD$</span> are congruent, we can find within the angle <span data-ref="0245.g1:< C A D )">$CAD$</span> a half-ray <span data-ref="0245.g1:[ A D''' ]">$CAD'''$</span> such that the angles <span data-ref="0245.g1:< B A D'' )">$BAD''$</span>, <span data-ref="0245.g1:< C A D''' )">$CAD'''$</span> are congruent, and likewise the angles <span data-ref="0245.g1:< D A D'' )">$DAD''$</span>, <span data-ref="0245.g1:< D A D''' )">$DAD'''$</span> (by the last proposition).

But the angles <span data-ref="0245.g1:< B A D'' )">$BAD''$</span>, <span data-ref="0245.g1:< C A D'' )">$CAD''$</span> were congruent (see above); and it follows, by (4), that the angles <span data-ref="0245.g1:< C A D'' )">$CAD''$</span>, <span data-ref="0245.g1:< C A D''' )">$CAD'''$</span> are congruent: which is impossible, since it contradicts postulate (8).

Therefore etc.

Euclid I. 5 follows directly by applying the postulate (11) above to $ABC$, $ACB$ as distinct triangles.

Postulates (9), (10) above give in substance the proposition that "the sums or differences of segments, or of angles, respectively equal, are equal."

Lastly, Hilbert proves Eucl. I. 8 by means of the theorem of Eucl. I. 5 and the proposition just stated as applied to angles.

![Diagram for Hilbert's proof of Euclid I. 8](0245.g2.svg)

<span data-ref="0245.g2:< A B C >">$ABC$</span>, <span data-ref="0245.g2:< A' B' C' >">$A'B'C'$</span> being the given triangles with three sides respectively congruent, we suppose an angle <span data-ref="0245.g2:< C B A'' )">$CBA''$</span> to be determined, on the side of <span data-ref="0245.g2:/ B C /">$BC$</span> opposite to <span data-ref="0245.g2:A">$A$</span>, congruent with the angle <span data-ref="0245.g2:< A' B' C' )">$A'B'C'$</span>, and we make <span data-ref="0245.g2:/ B A'' /">$BA''$</span> equal to <span data-ref="0245.g2:/ A' B' /">$A'B'$</span>.

The proof is obvious, being equivalent to the alternative proof often given in our text-books for Eucl. I. 8.
