Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
--------------------------------------------

- **Greek Text of Common Notion 5**: ``καὶ τὸ ὅλον τοῦ μέρους μεῖζόν [ἐστιν].`` ("And the whole is greater than the part.") Heath notes Tannery's objection that this replaces the older geometrical phraseology in Euclid I. 6 ("the less to the greater: which is absurd") and is likely a later generalization, though accepted by Proclus.
- **Clavius's Axiom**: Note the exact wording printed by Heath: ``Clavius added the axiom that the whole is the equal to the sum of its parts.`` ("the equal" preserved verbatim).
- **Interpolated Common Notion [9]**: "Two straight lines do not enclose (or contain) a space," derived from Euclid I. 4 and found in two major MSS after Postulate 5 (or as Common Notion 9).
- **Pappus's Additional Axioms ($g$), ($h$), ($k$)**: Includes homeomericity of planes/lines ($g$), dimensional division/bounding ($h$, along with An-Nairīzī's sub-clauses $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$ noting the intersection of lines in a point needed for I. 1), and the potential infinity of magnitudes ($k$): ``τὸ ἄπειρον ἐν τοῖς μεγέθεσίν ἐστιν καὶ τῇ προσθέσει καὶ τῇ ἐπικαθαιρέσει, δυνάμει δὲ ἑκάτερον``.
- **Aristotelian Terminology for Division/Subtraction**:

  - ``τὸ ἄπειρον`` (the infinite / unlimited)
  - ``διαίρεσις`` (division) vs. ``σύνθεσις`` (addition)
  - ``ἀφαίρεσις`` (subtraction) and ``καθαίρεσις`` / ``ἐπικαθαίρεσις`` (diminution / successive diminution)
