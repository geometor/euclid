## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Aristotle's Potential vs. Actual Infinite (*Physics* III. 6–7)**:
  - `ἄπειρον` (infinite / unlimited)
  - `δυνάμει` (potentially) vs. `ἐνεργείᾳ` (in actuality)
  - `τόδε τι` (a particular substance/thing)
  - `οὐρανός` (the universe / heavens)
- **Convergence of Geometric Series & Exhaustion (206 a 25–b 13)**: Heath translates and paraphrases Aristotle's crucial mathematical observation in *Physics* III. 6: adding terms that decrease in a constant geometric ratio within a finite magnitude converges without exhausting the magnitude, whereas subtracting a fixed minimum magnitude (`one and the same magnitude whatever it is`) inevitably exhausts any finite magnitude—an explicit precursor to Euclid X. 1 and the Axiom of Archimedes/Eudoxus.
- **Number vs. Magnitude (207 b 1–5)**: Highlights the classic Greek duality between discrete number (bounded below by unity, unbounded above) and continuous physical magnitude (infinitely divisible below, bounded above by the finite cosmos `οὐρανός`).
