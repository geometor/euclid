Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
--------------------------------------------

- **Mathematicians and the Finite Line** (*Physics* **III. 7, 207 b 27**):

  - ``ἀδιεξίτητον`` (that which cannot be gone through / untraversable).
  - Heath highlights how Aristotle's statement—that geometers do not need an actually infinite line, only a finite segment as long as they please (``ὅσην βούλονται``)—aligns both with Euclid's Postulate 2 and with Bernhard Riemann's distinction between *unboundedness* and *infinite extent*.

- **Conflict Between Aristotle and Eudoxus's Lemma**: Heath contrasts Aristotle's physical bound on magnitude addition with the Eudoxian-Archimedean axiom quoted from the preface to Archimedes' *Quadrature of a Parabola* (which corresponds to Euclid V. Def. 4 and X. 1), explaining why Archimedes explicitly defended the lemma by appealing to the accepted proofs for circles (XII. 2), pyramids (XII. 7), and spheres (XII. 18).
- **Principle of Continuity &** ``διορισμός``: Introduces the foundational gap in Euclid's *Elements* regarding the existence of points of intersection of circles and lines (such as the two circles in Book I, Prop. 1), contrasting their unpostulated intersections with Postulate 5, which acts as a ``διορισμός`` (*discrimination*, or condition of possibility) guaranteeing the intersection of two straight lines.
