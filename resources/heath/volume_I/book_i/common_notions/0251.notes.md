## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Line-Circle Intersection via Dedekind's Cut**: To prove that a line $r$ having a point $A$ inside and a point $B$ outside a circle $C$ (centre $O$, radius $R$) intersects $C$ in two points, Heath (following Vitali) notes two prerequisites independent of circle intersections:
  1. Properties of the perpendicular $OP$ and obliques from $O$ to $r$ (monotonicity with respect to projection length).
  2. The triangle inequality (any side of a triangle is less than the sum of the other two).
- **Dedekind Cut on Segment $PB$**: Since $OP < OA < R$, the foot $P$ of the perpendicular lies inside $C$, and on $PB$ the distance $OX$ increases strictly with the projection $PX$, partitioning $AB$ (and $PB$) into points $H$ with $OH < R$ and points $K$ with $OK \geqq R$.
