## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Completion of Line-Circle Intersection Proof**: Uses a *reductio ad absurdum* with the triangle inequality ($OM' < OM + MM' = OM + \sigma < R$) to show that the Dedekind dividing point $M$ satisfies $OM = R$, and obtains the second intersection by symmetry across the perpendicular foot $P$.
- **Intersections of Two Circles (Lemma)**: To reduce the circle-circle intersection theorem to Dedekind's Postulate on circular arcs, Heath first establishes a Lemma: if circle $C$ (centre $O$, radius $R$) has a point $X$ inside and a point $Y$ outside circle $C'$ (centre $O'$, radius $R'$), then the line of centres $OO'$ meets $C$ in two antipodal points $A, B$, one inside $C'$ and one outside $C'$.
  - Case (1) ($A$ on $O'O$ produced) uses $\triangle OO'Y$ to prove $AO' > R'$ ($A$ outside $C'$).
  - Case (2) ($A$ on segment $OO'$) uses $\triangle OO'X$ to prove $AO' < R'$ ($A$ inside $C'$).
