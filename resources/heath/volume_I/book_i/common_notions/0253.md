(3) Thirdly, suppose <span data-ref="0253.g1:A">$A$</span> to lie on <span data-ref="0253.g1:/ O O' /">$OO'$</span> produced.

Then

$$R = OA = OO' + O'A \ldots\ldots\ldots (\gamma).$$

And, in the triangle <span data-ref="0253.g1:< O O' X >">$OO'X$</span>,

$$OX < OO' + O'X,$$

that is

$$R < OO' + O'X,$$

whence, by $(\gamma)$,

$$OO' + O'A < OO' + O'X,$$

or

$$O'A < O'X,$$

and <span data-ref="0253.g1:A">$A$</span> lies *inside* <span data-ref="0253.g1:( O' C' )">$C'$</span>.

It is to be observed that one of the two points <span data-ref="0253.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0253.g1:B">$B$</span> is in the position of case (1) and the other in the position of either case (2) or case (3): whence we must conclude that one of the two points <span data-ref="0253.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0253.g1:B">$B$</span> is *inside* and the other *outside* the circle <span data-ref="0253.g1:( O' C' )">$C'$</span>.

#### *Proof of theorem.*

The circle <span data-ref="0253.g2:( O A )">$C$</span> is divided by the points <span data-ref="0253.g2:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0253.g2:B">$B$</span> into two semicircles. Consider one of them, and suppose it to be described by a point moving from <span data-ref="0253.g2:A">$A$</span> to <span data-ref="0253.g2:B">$B$</span>.

![Intersections of two circles, Proof of theorem](0253.g2.svg)

Take two separate points <span data-ref="0253.g2:P">$P$</span>, <span data-ref="0253.g2:Q">$Q$</span> on it and, to fix our ideas, suppose that <span data-ref="0253.g2:P">$P$</span> precedes <span data-ref="0253.g2:Q">$Q$</span>.

Comparing the triangles <span data-ref="0253.g2:< O O' P >">$OO'P$</span>, <span data-ref="0253.g2:< O O' Q >">$OO'Q$</span>, we observe that one side <span data-ref="0253.g2:/ O O' /">$OO'$</span> is common, <span data-ref="0253.g2:/ O P /">$OP$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0253.g2:/ O Q /">$OQ$</span>, and the angle <span data-ref="0253.g2:< P O O' )">$POO'$</span> is less than the angle <span data-ref="0253.g2:< Q O O' )">$QOO'$</span>.

Therefore <span data-ref="0253.g2:/ O' P /">$O'P$</span> < <span data-ref="0253.g2:/ O' Q /">$O'Q$</span>.

Now, considering the semicircle <span data-ref="0253.g2:( O A )< A O B >">$APQB$</span> as divided into two parts, so that the points of the first part are inside the circle <span data-ref="0253.g2:( O' C' )">$C'$</span>, and those of the second part on the circumference of <span data-ref="0253.g2:( O' C' )">$C'$</span> or outside it, we have the conditions necessary for the applicability of the Postulate of Dedekind (which is true for arcs of circles as for straight lines); whence *there exists a point* <span data-ref="0253.g2:M">$M$</span> *separating the two parts.*

I say that <span data-ref="0253.g2:/ O' M /">$O'M$</span> = <span data-ref="0253.g2:( O' C' )">$R'$</span>.

For, if not, suppose <span data-ref="0253.g2:/ O' M /">$O'M$</span> < <span data-ref="0253.g2:( O' C' )">$R'$</span>.

If then <span data-ref="0253.g2:/ M M' /">$\sigma$</span> signifies the difference between <span data-ref="0253.g2:( O' C' )">$R'$</span> and <span data-ref="0253.g2:/ O' M /">$O'M$</span>, suppose a point <span data-ref="0253.g2:M'">$M'$</span>, which *follows* <span data-ref="0253.g2:M">$M$</span>, taken on the semicircle such that the chord <span data-ref="0253.g2:/ M M' /">$MM'$</span> is not greater than <span data-ref="0253.g2:/ M M' /">$\sigma$</span> (for a way of doing this see below).

Then, in the triangle <span data-ref="0253.g2:< O' M M' >">$O'MM'$</span>,

$$O'M' < O'M + MM' < O'M + \sigma,$$

and therefore

$$O'M' < R'.$$

It follows that <span data-ref="0253.g2:M'">$M'$</span>, a point on the arc <span data-ref="0253.g2:( O A )< M O B >">$MB$</span>, is inside the circle <span data-ref="0253.g2:( O' C' )">$C'$</span>: which is absurd.

Similarly it may be proved that <span data-ref="0253.g2:/ O' M /">$O'M$</span> is not greater than <span data-ref="0253.g2:( O' C' )">$R$</span>.

Hence <span data-ref="0253.g2:/ O' M /">$O'M$</span> = <span data-ref="0253.g2:( O' C' )">$R$</span>.
