### PROPOSITION 2.

<p class="enunciation">To place at a given point (as an extremity) a straight line equal to a given straight line.</p>

![Proposition 2 figure](0258.g1.svg)

Let $A$ be the given point, and $BC$ the given straight line.

Thus it is required to place at the point $A$ (as an extremity) a straight line equal to the given straight line $BC$.

From the point $A$ to the point $B$ let the straight line $AB$ be joined; <span class="justification">[Post. 1]</span>  
and on it let the equilateral triangle $DAB$ be constructed. <span class="justification">[I. 1]</span>

Let the straight lines $AE$, $BF$ be produced in a straight line with $DA$, $DB$; <span class="justification">[Post. 2]</span>  
with centre $B$ and distance $BC$ let the circle $CGH$ be described; <span class="justification">[Post. 3]</span>  
and again, with centre $D$ and distance $DG$ let the circle $GKL$ be described. <span class="justification">[Post. 3]</span>

Then, since the point $B$ is the centre of the circle $CGH$,
<p class="assertion">$BC$ is equal to $BG$.</p>

<sup>20</sup> Again, since the point $D$ is the centre of the circle $GKL$,
<p class="assertion">$DL$ is equal to $DG$.</p>

And in these $DA$ is equal to $DB$;  
therefore the remainder $AL$ is equal to the remainder $BG$. <span class="justification">[C.N. 3]</span>

<sup>25</sup> But $BC$ was also proved equal to $BG$;  
therefore each of the straight lines $AL$, $BC$ is equal to $BG$.

And things which are equal to the same thing are also equal to one another; <span class="justification">[C.N. 1]</span>
<p class="assertion"><sup>30</sup> therefore $AL$ is also equal to $BC$.</p>

Therefore at the given point $A$ the straight line $AL$ is placed equal to the given straight line $BC$.

<p class="qed">(Being) what it was required to do.</p>
