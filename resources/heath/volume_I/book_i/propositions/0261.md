Proclus contrives to make a number of “cases” out of this proposition also, and gives as many as eight figures. But he only produces this variety by practically incorporating the construction of the preceding proposition, instead of assuming it as we are entitled to do. If Prop. 2 is assumed, there is really only one “case” of the present proposition, for Potts' distinction between two cases according to the particular extremity of the straight line from which the given length has to be cut off scarcely seems to be worth making.

### PROPOSITION 4.

<p class="enunciation">If two triangles have the two sides equal to two sides respectively, and have the angles contained by the equal straight lines equal, they will also have the base equal to the base, the triangle will be equal to the triangle, and the remaining angles will be equal to the remaining angles respectively, namely those which the equal sides subtend.</p>

![Proposition 4 figure](0261.g1.svg)

Let <span data-ref="0261.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span>, <span data-ref="0261.g1:< D E F >">$DEF$</span> be two triangles having the two sides <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> equal to the two sides <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>, <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span> respectively, namely <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> to <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> and <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> to <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span>, and the angle <span data-ref="0261.g1:< B A C )">$BAC$</span> equal to the angle <span data-ref="0261.g1:< E D F )">$EDF$</span>.

I say that the base <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is also equal to the base <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, the triangle <span data-ref="0261.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> will be equal to the triangle <span data-ref="0261.g1:< D E F >">$DEF$</span>, and the remaining angles will be equal to the remaining angles respectively, namely those which the equal sides subtend, that is, the angle <span data-ref="0261.g1:< A B C )">$ABC$</span> to the angle <span data-ref="0261.g1:< D E F )">$DEF$</span>, and the angle <span data-ref="0261.g1:< A C B )">$ACB$</span> to the angle <span data-ref="0261.g1:< D F E )">$DFE$</span>.

For, if the triangle <span data-ref="0261.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> be applied to the triangle <span data-ref="0261.g1:< D E F >">$DEF$</span>,  
and if the point <span data-ref="0261.g1:A">$A$</span> be placed  
<p class="sub-clause">on the point <span data-ref="0261.g1:D">$D$</span></p>
and the straight line <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>  
<p class="sub-clause">on <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>,</p>
then the point <span data-ref="0261.g1:B">$B$</span> will also coincide with <span data-ref="0261.g1:E">$E$</span>, because <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>.
