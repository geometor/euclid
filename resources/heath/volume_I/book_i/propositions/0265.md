### PROPOSITION 5.

<p class="enunciation">In isosceles triangles the angles at the base are equal to one another, and, if the equal straight lines be produced further, the angles under the base will be equal to one another.</p>

![Proposition 5 figure](0265.g1.svg)

Let <span data-ref="0265.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> be an isosceles triangle having the side <span data-ref="0265.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>  
<sup>5</sup> equal to the side <span data-ref="0265.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>;  
and let the straight lines <span data-ref="0265.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>, <span data-ref="0265.g1:/ C E /">$CE$</span> be produced further in a straight line with <span data-ref="0265.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0265.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>. <span class="justification">[Post. 2]</span>

I say that the angle <span data-ref="0265.g1:< A B C )">$ABC$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0265.g1:< A C B )">$ACB$</span>, and the angle <span data-ref="0265.g1:< C B D )">$CBD$</span> to the angle <span data-ref="0265.g1:< B C E )">$BCE$</span>.

and let the straight lines <span data-ref="0265.g1:/ F C /">$FC$</span>, <span data-ref="0265.g1:/ G B /">$GB$</span> be joined. <span class="justification">[Post. 1]</span>

<p class="assertion"><sup>20</sup> Therefore the base <span data-ref="0265.g1:/ F C /">$FC$</span> is equal to the base <span data-ref="0265.g1:/ G B /">$GB$</span>,</p>
