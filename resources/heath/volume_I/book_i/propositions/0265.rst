PROPOSITION 5.
--------------

.. enunciation::

   In isosceles triangles the angles at the base are equal to one another, and, if the equal straight lines\ [#f1]_ be produced further, the angles under the base will be equal to one another.

.. picture:: 0265.g1.svg
   :alt: Proposition 5 figure

Let :g:`$ABC$ <0265.g1:< A B C >>` be an :eq:`isosceles <0265.g1:/ A B / = 0265.g1:/ A C />` triangle having the side :g:`$AB$ <0265.g1:/ A B />` :eq:`equal <0265.g1:/ A B / = 0265.g1:/ A C />` to the side :g:`$AC$ <0265.g1:/ A C />`;

and let the straight lines :g:`$BD$ <0265.g1:/ B D />`, :g:`$CE$ <0265.g1:/ C E />` be produced further in a straight line with :g:`$AB$ <0265.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$AC$ <0265.g1:/ A C />`. :just:`[Post. 2] <I.post.2>`

I say that the angle :g:`$ABC$ <0265.g1:< A B C )>` is :eq:`equal <0265.g1:< A B C ) = 0265.g1:< A C B ); 0265.g1:< C B D ) = 0265.g1:< B C E )>` to the angle :g:`$ACB$ <0265.g1:< A C B )>`, and the angle :g:`$CBD$ <0265.g1:< C B D )>` to the angle :g:`$BCE$ <0265.g1:< B C E )>`.

Let a point :g:`$F$ <0265.g1:F>` be taken at random on :g:`$BD$ <0265.g1:/ B D />`;\ [#f2]_ from :g:`$AE$ <0265.g1:/ A E />` the greater let :g:`$AG$ <0265.g1:/ A G />` be cut off :eq:`equal <0265.g1:/ A G / = 0265.g1:/ A F />` to :g:`$AF$ <0265.g1:/ A F />` the less; :just:`[I. 3] <I.3>`

and let the straight lines :g:`$FC$ <0265.g1:/ F C />`, :g:`$GB$ <0265.g1:/ G B />` be joined. :just:`[Post. 1] <I.post.1>`

Then, since :g:`$AF$ <0265.g1:/ A F />` is :eq:`equal <0265.g1:/ A F / = 0265.g1:/ A G /; 0265.g1:/ A B / = 0265.g1:/ A C />` to :g:`$AG$ <0265.g1:/ A G />` and :g:`$AB$ <0265.g1:/ A B />` to :g:`$AC$ <0265.g1:/ A C />`,

.. subclause::

   the two sides :g:`$FA$ <0265.g1:/ F A />`, :g:`$AC$ <0265.g1:/ A C />` are :eq:`equal <0265.g1:/ F A / = 0265.g1:/ G A /; 0265.g1:/ A C / = 0265.g1:/ A B />` to the two sides :g:`$GA$ <0265.g1:/ G A />`, :g:`$AB$ <0265.g1:/ A B />`, respectively;\ [#f3]_

and they contain a common angle, the angle :g:`$FAG$ <0265.g1:< F A G )>`.

.. assertion::

   Therefore the base :g:`$FC$ <0265.g1:/ F C />` is :eq:`equal <0265.g1:/ F C / = 0265.g1:/ G B />` to the base :g:`$GB$ <0265.g1:/ G B />`,

and the triangle :g:`$AFC$ <0265.g1:< A F C >>` is :eq:`equal <0265.g1:< A F C > = 0265.g1:< A G B >>` to the triangle :g:`$AGB$ <0265.g1:< A G B >>`, and the remaining angles will be :eq:`equal <0265.g1:< A C F ) = 0265.g1:< A B G ); 0265.g1:< A F C ) = 0265.g1:< A G B )>` to the remaining angles respectively, namely those which the :eq:`equal <0265.g1:/ A F / = 0265.g1:/ A G /; 0265.g1:/ A C / = 0265.g1:/ A B />` sides subtend,

.. subclause::

   that is, the angle :g:`$ACF$ <0265.g1:< A C F )>` to the angle :g:`$ABG$ <0265.g1:< A B G )>`,

   and the angle :g:`$AFC$ <0265.g1:< A F C )>` to the angle :g:`$AGB$ <0265.g1:< A G B )>`.

And, since the whole :g:`$AF$ <0265.g1:/ A F />` is :eq:`equal <0265.g1:/ A F / = 0265.g1:/ A G />` to the whole :g:`$AG$ <0265.g1:/ A G />`, and in these :g:`$AB$ <0265.g1:/ A B />` is :eq:`equal <0265.g1:/ A B / = 0265.g1:/ A C />` to :g:`$AC$ <0265.g1:/ A C />`,

.. assertion::
