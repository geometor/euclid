Summary

- **Content**:

  - Continuation and completion of the quotation from Aristotle's *Prior Analytics* (I. 24, 41 b 13—22) illustrating a pre-Euclidean proof of I. 5.
  - Figure ``0267.g1.svg`` illustrating Aristotle's circle with chord $AB$, centre $O$, rectilinear angles $E, F$ (or $A, B$), and horn/mixed angles $C, D$.
  - Three detailed commentary notes by Heath analysing Aristotle's mathematical language:

    1. Points $A, B$ described as "drawn" (ἠγμέναι) to the centre as if they were the radii themselves (with parallel noted in Eucl. IV. 4).
    2. The notation "angle $AC$" designating the compound angle $A + C$ (rectilinear angle $A$ or $E$ plus mixed angle $C$).
    3. The survival of the ancient phrases "angle *of* a semicircle" (III. 16) and "angle *of* a segment" (III. Def. 7).

  - Historical commentary on the role of "mixed" angles in pre-Euclidean geometry, citations of Heron's *Catoptrica* (Prop. 9) and the Pseudo-Euclidean *Catoptrica* (Euclid, Vol. VII., p. 294), and Thales' proposition on the bisection of circles by a diameter.

Polytonic Greek & Philological Details
--------------------------------------

- ``ἠγμέναι`` (item 1):

  - Form: Perfect passive participle, nominative feminine plural of ``ἄγω`` (*ágō*, "to draw, lead").
  - Breathings and accents verified: smooth breathing on initial ``ἠ-`` (psili) and acute on ``-έ-`` (tonos/oxeia).

- **Technical Phraseology**:

  - Heath italicizes "*of*" in ``“angle *of* a semicircle”`` and ``“angle *of* a segment”`` to emphasize that the English preposition reflects the Greek genitive article in Euclid's technical definitions (ἡ τοῦ ἡμικυκλίου γωνία and ἡ τοῦ τμήματος γωνία).
  - Heath emphasizes ``*one*`` in italics in ``in *one* semicircle`` and ``radii of *one* circle`` to distinguish between angles within a single semicircle and angles across different semicircles of unequal sizes.
