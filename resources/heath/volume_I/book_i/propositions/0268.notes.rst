Transcription Notes: Folio 0268 (Page 254)

Summary
-------

- **Content**:

  - Conclusion of Heath's commentary on the Aristotelian pre-Euclidean proof of I. 5, observing that Euclid's order and methods began at the very threshold of the subject.
  - Subsection ``### Proof without producing the sides.`` discussing the alternative proof recorded by Proclus (pp. 248, 22—249, 19), where points $D, E$ are taken directly on $AB, AC$ instead of producing them.
  - Subsection ``### Pappus' proof.`` documenting the famous proof by Pappus recorded by Proclus (pp. 249, 20—250, 12), which requires no auxiliary construction whatever.
  - Embedded graphic ``0268.g1.svg`` showing isosceles triangle $ABC$.
  - Verbatim quotation of Pappus' proof treating triangle $ABC$ as two congruent triangles ($ABC$ and $ACB$).
  - Critical analysis comparing Pappus' proof with the modern textbook method of "picking up the triangle, turning it over, and replacing it upon *itself*", referencing C. L. Dodgson's (*Lewis Carroll*) critique in *Euclid and his modern Rivals* (p. 47). Heath defends Pappus' method as conceptually superior because it views the single triangle under two aspects rather than assuming physical motion or a hypothetical second triangle.

Editorial & Typographical Findings
----------------------------------

- **Unclosed Parenthesis in Pappus' Proof**:

  - In Heath's printed text, the clause justifying the angle equality reads:
    ``(for these are the angles subtended by the equal sides $AB$, $AC$.``
    with an opening parenthesis ``(`` and terminal period ``.``, but no closing parenthesis ``)`` before the conclusion sentence ``Therefore in isosceles triangles...``.
  - This is an apparent typographical omission in the 1908 Cambridge edition. In accordance with zero-OCR fidelity standards, the text is transcribed faithfully as printed, with the omission documented here.

- **Emphasis and Typography**:

  - Italicized words preserved:

    - ``*produced*`` (contrasting internal segments with produced lines).
    - ``*itself*`` (superposition of a triangle upon itself).
    - ``*trace*`` (hypothetical impression left by a moved figure).
    - ``*result*`` vs. ``*process*`` (quoting previous theorems vs. repeating the congruence argument).
    - ``*two*`` (Pappus' conceptualization of two triangles in one).

  - Work citation: *Euclid and his modern Rivals*, p. 47 (by C. L. Dodgson).
