# Transcription Notes: Folio 0270 (Page 256)

## Summary
- **Content**:
  - Conclusion of the proof of Book I, Proposition 6 (`and the angle DBC is equal to...`), ending with `Therefore etc.` and `Q. E. D.`.
  - Analytical commentary on Euclid's tacit assumption regarding the ordering of points/rays when $D$ lies between $A$ and $B$.
  - Note on the first usage of the method of *reductio ad absurdum* in the *Elements* (with cross-reference to pp. 136, 140).
  - Subsection `### Geometrical Conversion.` discussing:
    - Distinction between *geometrical* conversion and *logical* conversion.
    - Augustus De Morgan's analysis (*Companion to the Almanac*, 1849, p. 7) proving Proposition 6 as a purely logical deduction from I. 5 and I. 18.
    - Proclus' classification of geometrical conversion (p. 252, 5 sqq.), beginning description of the *leading form* (προηγουμένη).
- **Folio Continuity**:
  - Preceding: Continues from Folio 0269 (page 255), which ends with `the two sides DB, BC are equal to the two sides AC, CB respectively;`, seamlessly resuming with `and the angle DBC is equal to the angle ACB;...`.
  - Succeeding: Concludes mid-phrase on the last line with `(1) The leading form (προηγουμένη), the conversion *par excellence* (ἡ κυρίως`, continuing directly onto Folio 0271 (page 257) with `ἀντιστροφή), is the complete or simple conversion in which...` to complete the term `(ἡ κυρίως ἀντιστροφή)`.
