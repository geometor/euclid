# Transcription Notes: Folio 0271 (Page 257)

## Summary
- **Content**:
  - Continuation of Heath's commentary on Euclid I. 6 regarding Proclus' classification of geometrical conversion (ἀντιστροφή).
  - Analysis of the second mode of conversion: *partial* conversion, exemplified by the relationship between I. 4 and I. 8.
  - Discussion of the distinction between *leading* theorems (προηγούμενα, which take the genus as hypothesis and prove a property) and *converse* theorems (which take the property as hypothesis and infer the genus).
  - Subsection `### Converse of *second part* of I. 5.` addressing Proclus' question regarding why Euclid did not convert the second part of I. 5 (the angles under the base).
  - Heath's presentation of an independent proof adapting Euclid's reduction method in I. 6, accompanied by diagram `0271.g1.png`.
- **Folio Continuity**:
  - **Preceding folio**: Folio 0270 (page 256) ended mid-sentence with: `(1) The leading form (προηγουμένη), the conversion *par excellence* (ἡ κυρίως`. Folio 0271 begins directly with `ἀντιστροφή), is the complete or simple conversion...`.
  - **Succeeding folio**: Folio 0271 ends at the conclusion of the auxiliary construction step: `Join $CH$.`. Folio 0272 (page 258) immediately proceeds with the congruence argument: `Then we have, in the two triangles $HFC$, $AGB$,...`.

## Editorial & Typographical Findings
- **Polytonic Greek**:
  - `ἀντιστροφή)`: alpha with smooth breathing (ἀ), acute on eta (ή). Completes the phrase `(ἡ κυρίως ἀντιστροφή)` begun on page 256.
  - `(προηγούμενα)`: neuter plural participle with acute on upsilon (ύ), referring to τὰ προηγούμενα θεωρήματα (leading theorems).
- **Typography and Emphasis**:
  - `*partial*`: contrasting partial conversion with complete conversion.
  - `*leading*` and `*converse*`: distinguishing primary theorems from secondary converse theorems.
  - `*leading theorem*` and `*converse*`: applied specifically to I. 5 and I. 6.
  - Heading: `### Converse of *second part* of I. 5.` reflects Heath's italicized emphasis on *second part*.
  - `*objections*`: translates Proclus' technical term ἐνστάσεις.
  - `*if the angles formed by producing two sides of a triangle beyond the base are equal, the triangle is isosceles*`: italicized proposition statement.
- **Graphic 0271.g1.png**:
  - The diagram illustrates the proof adapting Euclid's method.
  - Points labeled: $A$ at the apex; $H, B, F, D$ along the left side; $C, G, E$ along the right side; base $BC$; transversal segments $FC$, $GB$, $HC$; dashed line $FG$.
  - **Lettering Observation**: The terminal label at the bottom right of the right ray corresponds to point $E$ (the extension of $AC$ in Euclid I. 5, matching $D$ on the extension of $AB$). In the print impression, the letter has a slightly rounded contour, but it unambiguously represents $E$.
