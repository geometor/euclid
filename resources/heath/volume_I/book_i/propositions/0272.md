### Alternative proofs of I. 6.

Todhunter points out that I. 6, not being wanted till II. 4, could be postponed till later and proved by means of I. 26. Bisect the angle <span data-ref="0272.g1:< B A C )">$BAC$</span> by a straight line meeting the base at <span data-ref="0272.g1:D">$D$</span>. Then the triangles <span data-ref="0272.g1:< A B D >">$ABD$</span>, <span data-ref="0272.g1:< A C D >">$ACD$</span> are equal in all respects.

Another method depending on I. 26 is given by an-Nairīzī after that proposition.

![An-Nairizi Proof of I. 6 Diagram](0272.g1.svg)

Measure equal lengths <span data-ref="0272.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>, <span data-ref="0272.g1:/ C E /">$CE$</span> along the sides <span data-ref="0272.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span>, <span data-ref="0272.g1:/ C A /">$CA$</span>.  
Join <span data-ref="0272.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span>, <span data-ref="0272.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>.  
Then [I. 4] the triangles <span data-ref="0272.g1:< D B C >">$DBC$</span>, <span data-ref="0272.g1:< E C B >">$ECB$</span> are equal in all respects;  
therefore <span data-ref="0272.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span>, <span data-ref="0272.g1:/ D C /">$DC$</span> are equal, and the angles <span data-ref="0272.g1:< B E C )">$BEC$</span>, <span data-ref="0272.g1:< C D B )">$CDB$</span> are equal.

The supplements of the latter angles are equal [I. 13], and hence the triangles <span data-ref="0272.g1:< A B E >">$ABE$</span>, <span data-ref="0272.g1:< A C D >">$ACD$</span> have two angles equal respectively and the side <span data-ref="0272.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span> equal to the side <span data-ref="0272.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>.

Therefore [I. 26] <span data-ref="0272.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0272.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>.

### PROPOSITION 7.

<p class="enunciation">Given two straight lines constructed on a straight line (from its extremities) and meeting in a point, there cannot be constructed on the same straight line (from its extremities), and on the same side of it, two other straight lines meeting in another point and equal to the former two respectively, namely each to that which has the same extremity with it.</p>
