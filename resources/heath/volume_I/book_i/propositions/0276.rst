:title: Proposition 8

Then, :g:`$BC$ <0275.g1:/ B C />` coinciding with :g:`$EF$ <0275.g1:/ E F />`,

.. subclause::

   :g:`$BA$ <0275.g1:/ B A />`, :g:`$AC$ <0275.g1:/ A C />`\ [#f1]_ will also coincide with :g:`$ED$ <0275.g1:/ E D />`, :g:`$DF$ <0275.g1:/ D F />`;

for, if the base :g:`$BC$ <0275.g1:/ B C />` coincides with the base :g:`$EF$ <0275.g1:/ E F />`, and the sides :g:`$BA$ <0275.g1:/ B A />`, :g:`$AC$ <0275.g1:/ A C />` do not coincide with :g:`$ED$ <0275.g1:/ E D />`, :g:`$DF$ <0275.g1:/ D F />` but fall beside them\ [#f2]_ as :g:`$EG$ <0275.g1:/ E G />`, :g:`$GF$ <0275.g1:/ G F />`,

.. subclause::

   then, given two straight lines constructed on a straight line (from its extremities) and meeting in a point, there will have been constructed on the same straight line (from its extremities), and on the same side of it, two other straight lines meeting in another point and equal to the former two respectively, namely each to that which has the same extremity with it.

But they cannot be so constructed. :just:`[I. 7] <I.7>`

Therefore it is not possible that, if the base :g:`$BC$ <0275.g1:/ B C />` be applied to the base :g:`$EF$ <0275.g1:/ E F />`, the sides :g:`$BA$ <0275.g1:/ B A />`, :g:`$AC$ <0275.g1:/ A C />` should not coincide with :g:`$ED$ <0275.g1:/ E D />`, :g:`$DF$ <0275.g1:/ D F />`;

.. assertion::

   they will therefore coincide,

so that the angle :g:`$BAC$ <0275.g1:< B A C )>` will also coincide with the angle :g:`$EDF$ <0275.g1:< E D F )>`, and will be equal to it.

.. qed::

   If therefore etc. :just:`Q. E. D.`

----

.. [#f1] **BA, AC.** The text has here “:g:`$BA$ <0275.g1:/ B A />`, :g:`$CA$ <0275.g1:/ C A />`.”

.. [#f2] **fall beside them.** The Greek has the future, παραλλάξουσι. παραλλάττω means “to pass by without touching,” “to miss” or “to deviate.”

As pointed out above (p. 257) I. 8 is a *partial* converse of I. 4.

It is to be observed that in I. 8 :person:`Euclid <euclid>` is satisfied with proving the equality of the vertical angles and does not, as in I. 4, add that the triangles are equal, and the remaining angles are equal respectively. The reason is no doubt (as pointed out by :person:`Proclus <proclus>` and by :person:`Savile <savile>` after him) that, when once the vertical angles are proved equal, the rest follows from I. 4, and there is no object in proving again what has been proved already.

:person:`Aristotle <aristotle>` has an allusion to the theorem of this proposition in *Meteorologica* III. 3, 373 a 5—16. He is speaking of the rainbow and observes that, if equal rays be reflected from one and the same point to one and the same point, the points at which reflection takes place are on the circumference of a circle. “For let the broken lines :g:`$ACB$ <0277.g1:< A C B >>`, :g:`$AFB$ <0277.g1:< A F B >>`, :g:`$ADB$ <0277.g1:< A D B >>` be all reflected from the point :g:`$A$ <0277.g1:A>` to the point :g:`$B$ <0277.g1:B>` (in such a way that) :g:`$AC$ <0277.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$AF$ <0277.g1:/ A F />`, :g:`$AD$ <0277.g1:/ A D />` are all equal to one another, and the lines (terminating) at :g:`$B$ <0277.g1:B>`, i.e. :g:`$CB$ <0277.g1:/ C B />`, :g:`$FB$ <0277.g1:/ F B />`, :g:`$DB$ <0277.g1:/ D B />`, are likewise all equal; and let :g:`$AEB$ <0277.g1:/ A E B />` be joined. It follows that the triangles are *equal*; for they are upon the equal (base) :g:`$AEB$ <0277.g1:/ A E B />`.”
