### Philo's proof of I. 8.

This alternative proof avoids the use of I. 7, and it is elegant; but it is inconvenient in one respect, since three cases have to be distinguished. Proclus gives the proof in the following order (pp. 266, 15—268, 14).

Let <span data-ref="0277.g2:< A B C >">$ABC$</span>, <span data-ref="0277.g2:< D E F >">$DEF$</span> be two triangles having the sides <span data-ref="0277.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0277.g2:/ A C /">$AC$</span> equal to the sides <span data-ref="0277.g2:/ D E /">$DE$</span>, <span data-ref="0277.g2:/ D F /">$DF$</span> respectively, and the base <span data-ref="0277.g2:/ B C /">$BC$</span> equal to the base <span data-ref="0277.g2:/ E F /">$EF$</span>.

Let the triangle <span data-ref="0277.g2:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> be applied to the triangle <span data-ref="0277.g2:< D E F >">$DEF$</span>, so that <span data-ref="0277.g2:B">$B$</span> is placed on <span data-ref="0277.g2:E">$E$</span> and <span data-ref="0277.g2:/ B C /">$BC$</span> on <span data-ref="0277.g2:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, but so that <span data-ref="0277.g2:A">$A$</span> falls on the opposite side of <span data-ref="0277.g2:/ E F /">$EF$</span> from <span data-ref="0277.g2:D">$D$</span>, taking the position <span data-ref="0277.g2:G">$G$</span>. Then <span data-ref="0277.g2:C">$C$</span> will coincide with <span data-ref="0277.g2:F">$F$</span>, since <span data-ref="0277.g2:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0277.g2:/ E F /">$EF$</span>.

Now <span data-ref="0277.g2:/ F G /">$FG$</span> will either be in a straight line with <span data-ref="0277.g2:/ D F /">$DF$</span>, or make an angle with it, and in the latter case the angle will either be *interior* (κατὰ τὸ ἐντός) to the figure or *exterior* (κατὰ τὸ ἐκτός).

I. Let <span data-ref="0277.g2:/ F G /">$FG$</span> be in a straight line with <span data-ref="0277.g2:/ D F /">$DF$</span>.  
Then, since <span data-ref="0277.g2:/ D E /">$DE$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0277.g2:/ E G /">$EG$</span>, and <span data-ref="0277.g2:/ D F G /">$DFG$</span> is a straight line,  
<span data-ref="0277.g2:< D E G >">$DEG$</span> is an isosceles triangle, and the angle at <span data-ref="0277.g2:< E D G )">$D$</span> is equal to the angle at <span data-ref="0277.g2:< E G D )">$G$</span>.

II. Let <span data-ref="0277.g3:/ D F /">$DF$</span>, <span data-ref="0277.g3:/ F G /">$FG$</span> form an angle *interior* to the figure.  
Let <span data-ref="0277.g3:/ D G /">$DG$</span> be joined.  
Then, since <span data-ref="0277.g3:/ D E /">$DE$</span>, <span data-ref="0277.g3:/ E G /">$EG$</span> are equal,  
the angle <span data-ref="0277.g3:< E D G )">$EDG$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0277.g3:< E G D )">$EGD$</span>.  
Again, since <span data-ref="0277.g3:/ D F /">$DF$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0277.g3:/ F G /">$FG$</span>,  
the angle <span data-ref="0277.g3:< F D G )">$FDG$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0277.g3:< F G D )">$FGD$</span>.  
Therefore, by addition,  
the whole angle <span data-ref="0277.g3:< E D F )">$EDF$</span> is equal to the whole angle <span data-ref="0277.g3:< E G F )">$EGF$</span>.
