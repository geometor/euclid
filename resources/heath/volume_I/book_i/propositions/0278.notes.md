## Content Analysis
1. **Philo's Proof of Proposition 8 (Case III & Conclusion)**:
   - Case III: $DF, FG$ form an angle exterior to the figure (re-entrant vertex). Subtraction of base angles of $\triangle FDG$ from $\triangle EDG$ yields $\angle EDF = \angle EGF = \angle BAC$. Illustrated by Graphic `0278.g1.png`.
   - Concluding terminological note on Greek conventions: ancient Greek geometers did not admit angles $\ge 180^\circ$ (re-entrant angles) as angles; thus the re-entrant angle of quadrilateral $DEGF$ is ignored and $\angle DFG$ is described as being "outside" (*outside*) the figure.

2. **Proposition 9**:
   - **Enunciation**: *To bisect a given rectilineal angle.*
   - **Setting-out & Construction**: Given $\angle BAC$, take $D$ at random on $AB$, cut off $AE = AD$ from $AC$ [I. 3], join $DE$, construct equilateral triangle $DEF$ on $DE$, join $AF$.
   - **Proof**: Congruence of triangles $DAF$ and $EAF$ by I. 8 ($DA = EA$, $AF$ common, $DF = EF$), hence $\angle DAF = \angle EAF$.
   - **Conclusion**: Q. E. F. (*quod erat faciendum*), as this is a problem/construction.
   - Illustrated by Graphic `0278.g2.png`.

3. **Commentary on Proposition 9**:
   - Note on Euclid's omitted specification: Euclid tacitly assumes $DEF$ is constructed on the side of $DE$ opposite to $A$, leaving it to be inferred from the diagram.
   - Proclus' treatment of hypothetical objections regarding space below $DE$: constructing the equilateral triangle towards $A$ yields three cases depending on whether vertex $F$ falls on $A$, above $A$, or below $A$. Continues seamlessly onto Folio 0279 (page 265).
