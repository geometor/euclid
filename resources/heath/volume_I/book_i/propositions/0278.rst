III. Let :g:`$DF$ <0278.g1:/ D F />`, :g:`$FG$ <0278.g1:/ F G />` form an angle *exterior* to the figure. Let :g:`$DG$ <0278.g1:/ D G />` be joined. The proof proceeds as in the last case, except that subtraction takes the place of addition, and the remaining angle :g:`$EDF$ <0278.g1:< E D F )>` is equal to the remaining angle :g:`$EGF$ <0278.g1:< E G F )>`.

Therefore in all three cases the angle :g:`$EDF$ <0278.g1:< E D F )>` is equal to the angle :g:`$EGF$ <0278.g1:< E G F )>`, that is, to the angle :g:`$BAC$ <0278.g1:< B A C )>`.

It will be observed that, in accordance with the practice of the Greek geometers in not recognising as an “angle” any angle not less than two right angles, the re-entrant angle of the quadrilateral :g:`$DEGF$ <0278.g1:< D E G F >>` is ignored and the angle :g:`$DFG$ <0278.g1:< D F G )>` is said to be *outside* the figure.

PROPOSITION 9.
--------------

.. enunciation::

   To bisect a given rectilineal angle.

.. picture:: 0278.g2.svg
   :alt: Proposition 9 figure

Let the angle :g:`$BAC$ <0278.g2:< B A C )>` be the given rectilineal angle. Thus it is required to bisect it.

Let a point :g:`$D$ <0278.g2:D>` be taken at random on :g:`$AB$ <0278.g2:/ A B />`; let :g:`$AE$ <0278.g2:/ A E />` be cut off from :g:`$AC$ <0278.g2:/ A C />` :eq:`equal <0278.g2:/ A E / = 0278.g2:/ A D />` to :g:`$AD$ <0278.g2:/ A D />`; :just:`[I. 3] <I.3>`

let :g:`$DE$ <0278.g2:/ D E />` be joined, and on :g:`$DE$ <0278.g2:/ D E />` let the :eq:`equilateral <0278.g2:/ D E / = 0278.g2:/ D F / = 0278.g2:/ E F />` triangle :g:`$DEF$ <0278.g2:< D E F >>` be constructed; let :g:`$AF$ <0278.g2:/ A F />` be joined.

I say that the angle :g:`$BAC$ <0278.g2:< B A C )>` has been :eq:`bisected <0278.g2:< D A F ) = 0278.g2:< E A F )>` by the straight line :g:`$AF$ <0278.g2:/ A F />`.

For, since :g:`$AD$ <0278.g2:/ A D />` is :eq:`equal <0278.g2:/ A D / = 0278.g2:/ A E />` to :g:`$AE$ <0278.g2:/ A E />`, and :g:`$AF$ <0278.g2:/ A F />` is common,

.. assertion::

   the two sides :g:`$DA$ <0278.g2:/ D A />`, :g:`$AF$ <0278.g2:/ A F />` are :eq:`equal <0278.g2:/ D A / = 0278.g2:/ E A />` to the two sides :g:`$EA$ <0278.g2:/ E A />`, :g:`$AF$ <0278.g2:/ A F />` respectively.

And the base :g:`$DF$ <0278.g2:/ D F />` is :eq:`equal <0278.g2:/ D F / = 0278.g2:/ E F />` to the base :g:`$EF$ <0278.g2:/ E F />`;

.. assertion:: [I. 8] <I.8>

   therefore the angle :g:`$DAF$ <0278.g2:< D A F )>` is :eq:`equal <0278.g2:< D A F ) = 0278.g2:< E A F )>` to the angle :g:`$EAF$ <0278.g2:< E A F )>`.

Therefore the given rectilineal angle :g:`$BAC$ <0278.g2:< B A C )>` has been :eq:`bisected <0278.g2:< D A F ) = 0278.g2:< E A F )>` by the straight line :g:`$AF$ <0278.g2:/ A F />`.

.. qed::

   Q. E. F.

----

It will be observed from the translation of this proposition that :person:`Euclid <euclid>` does not say, in his description of the construction, that the equilateral triangle should be constructed on the side of :g:`$DE$ <0278.g2:/ D E />` opposite to :g:`$A$ <0278.g2:A>`; he leaves this to be inferred from his figure. There is no particular value in :person:`Proclus <proclus>`’ explanation as to how we should proceed in case any one should assert that he could not recognise the existence of any space below :g:`$DE$ <0278.g2:/ D E />`. He supposes, then, the equilateral triangle described on the side of :g:`$DE$ <0278.g2:/ D E />` towards :g:`$A$ <0278.g2:A>`, and hence has to consider three cases according as the vertex of the equilateral triangle falls on :g:`$A$ <0278.g2:A>`, above :g:`$A$ <0278.g2:A>` or below it.
