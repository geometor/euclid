# Transcription Notes: Folio 0279 (Page 265)

## Summary
- **Content**:
  - Continuation of Proclus’ alternative constructions for Euclid I. 9 from Folio 0278 (page 264): case where equilateral triangle $ADE$ is constructed towards $A$ on $DE$, points $F$ and $G$ taken equal on $AD$ and $AE$, lines $DG$ and $EF$ intersecting at $H$, and $AH$ bisecting the angle.
  - Figure `0279.g1.png` illustrating Proclus’ alternative construction.
  - Proclus’ refutation of the objection asserting that vertex $F$ of the equilateral triangle described on $DE$ (opposite to $A$) might fall on one of the arms of angle $BAC$ or outside it altogether.
  - Figure `0279.g2.png` displaying the two objection cases.
  - Demonstration that both cases lead to a contradiction via the second part of I. 5 (angles under the base of an isosceles triangle are equal).
  - Proclus’ remark (p. 271, 15—19) emphasizing that the angle must be *rectilineal*, noting the difficulty/impossibility of bisecting non-rectilineal or “horn-like” angles (formed between circle and tangent).
  - Subsection `### Trisection of an angle.` introducing Proclus’ historical note (p. 272, 1—12) on the trisection of angles and angle division in any ratio using higher/“mixed” curves: the conchoid of Nicomedes, the quadratrix of Hippias and Nicomedes, and the Archimedean spiral.
- **Folio Continuity**:
  - Connects directly from Folio 0278 (page 264), which ended: `The second and third cases do not differ`, continuing on page 265: `substantially from Euclid’s.`
  - Continues onto Folio 0280 (page 266), which details the constructions: `(a) Trisection by means of the conchoid.` and `(b) Use of the quadratrix.`
