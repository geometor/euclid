#### (a) Trisection by means of the conchoid.

I have already spoken of the conchoid of Nicomedes (note on Def. 2, pp. 160—1); it remains to show how it could be used for trisecting an angle. Pappus explains this (iv. pp. 274—5) as follows.

Let <span data-ref="0280.g1:< A B C )">$ABC$</span> be the given acute angle, and from any point <span data-ref="0280.g1:A">$A$</span> in <span data-ref="0280.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> draw <span data-ref="0280.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> perpendicular to <span data-ref="0280.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>.

![Trisection of Angle by Conchoid of Nicomedes](0280.g1.svg)

Complete the parallelogram <span data-ref="0280.g1:< F B C A >">$FBCA$</span> and produce <span data-ref="0280.g1:/ F A /">$FA$</span> to a point <span data-ref="0280.g1:E">$E$</span> such that, if <span data-ref="0280.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span> be joined, <span data-ref="0280.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span> intercepts between <span data-ref="0280.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> and <span data-ref="0280.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> a length <span data-ref="0280.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> equal to twice <span data-ref="0280.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>.

I say that the angle <span data-ref="0280.g1:< E B C )">$EBC$</span> is one-third of the angle <span data-ref="0280.g1:< A B C )">$ABC$</span>.

For, joining <span data-ref="0280.g1:A">$A$</span> to <span data-ref="0280.g1:G">$G$</span>, the middle point of <span data-ref="0280.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>, we have the three straight lines <span data-ref="0280.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0280.g1:/ D G /">$DG$</span>, <span data-ref="0280.g1:/ E G /">$EG$</span> equal, and the angle <span data-ref="0280.g1:< A G D )">$AGD$</span> is double of the angle <span data-ref="0280.g1:< A E D )">$AED$</span> or <span data-ref="0280.g1:< E B C )">$EBC$</span>.

But <span data-ref="0280.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> is double of <span data-ref="0280.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>;  
therefore <span data-ref="0280.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, which is equal to <span data-ref="0280.g1:/ D G /">$DG$</span>, is equal to <span data-ref="0280.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>.  
Hence the angle <span data-ref="0280.g1:< A G D )">$AGD$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0280.g1:< A B G )">$ABG$</span>.  
Therefore the angle <span data-ref="0280.g1:< A B D )">$ABD$</span> is also double of the angle <span data-ref="0280.g1:< E B C )">$EBC$</span>;  
so that the angle <span data-ref="0280.g1:< E B C )">$EBC$</span> is one-third of the angle <span data-ref="0280.g1:< A B C )">$ABC$</span>.

So far Pappus, who reduces the construction to the drawing of <span data-ref="0280.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span> so that <span data-ref="0280.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> shall be equal to twice <span data-ref="0280.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>.

This is what the conchoid constructed with <span data-ref="0280.g1:B">$B$</span> as *pole*, <span data-ref="0280.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> as *directrix*, and *distance* equal to twice <span data-ref="0280.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> enables us to do; for that conchoid cuts <span data-ref="0280.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> in the required point <span data-ref="0280.g1:E">$E$</span>.

#### (b) Use of the quadratrix.

The plural *quadratrices* in the above passage is a Hellenism for the singular *quadratrix*, which was a curve discovered by Hippias of Elis about 420 B.C. According to Proclus (p. 356, 11) Hippias proved its properties; and we are told (1) in the passage quoted above that Nicomedes also investigated it and that it was used for trisecting an angle, and (2) by Pappus (iv. pp. 250, 33—252, 4) that it was used by Dinostratus and Nicomedes and some more recent writers for squaring the circle, whence its name. It is described thus (Pappus iv. p. 252).

Suppose that <span data-ref="0280.g2:< A B C D >">$ABCD$</span> is a square and <span data-ref="0280.g2:( B E D )">$BED$</span> a quadrant of a circle with centre <span data-ref="0280.g2:A">$A$</span>.

![Quadratrix of Hippias Diagram](0280.g2.svg)

Suppose (1) that a radius of the circle moves uniformly about <span data-ref="0280.g2:A">$A$</span> from the position <span data-ref="0280.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span> to the position <span data-ref="0280.g2:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, and (2) that *in the same time* the line <span data-ref="0280.g2:/ B C /">$BC$</span> moves uniformly, always parallel to itself, and with its extremity <span data-ref="0280.g2:B">$B$</span> moving along <span data-ref="0280.g2:/ B A /">$BA$</span>, from the position <span data-ref="0280.g2:/ B C /">$BC$</span> to the position <span data-ref="0280.g2:/ A D /">$AD$</span>.

Then the radius <span data-ref="0280.g2:/ A E /">$AE$</span> and the moving line <span data-ref="0280.g2:/ B C /">$BC$</span> determine at any instant by their intersection a point <span data-ref="0280.g2:F">$F$</span>.

The locus of <span data-ref="0280.g2:F">$F$</span> is the *quadratrix*.
