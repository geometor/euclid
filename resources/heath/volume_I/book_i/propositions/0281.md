The property of the curve is that, if <span data-ref="0280.g2:F">$F$</span> is any point, the arc <span data-ref="0280.g2:( B E D )">$BED$</span> is to the arc <span data-ref="0280.g2:( E D )">$ED$</span> as <span data-ref="0280.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0280.g2:/ F H /">$FH$</span>.

In other words, if <span data-ref="0280.g2:< F A D )">$\phi$</span> is the angle <span data-ref="0280.g2:< F A D )">$FAD$</span>, <span data-ref="0280.g2:/ A F /">$\rho$</span> the radius vector <span data-ref="0280.g2:/ A F /">$AF$</span> and <span data-ref="0280.g2:/ A D /">$a$</span> the side of the square,

$$(\rho \sin \phi)/a = \phi/\frac{1}{2}\pi.$$

Now the angle <span data-ref="0280.g2:< E A D )">$EAD$</span> can not only be *trisected* but *divided in any given ratio* by means of the quadratrix (Pappus iv. p. 286).

For let <span data-ref="0280.g2:/ F H /">$FH$</span> be divided at <span data-ref="0280.g2:K">$K$</span> in the given ratio.

Draw <span data-ref="0280.g2:/ K L /">$KL$</span> parallel to <span data-ref="0280.g2:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, meeting the curve in <span data-ref="0280.g2:L">$L$</span>; join <span data-ref="0280.g2:/ A L /">$AL$</span> and produce it to meet the circle in <span data-ref="0280.g2:N">$N$</span>.

Then the angles <span data-ref="0280.g2:< E A N )">$EAN$</span>, <span data-ref="0280.g2:< N A D )">$NAD$</span> are in the ratio of <span data-ref="0280.g2:/ F K /">$FK$</span> to <span data-ref="0280.g2:/ K H /">$KH$</span>, as is easily proved.

#### (c) Use of the *spiral of Archimedes*.

The trisection of an angle, or the division of an angle in any ratio, by means of the *spiral of Archimedes* is of course an equally simple matter.  Suppose any angle included between the two radii vectores $OA$ and $OB$ of the spiral, and let it be required to cut the angle $AOB$ in a given ratio.  Since the radius vector increases proportionally with the angle described by the vector which generates the curve (reckoned from the original position of the vector coinciding with the initial line to the particular position assumed), we have only to take the radius vector $OB$ (the greater of the two $OA$, $OB$), mark off $OC$ along it equal to $OA$, cut $CB$ in the given ratio (at $D$ say), and then draw the circle with centre $O$ and radius $OD$ cutting the spiral in $E$.  Then $OE$ will divide the angle $AOB$ in the required manner.

### PROPOSITION 10.

<p class="enunciation">To bisect a given finite straight line.</p>

![Proposition 10 figure](0281.g1.svg)

Let <span data-ref="0281.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> be the given finite straight line.  
Thus it is required to bisect the finite straight line <span data-ref="0281.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>.

Let the equilateral triangle <span data-ref="0281.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> be constructed on it, <span class="justification">[I. 1]</span>  
and let the angle <span data-ref="0281.g1:< A C B )">$ACB$</span> be bisected by the straight line <span data-ref="0281.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>; <span class="justification">[I. 9]</span>

I say that the straight line <span data-ref="0281.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> has been bisected at the point <span data-ref="0281.g1:D">$D$</span>.

For, since <span data-ref="0281.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0281.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span>,  
and <span data-ref="0281.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> is common,  
<p class="assertion">the two sides <span data-ref="0281.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0281.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> are equal to the two sides <span data-ref="0281.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, <span data-ref="0281.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> respectively;</p>
and the angle <span data-ref="0281.g1:< A C D )">$ACD$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0281.g1:< B C D )">$BCD$</span>;  
<p class="assertion">therefore the base <span data-ref="0281.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> is equal to the base <span data-ref="0281.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>. <span class="justification">[I. 4]</span></p>

Therefore the given finite straight line <span data-ref="0281.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> has been bisected at <span data-ref="0281.g1:D">$D$</span>.

<p class="qed">Q. E. F.</p>
