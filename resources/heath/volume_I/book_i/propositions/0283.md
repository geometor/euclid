### PROPOSITION 11.

<p class="enunciation">To draw a straight line at right angles to a given straight line from a given point on it.</p>

![Proposition 11 figure](0283.g1.png)

Let $AB$ be the given straight line, and $C$ the given point on it.

<sup>5</sup> Thus it is required to draw from the point $C$ a straight line at right angles to the straight line $AB$.

Let a point $D$ be taken at random on $AC$;  
<sup>10</sup> let $CE$ be made equal to $CD$; <span class="justification">[I. 3]</span>  
on $DE$ let the equilateral triangle $FDE$ be constructed, <span class="justification">[I. 1]</span>  
and let $FC$ be joined;

I say that the straight line $FC$ has been drawn at right  
<sup>15</sup> angles to the given straight line $AB$ from $C$ the given point on it.

For, since $DC$ is equal to $CE$,  
and $CF$ is common,  
<p class="assertion"><sup>20</sup> the two sides $DC$, $CF$ are equal to the two sides $EC$, $CF$ respectively;</p>
and the base $DF$ is equal to the base $FE$;  
<p class="assertion">therefore the angle $DCF$ is equal to the angle $ECF$; <span class="justification">[I. 8]</span></p>
and they are adjacent angles.

But, when a straight line set up on a straight line makes  
<sup>25</sup> the adjacent angles equal to one another, each of the equal angles is right; <span class="justification">[Def. 10]</span>  
<p class="assertion">therefore each of the angles $DCF$, $FCE$ is right.</p>

Therefore the straight line $CF$ has been drawn at right angles to the given straight line $AB$ from the given point  
<sup>30</sup> $C$ on it.

<p class="qed">Q. E. F.</p>
