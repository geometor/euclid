## Content Analysis
1. **Proposition 11**:
   - **Enunciation**: *To draw a straight line at right angles to a given straight line from a given point on it.*
   - **Setting-out & Specification**: Straight line $AB$, point $C$ on it. Required to draw from $C$ a straight line at right angles to $AB$.
   - **Construction**: Take random point $D$ on $AC$; make $CE = CD$ [I. 3]; construct equilateral triangle $FDE$ on $DE$ [I. 1]; join $FC$.
   - **Proof**: Triangles $DCF$ and $ECF$ are congruent by I. 8 ($DC = CE$, $CF$ common, $DF = FE$), hence adjacent angles $\angle DCF = \angle ECF$. By Definition 10, both angles are right angles; therefore $CF \perp AB$ at $C$.
   - **Conclusion**: Q. E. F. (*quod erat faciendum*).
   - Illustrated by Graphic `0283.g1.png`.

2. **Philological Note on Line 10**:
   - **Lemma**: `10. let CE be made equal to CD.`
   - Greek passive usage: The verb κείσθω (and other forms of κεῖμαι) serves as the passive for τίθημι ("to place"), frequently employed in the mathematical sense of *making* one straight line equal to another.

3. **Commentary by Augustus De Morgan**:
   - De Morgan (*The Connexion of Number and Magnitude*, etc.) observes that Proposition 11 is conceptually equivalent to bisecting the "flat angle" (straight angle, $180^\circ$) formed by a straight line and its continuation, utilizing the identical construction to Proposition 9.
   - Text ends mid-sentence at page boundary: `This is certainly`.
