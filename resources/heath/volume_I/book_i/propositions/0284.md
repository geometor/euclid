Apollonius gave a construction for this proposition (see Proclus, p. 282, 8) differing from Euclid’s in much the same way as his construction for bisecting a straight line differed from that of I. 10. Instead of assuming an equilateral triangle drawn without repeating the process of I. 1, Apollonius takes <span data-ref="0284.g1:D">$D$</span> and <span data-ref="0284.g1:E">$E$</span> equidistant from <span data-ref="0284.g1:C">$C$</span> as in Euclid, and then draws circles in the manner of I. 1 meeting at <span data-ref="0284.g1:F">$F$</span>.

![Apollonius Construction for I. 11](0284.g1.svg)

This necessitates proving again that <span data-ref="0284.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0284.g1:/ F E /">$FE$</span>; whereas Euclid’s assumption of the construction of I. 1 in the words “let the equilateral triangle <span data-ref="0284.g1:< F D E >">$FDE$</span> be constructed” enables him to dispense with the drawing of circles and with the proof that <span data-ref="0284.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0284.g1:/ F E /">$FE$</span> at the same time. While however the substitution of Apollonius’ constructions for I. 10 and 11 would show faulty arrangement in a theoretical treatise like Euclid’s, they are entirely suitable for what we call *practical geometry*, and such may have been Apollonius’ object in these constructions and in his alternative for I. 23.

Proclus gives a construction for drawing a straight line at right angles to another straight line but from *one end* of it, instead of from an intermediate point on it, it being supposed (for the sake of argument) that we are not permitted to *produce* the straight line. In the commentary of an-Nairīzī (ed. Besthorn-Heiberg, pp. 73—4; ed. Curtze, pp. 54—5) this construction is attributed to Heron.

Let it be required to draw from <span data-ref="0284.g2:A">$A$</span> a straight line at right angles to <span data-ref="0284.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span>.  
On <span data-ref="0284.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span> take any point <span data-ref="0284.g2:C">$C$</span>, and in the manner of the proposition draw <span data-ref="0284.g2:/ C E /">$CE$</span> at right angles to <span data-ref="0284.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span>.  
From <span data-ref="0284.g2:/ C E /">$CE$</span> cut off <span data-ref="0284.g2:/ C D /">$CD$</span> equal to <span data-ref="0284.g2:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, bisect the angle <span data-ref="0284.g2:< A C E )">$ACE$</span> by the straight line <span data-ref="0284.g2:/ C F /">$CF$</span>, [I. 9] and draw <span data-ref="0284.g2:/ D F /">$DF$</span> at right angles to <span data-ref="0284.g2:/ C E /">$CE$</span> meeting <span data-ref="0284.g2:/ C F /">$CF$</span> in <span data-ref="0284.g2:F">$F$</span>. Join <span data-ref="0284.g2:/ F A /">$FA$</span>.

![Heron Construction of Perpendicular at Extremity of Straight Line](0284.g2.svg)

Then the angle <span data-ref="0284.g2:< F A C )">$FAC$</span> will be a right angle.

For, since, in the triangles <span data-ref="0284.g2:< A C F >">$ACF$</span>, <span data-ref="0284.g2:< D C F >">$DCF$</span>, the two sides <span data-ref="0284.g2:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0284.g2:/ C F /">$CF$</span> are equal to the two sides <span data-ref="0284.g2:/ D C /">$DC$</span>, <span data-ref="0284.g2:/ C F /">$CF$</span> respectively, and the included angles <span data-ref="0284.g2:< A C F )">$ACF$</span>, <span data-ref="0284.g2:< D C F )">$DCF$</span> are equal,  
the triangles are equal in all respects. [I. 4]

Therefore the angle at <span data-ref="0284.g2:A">$A$</span> is equal to the angle at <span data-ref="0284.g2:D">$D$</span>, and is accordingly a right angle.
