## Content Analysis
1. **Proposition 12 (Continuation and Completion)**:
   - **Specification & Construction** (lines 5–17): Given straight line $AB$, point $C$ not on it. Arbitrary point $D$ taken on opposite side of $AB$; circle $EFG$ drawn with centre $C$, radius $CD$ [Post. 3]; chord $EG$ bisected at $H$ [I. 10]; lines $CG, CH, CE$ joined [Post. 1].
   - **Proof** (lines 18–32): In $\triangle CGH$ and $\triangle CEH$, $GH = HE$, $HC$ common, base $CG = CE$ (radii of circle $EFG$). By I. 8, $\angle CHG = \angle EHC$. As these are adjacent angles, each is a right angle [Def. 10], hence $CH \perp AB$.
   - **Conclusion**: Q. E. F. (*quod erat faciendum*).
   - **Marginal Line Numbers**: Faithfully formatted as superscripts (`<sup>5</sup>`, `<sup>10</sup>`, `<sup>15</sup>`, `<sup>20</sup>`, `<sup>25</sup>`, `<sup>30</sup>`).

2. **Philological Notes**:
   - **Note 2 (Line 2)**: *κάθετον εὐθεῖαν γραμμήν*. Full Greek expression for a perpendicular. κάθετος from καθίημι ("to let fall" or "let down"), corresponding to a plumb-line. Note that ἡ κάθετος is commonly used elliptically without γραμμή.
   - **Note 10 (Line 10)**: ἐπὶ τὰ ἕτερα μέρη τῆς $AB$ ("towards the other parts of the straight line $AB$"). Compared with ἐπὶ τὰ αὐτὰ μέρη (Postulate 5) and ἐφ᾽ ἑκάτερα τὰ μέρη (Definition 23).

3. **Historical Commentary (Proclus on Oenopides)**:
   - Proclus (*In primum Euclidis Elementorum librum commentarii*, ed. Friedlein, p. 283, 7—10) records that this problem was first investigated by Oenopides of Chios (5th century B.C.) for astronomical applications.
   - Oenopides referred to the perpendicular in an archaic manner as "(a line drawn) gnomon-wise" (sentence concludes on Folio 0286).
