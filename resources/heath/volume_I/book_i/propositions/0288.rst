:title: Proposition 12

If the circle intersects :g:`$AB$ <0288.g1:[ A B ]>` as before in :g:`$G$ <0288.g1:G>`, :g:`$E$ <0288.g1:E>`, let :g:`$H$ <0288.g1:H>` be the middle point of :g:`$GE$ <0288.g1:/ G E />`, and suppose, if possible, that the circle also intersects :g:`$AB$ <0288.g1:[ A B ]>` in any other point :g:`$K$ <0288.g1:K>` on :g:`$AH$ <0288.g1:/ A H />`.

| From :g:`$H$ <0288.g1:H>`, on the side of :g:`$AB$ <0288.g1:[ A B ]>` opposite to :g:`$C$ <0288.g1:C>`, draw :g:`$HL$ <0288.g1:/ H L />` at right angles to :g:`$AB$ <0288.g1:[ A B ]>`, and make :g:`$HL$ <0288.g1:/ H L />` equal to :g:`$HC$ <0288.g1:/ H C />`.
| Join :g:`$CG$ <0288.g1:/ C G />`, :g:`$LG$ <0288.g1:/ L G />`, :g:`$CK$ <0288.g1:/ C K />`, :g:`$LK$ <0288.g1:/ L K />`.

.. picture:: 0288.g1.svg
   :alt: Heath Reflection Diagram for I. 12 via I. 7

| Now, in the triangles :g:`$CHG$ <0288.g1:< C H G >>`, :g:`$LHG$ <0288.g1:< L H G >>`,
| :g:`$CH$ <0288.g1:/ C H />` is equal to :g:`$LH$ <0288.g1:/ L H />`, and :g:`$HG$ <0288.g1:/ H G />` is common.
| Also the angles :g:`$CHG$ <0288.g1:< C H G )>`, :g:`$LHG$ <0288.g1:< L H G )>`, being both right, are equal.
| Therefore the base :g:`$CG$ <0288.g1:/ C G />` is equal to the base :g:`$LG$ <0288.g1:/ L G />`.
| Similarly we prove that :g:`$CK$ <0288.g1:/ C K />` is equal to :g:`$LK$ <0288.g1:/ L K />`.
| But, by hypothesis, since :g:`$K$ <0288.g1:K>` is on the circle,
| :g:`$CK$ <0288.g1:/ C K />` is equal to :g:`$CG$ <0288.g1:/ C G />`.
| Therefore :g:`$CG$ <0288.g1:/ C G />`, :g:`$CK$ <0288.g1:/ C K />`, :g:`$LG$ <0288.g1:/ L G />`, :g:`$LK$ <0288.g1:/ L K />` are all equal.

| Now the next proposition, I. 13, will tell us that :g:`$CH$ <0288.g1:/ C H />`, :g:`$HL$ <0288.g1:/ H L />` are in a straight line; but we will not assume this. Join :g:`$CL$ <0288.g1:/ C L />`.
| Then on the same base :g:`$CL$ <0288.g1:/ C L />` and on the same side of it we have two pairs of straight lines drawn from :g:`$C$ <0288.g1:C>`, :g:`$L$ <0288.g1:L>` to :g:`$G$ <0288.g1:G>` and :g:`$K$ <0288.g1:K>` such that :g:`$CG$ <0288.g1:/ C G />` is equal to :g:`$CK$ <0288.g1:/ C K />` and :g:`$LG$ <0288.g1:/ L G />` to :g:`$LK$ <0288.g1:/ L K />`.
| But this is impossible [I. 7].
| Therefore the circle cannot cut :g:`$BA$ <0288.g1:/ B A />` or :g:`$BA$ <0288.g1:[ B A ]>` produced in any point other than :g:`$G$ <0288.g1:G>` on that side of :g:`$CL$ <0288.g1:/ C L />` on which :g:`$G$ <0288.g1:G>` is.
| Similarly it cannot cut :g:`$AB$ <0288.g1:/ A B />` or :g:`$AB$ <0288.g1:[ A B ]>` produced at any point other than :g:`$E$ <0288.g1:E>` on the other side of :g:`$CL$ <0288.g1:/ C L />`.

The only possibility left therefore is that the circle might cut :g:`$AB$ <0288.g1:[ A B ]>` in the same point as that in which :g:`$CL$ <0288.g1:/ C L />` cuts it. But this is shown to be impossible by an adaptation of the proof of I. 7.

| For the assumption is that there may be some point :g:`$M$ <0288.g2:M>` on :g:`$CL$ <0288.g2:/ C L />` such that :g:`$CM$ <0288.g2:/ C M />` is equal to :g:`$CG$ <0288.g2:/ C G />` and :g:`$LM$ <0288.g2:/ L M />` to :g:`$LG$ <0288.g2:/ L G />`.
| If possible, let this be the case, and produce :g:`$CG$ <0288.g2:/ C G />` to :g:`$N$ <0288.g2:N>`.

.. picture:: 0288.g2.svg
   :alt: Heath Diagram for Point M on CL

| Then, since :g:`$CM$ <0288.g2:/ C M />` is equal to :g:`$CG$ <0288.g2:/ C G />`,
| the angle :g:`$NGM$ <0288.g2:< N G M )>` is equal to the angle :g:`$GML$ <0288.g2:< G M L )>` [I. 5, part 2].
| Therefore the angle :g:`$GML$ <0288.g2:< G M L )>` is greater than the angle :g:`$MGL$ <0288.g2:< M G L )>`.
| Again, since :g:`$LG$ <0288.g2:/ L G />` is equal to :g:`$LM$ <0288.g2:/ L M />`,
| the angle :g:`$GML$ <0288.g2:< G M L )>` is equal to the angle :g:`$MGL$ <0288.g2:< M G L )>`.
| But it was also greater: which is impossible.
| Hence the circle in the original figure cannot cut :g:`$AB$ <0288.g1:[ A B ]>` in the point in which :g:`$CL$ <0288.g1:/ C L />` cuts it.
| Therefore the circle cannot cut :g:`$AB$ <0288.g1:[ A B ]>` in any point whatever except :g:`$G$ <0288.g1:G>` and :g:`$E$ <0288.g1:E>`.

[This proof of course does not prove that :g:`$CK$ <0288.g1:/ C K />` is less than :g:`$CG$ <0288.g1:/ C G />`, but only that it is not equal to it. The proposition that, of the obliques drawn from :g:`$C$ <0288.g1:C>` to :g:`$AB$ <0288.g1:[ A B ]>`, that is less the foot of which is nearer to :g:`$H$ <0288.g1:H>` can only be proved later. The proof by I. 7 also fails, under the :person:`Riemann <riemann>` hypothesis, if :g:`$C$ <0288.g1:C>`, :g:`$L$ <0288.g1:L>` are the poles of the straight line :g:`$AB$ <0288.g1:[ A B ]>`, since the broken lines :g:`$CGL$ <0288.g1:/ C G L />`, :g:`$CKL$ <0288.g1:/ C K L />` etc. become equal straight lines, all perpendicular to :g:`$AB$ <0288.g1:[ A B ]>`.]
