### PROPOSITION 13.

<p class="enunciation">If a straight line set up on a straight line make angles, it will make either two right angles or angles equal to two right angles.</p>

![Proposition 13 figure](0289.g1.svg)

For let any straight line $AB$ set up on the straight line  
<sup>5</sup> $CD$ make the angles $CBA$, $ABD$;  
I say that the angles $CBA$, $ABD$ are either two right angles or equal to two right angles.

Now, if the angle $CBA$ is equal to  
<sup>10</sup> the angle $ABD$,  
<p class="assertion">they are two right angles. <span class="justification">[Def. 10]</span></p>

But, if not, let $BE$ be drawn from the point $B$ at right  
angles to $CD$; <span class="justification">[I. 11]</span>  
<p class="assertion">therefore the angles $CBE$, $EBD$ are two right angles.</p>

<sup>15</sup> Then, since the angle $CBE$ is equal to the two angles  
$CBA$, $ABE$,  
<p class="sub-clause">let the angle $EBD$ be added to each;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the angles $CBE$, $EBD$ are equal to the three angles $CBA$, $ABE$, $EBD$. <span class="justification">[C. N. 2]</span></p>

<sup>20</sup> Again, since the angle $DBA$ is equal to the two angles  
$DBE$, $EBA$,  
<p class="sub-clause">let the angle $ABC$ be added to each;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the angles $DBA$, $ABC$ are equal to the three angles $DBE$, $EBA$, $ABC$. <span class="justification">[C. N. 2]</span></p>

<sup>25</sup> But the angles $CBE$, $EBD$ were also proved equal to  
the same three angles;  
and things which are equal to the same thing are also  
equal to one another; <span class="justification">[C. N. 1]</span>  
<p class="assertion">therefore the angles $CBE$, $EBD$ are also equal to the <sup>30</sup> angles $DBA$, $ABC$.</p>
