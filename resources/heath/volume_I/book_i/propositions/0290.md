**17. let the angle <span data-ref="0289.g1:< E B D )">$EBD$</span> be added to each**, literally “let the angle <span data-ref="0289.g1:< E B D )">$EBD$</span> be added (so as to be) common,” κοινὴ προσκείσθω ἡ ὑπὸ ΕΒΔ. Similarly κοινὴ ἀφηρήσθω is used of subtracting a straight line or angle from each of two others. “Let the common angle <span data-ref="0289.g1:< E B D )">$EBD$</span> be added” is clearly an inaccurate translation, for the angle is not common before it is added, i.e. the κοινὴ is proleptic. “Let the common angle be subtracted” as a translation of κοινὴ ἀφηρήσθω would be less unsatisfactory, it is true, but, as it is desirable to use corresponding words when translating the two expressions, it seems hopeless to attempt to keep the word “common,” and I have therefore said “to each” and “from each” simply.

### PROPOSITION 14.

<p class="enunciation">If with any straight line, and at a point on it, two straight lines not lying on the same side make the adjacent angles equal to two right angles, the two straight lines will be in a straight line with one another.</p>

![Proposition 14 figure](0290.g1.svg)

<sup>5</sup> For with any straight line <span data-ref="0290.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, and at the point <span data-ref="0290.g1:B">$B$</span> on it, let the two straight lines <span data-ref="0290.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, <span data-ref="0290.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> not lying on the same side make the adjacent angles <span data-ref="0290.g1:< A B C )">$ABC$</span>, <span data-ref="0290.g1:< A B D )">$ABD$</span> equal to two right angles;  
I say that <span data-ref="0290.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> is in a straight line with <span data-ref="0290.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span>.

<sup>10</sup> For, if <span data-ref="0290.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> is not in a straight line with <span data-ref="0290.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, let <span data-ref="0290.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span> be in a straight line with <span data-ref="0290.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span>.

Then, since the straight line <span data-ref="0290.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> stands on the straight line <span data-ref="0290.g1:/ C B E /">$CBE$</span>,  
<p class="assertion"><sup>15</sup> the angles <span data-ref="0290.g1:< A B C )">$ABC$</span>, <span data-ref="0290.g1:< A B E )">$ABE$</span> are equal to two right angles. <span class="justification">[I. 13]</span></p>

But the angles <span data-ref="0290.g1:< A B C )">$ABC$</span>, <span data-ref="0290.g1:< A B D )">$ABD$</span> are also equal to two right angles;  
<p class="assertion">therefore the angles <span data-ref="0290.g1:< C B A )">$CBA$</span>, <span data-ref="0290.g1:< A B E )">$ABE$</span> are equal to the angles <span data-ref="0290.g1:< C B A )">$CBA$</span>, <span data-ref="0290.g1:< A B D )">$ABD$</span>. <span class="justification">[Post. 4 and C. N. 1]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">Let the angle <span data-ref="0290.g1:< C B A )">$CBA$</span> be subtracted from each;</p>
<sup>20</sup> therefore the remaining angle <span data-ref="0290.g1:< A B E )">$ABE$</span> is equal to the remaining angle <span data-ref="0290.g1:< A B D )">$ABD$</span>, <span class="justification">[C. N. 3]</span>  
<p class="assertion">the less to the greater: which is impossible.</p>

Therefore <span data-ref="0290.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span> is not in a straight line with <span data-ref="0290.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span>.

Similarly we can prove that neither is any other straight  
<sup>25</sup> line except <span data-ref="0290.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>.
