Here the Porism is geometrical, and springs out of a theorem; VII. 2 affords an instance of an arithmetical Porism. As an instance of a Porism to a problem Proclus cites “that which is found in the second Book” (τὸ ἐν τῷ δευτέρῳ βιβλίῳ κείμενον); but as to this see notes on II. 4 and IV. 15.

The present Porism, says Proclus, formed the basis of “that paradoxical theorem which proves that only the following three (regular) polygons can fill up the whole space surrounding one point, the equilateral triangle, the square, and the equilateral and equiangular hexagon.” We can in fact place round a point in this manner six equilateral triangles, three regular hexagons, or four squares. “But only the angles of these regular figures, to the number specified, can make up four right angles: a theorem due to the Pythagoreans.”

Proclus further adds that it results from the Porism that, if any number of straight lines intersect one another at one point, the sum of all the angles so formed will still be equal to four right angles. This is of course what is generally given in the text-books as Corollary 2.

### PROPOSITION 16.

<p class="enunciation">In any triangle, if one of the sides be produced, the exterior angle is greater than either of the interior and opposite angles.</p>

![Proposition 16 figure](0293.g1.svg)

Let <span data-ref="0293.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> be a triangle, and let one side of it <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> be produced to <span data-ref="0293.g1:D">$D$</span>;  
<sup>5</sup> I say that the exterior angle <span data-ref="0293.g1:< A C D )">$ACD$</span> is greater than either of the interior and opposite angles <span data-ref="0293.g1:< C B A )">$CBA$</span>, <span data-ref="0293.g1:< B A C )">$BAC$</span>.

Let <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> be bisected at <span data-ref="0293.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span class="justification">[I. 10]</span>  
and let <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span> be joined and produced  
<sup>10</sup> in a straight line to <span data-ref="0293.g1:F">$F$</span>;  
let <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> be made equal to <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span>, <span class="justification">[I. 3]</span>  
let <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ F C /">$FC$</span> be joined, <span class="justification">[Post. 1]</span>  
and let <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> be drawn through to <span data-ref="0293.g1:G">$G$</span>. <span class="justification">[Post. 2]</span>

Then, since <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ E C /">$EC$</span>,  
<sup>15</sup> and <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span> to <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>,  
<p class="assertion">the two sides <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span>, <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span> are equal to the two sides <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ C E /">$CE$</span>, <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> respectively;</p>
and the angle <span data-ref="0293.g1:< A E B )">$AEB$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0293.g1:< F E C )">$FEC$</span>,  
<p class="sub-clause">for they are vertical angles. <span class="justification">[I. 15]</span></p>

<p class="assertion"><sup>20</sup> Therefore the base <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is equal to the base <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ F C /">$FC$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">and the triangle <span data-ref="0293.g1:< A B E >">$ABE$</span> is equal to the triangle <span data-ref="0293.g1:< C F E >">$CFE$</span>,</p>
and the remaining angles are equal to the remaining angles respectively, namely those which the equal sides subtend; <span class="justification">[I. 4]</span>  
<p class="assertion">therefore the angle <span data-ref="0293.g1:< B A E )">$BAE$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0293.g1:< E C F )">$ECF$</span>.</p>
