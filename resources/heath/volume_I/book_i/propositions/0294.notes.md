## Editorial & Typographical Findings
- **Greek Typography & Apparent Typo in Heath (`ἀπεναντίον`)**:
  - In Note 2, Heath prints `τῶν ἐντὸς καὶ ἀπεναντίον γωνιῶν`.
  - Grammatically, the genitive plural feminine/neuter form of ἀπεναντίος modifying γωνιῶν is ἀπεναντίων (with omega). Heath prints `ἀπεναντίον` (with omicron nu).
  - The Perseus TEI XML also mirrors `ἀπεναντίον`. This reading is faithfully transcribed as printed in Heath's text.
- **Printed Misprint in Heath's Text (`inierior`)**:
  - In the paragraph discussing the combined enunciation from Proclus (p. 307, 1—12), line 3 of the quotation in Heath's print reads:
    `exterior angle of the triangle is greater than either of the inierior and opposite`
  - The word `inierior` was set with an `i` instead of `t` (or broken type). In transcription, this obvious typographical slip has been corrected to `interior`.
- **Greek Accents & Breathings**:
  - Note 1: `ἡ ἐκτὸς γωνία` — rough breathing on `ἡ`; smooth breathing and grave on `ἐκτὸς`; acute on iota of `γωνία`.
  - Note 12: `διήχθω` — acute on eta; `ἐκβεβλήσθω` — smooth breathing on epsilon, acute on eta.
- **Riemannian Parameter Notation ($\Delta$)**:
  - Heath uses the uppercase Greek Delta ($\Delta$, $2\Delta$, $4\Delta$) to denote quadrant distance in elliptic space, transcribed as mathematical LaTeX symbols `$\Delta$`, `$2\Delta$`, `$4\Delta$`.
