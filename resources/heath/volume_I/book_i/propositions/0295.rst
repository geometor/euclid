| Then, since :g:`$AB$ <0295.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$AC$ <0295.g1:/ A C />` are equal, the angles :g:`$ABC$ <0295.g1:< A B C )>`, :g:`$ACB$ <0295.g1:< A C B )>` are equal.
| Similarly, since :g:`$AB$ <0295.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$AD$ <0295.g1:/ A D />` are equal, the angles :g:`$ABD$ <0295.g1:< A B D )>`, :g:`$ADB$ <0295.g1:< A D B )>` are equal.
| Therefore the angle :g:`$ACB$ <0295.g1:< A C B )>` is equal to the angle :g:`$ADC$ <0295.g1:< A D C )>`, i.e. the exterior angle to the interior and opposite angle: which is impossible.

.. picture:: 0295.g1.svg
   :alt: Proclus Proof of Impossibility of Three Equal Lines to Same Straight Line

:person:`Proclus <proclus>` next (p. 308, 14 sqq.) undertakes to prove by means of I. 16 that, *if a straight line falling on two straight lines make the exterior angle equal to the interior and opposite angle, the two straight lines will not form a triangle or meet*, for in that case the same angle would be both greater and equal.

The proof is really equivalent to that of Eucl. I. 27. If :g:`$BE$ <0295.g2:/ B E />` falls on the two straight lines :g:`$AB$ <0295.g2:/ A B />`, :g:`$CD$ <0295.g2:/ C D />` in such a way that the angle :g:`$CDE$ <0295.g2:< C D E )>` is equal to the interior and opposite angle :g:`$ABD$ <0295.g2:< A B D )>`, :g:`$AB$ <0295.g2:/ A B />` and :g:`$CD$ <0295.g2:/ C D />` cannot form a triangle or meet. For, if they did, then (by I. 16) the angle :g:`$CDE$ <0295.g2:< C D E )>` would be greater than the angle :g:`$ABD$ <0295.g2:< A B D )>`, while by the hypothesis it is at the same time equal to it.

.. picture:: 0295.g2.svg
   :alt: Proclus Equivalent of I. 27 Figure

Hence, says :person:`Proclus <proclus>`, in order that :g:`$BA$ <0295.g2:/ B A />`, :g:`$DC$ <0295.g2:/ D C />` may form a triangle it is necessary for them to *approach* one another in the sense of being turned round one pair of corresponding extremities, e.g. :g:`$B$ <0295.g2:B>`, :g:`$D$ <0295.g2:D>`, so that the other extremities :g:`$A$ <0295.g2:A>`, :g:`$C$ <0295.g2:C>` come nearer. This may be brought about in one of three ways: (1) :g:`$AB$ <0295.g2:/ A B />` may remain fixed and :g:`$CD$ <0295.g2:/ C D />` be turned about :g:`$D$ <0295.g2:D>` so that the angle :g:`$CDE$ <0295.g2:< C D E )>` increases; (2) :g:`$CD$ <0295.g2:/ C D />` may remain fixed and :g:`$AB$ <0295.g2:/ A B />` be turned about :g:`$B$ <0295.g2:B>` so that the angle :g:`$ABD$ <0295.g2:< A B D )>` becomes smaller; (3) both :g:`$AB$ <0295.g2:/ A B />` and :g:`$CD$ <0295.g2:/ C D />` may move so as to make the angle :g:`$ABD$ <0295.g2:< A B D )>` smaller and the angle :g:`$CDE$ <0295.g2:< C D E )>` larger at the same time. The reason, then, of the straight lines :g:`$AB$ <0295.g2:/ A B />`, :g:`$CD$ <0295.g2:/ C D />` coming to form a triangle or to meet is (says :person:`Proclus <proclus>`) *the movement of the straight lines*.

Though he does not mention it here, :person:`Proclus <proclus>` does in another passage (p. 371, 2—10, quoted on p. 207 above) hint at the possibility that, while I. 16 may remain universally true, either of the straight lines :g:`$BA$ <0295.g2:/ B A />`, :g:`$DC$ <0295.g2:/ D C />` (or both together) may be turned through any angle not greater than a certain finite angle and yet may not meet (the Bolyai-Lobachewsky hypothesis).

PROPOSITION 17.
---------------

.. enunciation::

   In any triangle two angles taken together in any manner are less than two right angles.

| Let :g:`$ABC$ <0296.g1:< A B C >>` be a triangle;
| I say that two angles of the triangle :g:`$ABC$ <0296.g1:< A B C >>` taken together in any manner are less than two right angles.

For let :g:`$BC$ <0296.g1:/ B C />` be produced to :g:`$D$ <0296.g1:D>`. :just:`[Post. 2] <I.post.2>`

Then, since the angle :g:`$ACD$ <0296.g1:< A C D )>` is an exterior angle of the triangle :g:`$ABC$ <0296.g1:< A B C >>`,

.. assertion::

   it is greater than the interior and opposite angle :g:`$ABC$ <0296.g1:< A B C )>`.
