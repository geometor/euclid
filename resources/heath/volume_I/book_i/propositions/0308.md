That the construction of the proposition gives only *two* points of intersection between the circles, and therefore only two triangles satisfying the condition, one on each side of <span data-ref="0306.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span>, is clear from **I. 7**, which, as before pointed out, takes the place, in Book **I.**, of **III. 10** proving that two circles cannot intersect in more points than two.
