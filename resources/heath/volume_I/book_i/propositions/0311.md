**10.** I have naturally left out the well-known words added by Simson in order to avoid the necessity of considering three cases: “Of the two sides <span data-ref="0310.g2:/ D E /">$DE$</span>, <span data-ref="0310.g2:/ D F /">$DF$</span> let <span data-ref="0310.g2:/ D E /">$DE$</span> be the side which is not greater than the other.” I doubt whether Euclid could have been induced to insert the words himself, even if it had been represented to him that their omission meant leaving two possible cases out of consideration. His habit and that of the great Greek geometers was, not to set out all possible cases, but to give as a rule one case, generally the most difficult, as here, and to leave the others to the reader to work out for himself. We have already seen one instance in I. 7.

Proclus of course gives the other two cases which arise if we do not first provide that <span data-ref="0310.g2:/ D E /">$DE$</span> is not greater than <span data-ref="0310.g2:/ D F /">$DF$</span>.

(1) In the first case <span data-ref="0311.g1:G">$G$</span> may fall on <span data-ref="0311.g1:[ E F ]">$EF$</span> produced, and it is then obvious that <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ E G /">$EG$</span> is greater than <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>.

![Folio 0311 Figure 1 - Proclus Case 1 for Proposition 24 (G on EF produced)](0311.g1.svg)

(2) In the second case <span data-ref="0311.g2:/ E G /">$EG$</span> may fall below <span data-ref="0311.g2:/ E F /">$EF$</span>.

If so, by I. 21, <span data-ref="0311.g2:/ D F /">$DF$</span>, <span data-ref="0311.g2:/ F E /">$FE$</span> are together less than <span data-ref="0311.g2:/ D G /">$DG$</span>, <span data-ref="0311.g2:/ G E /">$GE$</span>.  
But <span data-ref="0311.g2:/ D F /">$DF$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0311.g2:/ D G /">$DG$</span>; therefore <span data-ref="0311.g2:/ E F /">$EF$</span> is less than <span data-ref="0311.g2:/ E G /">$EG$</span>, i.e. than <span data-ref="0311.g2:/ B C /">$BC$</span>.

![Folio 0311 Figure 2 - Proclus Case 2 for Proposition 24 (EG below EF)](0311.g2.svg)

These two cases are therefore decidedly simpler than the case taken by Euclid as typical, and could well be left to the ingenuity of the learner.
