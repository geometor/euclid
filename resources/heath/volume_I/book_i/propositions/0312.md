De Morgan would make the following proposition precede: *Every straight line drawn from the vertex of a triangle to the base is less than the greater of the two sides, or than either if they are equal*, and he would prove it by means of the proposition relating to perpendicular and obliques given above, p. 291.

But it is easy to prove directly that <span data-ref="0312.g1:F">$F$</span> falls below <span data-ref="0312.g1:/ E G /">$EG$</span>, if <span data-ref="0312.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> is not greater than <span data-ref="0312.g1:/ D G /">$DG$</span>, by the method employed by Pfleiderer, Lardner, and Todhunter.

Let <span data-ref="0312.g1:[ D F ]">$DF$</span>, produced if necessary, meet <span data-ref="0312.g1:/ E G /">$EG$</span> in <span data-ref="0312.g1:H">$H$</span>.

**Alternative proof.**

Lastly, the modern alternative proof is worth giving.

![Folio 0312 Figure 2 - Modern Alternative Proof of Proposition 24](0312.g2.svg)

Let <span data-ref="0312.g2:/ D H /">$DH$</span> bisect the angle <span data-ref="0312.g2:< F D G )">$FDG$</span> (after the triangle <span data-ref="0312.g2:< D E G >">$DEG$</span> has been made equal in all respects to the triangle <span data-ref="0312.g2:< A B C >">$ABC$</span>, as in the proposition), and let <span data-ref="0312.g2:/ D H /">$DH$</span> meet <span data-ref="0312.g2:/ E G /">$EG$</span> in <span data-ref="0312.g2:H">$H$</span>. Join <span data-ref="0312.g2:/ H F /">$HF$</span>.

Then, in the triangles <span data-ref="0312.g2:< F D H >">$FDH$</span>, <span data-ref="0312.g2:< G D H >">$GDH$</span>,  
the two sides <span data-ref="0312.g2:/ F D /">$FD$</span>, <span data-ref="0312.g2:/ D H /">$DH$</span> are equal to the two sides <span data-ref="0312.g2:/ G D /">$GD$</span>, <span data-ref="0312.g2:/ D H /">$DH$</span>,  
and the included angles <span data-ref="0312.g2:< F D H )">$FDH$</span>, <span data-ref="0312.g2:< G D H )">$GDH$</span> are equal;

Proclus (p. 339, 11 sqq.) answers by anticipation the possible question that might occur to any one on this proposition, viz. why does Euclid not compare the areas of the triangles as he does in I. 4?
