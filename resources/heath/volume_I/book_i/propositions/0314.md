De Morgan points out that this proposition (as also I. 8) is a purely *logical* consequence of I. 4 and I. 24 in the same way as I. 19 and I. 6 are purely *logical* consequences of I. 18 and I. 5. If <span data-ref="0313.g1:/ B C /">$a$</span>, <span data-ref="0313.g1:/ A C /">$b$</span>, <span data-ref="0313.g1:/ A B /">$c$</span> denote the sides, <span data-ref="0313.g1:< B A C )">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0313.g1:< A B C )">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0313.g1:< A C B )">$C$</span> the angles opposite to them in a triangle <span data-ref="0313.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span>, and <span data-ref="0313.g1:/ E F /">$a'$</span>, <span data-ref="0313.g1:/ D F /">$b'$</span>, <span data-ref="0313.g1:/ D E /">$c'$</span>, <span data-ref="0313.g1:< E D F )">$A'$</span>, <span data-ref="0313.g1:< D E F )">$B'$</span>, <span data-ref="0313.g1:< D F E )">$C'$</span> the sides and opposite angles respectively in a triangle <span data-ref="0313.g1:< D E F >">$A'B'C'$</span>, I. 4 and I. 24 tell us that, <span data-ref="0313.g1:/ A C /">$b$</span>, <span data-ref="0313.g1:/ A B /">$c$</span> being respectively equal to <span data-ref="0313.g1:/ D F /">$b'$</span>, <span data-ref="0313.g1:/ D E /">$c'$</span>,
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\text{(1) if } A \text{ is equal to } A'\text{, then } a \text{ is equal to } a'\text{,} \\
&\text{(2) if } A \text{ is less than } A'\text{, then } a \text{ is less than } a'\text{,} \\
&\text{(3) if } A \text{ is greater than } A'\text{, then } a \text{ is greater than } a'\text{;}
\end{aligned}
$$
and it follows logically that,
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\text{(1) if } a \text{ is equal to } a'\text{, the angle } A \text{ is equal to the angle } A'\text{,} & [\textbf{I. 8}] \\
&\left.
\begin{aligned}
&\text{(2) if } a \text{ is less than } a'\text{, } A \text{ is less than } A'\text{,} \\
&\text{(3) if } a \text{ is greater than } a'\text{, } A \text{ is greater than } A'\text{.}
\end{aligned}
\right\} & [\textbf{I. 25}]
\end{aligned}
$$

Two alternative proofs of this theorem are given by Proclus (pp. 345–7), and they are both interesting. Moreover both are *direct*.

**I. Proof by Menelaus of Alexandria.**

Let <span data-ref="0314.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span>, <span data-ref="0314.g1:< D E F >">$DEF$</span> be two triangles having the two sides <span data-ref="0314.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span>, <span data-ref="0314.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> equal to the two sides <span data-ref="0314.g1:/ E D /">$ED$</span>, <span data-ref="0314.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span>, but the base <span data-ref="0314.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> greater than the base <span data-ref="0314.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>.

![Folio 0314 Figure 1 - Menelaus Direct Proof of Proposition 25](0314.g1.svg)

Then shall the angle at <span data-ref="0314.g1:< B A C )">$A$</span> be greater than the angle at <span data-ref="0314.g1:< E D F )">$D$</span>.

From <span data-ref="0314.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> cut off <span data-ref="0314.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span> equal to <span data-ref="0314.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>. At <span data-ref="0314.g1:B">$B$</span>, on the straight line <span data-ref="0314.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, make the angle <span data-ref="0314.g1:< G B H )">$GBH$</span> (on the side of <span data-ref="0314.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span> remote from <span data-ref="0314.g1:A">$A$</span>) equal to the angle <span data-ref="0314.g1:< F E D )">$FED$</span>.  
Make <span data-ref="0314.g1:/ B H /">$BH$</span> equal to <span data-ref="0314.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>; join <span data-ref="0314.g1:/ H G /">$HG$</span>, and produce it to meet <span data-ref="0314.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> in <span data-ref="0314.g1:K">$K$</span>.  
Join <span data-ref="0314.g1:/ A H /">$AH$</span>.

Then, since the two sides <span data-ref="0314.g1:/ G B /">$GB$</span>, <span data-ref="0314.g1:/ B H /">$BH$</span> are equal to the two sides <span data-ref="0314.g1:/ F E /">$FE$</span>, <span data-ref="0314.g1:/ E D /">$ED$</span> respectively,  
and the angles contained by them are equal,  
<span data-ref="0314.g1:/ H G /">$HG$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0314.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span> or <span data-ref="0314.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>,  
and the angle <span data-ref="0314.g1:< B H G )">$BHG$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0314.g1:< E D F )">$EDF$</span>.  
Now <span data-ref="0314.g1:/ H K /">$HK$</span> is greater than <span data-ref="0314.g1:/ H G /">$HG$</span> or <span data-ref="0314.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>,  
and *a fortiori* greater than <span data-ref="0314.g1:/ A K /">$AK$</span>;  
therefore the angle <span data-ref="0314.g1:< K A H )">$KAH$</span> is greater than the angle <span data-ref="0314.g1:< K H A )">$KHA$</span>.  
And, since <span data-ref="0314.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0314.g1:/ B H /">$BH$</span>,  
the angle <span data-ref="0314.g1:< B A H )">$BAH$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0314.g1:< B H A )">$BHA$</span>.  
Therefore, by addition,  
the whole angle <span data-ref="0314.g1:< B A C )">$BAC$</span> is greater than the whole angle <span data-ref="0314.g1:< B H G )">$BHG$</span>,  
that is, greater than the angle <span data-ref="0314.g1:< E D F )">$EDF$</span>.
