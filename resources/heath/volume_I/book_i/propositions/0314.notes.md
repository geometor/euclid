    - Heading: `**I. Proof by Menelaus of Alexandria.**`
    - Setting-out, Specification, and Construction:
      - Triangle $ABC$ with base $BC > EF$.
      - Cut off $BG = EF$ along $BC$; at $B$ construct $\angle GBH = \angle FED$ on the opposite side of $BC$ from $A$; make $BH = DE$.
      - Join $HG$ and produce to meet $AC$ at $K$; join $AH$.
    - Demonstration diagram: `0314.g1.png`.
    - Demonstration:
      - $\triangle GBH \cong \triangle FED$ (SAS via **I. 4**), whence $HG = DF = AC$ and $\angle BHG = \angle EDF$.
      - Since $G$ lies on $HK$ and $K$ lies on $AC$, $HK > HG = AC > AK$; therefore in $\triangle AHK$, $\angle KAH > \angle KHA$ (**I. 18**).
      - Since $AB = DE = BH$, $\triangle ABH$ is isosceles, so $\angle BAH = \angle BHA$ (**I. 5**).
      - Addition of inequalities yields $\angle BAC = \angle KAH + \angle BAH > \angle KHA + \angle BHA = \angle BHG = \angle EDF$.
