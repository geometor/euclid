**II. Heron's proof.**

Let the triangles be given as before.  
Since <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is greater than <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, produce <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> to <span data-ref="0315.g1:G">$G$</span> so that <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ E G /">$EG$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>.  
Produce <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ E D /">$ED$</span> to <span data-ref="0315.g1:H">$H$</span> so that <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ D H /">$DH$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span>. The circle with centre <span data-ref="0315.g1:D">$D$</span> and radius <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span> will then pass through <span data-ref="0315.g1:H">$H$</span>. Let it be described, as <span data-ref="0315.g1:( F K H )">$FKH$</span>.

![Folio 0315 Figure 1 - Heron Direct Proof of Proposition 25](0315.g1.svg)

Now, since <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span>, <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> are together greater than <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>,  
and <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span>, <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> are equal to <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ E D /">$ED$</span>, <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ D H /">$DH$</span> respectively,  
while <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ E G /">$EG$</span>,  
<span data-ref="0315.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span> is greater than <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ E G /">$EG$</span>.  
Therefore the circle with centre <span data-ref="0315.g1:E">$E$</span> and radius <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ E G /">$EG$</span> will cut <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span>, and therefore will cut the circle already drawn. Let it cut that circle in <span data-ref="0315.g1:K">$K$</span>, and join <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ D K /">$DK$</span>, <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ K E /">$KE$</span>.  
Then, since <span data-ref="0315.g1:D">$D$</span> is the centre of the circle <span data-ref="0315.g1:( F K H )">$FKH$</span>,  
<span data-ref="0315.g1:/ D K /">$DK$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span> or <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>.  
Similarly, since <span data-ref="0315.g1:E">$E$</span> is the centre of the circle <span data-ref="0315.g1:( E G )">$KG$</span>,  
<span data-ref="0315.g1:/ E K /">$EK$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ E G /">$EG$</span> or <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>,  
And <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>.  
Therefore the two sides <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span>, <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> are equal to the two sides <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ E D /">$ED$</span>, <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ D K /">$DK$</span> respectively;  
and the base <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is equal to the base <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ E K /">$EK$</span>;  
therefore the angle <span data-ref="0315.g1:< B A C )">$BAC$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0315.g1:< E D K )">$EDK$</span>.  
Therefore the angle <span data-ref="0315.g1:< B A C )">$BAC$</span> is greater than the angle <span data-ref="0315.g1:< E D F )">$EDF$</span>.

### PROPOSITION 26.

<p class="enunciation">
If two triangles have the two angles equal to two angles respectively, and one side equal to one side, namely, either the side adjoining the equal angles, or that subtending one of the equal angles, they will also have the remaining sides equal to <sup>5</sup>the remaining sides and the remaining angle to the remaining angle.
</p>
