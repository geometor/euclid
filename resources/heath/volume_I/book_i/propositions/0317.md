Again, let sides subtending equal angles be equal, as <span data-ref="0316.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> to <span data-ref="0316.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>;  
I say again that the remaining sides will be equal to the remaining sides, namely <span data-ref="0316.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> to <span data-ref="0316.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span> and <span data-ref="0316.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> to <span data-ref="0316.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, and <sup>45</sup>further the remaining angle <span data-ref="0316.g1:< B A C )">$BAC$</span> is equal to the remaining angle <span data-ref="0316.g1:< E D F )">$EDF$</span>.

For, if <span data-ref="0316.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is unequal to <span data-ref="0316.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, one of them is greater.  
Let <span data-ref="0316.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> be greater, if possible, and let <span data-ref="0316.g1:/ B H /">$BH$</span> be made equal to <span data-ref="0316.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>; let <span data-ref="0316.g1:/ A H /">$AH$</span> be joined.  
<sup>50</sup>Then, since <span data-ref="0316.g1:/ B H /">$BH$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0316.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, and <span data-ref="0316.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> to <span data-ref="0316.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>,  
the two sides <span data-ref="0316.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0316.g1:/ B H /">$BH$</span> are equal to the two sides <span data-ref="0316.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>, <span data-ref="0316.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> respectively, and they contain equal angles;  
<p class="sub-clause">therefore the base <span data-ref="0316.g1:/ A H /">$AH$</span> is equal to the base <span data-ref="0316.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span>,</p>
<p class="sub-clause">and the triangle <span data-ref="0316.g1:< A B H >">$ABH$</span> is equal to the triangle <span data-ref="0316.g1:< D E F >">$DEF$</span>,</p>
<p class="sub-clause"><sup>55</sup>and the remaining angles will be equal to the remaining angles, namely those which the equal sides subtend; <span class="justification">[I. 4]</span></p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the angle <span data-ref="0316.g1:< B H A )">$BHA$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0316.g1:< E F D )">$EFD$</span>.</p>

But the angle <span data-ref="0316.g1:< E F D )">$EFD$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0316.g1:< B C A )">$BCA$</span>;  
<p class="assertion">therefore, in the triangle <span data-ref="0316.g1:< A H C >">$AHC$</span>, the exterior angle <span data-ref="0316.g1:< B H A )">$BHA$</span> is <sup>60</sup>equal to the interior and opposite angle <span data-ref="0316.g1:< B C A )">$BCA$</span>:</p>
<p class="assertion">which is impossible. <span class="justification">[I. 16]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore <span data-ref="0316.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is not unequal to <span data-ref="0316.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">and is therefore equal to it.</p>

But <span data-ref="0316.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is also equal to <span data-ref="0316.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>;  
<sup>65</sup>therefore the two sides <span data-ref="0316.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0316.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> are equal to the two sides <span data-ref="0316.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>, <span data-ref="0316.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> respectively, and they contain equal angles;  
<p class="sub-clause">therefore the base <span data-ref="0316.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is equal to the base <span data-ref="0316.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span>,</p>
<p class="sub-clause">the triangle <span data-ref="0316.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> equal to the triangle <span data-ref="0316.g1:< D E F >">$DEF$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">and the remaining angle <span data-ref="0316.g1:< B A C )">$BAC$</span> equal to the remaining angle <sup>70</sup><span data-ref="0316.g1:< E D F )">$EDF$</span>. <span class="justification">[I. 4]</span></p>

<p class="qed">Therefore etc. <span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

**2—3. the side adjoining the equal angles,** πλευρὰν τὴν πρὸς ταῖς ἴσαις γωνίαις.

**29. is by hypothesis equal.** ὑπόκειται ἴση, according to the elegant Greek idiom. ὑπόκειμαι is used for the passive of ὑποτίθημι, as κεῖμαι is used for the passive of τίθημι, and so with the other compounds. Cf. προσκεῖσθαι, “to be added.”

The alternative method of proving this proposition, viz. by applying one triangle to the other, was very early discovered, at least so far as regards the case where the equal sides are adjacent to the equal angles in each. An-Nairīzī gives it for this case, observing that the proof is one which he had found, but of which he did not know the author.
