Again, let sides subtending equal angles be equal, as $AB$ to $DE$;  
I say again that the remaining sides will be equal to the remaining sides, namely $AC$ to $DF$ and $BC$ to $EF$, and <sup>45</sup>further the remaining angle $BAC$ is equal to the remaining angle $EDF$.

For, if $BC$ is unequal to $EF$, one of them is greater.  
Let $BC$ be greater, if possible, and let $BH$ be made equal to $EF$; let $AH$ be joined.  
<sup>50</sup>Then, since $BH$ is equal to $EF$, and $AB$ to $DE$,  
the two sides $AB$, $BH$ are equal to the two sides $DE$, $EF$ respectively, and they contain equal angles;  
<p class="sub-clause">therefore the base $AH$ is equal to the base $DF$,</p>
<p class="sub-clause">and the triangle $ABH$ is equal to the triangle $DEF$,</p>
<p class="sub-clause"><sup>55</sup>and the remaining angles will be equal to the remaining angles, namely those which the equal sides subtend; <span class="justification">[I. 4]</span></p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the angle $BHA$ is equal to the angle $EFD$.</p>

But the angle $EFD$ is equal to the angle $BCA$;  
<p class="assertion">therefore, in the triangle $AHC$, the exterior angle $BHA$ is <sup>60</sup>equal to the interior and opposite angle $BCA$:</p>
<p class="assertion">which is impossible. <span class="justification">[I. 16]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore $BC$ is not unequal to $EF$,</p>
<p class="assertion">and is therefore equal to it.</p>

But $AB$ is also equal to $DE$;  
<sup>65</sup>therefore the two sides $AB$, $BC$ are equal to the two sides $DE$, $EF$ respectively, and they contain equal angles;  
<p class="sub-clause">therefore the base $AC$ is equal to the base $DF$,</p>
<p class="sub-clause">the triangle $ABC$ equal to the triangle $DEF$,</p>
<p class="assertion">and the remaining angle $BAC$ equal to the remaining angle <sup>70</sup>$EDF$. <span class="justification">[I. 4]</span></p>

<p class="qed">Therefore etc. <span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

**2—3. the side adjoining the equal angles,** πλευρὰν τὴν πρὸς ταῖς ἴσαις γωνίαις.

**29. is by hypothesis equal.** ὑπόκειται ἴση, according to the elegant Greek idiom. ὑπόκειμαι is used for the passive of ὑποτίθημι, as κεῖμαι is used for the passive of τίθημι, and so with the other compounds. Cf. προσκεῖσθαι, “to be added.”

The alternative method of proving this proposition, viz. by applying one triangle to the other, was very early discovered, at least so far as regards the case where the equal sides are adjacent to the equal angles in each. An-Nairīzī gives it for this case, observing that the proof is one which he had found, but of which he did not know the author.
