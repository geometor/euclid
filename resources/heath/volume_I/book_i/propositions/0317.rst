:title: Proposition 26

Again, let sides subtending :eq:`equal <0316.g1:< A B C ) = 0316.g1:< D E F ); 0316.g1:< B C A ) = 0316.g1:< E F D )>` angles be :eq:`equal <0316.g1:/ A B / = 0316.g1:/ D E />`, as :g:`$AB$ <0316.g1:/ A B />` to :g:`$DE$ <0316.g1:/ D E />`;

I say again that the remaining sides will be :eq:`equal <0316.g1:/ A C / = 0316.g1:/ D F /; 0316.g1:/ B C / = 0316.g1:/ E F />` to the remaining sides, namely :g:`$AC$ <0316.g1:/ A C />` to :g:`$DF$ <0316.g1:/ D F />` and :g:`$BC$ <0316.g1:/ B C />` to :g:`$EF$ <0316.g1:/ E F />`, and further the remaining angle :g:`$BAC$ <0316.g1:< B A C )>` is :eq:`equal <0316.g1:< B A C ) = 0316.g1:< E D F )>` to the remaining angle :g:`$EDF$ <0316.g1:< E D F )>`.

For, if :g:`$BC$ <0316.g1:/ B C />` is unequal to :g:`$EF$ <0316.g1:/ E F />`, one of them is greater.

Let :g:`$BC$ <0316.g1:/ B C />` be greater, if possible, and let :g:`$BH$ <0316.g1:/ B H />` be made :eq:`equal <0316.g1:/ B H / = 0316.g1:/ E F />` to :g:`$EF$ <0316.g1:/ E F />`; let :g:`$AH$ <0316.g1:/ A H />` be joined. Then, since :g:`$BH$ <0316.g1:/ B H />` is :eq:`equal <0316.g1:/ B H / = 0316.g1:/ E F /; 0316.g1:/ A B / = 0316.g1:/ D E />` to :g:`$EF$ <0316.g1:/ E F />`, and :g:`$AB$ <0316.g1:/ A B />` to :g:`$DE$ <0316.g1:/ D E />`, the two sides :g:`$AB$ <0316.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$BH$ <0316.g1:/ B H />` are :eq:`equal <0316.g1:/ A B / = 0316.g1:/ D E /; 0316.g1:/ B H / = 0316.g1:/ E F />` to the two sides :g:`$DE$ <0316.g1:/ D E />`, :g:`$EF$ <0316.g1:/ E F />` respectively, and they contain :eq:`equal <0316.g1:< A B C ) = 0316.g1:< D E F )>` angles;

.. subclause::

   therefore the base :g:`$AH$ <0316.g1:/ A H />` is :eq:`equal <0316.g1:/ A H / = 0316.g1:/ D F />` to the base :g:`$DF$ <0316.g1:/ D F />`,

.. subclause::

   and the triangle :g:`$ABH$ <0316.g1:< A B H >>` is :eq:`equal <0316.g1:< A B H > = 0316.g1:< D E F >>` to the triangle :g:`$DEF$ <0316.g1:< D E F >>`,

.. subclause:: [I. 4] <I.4>

   and the remaining angles will be equal to the remaining angles, namely those which the equal sides subtend;

.. assertion::

   therefore the angle :g:`$BHA$ <0316.g1:< B H A )>` is :eq:`equal <0316.g1:< B H A ) = 0316.g1:< E F D )>` to the angle :g:`$EFD$ <0316.g1:< E F D )>`.

But the angle :g:`$EFD$ <0316.g1:< E F D )>` is :eq:`equal <0316.g1:< E F D ) = 0316.g1:< B C A )>` to the angle :g:`$BCA$ <0316.g1:< B C A )>`;

.. assertion::

   therefore, in the triangle :g:`$AHC$ <0316.g1:< A H C >>`, the exterior angle :g:`$BHA$ <0316.g1:< B H A )>` is :eq:`equal <0316.g1:< B H A ) = 0316.g1:< B C A )>` to the interior and opposite angle :g:`$BCA$ <0316.g1:< B C A )>`:

.. assertion:: [I. 16] <I.16>

   which is impossible.

.. assertion::

   Therefore :g:`$BC$ <0316.g1:/ B C />` is not unequal to :g:`$EF$ <0316.g1:/ E F />`,

.. assertion::

   and is therefore :eq:`equal <0316.g1:/ B C / = 0316.g1:/ E F />` to it.

But :g:`$AB$ <0316.g1:/ A B />` is also :eq:`equal <0316.g1:/ A B / = 0316.g1:/ D E />` to :g:`$DE$ <0316.g1:/ D E />`; therefore the two sides :g:`$AB$ <0316.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$BC$ <0316.g1:/ B C />` are :eq:`equal <0316.g1:/ A B / = 0316.g1:/ D E /; 0316.g1:/ B C / = 0316.g1:/ E F />` to the two sides :g:`$DE$ <0316.g1:/ D E />`, :g:`$EF$ <0316.g1:/ E F />` respectively, and they contain :eq:`equal <0316.g1:< A B C ) = 0316.g1:< D E F )>` angles;

.. subclause::

   therefore the base :g:`$AC$ <0316.g1:/ A C />` is :eq:`equal <0316.g1:/ A C / = 0316.g1:/ D F />` to the base :g:`$DF$ <0316.g1:/ D F />`,

.. subclause::

   the triangle :g:`$ABC$ <0316.g1:< A B C >>` :eq:`equal <0316.g1:< A B C > = 0316.g1:< D E F >>` to the triangle :g:`$DEF$ <0316.g1:< D E F >>`,

.. assertion:: [I. 4] <I.4>

   and the remaining angle :g:`$BAC$ <0316.g1:< B A C )>` :eq:`equal <0316.g1:< B A C ) = 0316.g1:< E D F )>` to the remaining angle :g:`$EDF$ <0316.g1:< E D F )>`.

.. qed::

   Therefore etc. :just:`Q. E. D.`

.. [#f1] **the side adjoining the equal angles,** πλευρὰν τὴν πρὸς ταῖς ἴσαις γωνίαις.

.. [#f2] **is by hypothesis equal.** ὑπόκειται ἴση, according to the elegant Greek idiom. ὑπόκειμαι is used for the passive of ὑποτίθημι, as κεῖμαι is used for the passive of τίθημι, and so with the other compounds. Cf. προσκεῖσθαι, “to be added.”

The alternative method of proving this proposition, viz. by applying one triangle to the other, was very early discovered, at least so far as regards the case where the equal sides are adjacent to the equal angles in each. An-Nairīzī gives it for this case, observing that the proof is one which he had found, but of which he did not know the author.
