# Transcription Notes: Folio 0318 (Page 304)

## 1. Overview
- **Book / Section**: Book I, Propositions.
- **Folio**: `0318` (Page 304).
- **Running Header**: `304                            BOOK I                            [I. 26`.
- **Contents**:
  - **Commentary on Proposition 26 (Thales and Distance of Ships at Sea)**:
    - **Proclus citation of Eudemus** (p. 352, 13–18): Eudemus in his *History of Geometry* attributes I. 26 to Thales, noting that Thales' method of proving the distance of ships at sea required this theorem.
    - **Bretschneider's hypothesis** (*Die Geometrie und die Geometer vor Eukleides*, § 30): Observation of angle $\angle CAB$ from the top of a tower $AB$ of known height, determining the right-angled triangle $ABC$ with base $BC$ (distance to ship $C$) "in a moment".
    - **Cantor's hypothesis** (*Gesch. d. Math.* I₃, p. 145): Links the problem to the Egyptian *Seqt* in the Rhind Papyrus (ratio of half-base to height, i.e., cotangent of slope), using a small similar triangle $ADE$ with common angle $A$.
    - **Max C. P. Schmidt's instrument hypothesis** (*Kulturhistorische Beiträge zur Kenntnis des griechischen und römischen Altertums*, 1906, p. 32): A graduated wooden or bronze right angle positioned at $ADE$ (with $A$ at eye level), reading lengths $AD, DE$, and calculating $BC$ via the rule of three.
    - **Heath's critique**: Highlights the fundamental tension between these similar-triangle hypotheses and Eudemus' explicit statement that Thales used I. 26 (equality of *two* congruent triangles, rather than proportionality in similar triangles).
    - **Continuity**: Folio concludes mid-sentence at `But, if Thales`, which completes on Folio 0319 (page 305) with `practically used proportions...`.
