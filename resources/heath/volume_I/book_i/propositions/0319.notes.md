    - Heath's alternative reconstruction of Thales' method: using an instrument consisting of a vertical stick (plumb-line) with a movable cross-piece on top of a tower, sighting the ship, rotating the instrument to sight a reference mark on shore, and measuring the distance from the base of the tower to that point.
    - Analogy with Thales' pyramid measurement method recorded by Diogenes Laertius (I. 27, p. 6, ed. Cobet) on the authority of Hieronymus of Rhodes (B.C. 290—230): waiting until *our shadows are of the same length as ourselves*.

    - Proclus's systematic analysis (p. 347, 20 sqq.) of the conditions for triangle equality:
      - (1) equality of sides alone: only three sides equal suffices (Euclid I. 8);
      - (2) equality of angles alone: no hypothesis of this class establishes equality;
      - (3) equality of sides and angles combined (continues onto page 306).
