If it is desired to avoid the ambiguity and secure that the triangles may be congruent, we can introduce the necessary conditions into the enunciation, on the analogy of Eucl. **VI.** 7.

*If two triangles have two sides of the one equal to two sides of the other respectively, and the angles opposite to a pair of equal sides equal, then, if the angles opposite to the other pair of equal sides are both acute, or both obtuse, or if one of them is a right angle, the two triangles are equal in all respects.*

The proof of the three cases (by *reductio ad absurdum*) was given by Todhunter.

### PROPOSITION 27.

<p class="enunciation">
If a straight line falling on two straight lines make the alternate angles equal to one another, the straight lines will be parallel to one another.
</p>

![Folio 0321 Figure 1 - Proposition 27](0321.g1.svg)

For let the straight line <span data-ref="0321.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> falling on the two straight  
<sup>5</sup>lines <span data-ref="0321.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0321.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> make the alternate angles <span data-ref="0321.g1:< A E F )">$AEF$</span>, <span data-ref="0321.g1:< E F D )">$EFD$</span> equal to one another;  
I say that <span data-ref="0321.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is parallel to <span data-ref="0321.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>.

For, if not, <span data-ref="0321.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0321.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> when produced will meet either in the direction  
<sup>10</sup>of <span data-ref="0321.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0321.g1:D">$D$</span> or towards <span data-ref="0321.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0321.g1:C">$C$</span>.  
Let them be produced and meet, in the direction of <span data-ref="0321.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0321.g1:D">$D$</span>, at <span data-ref="0321.g1:G">$G$</span>.

Then, in the triangle <span data-ref="0321.g1:< G E F >">$GEF$</span>, the exterior angle <span data-ref="0321.g1:< A E F )">$AEF$</span> is equal to the interior and opposite  
<sup>15</sup>angle <span data-ref="0321.g1:< E F G )">$EFG$</span>:  
<p class="assertion">which is impossible. <span class="justification">[I. 16]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore <span data-ref="0321.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0321.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> when produced will not meet in the direction of <span data-ref="0321.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0321.g1:D">$D$</span>.</p>

<p class="assertion">Similarly it can be proved that neither will they meet  
<sup>20</sup>towards <span data-ref="0321.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0321.g1:C">$C$</span>.</p>
