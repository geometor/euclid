- **Contents**:
  - **Aristotle on Parallels (Conclusion of commentary on I. 27)**:
    - Continuation of passage (1) from page 308: explanation of universal demonstration (*Anal. Post.* I. 5) applied to angles equal to two right angles in any way whatever vs. each being a right angle.
    - Passage (2) from *Anal. Prior.* II. 17, 66 a 11—15: false conclusion that parallels meet drawn from assumptions *(a)* interior angle greater than exterior or *(b)* sum of angles of triangle greater than two right angles.
    - Heath's critique of J. L. Heiberg's interpretation (*Mathematisches zu Aristoteles*, pp. 18—19). Heiberg argued passage (1) refers to Eucl. I. 28 because of `ὅτι ὡδὶ ἴσαι` and interpreted `αἱ ὀρθαὶ οὐ συμπίπτουσι` as perpendicular lines. Heath agrees passage (1) refers to I. 28, but argues alternative *(a)* in passage (2) does not suggest it, showing that *(a)* and *(b)* refer to I. 27 (*reductio ad absurdum* via I. 16).
    - Conclusion that I. 27 and I. 28 are pre-Euclidean and that Euclid's innovation was the introduction of Postulate 5 in I. 29.
  - **De Morgan Formal Logic Analysis**:
    - Augustus De Morgan's observation that I. 27 is a logical equivalent of I. 16:
      - Let $A$ = “straight lines forming a triangle with a transversal”
      - Let $B$ = “straight lines making angles with a transversal on the same side which are together less than two right angles”
      - Logical deduction: All $A$ is $B \implies$ All not-$B$ is not-$A$.
  - **Proposition 28 (Opening)**:
    - Heading: `### PROPOSITION 28.`
    - Enunciation: *“If a straight line falling on two straight lines make the exterior angle equal to the interior and opposite angle on the same side, or the interior angles on the same side equal to two right angles, the straight lines will be parallel to one another.”*
    - The demonstration and commentary follow on folio 0324 (page 310).
