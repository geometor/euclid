    - Demonstration (ἀπόδειξις) in two cases:
      - **Case 1**: Exterior angle equals interior opposite: $\angle EGB = \angle GHD$. But $\angle EGB = \angle AGH$ [I. 15, vertically opposite], hence $\angle AGH = \angle GHD$. As they are alternate, $AB \parallel CD$ [I. 27].
      - **Case 2**: Interior angles on the same side sum to two right angles: $\angle BGH + \angle GHD = 2\text{R}$. Since $\angle AGH + \angle BGH = 2\text{R}$ [I. 13, adjacent angles on a straight line], $\angle AGH + \angle BGH = \angle BGH + \angle GHD$. Subtracting common $\angle BGH$ yields remaining $\angle AGH = \text{remaining } \angle GHD$ (alternate), hence $AB \parallel CD$ [I. 27].
    - Conclusion: *"Therefore etc."* and *"Q. E. D."*
  - **Commentary on Proposition 28**:

    - Proclus’ commentary (pp. 358—9): Euclid could have stated six criteria of parallelism rather than three, by considering all non-adjacent angle pairs formed by the transversal.
    - Classification: pairs on the *same* side of the transversal vs. pairs on *different* sides.
