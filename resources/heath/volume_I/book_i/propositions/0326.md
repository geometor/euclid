<sup>20</sup> But the angles <span data-ref="0325.g1:< A G H )">$AGH$</span>, <span data-ref="0325.g1:< B G H )">$BGH$</span> are equal to two right angles; <span class="justification">[I. 13]</span>  
<p class="assertion">therefore the angles <span data-ref="0325.g1:< B G H )">$BGH$</span>, <span data-ref="0325.g1:< G H D )">$GHD$</span> are less than two right angles.</p>
But straight lines produced indefinitely from angles less than two right angles meet; <span class="justification">[Post. 5]</span>  
<p class="assertion"><sup>25</sup> therefore <span data-ref="0325.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0325.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, if produced indefinitely, will meet;</p>
<p class="assertion">but they do not meet, because they are by hypothesis parallel.</p>
<p class="assertion">Therefore the angle <span data-ref="0325.g1:< A G H )">$AGH$</span> is not unequal to the angle <span data-ref="0325.g1:< G H D )">$GHD$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">and is therefore equal to it.</p>
<sup>30</sup> Again, the angle <span data-ref="0325.g1:< A G H )">$AGH$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0325.g1:< E G B )">$EGB$</span>; <span class="justification">[I. 15]</span>  
<p class="assertion">therefore the angle <span data-ref="0325.g1:< E G B )">$EGB$</span> is also equal to the angle <span data-ref="0325.g1:< G H D )">$GHD$</span>. <span class="justification">[C. N. 1]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">therefore the angles <span data-ref="0325.g1:< E G B )">$EGB$</span>, <span data-ref="0325.g1:< B G H )">$BGH$</span> are equal to the <sup>35</sup>angles <span data-ref="0325.g1:< B G H )">$BGH$</span>, <span data-ref="0325.g1:< G H D )">$GHD$</span>. <span class="justification">[C. N. 2]</span></p>
But the angles <span data-ref="0325.g1:< E G B )">$EGB$</span>, <span data-ref="0325.g1:< B G H )">$BGH$</span> are equal to two right angles; <span class="justification">[I. 13]</span>

**23. straight lines produced indefinitely from angles less than two right angles,** αἱ δὲ ἀπ᾽ ἐλασσόνων ἢ δύο ὀρθῶν ἐκβαλλόμεναι εἰς ἄπειρον συμπίπτουσιν, a variation from the more explicit language of Postulate 5. A good deal is left to be understood, namely that the straight lines begin from points at which they meet a transversal, and make with it internal angles on the same side the sum of which is less than two right angles.

**26. because they are by hypothesis parallel,** literally “because they are supposed parallel,” διὰ τὸ παραλλήλους αὐτὰς ὑποκεῖσθαι.

### Proof by "Playfair's" axiom.

If, instead of Postulate 5, it is preferred to use “Playfair's” axiom in the proof of this proposition, we proceed thus.

To prove that the alternate angles <span data-ref="0326.g1:< A G H )">$AGH$</span>, <span data-ref="0326.g1:< G H D )">$GHD$</span> are equal.

If they are not equal, draw another straight line <span data-ref="0326.g1:/ K L /">$KL$</span> through <span data-ref="0326.g1:G">$G$</span> making the angle <span data-ref="0326.g1:< K G H )">$KGH$</span> equal to the angle <span data-ref="0326.g1:< G H D )">$GHD$</span>.

![Folio 0326 Figure 1 - Proof of Proposition 29 by Playfair's Axiom](0326.g1.svg)

Then, since the angles <span data-ref="0326.g1:< K G H )">$KGH$</span>, <span data-ref="0326.g1:< G H D )">$GHD$</span> are equal,  
&emsp;&emsp;<span data-ref="0326.g1:/ K L /">$KL$</span> is parallel to <span data-ref="0326.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>. [I. 27]

Therefore *two straight lines <span data-ref="0326.g1:/ K L /">$KL$</span>, <span data-ref="0326.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> intersecting at <span data-ref="0326.g1:G">$G$</span> are both parallel to the straight line <span data-ref="0326.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>:*  
*which is impossible* (by the axiom).

Therefore the angle <span data-ref="0326.g1:< A G H )">$AGH$</span> cannot but be equal to the angle <span data-ref="0326.g1:< G H D )">$GHD$</span>.

The rest of the proposition follows as in Euclid.
