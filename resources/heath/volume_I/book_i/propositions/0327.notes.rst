Summary

- **Content**:

  - Subsection ``### Proof of Euclid's Postulate 5 from "Playfair's" axiom.``
  - Direct deduction of Euclid's Postulate 5 from Playfair's axiom in two parts:

    1. Proving that straight lines $AB$, $CD$ must meet in one direction or the other (using I. 27 and Playfair's axiom to avoid two intersecting parallels through $E$).
    2. Proving that they must meet towards $A, C$ (the side where interior angles sum to less than two right angles, by I. 13 and triangle angle properties).

  - Comparative survey of modern 19th-century geometry textbooks and their pedagogical adoption of Playfair's axiom versus Euclid's Postulate 5:

    - Rausenberger stands almost alone in retaining Euclid's Postulate 5.
    - Hilbert, Henrici and Treutlein, Rouché and De Comberousse, Enriques and Amaldi all adopt Playfair's axiom.
    - De Morgan's proposed pedagogical sequence (introducing the deduction before I. 29).

  - C. L. Dodgson's (*Euclid and his modern Rivals*, pp. 44–6) defense of Euclid's Postulate 5 against Playfair's axiom on logical and pedagogical grounds.
  - Introduction of Dodgson's terminology: "separational" lines, property $(\alpha)$, and property $(\beta)$.
