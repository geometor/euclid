Hence, if you assert anything of class ($\beta$), the logical effect is more extensive than if you assert it of class ($\alpha$); for you assert it, not only of that portion of class ($\beta$) which is known to be included in class ($\alpha$), but also of the unknown (but possibly existing) portion which is *not* so included.

(2) Euclid's Postulate puts before the beginner clear and *positive* conceptions, a pair of straight lines, a transversal, and two angles together less than two right angles, whereas "Playfair's" axiom requires him to realise a pair of straight lines which never meet though produced to infinity: a *negative* conception which does not convey to the mind any clear notion of the relative position of the lines. And (p. 68) Euclid's Postulate gives a direct criterion for judging that two straight lines meet, a criterion which is constantly required, e.g. in **I.** 44. It is true that the Postulate can be *deduced* from "Playfair's" axiom, but editors frequently omit to deduce it, and then tacitly assume it afterwards: which is the least justifiable course of all.

### PROPOSITION 30.

<p class="enunciation">
Straight lines parallel to the same straight line are also parallel to one another.
</p>

![Folio 0328 Figure 1 - Proposition 30](0328.g1.svg)

Let each of the straight lines <span data-ref="0328.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0328.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> be parallel to <span data-ref="0328.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>;  
I say that <span data-ref="0328.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is also parallel to <span data-ref="0328.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>.

<sup>5</sup>For let the straight line <span data-ref="0328.g1:/ G K /">$GK$</span> fall upon them;

Then, since the straight line <span data-ref="0328.g1:/ G K /">$GK$</span> has fallen on the parallel straight lines <span data-ref="0328.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0328.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>,  
<p class="assertion"><sup>10</sup>the angle <span data-ref="0328.g1:< A G K )">$AGK$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0328.g1:< G H F )">$GHF$</span>. <span class="justification">[I. 29]</span></p>

Again, since the straight line <span data-ref="0328.g1:/ G K /">$GK$</span> has fallen on the parallel straight lines <span data-ref="0328.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, <span data-ref="0328.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>,  
<p class="assertion">the angle <span data-ref="0328.g1:< G H F )">$GHF$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0328.g1:< G K D )">$GKD$</span>. <span class="justification">[I. 29]</span></p>

<sup>15</sup>But the angle <span data-ref="0328.g1:< A G K )">$AGK$</span> was also proved equal to the angle <span data-ref="0328.g1:< G H F )">$GHF$</span>;  
<p class="assertion">therefore the angle <span data-ref="0328.g1:< A G K )">$AGK$</span> is also equal to the angle <span data-ref="0328.g1:< G K D )">$GKD$</span>; <span class="justification">[C. N. 1]</span></p>
<p class="sub-clause">and they are alternate.</p>

<p class="assertion"><sup>20</sup>Therefore <span data-ref="0328.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is parallel to <span data-ref="0328.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>.</p>

<p class="qed">Q. E. D.</p>
