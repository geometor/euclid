## Summary
- **Content**:
  - Conclusion of C. L. Dodgson's (*Euclid and his modern Rivals*, pp. 44–6) defense of Euclid's Postulate 5:
    - Completion of argument (1): class $(\beta)$ may be logically wider than class $(\alpha)$, meaning Playfair's axiom carries a more extensive and unverified assumption than Euclid's Postulate 5.
    - Argument (2): Euclid's Postulate presents clear and *positive* geometric conceptions (two straight lines, a transversal, angles summing to less than two right angles) providing a direct criterion for intersection (vital in I. 44), whereas Playfair's axiom relies on a *negative* infinite conception (lines never meeting to infinity) and is often assumed without proof by modern editors.

    - Enunciation: *Straight lines parallel to the same straight line are also parallel to one another.*
    - Full demonstration (lines 1–20) with alternate interior angle equality via I. 29 and Common Notion 1.
