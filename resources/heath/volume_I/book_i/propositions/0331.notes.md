## Summary
- **Content**:
  - Conclusion of the proof of Book I, Proposition 32:
    - Construction: through point $C$, drawing $CE$ parallel to $AB$ via [I. 31].
    - Demonstration: Alternate angles $BAC$, $ACE$ equal via [I. 29]; exterior angle $ECD$ equals interior opposite angle $ABC$ via [I. 29]; sum of angle $ACE$ and $ECD$ gives exterior angle $ACD$ equal to $BAC + ABC$; adding $ACB$ to both sides and applying [I. 13] proves the sum of the three angles of triangle $ABC$ equals two right angles.
    - Concluding formula: `Therefore etc.` and `Q. E. D.`.
  - Beginning of Heath's extensive commentary on the history of the theorem in early Greek geometry:
    - Overview of the three early sources (Eutocius, Proclus, Diogenes Laertius).
    - **1.** Geminus cited by Eutocius (*Comm. on Apollonius' Conics*, ed. Heiberg, Vol. II, p. 170): "the ancients" (οἱ ἀρχαῖοι) investigated the angle-sum separately for equilateral, isosceles, and scalene triangles, before later geometers demonstrated the general property for *any* triangle.
    - **2.** Proclus (p. 379, 2–5) citing Eudemus the Peripatetic attributing the general theorem and demonstration to the Pythagoreans; identifying the "ancients" as contemporaries of Thales or his Egyptian teachers.
    - **3.** Pamphile (quoted by Diogenes Laertius, I. 24–5, p. 6, ed. Cobet) recording that Thales learned geometry from the Egyptians and inscribed a right-angled triangle in a circle (begins at bottom line).

- **Greek Text**:
  - `οἱ ἀρχαῖοι`: "the ancients".
  - Accents and breathings:
    - `οἱ`: omicron with iota dasia (rough breathing on diphthong, U+03BF U+1F31).
