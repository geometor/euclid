# Transcription Notes: Folio 0334 (Page 320)

## Summary
- **Content**:
  - **Aristotle on Universal Demonstration (Conclusion)**:
    - Continuation of Aristotle's quotation (*Anal. Post.* I. 5): even if one proves that each kind of triangle (equilateral, isosceles, scalene) has angles equal to two right angles, one does not know it of the triangle *in general* (*quâ* triangle), but only in a numerical sense (κατ᾽ ἀριθμόν), not notionally / essentially (κατ᾽ εἶδος).
    - Heath's linguistic and historical analysis: the phrase "used at one time to be proved" in Aristotle's second illustration denotes an actual historical fact, whereas "if any one should prove" in the third illustration introduces a purely hypothetical case. Geminus likely misread this pedagogical example as historical fact. Heath concludes that Eudemus' testimony remains firm: the general theorem and proof were Pythagorean.
  - **The Pythagorean Proof of Proposition 32**:
    - Handed down by Eudemus (preserved in Proclus, p. 379, 2—15).
    - Natural refinement from the older rectangle decomposition: after the theory of parallels was developed, drawing the full rectangle and perpendiculars was seen to be unnecessary.
    - Construction: through vertex $A$, draw line $DE$ parallel to base $BC$ [I. 31].
    - Embedded graphic `0334.g1.svg` illustrating triangle $ABC$ with parallel line $DAE$.
    - Demonstration:
      - Alternate interior angles $\angle DAB = \angle ABC$ and $\angle EAC = \angle ACB$ [I. 29].
      - Sum $\angle ABC + \angle ACB = \angle DAB + \angle EAC$.
      - Adding $\angle BAC$ yields $\angle ABC + \angle ACB + \angle BAC = \angle DAB + \angle BAC + \angle CAE = 2$ right angles (angles along the straight line $DE$).
  - **Euclid's Proof Pre-Euclidean**:
    - The angle sum of a triangle (I. 32) is Aristotle's quintessential mathematical example, often cited in shorthand formulas:
      - `τὸ δυσὶν ὀρθαῖς` (*Anal. Post.* I. 24, 85 b 5)
      - `ὑπάρχει παντὶ τριγώνῳ τὸ δύο` (*ibid.* 85 b 11)
    - Initiation of analysis of Aristotle, *Metaph.* 1051 a 24 and Heiberg's commentary.

## Editorial & Typographical Findings
- **Greek Text & Diacritics**:
  - `κατ᾽ ἀριθμόν`: smooth breathing on `ἀριθμόν`; acute on omicron.
  - `κατ᾽ εἶδος`: smooth breathing and circumflex over diphthong `εἶ`.
  - `τὸ δυσὶν ὀρθαῖς`: smooth breathing on `ὀρθαῖς`, circumflex on diphthong `αῖ`.
  - `ὑπάρχει παντὶ τριγώνῳ τὸ δύο`:
    - `ὑπάρχει`: rough breathing on initial upsilon; acute on alpha.
    - `παντὶ`: grave on iota.
    - `τριγώνῳ`: acute on first omega, iota subscripts on both omegas (`ῳ`).
    - `τὸ δύο`: acute on upsilon of `δύο`.
- **Latin Terms & Diacritics**:
  - `*quâ*`: circumflex accent over `a` (`â`), as in 19th/early 20th century philosophical typesetting.
  - `*ibid.*`, `*op. cit.*`, `*Metaph.*`, `*Anal. Post.*`.
- **Proof Layout & Cross-References**:
  - Steps of the Pythagorean proof follow standard indentation and Euclid references in the margin (`[**I.** 31]`, `[**I.** 29]`).
