# Transcription Notes: Folio 0335 (Page 321)

## Summary
- **Content**:
  - **Euclid's Construction Known to Aristotle (Conclusion)**:
    - Continuation from Folio 0334 of Heiberg's observation (*op. cit.* p. 19) on Aristotle, *Metaph.* 1051 a 24: "Why does the triangle make up two right angles? Because the angles about one point are equal to two right angles. If then the parallel to the side had been *drawn up* (ἀνῆκτο), the fact would at once have been clear from merely looking at the figure."
    - Heath notes that while "the angles about one point" could fit the Pythagorean proof, the participle "drawn upwards" (ἀνῆκτο) can only refer to Euclid's construction of drawing a parallel up from the base through an external vertex.
  - **Attempts at Proof Independently of Parallels**:
    - Mention of Legendre as the most indefatigable worker on these lines (referenced in earlier notes on Postulate 5).
  - **Thibaut's Method of Rotation (1809, 1818)**:
    - B. F. Thibaut, *Grundriss der reinen Mathematik*, Göttingen (2 ed. 1809, 3 ed. 1818).
    - Construction: straight line $BD$ (with base $CB$ produced to $D$) revolves successively:
      1. About $B$ into the position of $BA$;
      2. About $A$ into the position of $AC$ produced both ways;
      3. About $C$ into the position of $CB$ produced both ways.
    - Embedded figure `0335.g1.svg` illustrating triangle $ABC$ with rotation line $BD$.
    - Argument: $BD$ rotates through the sum of the three exterior angles, returning to the same straight line, hence sweeping out 4 right angles. Thus the exterior angles equal 4 right angles, and the interior angles equal 2 right angles.
  - **Critique of Thibaut's Method**:
    - The rotation occurs about *different points in the line*, combining translation with rotatory motion. It uncritically assumes as an axiom that the translation can be neglected.
    - Schumacher's letter to Gauss (3 May 1831) attempted a graphical representation of the rotatory motion about a single point.
    - Gauss's reply (17 May 1831; cited from Engel and Stäckel, *Die Theorie der Parallellinien von Euklid bis auf Gauss*, 1895, p. 230) pointed out that Schumacher assumed without proof that if intersecting lines (1) and (2) make angles $A, A''$ with transversal (3), and line (4) is cut by (1) at angle $A'$, then (4) is cut by (2) at angle $A''$—a proposition that is essentially equivalent to what was to be proved.
  - **Proclus' Pseudo-Proof by "Common Notions"**:
    - Proclus (p. 384, 13–21) claimed the theorem could be grasped by "common notions" alone by imagining two perpendiculars at the extremities of a segment revolving about their feet to converge and form a triangle.

## Editorial & Typographical Findings
- **Greek Text & Diacritics**:
  - `ἀνῆκτο`: smooth breathing on alpha (`ἀ`), circumflex on eta (`ῆ`); pluperfect passive 3rd person singular of ἀνάγω ("drawn up" / "produced upwards").
- **Apparent Typographical Error in Heath's Text (Gauss Citation)**:
  - In Heath's transcription of Gauss's letter to Schumacher of 17 May 1831 (line 42 of page 321), the text reads: `"make angles A, A'' with a straight line (3)..."`.
  - In Gauss's original German text (as printed in Engel & Stäckel, *Die Theorie der Parallellinien*, 1895, p. 230, and *Gauss Werke* VIII): `"...mit einer dritten (3), von der sie geschnitten werden, respective die Winkel A', A'' machen..."`.
  - Heath's English print inadvertently omits the prime on the first angle symbol ($A$ instead of $A'$). The transcription reproduces Heath's printed text `$A$, $A''$` faithfully while documenting this erratum here.
- **Italicization & Typography**:
  - `*Grundriss der reinen Mathematik*`, `*Die Theorie der Parallellinien von Euklid bis auf Gauss*`.
  - `*drawn up*`, `"*drawn upwards*"`.
  - `*different points in it*`, `*rotatory*`.
  - Citation page dash: Proclus `p. 384, 13–21` using standard en-dash.
