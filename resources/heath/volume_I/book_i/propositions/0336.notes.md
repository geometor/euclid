# Transcription Notes: Folio 0336 (Page 322)

## Summary
- **Content**:
  - **Critique of Proclus' Pseudo-Proof (Conclusion)**:
    - Continuation from Folio 0335 of Proclus' argument (p. 384, 13–21) that converging perpendiculars diminish the right angles by the exact amount added to the vertical angle.
    - Heath points out the hidden fallacies:
      1. It assumes that if perpendiculars approach ever so little, they will immediately form a triangle (assuming Postulate 5 itself).
      2. The fact regarding the vertical angle can only be established using alternate angles by drawing a perpendicular from the vertex to the base, which is a *parallel* to either of the original perpendiculars.
  - **Extension to Polygons**:
    - Discussion of two important corollaries to I. 32 added in Robert Simson's edition, both originally given by Proclus.
    - **Corollary 1**: *The sum of the interior angles of a convex rectilineal figure is equal to twice as many right angles as the figure has sides, less four.*
      - Proclus' proof: join one angular point $A$ to all other non-adjacent vertices, partitioning the $n$-gon into $n - 2$ triangles.
      - Sum of angles of all $n - 2$ triangles equals the sum of the interior angles: $2(n - 2) = (2n - 4)$ right angles.
      - Embedded figure `0336.g1.png` illustrating a heptagon ($ABCDEFG$) triangulated from vertex $A$.
    - **Corollary 2**: *The exterior angles of any convex rectilineal figure are together equal to four right angles.*
      - Proof: interior and exterior angles together at $n$ vertices equal $2n$ right angles. Subtracting $(2n - 4)$ interior right angles leaves 4 right angles.
      - Heath notes this property is already quoted by Aristotle as true of all rectilineal figures in two places (*Anal. Post.* I. 24, 85 b 38 and II. 17, 99 a 19).
  - **Proposition 33 (Enunciation & Setting-Out)**:
    - Heading: `### PROPOSITION 33.`
    - Enunciation: *The straight lines joining equal and parallel straight lines (at the extremities which are) in the same directions (respectively) are themselves also equal and parallel.*
    - Setting-out (lines 4–5 of proposition): `Let $AB$, $CD$ be equal and parallel, and let the straight lines $AC$, $BD$ join them (at the extremities which are) in the same directions (respectively);`.
    - Marginal line number 5 formatted as `<sup>5</sup>`.
- **Folio Continuity**:
  - **Preceding folio**: Folio 0335 ends mid-sentence within Proclus' quotation: `...so as to form a triangle, we see that,`. Folio 0336 opens: `to the extent to which they converge, they diminish the right angles...`.
