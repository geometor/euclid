  - **An-Nairīzī's Construction for Dividing a Straight Line into Equal Parts**:

    - Recorded by Abu'l-‘Abbās al-Faḍl ibn Ḥātim an-Nairīzī (ed. Curtze, p. 74; ed. Besthorn-Heiberg, pp. 141–3) following Euclid I. 31.
    - Heath places it after I. 34 because the proof relies upon the properties of parallelograms established in I. 33 and I. 34.
    - Specific case of trisection into three equal parts:

      - Given $AB$, perpendiculars $AC$ and $BD$ are erected on opposite sides.
      - Segments $AE = EC$ and $BF = FD$ are laid off.
      - Joining lines $ED$ and $CF$ intersect $AB$ at $G$ and $H$.
      - By drawing $HK \parallel AC$ (perpendicular to $AB$), $ECHK$ forms a parallelogram, yielding $KH = EC = EA$.
      - Triangles $EAG$ and $KHG$ are congruent (two angles and side $AE = HK$), proving $AG = GH$.
      - Similarly, $\triangle KHG \cong \triangle FBH$, establishing $GH = HB$, hence $AG = GH = HB$.

    - Generalization to division into $n$ equal parts:

      - Lay off ($n - 1$) successive equal lengths on each perpendicular ($AC$ and $BD$).
      - Connect the 1st division on $AC$ to the $(n - 1)$th on $BD$, the 2nd on $AC$ to the $(n - 2)$th on $BD$, etc.
      - The connecting segments divide $AB$ into $n$ equal segments.
