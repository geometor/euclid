# Transcription Notes: Folio 0344 (Page 330)

## Summary
- **Content**:
  - **Pappus on Linear Loci and Critique of Proclus**:
    - Continues from folio 0343: while Proclus and Eutocius speak of solid loci other than conics, Pappus restricts solid loci to conic sections.
    - Pappus (III. p. 54, 16—21) defines *linear* problems/curves (γραμμικά) as those requiring construction lines of more complex origin than circles and conics, namely curves such as *quadratrices*, *conchoids*, and *cissoids*.
    - In Pappus VII. p. 662, 13—15 (bracketed as an interpolation by Hultsch), *linear loci* are curves other than straight lines, circles, and conics.
    - Heath notes that Proclus' classification of the cylindrical helix among solid loci on the ground that it is generated on a solid figure seems to rest upon a misapprehension.
  - **Loci on Surfaces and Proclus' Conception of I. 35**:
    - J. L. Heiberg, referencing Euclid's lost two-book treatise *Loci on Surfaces* (τόποι πρὸς ἐπιφανείᾳ) and Pappus, concluded that "loci on lines" and "loci on surfaces" in Proclus mean loci that are themselves lines or surfaces.
    - Heath points out that Proclus' actual conception in his note on I. 35 (p. 395, 5) is different: Proclus considers "the whole space between the parallels" as the locus of the equal parallelograms, rather than the parallel line being the locus of the vertices.
    - Similar area-oriented conceptions in Proclus:
      - Euclid III. 21 (angles in the same segment) and III. 31 (right angle in a semicircle) viewed as regarding the circular segment/semicircle as an area-locus for triangles with equal vertical angles, rather than the circumference as a line-locus of the vertices.
      - Parallelograms inscribed in "the asymptotes and the hyperbola" regarded as having the area between the curve and asymptotes as their solid locus.
  - **Apollonius' Classification of Loci in *Plane Loci***:
    - Preserved by Pappus (VII. p. 660, 18 sqq.):
      1. **ἐφεκτικοί** (*holding-in*): locus of point is a point, of line a line, of surface a surface, of solid a solid.
      2. **διεξοδικοί** (*moving along*): line is locus of point, surface of line, solid of surface.
      3. **ἀναστροφικοί**: surface is locus of point, solid of line.
    - Heath concludes that on Proclus' view of I. 35, the space between parallels is an ἐφεκτικός locus (area as locus of area), so that a "locus on lines" in Proclus means a region bounded by lines.
  - **Chrysippus and Geminus on Locus-Theorems**:
    - Proclus (p. 395, 13—21) cites Geminus: Chrysippus likened locus-theorems to the Platonic/philosophical Ideas, which confine infinite particulars within determinate limits.

## Editorial & Philological Details
- **Polytonic Greek Orthography**:
  - `γραμμικά` (*linear*): oxia on ultima alpha.
  - `τόποι πρὸς ἐπιφανείᾳ` (*loci on surfaces*):
    - `τόποι`: oxia on omicron.
    - `πρὸς`: varia on omicron.
    - `ἐπιφανείᾳ`: psili on epsilon, oxia on diphthong ει, iota subscript on alpha (ᾳ).
  - Apollonius' tripartite scheme of loci:
    - `ἐφεκτικοί` (*holding-in*): psili on epsilon, oxia on diphthong οι.
    - `διεξοδικοί` (*moving along*): psili on epsilon (δι- prefix before ἐξ-), oxia on diphthong οι.
    - `ἀναστροφικοί` (*inversive* / *reflexive*): psili on alpha, oxia on diphthong οι.
    - `ἐφεκτικός` (singular): psili on epsilon, oxia on omicron.
