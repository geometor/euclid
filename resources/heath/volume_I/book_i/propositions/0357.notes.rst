1. **Continuation Paragraph (Proposition 44 Commentary)**:

   - The top paragraph continues Heath's evaluative commentary on Proposition 44 from page 342 (folio 0356).
   - On page 342, Heath introduced: *"This proposition will always remain one of the most impressive in all geometry when account is taken (1) of the great importance of the result"*

3. **Proclus's Citation of Eudemus (pp. 419, 15—420, 23)**:

   - Proclus quotes Eudemus of Rhodes (``οἱ περὶ τὸν Εὔδημον`` — literally "the school of Eudemus" or Eudemus and his circle) attributing the method of application of areas to the early Pythagoreans.
   - Polytonic Greek terms transcribed with exact diacritics:

     - ``οἱ περὶ τὸν Εὔδημον`` (*hoi peri ton Eudēmon*)
     - ``παραβολὴ τῶν χωρίων`` (*parabolē tōn chōriōn* — application of areas)
     - ``ὑπερβολή`` (*hyperbolē* — exceeding)
     - ``ἔλλειψις`` (*elleipsis* — falling-short)
     - ``παραβάλλειν`` (*paraballein* — to apply)
     - ``ὑπερβάλλειν`` (*hyperballein* — to exceed)
     - ``ἐλλείπειν`` (*elleipein* — to fall short)
     - ``σύστασις`` (*systasis* — construction)

   - Heath notes that later geometers (specifically Apollonius of Perga) transferred these three terms (*parabola*, *hyperbola*, *ellipse*) from plane area construction to the conic sections.
   - Heath includes a numerical illustration: applying an area of 12 feet to a straight line of 4 feet yielding a breadth of 3 feet for a rectangle.

4. **Plutarch Quotations on Pythagoras's Sacrifice**:

   - Heath cites two passages from Plutarch:

     - (1) *Non posse suaviter vivi secundum Epicurum*, c. 11: Pythagoras sacrificing an ox on the strength of his proposition (``διάγραμμα``), whether it was the hypotenuse theorem or the problem of application of an area. Heath notes the manuscript reading ``Apollodotus`` with parenthetical editorial doubt ``(?-rus)`` for ``Apollodorus``.
     - (2) *Symp.* VIII. 2, 4 (commencing on page 343 and concluding on page 344): Plutarch considers the problem of constructing a figure equal to one given figure and similar to another to be even more subtle and scientific than the hypotenuse theorem.

   - Greek term: ``διάγραμμα`` (*diagramma* — proposition / figure / diagram).
