1. **Conclusion of Apollonius and Conic Sections Commentary (from Folio 0358)**:

   - Folio 0359 begins by providing the coordinate expressions for the three conic sections whose introduction by Apollonius was set up at the bottom of page 344:

     - (1) **Parabola** (*parabole* / application):

$$y^2 = px$$

       The square on the ordinate ($y$) is equal to a rectangle applied to the parameter ($p$, *latus rectum*) as base with altitude equal to the abscissa ($x$).

     - (2) **Hyperbola** and **Ellipse** (*hyperbole* / exceeding, and *ellipsis* / falling-short):
       The square on the ordinate is equal to a rectangle applied to $p$ having the abscissa as width and exceeding (hyperbola) or falling short (ellipse) by a figure similar and similarly situated to the rectangle contained by the given diameter $d$ and $p$.

       - Hyperbola:

$$y^2 = px + \frac{x^2}{d^2} pd = px + \frac{p}{d} x^2$$

- Ellipse:

$$y^2 = px - \frac{p}{d} x^2$$

   - Heath points out that these geometrical symptomata correspond directly to the generalized quadratic forms solved in Book VI (VI. 28, 29), illustrating that Apollonius's naming of the curves directly reflected the ancient Pythagorean terminology of the application of areas.
   - Punctuation & Typography:

     - Terms *parabola*, *hyperbola*, and *ellipse* are italicized in the definitions.
     - Terms *application*, *exceeding*, and *falling-short* are italicized in the concluding remark.
     - Display equations retain Heath's punctuation: comma after $pd$, semicolon after the second hyperbola equation, and period after the ellipse equation.
