3. **Proclus Commentary: Equality of Squares on Equal Straight Lines (pp. 424—5)**:

   - Proclus proves the bidirectional theorem:

     - *Direct theorem*: Squares described on equal straight lines are equal. Obvious by drawing diagonals, dividing each square into two congruent triangles.
     - *Converse theorem*: If two squares are equal, the straight lines on which they are described are equal.

   - Proof of the converse:

     - Squares $AF$ ($ABFD$) and $CG$ ($BCGE$) are placed with $AB, BC$ in a straight line. Right angles ensure $FB, BG$ are collinear.
     - Quadrilateral lines $AF, FC, CG, GA$ are joined.
     - Equal squares imply $\triangle ABF = \triangle CBG$.
     - Adding common triangle $\triangle FBC$ gives $\triangle AFC = \triangle GFC$, placing vertices $A, G$ in the same parallels as line $FC$ ($AG \parallel CF$).

   - Page Boundary Note: The proof breaks at the foot of page 348 (``equal, and hence they must be in the same parallels.``) and concludes at the top of folio 0363 (page 349: ``Therefore $AG$, $CF$ are parallel...``).
