### PROPOSITION 47.

<p class="enunciation">In right-angled triangles the square on the side subtending<br>
the right angle is equal to the squares on the sides containing<br>
the right angle.</p>

Let <span data-ref="0363.g1:&lt; A B C &gt;">$ABC$</span> be a right-angled triangle having the angle  
<sup>5</sup> <span data-ref="0363.g1:&lt; B A C )">$BAC$</span> right;  
I say that the square on <span data-ref="0363.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is equal to the squares on <span data-ref="0363.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span>, <span data-ref="0363.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>.

![Folio 0363 Figure 1 - Proposition 47](0363.g1.svg)

For let there be described on <span data-ref="0363.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> the square <span data-ref="0363.g1:&lt; B D E C &gt;">$BDEC$</span>,  
<sup>10</sup> and on <span data-ref="0363.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span>, <span data-ref="0363.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> the squares <span data-ref="0363.g1:&lt; G B &gt;">$GB$</span>, <span data-ref="0363.g1:&lt; H C &gt;">$HC$</span>; <span class="justification">[I. 46]</span>  
through <span data-ref="0363.g1:A">$A$</span> let <span data-ref="0363.g1:/ A L /">$AL$</span> be drawn parallel to either <span data-ref="0363.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> or <span data-ref="0363.g1:/ C E /">$CE$</span>,  
and let <span data-ref="0363.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0363.g1:/ F C /">$FC$</span> be joined.

<sup>15</sup> Then, since each of the angles <span data-ref="0363.g1:&lt; B A C )">$BAC$</span>, <span data-ref="0363.g1:&lt; B A G )">$BAG$</span> is right,  
it follows that with a straight line <span data-ref="0363.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span>, and at the point <span data-ref="0363.g1:A">$A$</span> on it, the two straight lines  
<sup>20</sup> <span data-ref="0363.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0363.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span> not lying on the same side make the adjacent angles equal to two right angles;
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0363.g1:/ C A /">$CA$</span> is in a straight line with <span data-ref="0363.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>. <span class="justification">[I. 14]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause"><sup>25</sup> For the same reason<br>
<span data-ref="0363.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span> is also in a straight line with <span data-ref="0363.g1:/ A H /">$AH$</span>.</p>

And, since the angle <span data-ref="0363.g1:&lt; D B C )">$DBC$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0363.g1:&lt; F B A )">$FBA$</span>: for each is right:  
let the angle <span data-ref="0363.g1:&lt; A B C )">$ABC$</span> be added to each;
<p class="assertion"><sup>30</sup> therefore the whole angle <span data-ref="0363.g1:&lt; D B A )">$DBA$</span> is equal to the whole angle <span data-ref="0363.g1:&lt; F B C )">$FBC$</span>. <span class="justification">[C. N. 2]</span></p>
