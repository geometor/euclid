### PROPOSITION 47.

<p class="enunciation">In right-angled triangles the square on the side subtending<br>
the right angle is equal to the squares on the sides containing<br>
the right angle.</p>

Let $ABC$ be a right-angled triangle having the angle  
<sup>5</sup> $BAC$ right;  
I say that the square on $BC$ is equal to the squares on $BA$, $AC$.

![Folio 0363 Figure 1 - Proposition 47](0363.g1.svg)

For let there be described on $BC$ the square $BDEC$,  
<sup>10</sup> and on $BA$, $AC$ the squares $GB$, $HC$; <span class="justification">[I. 46]</span>  
through $A$ let $AL$ be drawn parallel to either $BD$ or $CE$,  
and let $AD$, $FC$ be joined.

<sup>15</sup> Then, since each of the angles $BAC$, $BAG$ is right,  
it follows that with a straight line $BA$, and at the point $A$ on it, the two straight lines  
<sup>20</sup> $AC$, $AG$ not lying on the same side make the adjacent angles equal to two right angles;
<p class="assertion">therefore $CA$ is in a straight line with $AG$. <span class="justification">[I. 14]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause"><sup>25</sup> For the same reason<br>
$BA$ is also in a straight line with $AH$.</p>

And, since the angle $DBC$ is equal to the angle $FBA$: for each is right:  
let the angle $ABC$ be added to each;
<p class="assertion"><sup>30</sup> therefore the whole angle $DBA$ is equal to the whole angle $FBC$. <span class="justification">[C. N. 2]</span></p>
