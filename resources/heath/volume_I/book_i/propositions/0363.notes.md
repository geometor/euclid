1. **Conclusion of Proclus Commentary on Proposition 46 (from Folio 0362)**:
   - Folio 0363 opens with the conclusion of Proclus's proof (p. 424, 21—425, 23) of the converse of Proposition 46: *if two squares are equal, the straight lines are equal on which they are described*.
   - Context from bottom of Folio 0362 (page 348):
     - Two equal squares $AF$, $CG$ are placed along a line such that $AB, BC$ are collinear.
     - With right angles at $B$, $FB$ and $BG$ are collinear. $AF, FC, CG, GA$ are joined.
     - Triangles $ABF$ and $CBG$ are equal. Adding $\triangle FBC$ to each yields $\triangle AFC = \triangle GFC$.
     - Since they have the same base $FC$, they must lie between the same parallels.
   - Text on Folio 0363:
     - "Therefore $AG$, $CF$ are parallel."
     - Alternate angles $\angle AFG, \angle FGC$ are each half a right angle ($45^\circ$), hence $AF, CG$ are parallel.
     - Thus $AFCG$ is a parallelogram, giving $AF = CG$.
     - Triangles $ABF, CBG$ have two angles and one side respectively equal;
     - Therefore side $AB = BC$, and $BF = BG$, establishing that equal squares have equal sides.
