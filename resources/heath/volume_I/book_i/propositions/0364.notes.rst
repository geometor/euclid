1. **Proposition 47 Proof Completion & Concluding Formula (Lines 31–56)**:

   - Folio 0364 concludes the demonstration of Proposition 47 begun on folio 0363:

     - **Congruence of Triangles $ABD$ and $FBC$ (Lines 31–36)**:
       Since $DB = BC$ (sides of square $BDEC$) and $FB = BA$ (sides of square $GB$), the two sides $AB, BD$ equal $FB, BC$ respectively, and included angle $\angle ABD = \angle FBC$ (proved on Folio 0363). Therefore, by I. 4, base $AD = FC$ and $\triangle ABD \cong \triangle FBC$.
     - **Shearing and Area Equivalence (Lines 37–45)**:

       - Parallelogram (rectangle) $BL$ is double of $\triangle ABD$ because they share the same base $BD$ and lie between the same parallels $BD, AL$ (I. 41).
       - Square $GB$ is double of $\triangle FBC$ because they share base $FB$ and lie between parallels $FB, GC$ (I. 41).
       - Heiberg brackets line 43: ``[But the doubles of equals are equal to one another.]``
       - Therefore parallelogram $BL$ is equal to square $GB$.

     - **Symmetric Case for Right Square (Lines 46–50)**:
       Similarly, joining $AE, BK$, parallelogram $CL$ is proved equal to square $HC$.
     - **Summation and Conclusion (Lines 50–56)**:
       Adding the two parallelograms gives the whole square $BDEC = \text{square } GB + \text{square } HC$ (Common Notion 2).
       Since $BDEC$ is on $BC$, and $GB, HC$ are on $BA, AC$, the square on $BC$ is equal to the squares on $BA, AC$.
     - Terminating formula: ``Therefore etc.`` and ``Q. E. D.`` (*quod erat demonstrandum* / ὅπερ ἔδει δεῖξαι).

2. **Textual Notes on Proposition 47**:

   - **Note on line 1** (``**1. the square on**``):

     - Heath explains the Greek phrase ``τὸ ἀπὸ...τετράγωνον`` (literally "the [figure] from [a given straight line]"), where the participle ``ἀναγραφέν`` (*anagraphen*, aorist passive) or ``ἀναγεγραμμένον`` (*anagegrammenon*, perfect passive) is understood.
     - Orthographic discrepancy: In Perseus TEI XML and in Heath's 1908 printed typography, the word appears typeset as ``τετρἁγωνον`` with a rough breathing (``\u1f01``) over alpha rather than the standard acute accent (``τετράγωνον``, ``\u03ac``). This appears to be a typesetting artifact of the 1908 Greek font; standard polytonic ``τετράγωνον`` has been transcribed in markdown.

   - **Note on lemma** ``**subtending the right angle**``:

     - Heath discusses the syntax of ``ὑποτεινούσης`` (*hypoteinousēs*): used with the simple accusative ``τὴν ὀρθὴν γωνίαν`` (*tēn orthēn gōnian*) instead of ``ὑπό`` + accusative.
     - Discrepancy with Perseus XML: Perseus TEI XML omits quotation marks around ``subtending`` (``subtending, is used with...``), whereas Heath's print clearly has ``“subtending,”``. Heath's printed text reading is preserved.

   - **Note on line 33** (``**33. the two sides AB, BD....**``):

     - Euclid writes ``DB, BA`` in the Greek text rather than corresponding order ``AB, BD``, while retaining the distributive formula ``ἑκατέρα ἑκατέρα`` (*hekatera hekatera*, "each to each" / "respectively").
     - Quotation marks around ``“DB, BA,”`` and ``“respectively.”`` are present in print and preserved in markdown.

   - **Note on line 44** (``**44. [But the doubles of equals are equal to one another.]**``):

     - Heiberg brackets these words as an interpolation because they cite an interpolated Common Notion (cf. notes on I. 37, p. 332, and on interpolated Common Notions, pp. 223–4).
