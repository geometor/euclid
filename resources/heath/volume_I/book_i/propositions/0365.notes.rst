1. **Heath's Commentary on the Discovery of Proposition 47**:

   - Folio 0365 continues the historical commentary on Proposition 47 begun on page 350 (folio 0364) concerning the attribution of the theorem of the square on the hypotenuse to Pythagoras.
   - Ancient witnesses cited:

     - **Plutarch**: Passages quoted previously in the note on I. 44 (pp. 343–4; *Non posse suaviter vivi secundum Epicurum*, c. 11; *Quaest. conviv.* / *Symp.* VIII. 2, 4).
     - **Diogenes Laertius**: VIII. 12 (quoting Apollodorus the calculator).
     - **Athenaeus**: x. 13 (Deipnosophistae).
     - **Cicero**: *De natura deorum* III. c. 36, § 88 (addressing the ox sacrifice rather than disputing the mathematical theorem).

   - Modern Critical Scholarship:

     - **Gustav Junge**: *“Wann haben die Griechen das Irrationale entdeckt?”* (*Novae Symbolae Joachimicae*, Halle a. S., 1907, pp. 221—264). Junge argued that these witnesses are late and that early Greek literature lacks explicit attribution to Pythagoras.
     - **Heath's Synthesis**: Heath counters Junge by demonstrating that:

       1. The distich of Apollodorus the “calculator” provides early evidence of a famous discovery by Pythagoras.
       2. Plutarch unhesitatingly attributed both the theorem of the hypotenuse and the application of areas to Pythagoras.
       3. The doctrine of the *gnomon* (γνώμων) permeating Euclid Book II is demonstrably Pythagorean, as witnessed by Philolaus (ed. Boeckh, 1819, p. 141) and Aristotle (*Phys.* III. 4, 203 a 10—15; *Cat.* 14, 15 a 30).
       4. Heron (? 3rd cent. A.D.) and Proclus credit Pythagoras with a rule for rational right-angled triangles ($m^2 + (\frac{m^2 - 1}{2})^2 = (\frac{m^2 + 1}{2})^2$).
       5. Discovery of the irrational ($\sqrt{2}$) as the ratio of the diagonal of a square to its side (scholium No. 1 to Book X.) directly presupposes knowledge of I. 47 for an isosceles right-angled triangle.

2. **Polytonic Greek & Philological Analysis**:

   - ``τὴν τῶν ἀλόγων πραγματείαν``: Heath cites the traditional manuscript reading of Proclus' summary (*In Primum Euclidis Elementorum Librum Commentarii*, p. 65, 19–21 Friedlein).
   - **Textual Emendation**: Heath notes the consensus emendation from ``τῶν ἀλόγων`` (*of irrationals*) to ``τῶν ἀναλόγων`` or ``τῶν ἀνὰ λόγον`` (*of proportionals*). This indicates that early Pythagorean theory addressed an arithmetical theory of proportion for commensurable magnitudes, before Eudoxus formulated the general theory in Euclid Book V.
   - All Greek accents, breathings, and subscript ligatures conform to standard polytonic typography:

     - ``τὴν`` (grave on η)
     - ``τῶν`` (circumflex on ω)
     - ``ἀλόγων`` (smooth breathing on α, acute on ο)
     - ``πραγματείαν`` (acute on ει)
     - ``ἀναλόγων`` (smooth breathing on first α, acute on ο)
     - ``ἀνὰ`` (smooth breathing on first α, grave on second α)
     - ``λόγον`` (acute on first ο)
