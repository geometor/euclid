2. **The Problem of Pythagoras' General Proof**:
   - Heath identifies the central historical puzzle: given that Pythagoras recognized the truth of the theorem for special rational right-angled triangles (such as 3, 4, 5) and the isosceles right-angled triangle (side ratio $1 : 1 : \sqrt{2}$), how did he establish the proposition generally?
   - Heath distinguishes two primary lines of inquiry:
     - **(1) Theory of Proportions & Similar Triangles** (Tannery): Discussed on this folio and continued onto Folio 0368.
     - **(2) Geometric Dissection / Geometrical Algebra** (Bretschneider & Hankel): Discussed on Folio 0368.

3. **Paul Tannery's Hypothesis & Pre-Eudoxian Proportion Theory**:
   - Citation: Paul Tannery, *La Géométrie grecque*, Paris, 1887, p. 105.
   - Tannery suggests that early Pythagorean geometry was sufficiently developed to prove the theorem via *similar triangles*.
   - **Heath's Critique & Crucial Insight**:
     - Any proof by similar triangles necessarily relies upon a theory of proportions.
     - Before Eudoxus of Cnidus (whose general theory of proportion independent of commensurability is preserved in Euclid's Book V), the Greek theory of proportion was restricted to commensurable magnitudes (arithmetical proportion).
     - Any proof of the general Pythagorean theorem given by Pythagoras using proportions was therefore strictly incomplete/inconclusive for incommensurable lengths.
     - Heath points out that this defect brilliantly accounts for why Euclid was obliged to invent an entirely new, independent proof in I. 47: Euclid had organized the *Elements* so that the Pythagorean theorem was needed early (in Book II), long before the rigorous Eudoxian theory of proportions could be introduced in Books V and VI.
     - Conversely, if the early Pythagorean proof had rested solely on the elementary planar geometry of Books I and II, Euclid would not have needed to construct a new proof.

4. **Proportion Proof Method (*a*) & Demonstration Graphic**:
   - **Method (*a*)**:
     - From a right-angled triangle $ABC$ with right angle at $A$, drop altitude $AD$ perpendicular to hypotenuse $BC$.
     - Similarity of triangles $\triangle ABC \sim \triangle DBA \implies \frac{CB}{BA} = \frac{BA}{BD} \implies CB \cdot BD = BA^2$.
     - Similarity of triangles $\triangle ABC \sim \triangle DAC \implies \frac{BC}{CA} = \frac{CA}{CD} \implies BC \cdot CD = CA^2$.
     - Adding these two relations gives:
       $$CB \cdot BD + BC \cdot CD = BC(BD + CD) = BC^2 = BA^2 + CA^2$$
   - **Connection to Euclid's Proof in I. 47**:
     - Heath notes that Euclid's proof is *in substance* identical with this method, differing only in the machinery employed: Euclid substitutes the relations of parallelograms and triangles on the same base and between the same parallels (I. 41) for the proportion-derived rectangle relations of Book VI.
