2. **Zeuthen's Second Construction (Similar Triangles & Small Rectangles)**:
   - Heath presents a second method from H. G. Zeuthen's 1904 Geneva paper combining method (*b*) from p. 354 (similar triangles formed by the altitude to the hypotenuse) with subdivision into small rectangles.
   - **Construction**:
     - Right triangle $ABC$ with right angle at $A$, sides $AB = 4, AC = 3$.
     - Altitude $AD$ is drawn perpendicular to $BC$.
     - Complete the rectangle on base $BC$ with altitude $AD$.
     - Parallels to $BC$ and $AD$ are drawn through the unit division points of $AB$ and $AC$, partitioning the large rectangle into small rectangles.
   - **Proof**:
     - The diagonals of all the small rectangles are of unit length; hence by similar triangles the small rectangles are congruent.
     - The rectangle on diagonal $AB = 4$ contains $4^2 = 16$ small rectangles.
     - The rectangle on diagonal $AC = 3$ contains $3^2 = 9$ small rectangles.
     - Since $\triangle ABD + \triangle ADC = \triangle ABC$, the rectangle on diagonal $BC$ contains $16 + 9 = 25$ small rectangles.
     - Thus the hypotenuse $BC = \sqrt{25} = 5$.
   - **Heath's Judgment**: Heath observes that while mathematically elegant, this demonstration is too sophisticated to represent the historical reasoning of the earliest Pythagoreans.

5. **Proclus on Pythagoras' and Plato's Formulas**:
   - **Source**: Proclus, *In primum Euclidis Elementorum librum commentarii* (ed. Gottfried Friedlein, Leipzig, 1873, pp. 428, 7—429, 8).
   - **Pythagoras' Rule (Generating Triples from Odd Numbers)**:
     - Given an odd number $m$:
       - Smaller side: $m$
       - Greater side: $\frac{m^2 - 1}{2}$
       - Hypotenuse: $\frac{m^2 - 1}{2} + 1 = \frac{m^2 + 1}{2}$
     - Numerical example: $m = 3 \implies \frac{9 - 1}{2} = 4, 4 + 1 = 5 \implies (3, 4, 5)$.
     - Identity: $m^2 + \left(\frac{m^2 - 1}{2}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{m^2 + 1}{2}\right)^2$.
   - **Plato's Rule (Generating Triples from Even Numbers)**:
     - Given an even number $2m$:
       - One side: $2m$
       - Other side: $m^2 - 1$
       - Hypotenuse: $m^2 + 1$
     - Numerical example: $2m = 4$ ($m = 2$) $\implies 2^2 - 1 = 3, 2^2 + 1 = 5 \implies (4, 3, 5)$.
     - Identity: $(2m)^2 + (m^2 - 1)^2 = (m^2 + 1)^2$.
