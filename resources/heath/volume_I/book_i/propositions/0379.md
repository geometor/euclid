Construct on <span data-ref="0379.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> the square <span data-ref="0379.g1:&lt; E G &gt;">$EG$</span>, and produce <span data-ref="0379.g1:/ D H /">$DH$</span> to <span data-ref="0379.g1:K">$K$</span> so that <span data-ref="0379.g1:/ D K /">$DK$</span> may be equal to <span data-ref="0379.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>.

![Folio 0379 Figure 1 - Proposition 47 Commentary (Thābit b. Qurra Proof)](0379.g1.svg)

It is then proved that, in the triangles <span data-ref="0379.g1:&lt; B A C &gt;">$BAC$</span>, <span data-ref="0379.g1:&lt; C F G &gt;">$CFG$</span>, <span data-ref="0379.g1:&lt; K H G &gt;">$KHG$</span>, <span data-ref="0379.g1:&lt; B D K &gt;">$BDK$</span>,
the sides <span data-ref="0379.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span>, <span data-ref="0379.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span>, <span data-ref="0379.g1:/ K H /">$KH$</span>, <span data-ref="0379.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> are all equal,
and
the sides <span data-ref="0379.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0379.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span>, <span data-ref="0379.g1:/ H G /">$HG$</span>, <span data-ref="0379.g1:/ D K /">$DK$</span> are all equal.

The angles included by the equal sides are all right angles; hence the four triangles are equal in all respects. [I. 4]

Hence <span data-ref="0379.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, <span data-ref="0379.g1:/ C G /">$CG$</span>, <span data-ref="0379.g1:/ G K /">$GK$</span>, <span data-ref="0379.g1:/ K B /">$KB$</span> are all equal.

Further the angles <span data-ref="0379.g1:&lt; D B K )">$DBK$</span>, <span data-ref="0379.g1:&lt; A B C )">$ABC$</span> are equal; hence, if we add to each the angle <span data-ref="0379.g1:&lt; D B C )">$DBC$</span>, the angle <span data-ref="0379.g1:&lt; K B C )">$KBC$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0379.g1:&lt; A B D )">$ABD$</span> and is therefore a right angle.

In the same way the angle <span data-ref="0379.g1:&lt; C G K )">$CGK$</span> is right; therefore <span data-ref="0379.g1:&lt; B C G K &gt;">$BCGK$</span> is a square, i.e. the square on <span data-ref="0379.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>.

Now the sum of the quadrilateral <span data-ref="0379.g1:&lt; G C L H &gt;">$GCLH$</span> and the triangle <span data-ref="0379.g1:&lt; L D B &gt;">$LDB$</span> together with two of the equal triangles make the squares on <span data-ref="0379.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0379.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, and together with the other two make the square on <span data-ref="0379.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>.

Therefore etc.

II. Another proof is easily arrived at by taking the particular case of Pappus' more general proposition given below in which the given triangle is right-angled and the parallelograms on the sides containing the right angles are squares. If the figure is drawn, it will be seen that, with no more than one additional line inserted, it contains Thābit's figure, so that Thābit's proof may have been practically derived from that of Pappus.

III. The most interesting of the remaining proofs seems to be that shown in the accompanying figure. It is given by J. W. Müller, *Systematische Zusammenstellung der wichtigsten bisher bekannten Beweise des Pythag. Lehrsatzes* (Nürnberg, 1819), and in the second edition (Mainz, 1821) of Ign. Hoffmann, *Der Pythag. Lehrsatz mit 32 theils bekannten theils neuen Beweisen* [3 more in second edition]. It appears to come from one of the scientific papers of Lionardo da Vinci (1452—1519).
