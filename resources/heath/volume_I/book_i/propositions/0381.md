Curtze) on I. 47, wherein he gives Heron's proof that the lines <span data-ref="0363.g1:/ A L /">$AL$</span>, <span data-ref="0363.g1:/ F C /">$FC$</span>, <span data-ref="0363.g1:/ B K /">$BK$</span> in Euclid's figure meet in a point. Heron proved this by means of three lemmas which would most naturally be proved from the principle of similitude as laid down in Book VI., but which Heron, as a *tour de force*, proved on the principles of Book I. only. The *first* lemma is to the following effect.

*If, in a triangle <span data-ref="0381.g1:&lt; A B C &gt;">$ABC$</span>, <span data-ref="0381.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> be drawn parallel to the base <span data-ref="0381.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, and if <span data-ref="0381.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span> be drawn from the vertex <span data-ref="0381.g1:A">$A$</span> to the middle point <span data-ref="0381.g1:F">$F$</span> of <span data-ref="0381.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, then <span data-ref="0381.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span> will also bisect <span data-ref="0381.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>.*

This is proved by drawing <span data-ref="0381.g1:/ H K /">$HK$</span> through <span data-ref="0381.g1:A">$A$</span> parallel to <span data-ref="0381.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> or <span data-ref="0381.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, and <span data-ref="0381.g1:/ H D L /">$HDL$</span>, <span data-ref="0381.g1:/ K E M /">$KEM$</span> through <span data-ref="0381.g1:D">$D$</span>, <span data-ref="0381.g1:E">$E$</span> respectively parallel to <span data-ref="0381.g1:/ A G F /">$AGF$</span>, and lastly joining <span data-ref="0381.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span>, <span data-ref="0381.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>.

Then the triangles <span data-ref="0381.g1:&lt; A B F &gt;">$ABF$</span>, <span data-ref="0381.g1:&lt; A F C &gt;">$AFC$</span> are equal (being on equal bases), and the triangles <span data-ref="0381.g1:&lt; D B F &gt;">$DBF$</span>, <span data-ref="0381.g1:&lt; E F C &gt;">$EFC$</span> are also equal (being on equal bases and between the same parallels).

Therefore, by subtraction, the triangles <span data-ref="0381.g1:&lt; A D F &gt;">$ADF$</span>, <span data-ref="0381.g1:&lt; A E F &gt;">$AEF$</span> are equal, and hence the parallelograms <span data-ref="0381.g1:&lt; A L &gt;">$AL$</span>, <span data-ref="0381.g1:&lt; A M &gt;">$AM$</span> are equal.

These parallelograms are between the same parallels <span data-ref="0381.g1:/ L M /">$LM$</span>, <span data-ref="0381.g1:/ H K /">$HK$</span>; therefore <span data-ref="0381.g1:/ L F /">$LF$</span>, <span data-ref="0381.g1:/ F M /">$FM$</span> are equal, whence <span data-ref="0381.g1:/ D G /">$DG$</span>, <span data-ref="0381.g1:/ G E /">$GE$</span> are also equal.

The *second* lemma is an extension of this to the case where <span data-ref="0381.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> meets <span data-ref="0381.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span>, <span data-ref="0381.g1:/ C A /">$CA$</span> produced beyond <span data-ref="0381.g1:A">$A$</span>.

The *third* lemma proves the converse of Euclid I. 43, that, *If a parallelogram <span data-ref="0381.g2:&lt; A B &gt;">$AB$</span> is cut into four others <span data-ref="0381.g2:&lt; A D G E &gt;">$ADGE$</span>, <span data-ref="0381.g2:&lt; D F &gt;">$DF$</span>, <span data-ref="0381.g2:&lt; F G C B &gt;">$FGCB$</span>, <span data-ref="0381.g2:&lt; C E &gt;">$CE$</span>, so that <span data-ref="0381.g2:&lt; D F &gt;">$DF$</span>, <span data-ref="0381.g2:&lt; C E &gt;">$CE$</span> are equal, the common vertex <span data-ref="0381.g2:G">$G$</span> will be on the diagonal <span data-ref="0381.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span>.*

Heron produces <span data-ref="0381.g2:/ A G /">$AG$</span> till it meets <span data-ref="0381.g2:/ C F /">$CF$</span> in <span data-ref="0381.g2:H">$H$</span>. Then, if we join <span data-ref="0381.g2:/ H B /">$HB$</span>, we have to prove that <span data-ref="0381.g2:/ A H B /">$AHB$</span> is one straight line. The proof is as follows. Since the areas <span data-ref="0381.g2:&lt; D F &gt;">$DF$</span>, <span data-ref="0381.g2:&lt; E C &gt;">$EC$</span> are equal, the triangles <span data-ref="0381.g2:&lt; D G F &gt;">$DGF$</span>, <span data-ref="0381.g2:&lt; E C G &gt;">$ECG$</span> are equal.

If we add to each the triangle <span data-ref="0381.g2:&lt; G C F &gt;">$GCF$</span>,
the triangles <span data-ref="0381.g2:&lt; E C F &gt;">$ECF$</span>, <span data-ref="0381.g2:&lt; D C F &gt;">$DCF$</span> are equal;
therefore <span data-ref="0381.g2:/ E D /">$ED$</span>, <span data-ref="0381.g2:/ C F /">$CF$</span> are parallel.

Now it follows from I. 34, 29 and 26 that the triangles <span data-ref="0381.g2:&lt; A K E &gt;">$AKE$</span>, <span data-ref="0381.g2:&lt; G K D &gt;">$GKD$</span> are equal in all respects;
therefore <span data-ref="0381.g2:/ E K /">$EK$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0381.g2:/ K D /">$KD$</span>.

Hence, by the second lemma,
<span data-ref="0381.g2:/ C H /">$CH$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0381.g2:/ H F /">$HF$</span>.

Therefore, in the triangles <span data-ref="0381.g2:&lt; F H B &gt;">$FHB$</span>, <span data-ref="0381.g2:&lt; C H G &gt;">$CHG$</span>,
the two sides <span data-ref="0381.g2:/ B F /">$BF$</span>, <span data-ref="0381.g2:/ F H /">$FH$</span> are equal to the two sides <span data-ref="0381.g2:/ G C /">$GC$</span>, <span data-ref="0381.g2:/ C H /">$CH$</span>,
and the angle <span data-ref="0381.g2:&lt; B F H )">$BFH$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0381.g2:&lt; G C H )">$GCH$</span>;
hence the triangles are equal in all respects,
and the angle <span data-ref="0381.g2:&lt; B H F )">$BHF$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0381.g2:&lt; G H C )">$GHC$</span>.

Adding to each the angle <span data-ref="0381.g2:&lt; G H F )">$GHF$</span>, we find that the angles <span data-ref="0381.g2:&lt; B H F )">$BHF$</span>, <span data-ref="0381.g2:&lt; F H G )">$FHG$</span> are equal to the angles <span data-ref="0381.g2:&lt; C H G )">$CHG$</span>, <span data-ref="0381.g2:&lt; G H F )">$GHF$</span>,
and therefore to two right angles.

Therefore <span data-ref="0381.g2:/ A H B /">$AHB$</span> is a straight line.
