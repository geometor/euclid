BOOK II.
========

DEFINITIONS.
------------

1. Any rectangular parallelogram is said to be :term:`**contained** <contained-rectangle>` by the two straight lines containing the right angle.

2. And in any parallelogrammic area let any one whatever of the parallelograms about its diameter with the two complements be called a :term:`**gnomon** <gnomon>`.

----

DEFINITION 1.
-------------

Πᾶν :term:`παραλληλόγραμμον <parallelogrammon>` :term:`ὀρθογώνιον <orthogonion>` περιέχεσθαι λέγεται ὑπὸ δύο τῶν τὴν ὀρθὴν :term:`γωνίαν <angle>` περιεχουσῶν :term:`εὐθειῶν <straight-line>`.

As the full expression in Greek for "the angle $BAC$" is "the angle contained by the (straight lines) $BA$, $AC$," ἡ ὑπὸ τῶν ΒΑ, ΑΓ περιεχομένη :term:`γωνία <angle>`, so the full expression for "the rectangle contained by $BA$, $AC$" is τὸ ὑπὸ τῶν ΒΑ, ΑΓ περιεχόμενον :term:`ὀρθογώνιον <orthogonion>`. In this case too ΒΑ, ΑΓ is commonly abbreviated by the Greek geometers into ΒΑΓ. Thus in :person:`Archimedes <archimedes>` and :person:`Apollonius <apollonius>` τὸ ὑπὸ ΒΑΓ or τὸ ὑπὸ τῶν ΒΑΓ means *the rectangle $BA$, $AC$*, just as ἡ ὑπὸ ΒΑΓ means *the angle $BAC$*; the gender of the article shows which is meant in each case. In the early Books :person:`Euclid <euclid>` uses the full expression τὸ ὑπὸ τῶν ΒΑ, ΑΓ; but the shorter form τὸ ὑπὸ τῶν ΒΑΓ is found from Book X. onwards. Cf. XII. 11, where τὰ (τμήματα) ἐπὶ τῶν ΘΟΕ, ΕΠΖ, ΖΡΗ, ΗΣΘ means the segments on the eight straight lines ΘΟ, ΟΕ, ΕΠ, ΠΖ, ΖΡ, ΡΗ, ΗΣ, ΣΘ.

DEFINITION 2.
-------------

Παντὸς δὲ :term:`παραλληλογράμμου <parallelogrammon>` χωρίου τῶν περὶ τὴν :term:`διάμετρον <diameter>` αὐτοῦ παραλληλογράμμων ἓν ὁποιονοῦν σὺν τοῖς δυσὶ :term:`παραπληρώμασι <parapleroma>` :term:`γνώμων <gnomon>` καλείσθω.

Meaning literally a thing enabling something to be *known*, *observed* or *verified*, a *teller* or *marker*, as we might say, the word *gnomon* (:term:`γνώμων <gnomon>`) was first used in the sense (1) in which it appears in a passage of :person:`Herodotus <herodotus>` (II. 109) stating that "the Greeks learnt the πόλος, the *gnomon* and the twelve parts of the day from the Babylonians." According to :person:`Suidas <suidas>`, it was :person:`Anaximander <anaximander>` (611—545 B.C.) who introduced the *gnomon* into Greece.
