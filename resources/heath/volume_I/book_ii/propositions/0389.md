# BOOK II. PROPOSITIONS.

### PROPOSITION 1.

<p class="enunciation">If there be two straight lines, and one of them be cut into<br />
any number of segments whatever, the rectangle contained by<br />
the two straight lines is equal to the rectangles contained by the<br />
uncut straight line and each of the segments.</p>

<sup>5</sup> Let $A$, $BC$ be two straight lines, and let $BC$ be cut at random at the points $D$, $E$;  
I say that the rectangle contained by $A$, $BC$ is equal to the rectangle contained by $A$, $BD$, that contained by $A$, $DE$ and <sup>10</sup> that contained by $A$, $EC$.

![Diagram](0389.g1.png)

For let $BF$ be drawn from $B$ at right angles to $BC$; <span class="justification">[I. 11]</span>  
let $BG$ be made equal to $A$, <span class="justification">[I. 3]</span>  
through $G$ let $GH$ be drawn <sup>15</sup> parallel to $BC$, <span class="justification">[I. 31]</span>  
and through $D$, $E$, $C$ let $DK$, $EL$, $CH$ be drawn parallel to $BG$.

<p class="assertion">Then $BH$ is equal to $BK$, $DL$, $EH$.</p>

<sup>20</sup> Now $BH$ is the rectangle $A$, $BC$, for it is contained by $GB$, $BC$, and $BG$ is equal to $A$;  
<p class="sub-clause">$BK$ is the rectangle $A$, $BD$, for it is contained by $GB$, $BD$, and $BG$ is equal to $A$;</p>  
<p class="sub-clause">and $DL$ is the rectangle $A$, $DE$, for $DK$, that is $BG$ <span class="justification">[I. 34]</span>, <sup>25</sup> is equal to $A$.</p>  
<p class="sub-clause">Similarly also $EH$ is the rectangle $A$, $EC$.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore the rectangle $A$, $BC$ is equal to the rectangle $A$, $BD$, the rectangle $A$, $DE$ and the rectangle $A$, $EC$.</p>

<p class="qed">Therefore etc. <span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>
