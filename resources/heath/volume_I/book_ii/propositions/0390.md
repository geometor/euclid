**20. the rectangle <span data-ref="0389.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0389.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>.** From this point onward I shall translate thus in cases where Euclid leaves out the word *contained* (περιεχόμενον). Though the word "rectangle" is also omitted in the Greek (the neuter article being sufficient to show that the rectangle is meant), it cannot be dispensed with in English. De Morgan advises the use of the expression "the rectangle *under* two lines." This does not seem to me a very good expression, and, if used in a translation from the Greek, it might suggest that ὑπό in τὸ ὑπό meant *under*, which it does not.

This proposition, the geometrical equivalent of the algebraical formula
\[
a(b + c + d + \dots) = ab + ac + ad + \dots,
\]
can, of course, easily be extended so as to correspond to the more general algebraical proposition that the product of an expression consisting of any number of terms added together and another expression also consisting of any number of terms added together is equal to the sum of all the products obtained by multiplying each term of one expression by all the terms of the other expression, one after another. The geometrical proof of the more general proposition would be effected by means of a figure showing all the rectangles corresponding to the partial products, in the same way as they are shown in the simpler case of II. 1; the difference would be that a series of parallels to <span data-ref="0389.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> would have to be drawn as well as the series of parallels to <span data-ref="0389.g1:/ B F /">$BF$</span>.

### PROPOSITION 2.

<p class="enunciation">If a straight line be cut at random, the rectangle contained<br />
by the whole and both of the segments is equal to the square on<br />
the whole.</p>

For let the straight line <span data-ref="0390.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> be cut at random at the point <span data-ref="0390.g1:C">$C$</span>;  
I say that the rectangle contained by <span data-ref="0390.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0390.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> together with the rectangle contained by <span data-ref="0390.g1:/ A B /">$BA$</span>, <span data-ref="0390.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is equal to the square on <span data-ref="0390.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>.

![Diagram](0390.g1.svg)

For let the square <span data-ref="0390.g1:< A D E B >">$ADEB$</span> be described on <span data-ref="0390.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> <span class="justification">[I. 46]</span>, and let <span data-ref="0390.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span> be drawn through <span data-ref="0390.g1:C">$C$</span> parallel to either <span data-ref="0390.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> or <span data-ref="0390.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span>. <span class="justification">[I. 31]</span>

<p class="assertion">Then <span data-ref="0390.g1:< A E >">$AE$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0390.g1:< A F >">$AF$</span>, <span data-ref="0390.g1:< C E >">$CE$</span>.</p>

Now <span data-ref="0390.g1:< A E >">$AE$</span> is the square on <span data-ref="0390.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>;  
<p class="sub-clause"><span data-ref="0390.g1:< A F >">$AF$</span> is the rectangle contained by <span data-ref="0390.g1:/ A B /">$BA$</span>, <span data-ref="0390.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, for it is contained by <span data-ref="0390.g1:/ A D /">$DA$</span>, <span data-ref="0390.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, and <span data-ref="0390.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0390.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>;</p>  
<p class="sub-clause">and <span data-ref="0390.g1:< C E >">$CE$</span> is the rectangle <span data-ref="0390.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0390.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, for <span data-ref="0390.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0390.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore the rectangle <span data-ref="0390.g1:/ A B /">$BA$</span>, <span data-ref="0390.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> together with the rectangle <span data-ref="0390.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0390.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is equal to the square on <span data-ref="0390.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>.</p>

<p class="qed">Therefore etc. <span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>
