:title: Proposition 4 (Continued)

But :g:`$CB$ <0393.g1:/ B C />` is equal to :g:`$GK$ <0393.g1:/ G K />`, and :g:`$CG$ <0393.g1:/ C G />` to :g:`$KB$ <0393.g1:/ B K />`; :just:`[I. 34] <I.34>`

therefore :g:`$GK$ <0393.g1:/ G K />` is also equal to :g:`$KB$ <0393.g1:/ B K />`;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$CGKB$ <0393.g1:< C G K B >>` is equilateral.

I say next that it is also right-angled.

For, since :g:`$CG$ <0393.g1:/ C G />` is parallel to :g:`$BK$ <0393.g1:/ B K />`, the angles :g:`$KBC$ <0393.g1:< K B C )>`, :g:`$GCB$ <0393.g1:< G C B )>` are equal to two right angles. :just:`[I. 29] <I.29>`

But the angle :g:`$KBC$ <0393.g1:< K B C )>` is right;

.. assertion::

   therefore the angle :g:`$BCG$ <0393.g1:< G C B )>` is also right,

so that the opposite angles :g:`$CGK$ <0393.g1:< C G K )>`, :g:`$GKB$ <0393.g1:< G K B )>` are also right. :just:`[I. 34] <I.34>`

Therefore :g:`$CGKB$ <0393.g1:< C G K B >>` is right-angled; and it was also proved equilateral;

.. assertion::

   therefore it is a square;

and it is described\ [#f2]_ on :g:`$CB$ <0393.g1:/ B C />`.

.. assertion::

   For the same reason :g:`$HF$ <0393.g1:< H F >>` is also a square;

and it is described on :g:`$HG$ <0393.g1:/ H G />`, that is :g:`$AC$ <0393.g1:/ A C />`. :just:`[I. 34] <I.34>`

.. assertion::

   Therefore the squares :g:`$HF$ <0393.g1:< H F >>`, :g:`$KC$ <0393.g1:< K C >>` are the squares on :g:`$AC$ <0393.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$CB$ <0393.g1:/ B C />`.

Now, since :g:`$AG$ <0393.g1:< A G >>` is equal to :g:`$GE$ <0393.g1:< G E >>`, and :g:`$AG$ <0393.g1:< A G >>` is the rectangle :g:`$AC$ <0393.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$CB$ <0393.g1:/ B C />`, for :g:`$GC$ <0393.g1:/ C G />` is equal to :g:`$CB$ <0393.g1:/ B C />`, therefore :g:`$GE$ <0393.g1:< G E >>` is also equal to the rectangle :g:`$AC$ <0393.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$CB$ <0393.g1:/ B C />`.

.. assertion::

   Therefore :g:`$AG$ <0393.g1:< A G >>`, :g:`$GE$ <0393.g1:< G E >>` are equal to twice the rectangle :g:`$AC$ <0393.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$CB$ <0393.g1:/ B C />`.

But the squares :g:`$HF$ <0393.g1:< H F >>`, :g:`$CK$ <0393.g1:< C K >>` are also the squares on :g:`$AC$ <0393.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$CB$ <0393.g1:/ B C />`; therefore the four areas\ [#f3]_ :g:`$HF$ <0393.g1:< H F >>`, :g:`$CK$ <0393.g1:< C K >>`, :g:`$AG$ <0393.g1:< A G >>`, :g:`$GE$ <0393.g1:< G E >>` are equal to the squares on :g:`$AC$ <0393.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$CB$ <0393.g1:/ B C />` and twice the rectangle contained by :g:`$AC$ <0393.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$CB$ <0393.g1:/ B C />`.

But :g:`$HF$ <0393.g1:< H F >>`, :g:`$CK$ <0393.g1:< C K >>`, :g:`$AG$ <0393.g1:< A G >>`, :g:`$GE$ <0393.g1:< G E >>` are the whole :g:`$ADEB$ <0393.g1:< A D E B >>`, which is the square on :g:`$AB$ <0393.g1:/ A B />`.

.. assertion::

   Therefore the square on :g:`$AB$ <0393.g1:/ A B />` is equal to the squares on :g:`$AC$ <0393.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$CB$ <0393.g1:/ B C />` and twice the rectangle contained by :g:`$AC$ <0393.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$CB$ <0393.g1:/ B C />`.

.. qed::

   Therefore etc. :just:`Q. E. D.`

.. [#f1] **twice the rectangle contained by the segments.** By a curious idiom this is in Greek "the rectangle *twice* contained by the segments." Similarly "twice the rectangle contained by :g:`$AC$ <0393.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$CB$ <0393.g1:/ B C />`" is expressed as "the rectangle *twice contained* by :g:`$AC$ <0393.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$CB$ <0393.g1:/ B C />`" (τὸ δὶς ὑπὸ τῶν ΑΓ, ΓΒ περιεχόμενον :term:`ὀρθογώνιον <orthogonion>`).

.. [#f2] **described; the squares** (before "on"). These words are not in the Greek, which simply says that the squares "are on" (εἰσὶν ἀπό) their respective sides.

.. [#f3] **areas.** It is necessary to supply some substantive (the Greek leaves it to be understood); and I prefer "areas" to "figures."
