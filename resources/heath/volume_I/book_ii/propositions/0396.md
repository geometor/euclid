The proposition II. 4 can also be extended to the case where a straight line is divided into any number of segments; for the figure will show in like manner that the square on the whole line is equal to the sum of the squares on all the parts together with twice the rectangles contained by every pair of the parts.

---

### PROPOSITION 5.

<p class="enunciation">If a straight line be cut into equal and unequal segments,<br />
the rectangle contained by the unequal segments of the whole<br />
together with the square on the straight line between the<br />
points of section is equal to the square on the half.</p>

For let a straight line <span data-ref="0396.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> be cut into equal segments at <span data-ref="0396.g1:C">$C$</span> and into unequal segments at <span data-ref="0396.g1:D">$D$</span>;  
I say that the rectangle contained by <span data-ref="0396.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0396.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> together with the square on <span data-ref="0396.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> is equal to the square on <span data-ref="0396.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span>.

![0396.g1](0396.g1.svg)

For let the square <span data-ref="0396.g1:< C E F B >">$CEFB$</span> be described on <span data-ref="0396.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span>, <span class="justification">[I. 46]</span>  
and let <span data-ref="0396.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span> be joined;  
through <span data-ref="0396.g1:D">$D$</span> let <span data-ref="0396.g1:/ D G /">$DG$</span> be drawn parallel to either <span data-ref="0396.g1:/ C E /">$CE$</span> or <span data-ref="0396.g1:/ B F /">$BF$</span>,  
through <span data-ref="0396.g1:H">$H$</span> again let <span data-ref="0396.g1:/ K M /">$KM$</span> be drawn parallel to either <span data-ref="0396.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> or <span data-ref="0396.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>,  
and again through <span data-ref="0396.g1:A">$A$</span> let <span data-ref="0396.g1:/ A K /">$AK$</span> be drawn parallel to either <span data-ref="0396.g1:/ C L /">$CL$</span> or <span data-ref="0396.g1:/ B M /">$BM$</span>. <span class="justification">[I. 31]</span>

Then, since the complement <span data-ref="0396.g1:< C H >">$CH$</span> is equal to the complement <span data-ref="0396.g1:< H F >">$HF$</span>, <span class="justification">[I. 43]</span>  
let <span data-ref="0396.g1:< D M >">$DM$</span> be added to each;  
<p class="assertion">therefore the whole <span data-ref="0396.g1:< C M >">$CM$</span> is equal to the whole <span data-ref="0396.g1:< D F >">$DF$</span>.</p>

But <span data-ref="0396.g1:< C M >">$CM$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0396.g1:< A L >">$AL$</span>,  
<p class="sub-clause"><span class="justification">[I. 36]</span>since <span data-ref="0396.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is also equal to <span data-ref="0396.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span>;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0396.g1:< A L >">$AL$</span> is also equal to <span data-ref="0396.g1:< D F >">$DF$</span>.</p>

Let <span data-ref="0396.g1:< C H >">$CH$</span> be added to each;  
<p class="assertion">therefore the whole <span data-ref="0396.g1:< A H >">$AH$</span> is equal to the gnomon <span data-ref="0396.g1:< N O P >">$NOP$</span>.</p>
