**3. between the points of section,** literally "between the sections," the word being the same (τομή) as that used of a conic *section*.

It will be observed that the gnomon is indicated in the figure by three separate letters and a dotted curve. This is no doubt a clearer way of showing what exactly the gnomon is than the method usual in our text-books. In this particular case the figure of the MSS. has *two* M's in it, the gnomon being ΜΝΞ. I have corrected the lettering to avoid confusion.

It is easily seen that this proposition and the next give exactly the theorem already alluded to under the last propositions, namely that *the difference of the squares on two straight lines is equal to the rectangle contained by their sum and difference*. The two given lines are, in II. 5, the lines <span data-ref="0397.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span> and <span data-ref="0397.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, and their sum and difference are respectively equal to <span data-ref="0397.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> and <span data-ref="0397.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span>. To show that II. 6 gives the same theorem we have only to make <span data-ref="0397.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> the greater line and <span data-ref="0397.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span> the less, i.e. to draw <span data-ref="0397.g1:/ C' D' /">$C'D'$</span> equal to <span data-ref="0397.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span>, measure <span data-ref="0397.g1:/ C' B' /">$C'B'$</span> along it equal to <span data-ref="0397.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, and then produce <span data-ref="0397.g1:/ C' B' /">$B'C'$</span> to <span data-ref="0397.g1:A'">$A'$</span>, making <span data-ref="0397.g1:/ A' C' /">$A'C'$</span> equal to <span data-ref="0397.g1:/ C' B' /">$B'C'$</span>, whence it is immediately clear that <span data-ref="0397.g1:/ A' D' /">$A'D'$</span> on the second line is equal to <span data-ref="0397.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> on the first, while <span data-ref="0397.g1:/ D' B' /">$D'B'$</span> is also equal to <span data-ref="0397.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span>, so that the rectangles <span data-ref="0397.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0397.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> and <span data-ref="0397.g1:/ A' D' /">$A'D'$</span>, <span data-ref="0397.g1:/ D' B' /">$D'B'$</span> are equal, while the difference of the squares on <span data-ref="0397.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span>, <span data-ref="0397.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> is equal to the difference of the squares on <span data-ref="0397.g1:/ C' D' /">$C'D'$</span>, <span data-ref="0397.g1:/ C' B' /">$C'B'$</span>.

#### Geometrical solution of a quadratic equation.

Suppose, in the figure of II. 5, that <span data-ref="0396.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> $= a$, <span data-ref="0396.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> $= x$;  
then
<p class="assertion">$ax - x^2 =$ the rectangle <span data-ref="0396.g1:< A H >">$AH$</span><br />
$=$ the gnomon <span data-ref="0396.g1:< N O P >">$NOP$</span>.</p>
Thus, if the area of the gnomon is given ($= b^2$, say), and if $a$ is given ($=$ <span data-ref="0396.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>), the problem of solving the equation
\[
ax - x^2 = b^2
\]
is, in the language of geometry, *To a given straight line ($a$) to apply a rectangle which shall be equal to a given square ($b^2$) and shall fall short by a square figure*, i.e. to construct the rectangle <span data-ref="0396.g1:< A H >">$AH$</span> or the gnomon <span data-ref="0396.g1:< N O P >">$NOP$</span>.
