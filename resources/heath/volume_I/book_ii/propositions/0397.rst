It is easily seen that this proposition and the next give exactly the theorem already alluded to under the last propositions, namely that *the difference of the squares on two straight lines is equal to the rectangle contained by their sum and difference*. The two given lines are, in II. 5, the lines :g:`$CB$ <0397.g1:/ C B />` and :g:`$CD$ <0397.g1:/ C D />`, and their sum and difference are respectively equal to :g:`$AD$ <0397.g1:/ A D />` and :g:`$DB$ <0397.g1:/ D B />`. To show that II. 6 gives the same theorem we have only to make :g:`$CD$ <0397.g1:/ C D />` the greater line and :g:`$CB$ <0397.g1:/ C B />` the less, i.e. to draw :g:`$C'D'$ <0397.g1:/ C' D' />` :eq:`equal <0397.g1:/ C' D' / = 0397.g1:/ C B />` to :g:`$CB$ <0397.g1:/ C B />`, measure :g:`$C'B'$ <0397.g1:/ C' B' />` along it :eq:`equal <0397.g1:/ C' B' / = 0397.g1:/ C D />` to :g:`$CD$ <0397.g1:/ C D />`, and then produce :g:`$B'C'$ <0397.g1:/ C' B' />` to :g:`$A'$ <0397.g1:A'>`, making :g:`$A'C'$ <0397.g1:/ A' C' />` :eq:`equal <0397.g1:/ A' C' / = 0397.g1:/ C' B' />` to :g:`$B'C'$ <0397.g1:/ C' B' />`, whence it is immediately clear that :g:`$A'D'$ <0397.g1:/ A' D' />` on the second line is :eq:`equal <0397.g1:/ A' D' / = 0397.g1:/ A D />` to :g:`$AD$ <0397.g1:/ A D />` on the first, while :g:`$D'B'$ <0397.g1:/ D' B' />` is also :eq:`equal <0397.g1:/ D' B' / = 0397.g1:/ D B />` to :g:`$DB$ <0397.g1:/ D B />`, so that the rectangles :g:`$AD$ <0397.g1:/ A D />`, :g:`$DB$ <0397.g1:/ D B />` and :g:`$A'D'$ <0397.g1:/ A' D' />`, :g:`$D'B'$ <0397.g1:/ D' B' />` are equal, while the difference of the squares on :g:`$CB$ <0397.g1:/ C B />`, :g:`$CD$ <0397.g1:/ C D />` is equal to the difference of the squares on :g:`$C'D'$ <0397.g1:/ C' D' />`, :g:`$C'B'$ <0397.g1:/ C' B' />`.

Perhaps the most important fact about II. 5, 6 is however their bearing on the

Geometrical solution of a quadratic equation.
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Suppose, in the figure of II. 5, that :g:`$AB$ <0396.g1:/ A B />` $= a$, :g:`$DB$ <0396.g1:/ D B />` $= x$; then

.. assertion::

   | $ax - x^2 =$ the rectangle :g:`$AH$ <0396.g1:< A H >>`
   | $=$ the gnomon :g:`$NOP$ <0396.g1:< N O P >>`.

Thus, if the area of the gnomon is given ($= b^2$, say), and if $a$ is given ($=$ :g:`$AB$ <0396.g1:/ A B />`), the problem of solving the equation
\[
ax - x^2 = b^2
\]
is, in the language of geometry, *To a given straight line ($a$) to apply a rectangle which shall be equal to a given square ($b^2$) and shall fall short by a square figure*, i.e. to construct the rectangle :g:`$AH$ <0396.g1:< A H >>` or the gnomon :g:`$NOP$ <0396.g1:< N O P >>`.
