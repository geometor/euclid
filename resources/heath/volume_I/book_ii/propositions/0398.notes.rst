Commentary Highlights
---------------------

- **Pythagorean Origin**: Quotes Proclus on the Pythagorean discovery of the application of areas (*parabole*), exceeding (*hyperbole*), and falling-short (*ellipsis*).
- **Simson's Construction**: Solves $ax - x^2 = b^2$ geometrically by constructing right triangle $OCD$ with hypotenuse $OD = a/2$ and leg $OC = b$, giving root $DB = x = a/2 - \sqrt{(a/2)^2 - b^2}$.
- **Condition of Possibility (Discriminant)**: The real root condition $b^2 \le (a/2)^2$ corresponds directly to Euclid II. 5, where the rectangle $AD \cdot DB$ attains its maximum when $D$ coincides with midpoint $C$, yielding $(a/2)^2$.
- **Two Real Solutions**: Explains why Greek geometry naturally focuses on one root ($x = BD$), while the conjugate root ($x = BE = a - x$) represents the complementary segment of the same symmetric partition $x + y = a, xy = b^2$.
