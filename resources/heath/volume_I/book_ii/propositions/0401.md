*Aristot. Phys.* pp. 61—68, ed. Diels) from Eudemus' *History of Geometry*. In this fragment Hippocrates (5th cent. B.C.) assumes the following construction.

<span data-ref="0401.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> being the diameter and <span data-ref="0401.g1:O">$O$</span> the centre of a semicircle, and <span data-ref="0401.g1:C">$C$</span> being the middle point of <span data-ref="0401.g1:/ O B /">$OB$</span> and <span data-ref="0401.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> at right angles to <span data-ref="0401.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, a straight line of length such that its square is $1\frac{1}{2}$ times the square on the radius (i.e. of length $a\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}$, where $a$ is the radius) is to be so placed, as <span data-ref="0401.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, between <span data-ref="0401.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> and the circumference <span data-ref="0401.g1:( O A )">$AD$</span> that it "verges towards <span data-ref="0401.g1:B">$B$</span>," that is, <span data-ref="0401.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> when produced passes through <span data-ref="0401.g1:B">$B$</span>.

![Diagram](0401.g1.svg)

Now the right-angled triangles <span data-ref="0401.g1:< B F C >">$BFC$</span>, <span data-ref="0401.g1:< B A E >">$BAE$</span> are similar, so that
$$BF : BC = BA : BE,$$
and therefore the rectangle $BE, BF = \text{rect. } BA, BC$
$$= \text{sq. on } BO.$$

In other words, <span data-ref="0401.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> ($= a\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}$) being given in length, <span data-ref="0401.g1:/ B F /">$BF$</span> ($= x$, say) has to be found such that
$$\left(\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}a + x\right)x = a^2;$$
or the quadratic equation
$$\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}ax + x^2 = a^2$$
has to be solved.

A straight line of length $a\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}$ would easily be constructed, for, in the figure, $CD^2 = AC \cdot CB = \frac{3}{4}a^2$, or $CD = \frac{1}{2}a\sqrt{3}$, and $a\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}$ is the diagonal of a square of which <span data-ref="0401.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> is the side.

There is no doubt that Hippocrates could have solved the equation by the geometrical construction given below, but he may have contemplated, on this occasion, the merely *mechanical* process of *placing* the straight line of the length required between <span data-ref="0401.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> and the circumference <span data-ref="0401.g1:( O A )">$AD$</span> and *moving* it until <span data-ref="0401.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0401.g1:F">$F$</span>, <span data-ref="0401.g1:B">$B$</span> were in a straight line. Zeuthen (*Die Lehre von den Kegelschnitten im Altertum*, pp. 270, 271) thinks this probable because, curiously enough, the fragment speaks immediately afterwards of "joining <span data-ref="0401.g1:B">$B$</span> to <span data-ref="0401.g1:F">$F$</span>."

To solve the equation
$$ax + x^2 = b^2$$
we have to find the rectangle <span data-ref="0401.g2:< A H >">$AH$</span>, or the gnomon <span data-ref="0401.g2:< N O P >">$NOP$</span>, which is equal in area to $b^2$ and has one of the sides containing the inner right angle equal to <span data-ref="0401.g2:/ C B /">$CB$</span> or $\frac{1}{2}a$. Thus we know $\left(\frac{1}{2}a\right)^2$ and $b^2$, and we have to find, by I. 47, a square equal to the sum of two given squares.

![Diagram](0401.g2.svg)

To do this Simson draws <span data-ref="0401.g2:/ B Q /">$BQ$</span> at right angles to <span data-ref="0401.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span> and equal to $b$, joins <span data-ref="0401.g2:/ C Q /">$CQ$</span> and, with centre <span data-ref="0401.g2:C">$C$</span> and radius <span data-ref="0401.g2:/ C Q /">$CQ$</span>, describes a circle cutting <span data-ref="0401.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span> produced in <span data-ref="0401.g2:D">$D$</span>. Thus <span data-ref="0401.g2:/ B D /">$BD$</span>, or $x$, is found.

Now the rectangle <span data-ref="0401.g2:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0401.g2:/ D B /">$DB$</span> together with the square on <span data-ref="0401.g2:/ C B /">$CB$</span>
is equal to the square on <span data-ref="0401.g2:/ C D /">$CD$</span>,
i.e. to the square on <span data-ref="0401.g2:/ C Q /">$CQ$</span>,
i.e. to the squares on <span data-ref="0401.g2:/ C B /">$CB$</span>, <span data-ref="0401.g2:/ B Q /">$BQ$</span>.
