## Commentary Highlights
- **Hippocrates' Lunes & Eudemus**: Cites the 5th-century B.C. fragment of Hippocrates preserved by Simplicius from Eudemus' *History of Geometry*, showing that the quadratic equation $\sqrt{3/2}ax + x^2 = a^2$ arose in early attempts to square the circle via lunes.
- **Simson's Geometric Construction**: Solves $ax + x^2 = b^2$ using right triangle $CBQ$ with legs $CB = a/2$ and $BQ = b$. By Pythagoras (I. 47), hypotenuse $CQ = \sqrt{(a/2)^2 + b^2}$. Describing a circle with centre $C$ and radius $CQ$ cuts line $AB$ produced at $D$, yielding segment $BD = x = \sqrt{(a/2)^2 + b^2} - a/2$.
