Therefore the rectangle <span data-ref="0401.g2:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0401.g2:/ D B /">$DB$</span> is equal to the square on <span data-ref="0401.g2:/ B Q /">$BQ$</span>, that is,
\[
ax + x^2 = b^2.
\]
From Euclid's point of view there would only be one solution in this case.

This proposition enables us also to solve the equation
\[
x^2 - ax = b^2
\]
in a similar manner.

We have only to suppose that $AB = a$, and <span data-ref="0401.g2:/ A D /">$AD$</span> (instead of <span data-ref="0401.g2:/ B D /">$BD$</span>) $= x$; then
\[
x^2 - ax = \text{the gnomon.}
\]
To find the gnomon we have its area ($b^2$) and the area, $CB^2$ or $\left(\frac{1}{2}a\right)^2$, by which the gnomon differs from $CD^2$. Thus we can find <span data-ref="0401.g2:D">$D$</span> (and therefore <span data-ref="0401.g2:/ A D /">$AD$</span> or $x$) by the same construction as that just given.

Converse propositions to II. 5, 6 are given by Pappus (VII. pp. 948—950) among his lemmas to the *Conics* of Apollonius to the effect that,
1. if <span data-ref="0400.g1:D">$D$</span> be a point dividing <span data-ref="0400.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> unequally, and <span data-ref="0400.g1:C">$C$</span> another point on <span data-ref="0400.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> such that the rectangle <span data-ref="0400.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0400.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> together with the square on <span data-ref="0400.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> is equal to the square on <span data-ref="0400.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, then
\[
AC \text{ is equal to } CB;
\]
2. if <span data-ref="0400.g1:D">$D$</span> be a point on <span data-ref="0400.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> produced, and <span data-ref="0400.g1:C">$C$</span> a point on <span data-ref="0400.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> such that the rectangle <span data-ref="0400.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0400.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> together with the square on <span data-ref="0400.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span> is equal to the square on <span data-ref="0400.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, then
\[

### PROPOSITION 7.

<p class="enunciation">If a straight line be cut at random, the square on the<br />
whole and that on one of the segments both together are equal<br />
to twice the rectangle contained by the whole and the said<br />
segment and the square on the remaining segment.</p>

For let a straight line <span data-ref="0402.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> be cut at random at the point <span data-ref="0402.g1:C">$C$</span>;  
I say that the squares on <span data-ref="0402.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0402.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> are equal to twice the rectangle contained by <span data-ref="0402.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0402.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> and the square on <span data-ref="0402.g1:/ C A /">$CA$</span>.

![Diagram](0402.g1.svg)

For let the square <span data-ref="0402.g1:&lt; A D E B &gt;">$ADEB$</span> be described on <span data-ref="0402.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span class="justification">[I. 46]</span>  
and let the figure be drawn.

Then, since <span data-ref="0402.g1:&lt; A G &gt;">$AG$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0402.g1:&lt; G E &gt;">$GE$</span>, <span class="justification">[I. 43]</span>  
let <span data-ref="0402.g1:&lt; C F &gt;">$CF$</span> be added to each;  
<p class="assertion">therefore the whole <span data-ref="0402.g1:&lt; A F &gt;">$AF$</span> is equal to the whole <span data-ref="0402.g1:&lt; C E &gt;">$CE$</span>.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore <span data-ref="0402.g1:&lt; A F &gt;">$AF$</span>, <span data-ref="0402.g1:&lt; C E &gt;">$CE$</span> are double of <span data-ref="0402.g1:&lt; A F &gt;">$AF$</span>.</p>

But <span data-ref="0402.g1:&lt; A F &gt;">$AF$</span>, <span data-ref="0402.g1:&lt; C E &gt;">$CE$</span> are the gnomon <span data-ref="0402.g1:&lt; K L M &gt;">$KLM$</span> and the square <span data-ref="0402.g1:&lt; C F &gt;">$CF$</span>;  
<p class="assertion">therefore the gnomon <span data-ref="0402.g1:&lt; K L M &gt;">$KLM$</span> and the square <span data-ref="0402.g1:&lt; C F &gt;">$CF$</span> are double of <span data-ref="0402.g1:&lt; A F &gt;">$AF$</span>.</p>
