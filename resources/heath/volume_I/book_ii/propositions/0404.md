For let [the straight line] $BD$ be produced in a straight line [with $AB$], and let $BD$ be made equal to $CB$;  
let the square $AEFD$ be described on $AD$, and let the figure be drawn double.

![Diagram](0404.g1.svg)

Then, since $CB$ is equal to $BD$, while $CB$ is equal to $GK$, and $BD$ to $KN$,  
therefore $GK$ is also equal to $KN$.

For the same reason  
<p class="assertion">$QR$ is also equal to $RP$.</p>

And, since $BC$ is equal to $BD$, and $GK$ to $KN$,  
therefore $CK$ is also equal to $KD$, and $GR$ to $RN$. <span class="justification">[I. 36]</span>

But $CK$ is equal to $RN$, for they are complements of the parallelogram $CP$; <span class="justification">[I. 43]</span>  
therefore $KD$ is also equal to $GR$;  
therefore the four areas $DK$, $CK$, $GR$, $RN$ are equal to one another.

<p class="assertion">Therefore the four are quadruple of $CK$.</p>

Again, since $CB$ is equal to $BD$,  
while $BD$ is equal to $BK$, that is $CG$,  
and $CB$ is equal to $GK$, that is $GQ$,  
<p class="assertion">therefore $CG$ is also equal to $GQ$.</p>

And, since $CG$ is equal to $GQ$, and $QR$ to $RP$,  
$AG$ is also equal to $MQ$, and $QL$ to $RF$. <span class="justification">[I. 36]</span>

But $MQ$ is equal to $QL$, for they are complements of the parallelogram $ML$; <span class="justification">[I. 43]</span>  
therefore $AG$ is also equal to $RF$;  
therefore the four areas $AG$, $MQ$, $QL$, $RF$ are equal to one another.

<p class="assertion">Therefore the four are quadruple of $AG$.</p>

But the four areas $CK$, $KD$, $GR$, $RN$ were proved to be quadruple of $CK$;  
<p class="assertion">therefore the eight areas, which contain the gnomon $STU$, are quadruple of $AK$.</p>
