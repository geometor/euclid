Now, since $AC$ is equal to $CE$,  
the square on $AC$ is also equal to the square on $CE$;  
<p class="assertion">therefore the squares on $AC$, $CE$ are double of the square on $AC$.</p>

But the square on $EA$ is equal to the squares on $AC$, $CE$, for the angle $ACE$ is right; <span class="justification">[I. 47]</span>  
<p class="assertion">therefore the square on $EA$ is double of the square on $AC$.</p>

Again, since $EG$ is equal to $GF$,  
the square on $EG$ is also equal to the square on $GF$;  
<p class="assertion">therefore the squares on $EG$, $GF$ are double of the square on $GF$.</p>

But the square on $EF$ is equal to the squares on $EG$, $GF$;  
<p class="assertion">therefore the square on $EF$ is double of the square on $GF$.</p>

But $GF$ is equal to $CD$; <span class="justification">[I. 34]</span>  
<p class="assertion">therefore the square on $EF$ is double of the square on $CD$.</p>

But the square on $EA$ is also double of the square on $AC$;  
<p class="assertion">therefore the squares on $AE$, $EF$ are double of the squares on $AC$, $CD$.</p>

And the square on $AF$ is equal to the squares on $AE$, $EF$, for the angle $AEF$ is right; <span class="justification">[I. 47]</span>  
<p class="assertion">therefore the square on $AF$ is double of the squares on $AC$, $CD$.</p>

But the squares on $AD$, $DF$ are equal to the square on $AF$, for the angle at $D$ is right; <span class="justification">[I. 47]</span>  
<p class="assertion">therefore the squares on $AD$, $DF$ are double of the squares on $AC$, $CD$.</p>
