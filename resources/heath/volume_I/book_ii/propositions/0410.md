the angles $CEF$, $EFD$ are equal to two right angles; <span class="justification">[I. 29]</span>  
therefore the angles $FEB$, $EFD$ are less than two right angles.

But straight lines produced from angles less than two right angles meet; <span class="justification">[I. Post. 5]</span>  
therefore $EB$, $FD$, if produced in the direction $B$, $D$, will meet.

Let them be produced and meet at $G$,  
and let $AG$ be joined.

Then, since $AC$ is equal to $CE$,  
the angle $EAC$ is also equal to the angle $AEC$; <span class="justification">[I. 5]</span>  
and the angle at $C$ is right;  
<p class="assertion">therefore each of the angles $EAC$, $AEC$ is half a right angle. <span class="justification">[I. 32]</span></p>

For the same reason  
each of the angles $CEB$, $EBC$ is also half a right angle;  
<p class="assertion">therefore the angle $AEB$ is right.</p>

And, since the angle $EBC$ is half a right angle,  
the angle $DBG$ is also half a right angle. <span class="justification">[I. 15]</span>

But the angle $BDG$ is also right,  
for it is equal to the angle $DCE$, they being alternate; <span class="justification">[I. 29]</span>  
<p class="sub-clause">therefore the remaining angle $DGB$ is half a right angle; <span class="justification">[I. 32]</span></p>
therefore the angle $DGB$ is equal to the angle $DBG$,  
<p class="assertion">so that the side $BD$ is also equal to the side $GD$. <span class="justification">[I. 6]</span></p>

Again, since the angle $EGF$ is half a right angle,  
and the angle at $F$ is right, for it is equal to the opposite angle, the angle at $C$, <span class="justification">[I. 34]</span>  
<p class="sub-clause">the remaining angle $FEG$ is half a right angle; <span class="justification">[I. 32]</span></p>
therefore the angle $EGF$ is equal to the angle $FEG$,  
<p class="assertion">so that the side $GF$ is also equal to the side $EF$. <span class="justification">[I. 6]</span></p>

Now, since the square on $EC$ is equal to the square on $CA$,  
the squares on $EC$, $CA$ are double of the square on $CA$.

But the square on $EA$ is equal to the squares on $EC$, $CA$; <span class="justification">[I. 47]</span>  
<p class="assertion">therefore the square on $EA$ is double of the square on $AC$. <span class="justification">[C. N. 1]</span></p>
