Again, since <span data-ref="0409.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0409.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>,  
the square on <span data-ref="0409.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span> is also equal to the square on <span data-ref="0409.g1:/ F E /">$FE$</span>;  
<p class="assertion">therefore the squares on <span data-ref="0409.g1:/ G F /">$GF$</span>, <span data-ref="0409.g1:/ F E /">$FE$</span> are double of the square on <span data-ref="0409.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>.</p>

But the square on <span data-ref="0409.g1:/ E G /">$EG$</span> is equal to the squares on <span data-ref="0409.g1:/ G F /">$GF$</span>, <span data-ref="0409.g1:/ F E /">$FE$</span>; <span class="justification">[I. 47]</span>  
<p class="assertion">therefore the square on <span data-ref="0409.g1:/ E G /">$EG$</span> is double of the square on <span data-ref="0409.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>.</p>

And <span data-ref="0409.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0409.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>; <span class="justification">[I. 34]</span>  
<p class="assertion">therefore the square on <span data-ref="0409.g1:/ E G /">$EG$</span> is double of the square on <span data-ref="0409.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>.</p>

But the square on <span data-ref="0409.g1:/ E A /">$EA$</span> was also proved double of the square on <span data-ref="0409.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>;  
<p class="assertion">therefore the squares on <span data-ref="0409.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span>, <span data-ref="0409.g1:/ E G /">$EG$</span> are double of the squares on <span data-ref="0409.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0409.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>.</p>

And the square on <span data-ref="0409.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span> is equal to the squares on <span data-ref="0409.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span>, <span data-ref="0409.g1:/ E G /">$EG$</span>; <span class="justification">[I. 47]</span>  
<p class="assertion">therefore the square on <span data-ref="0409.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span> is double of the squares on <span data-ref="0409.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0409.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>.</p>

But the squares on <span data-ref="0409.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0409.g1:/ D G /">$DG$</span> are equal to the square on <span data-ref="0409.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>; <span class="justification">[I. 47]</span>  
<p class="assertion">therefore the squares on <span data-ref="0409.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0409.g1:/ D G /">$DG$</span> are double of the squares on <span data-ref="0409.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0409.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>.</p>

And <span data-ref="0409.g1:/ D G /">$DG$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0409.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span>;  
<p class="assertion">therefore the squares on <span data-ref="0409.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0409.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> are double of the squares on <span data-ref="0409.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0409.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>.</p>

<p class="qed">Therefore etc. <span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

The alternative proof of this proposition by means of the principles exhibited in II. 1—8 follows the lines of that which I have given for the preceding proposition.

![0411.g1](0411.g1.svg)

It is at once obvious from the figure that the square on <span data-ref="0411.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> includes within it twice the square on <span data-ref="0411.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> together with once the square on <span data-ref="0411.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>. What is left over is the sum of the rectangles <span data-ref="0411.g1:< A H >">$AH$</span>, <span data-ref="0411.g1:< K E >">$KE$</span>. These, which are equivalent to <span data-ref="0411.g1:< B H >">$BH$</span>, <span data-ref="0411.g1:< G K >">$GK$</span>, make up the square on <span data-ref="0411.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> *less* the square on <span data-ref="0411.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>. Adding therefore the square <span data-ref="0411.g1:< B G >">$BG$</span> to each side, we have the required result.

Another alternative proof of the theorem which includes both II. 9 and 10 is worth giving. The theorem states that *the sum of the squares on the sum and difference of two given straight lines is equal to twice the sum of the squares on the lines*.
