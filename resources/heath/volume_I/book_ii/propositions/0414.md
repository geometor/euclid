Proclus further says (p. 27, 16—22) that the property of the *side-* and *diagonal-*numbers "is proved graphically (γραμμικῶς) in the second book of the Elements by *'him'* (ἀπ’ ἐκείνου). *For, if a straight line be bisected and a straight line be added to it, the square on the whole line including the added straight line and the square on the latter alone are double of the square on the half of the original straight line and of the square on the straight line made up of the half and of the added straight line.*" And this is simply Eucl. II. 10. Proclus then goes on to show specifically how this proposition was used to prove that, with the notation above used, the *diameter* corresponding to the *side* $a + d$ is $2a + d$. Let <span data-ref="0414.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> be a *side* and <span data-ref="0414.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> equal to it, while <span data-ref="0414.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> is the *diameter* corresponding to <span data-ref="0414.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, i.e. a straight line such that the square on it is double of the square on <span data-ref="0414.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>. (I use the figure supplied by Hultsch on p. 397 of Kroll's Vol. II.)

![0414.g1](0414.g1.svg)

Then, by the theorem of Eucl. II. 10, the squares on <span data-ref="0414.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0414.g1:/ D C /">$DC$</span> are double of the squares on <span data-ref="0414.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0414.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>.

But the square on <span data-ref="0414.g1:/ D C /">$DC$</span> (i.e. <span data-ref="0414.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span>) is double of the square on <span data-ref="0414.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>; therefore, by subtraction, the square on <span data-ref="0414.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> is double of the square on <span data-ref="0414.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>.

And the square on <span data-ref="0414.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span>, the *diagonal* corresponding to the *side* <span data-ref="0414.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>, is double the square of <span data-ref="0414.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>.

Therefore the square on <span data-ref="0414.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span> is equal to the square on <span data-ref="0414.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, so that <span data-ref="0414.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0414.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>.

That is, while the *side* <span data-ref="0414.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> is, with our notation, $a + d$, the corresponding *diagonal*, being equal to <span data-ref="0414.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, is $2a + d$.

In the above reference by Proclus to II. 10 ἀπ’ ἐκείνου "by *him*" must apparently mean ὑπ’ Εὐκλείδου, "by Euclid," although Euclid's name has not been mentioned in the chapter; the phrase would be equivalent to saying "in the second Book of the famous Elements." But, when Proclus says "this *is proved* in the second Book of the Elements," he does not imply that it had not been proved before; on the contrary, it is clear that the theorem had been proved by the Pythagoreans, and we have therefore here a confirmation of the inference from the part played by the *gnomon* and by I. 47 in Book II. that the whole of the substance of that Book was Pythagorean. For further detailed explanation of the passages of Proclus reference should be made to Hultsch's note in Kroll's Vol. II. pp. 393—400, and to the separate article, also by Hultsch, in the *Bibliotheca Mathematica* $\text{I}_3$, 1900, pp. 8—12.
