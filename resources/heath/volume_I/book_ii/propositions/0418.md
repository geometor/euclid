<p class="enunciation">perpendicular falls, and the straight line cut off outside by the<br />perpendicular towards the obtuse angle.</p>

Let <span data-ref="0418.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> be an obtuse-angled triangle having the angle <span data-ref="0418.g1:< B A C )">$BAC$</span> obtuse, and let <span data-ref="0418.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> be drawn from the point <span data-ref="0418.g1:B">$B$</span> perpendicular to <span data-ref="0418.g1:[ C A ]">$CA$</span> produced;  
I say that the square on <span data-ref="0418.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is greater than the squares on <span data-ref="0418.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span>, <span data-ref="0418.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> by twice the rectangle contained by <span data-ref="0418.g1:/ C A /">$CA$</span>, <span data-ref="0418.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>.

![0418.g1](0418.g1.svg)

For, since the straight line <span data-ref="0418.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> has been cut at random at the point <span data-ref="0418.g1:A">$A$</span>,  
the square on <span data-ref="0418.g1:/ D C /">$DC$</span> is equal to the squares on <span data-ref="0418.g1:/ C A /">$CA$</span>, <span data-ref="0418.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> and twice the rectangle contained by <span data-ref="0418.g1:/ C A /">$CA$</span>, <span data-ref="0418.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>. <span class="justification">[II. 4]</span>

Let the square on <span data-ref="0418.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> be added to each;  
<p class="assertion">therefore the squares on <span data-ref="0418.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, <span data-ref="0418.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> are equal to the squares on <span data-ref="0418.g1:/ C A /">$CA$</span>, <span data-ref="0418.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0418.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> and twice the rectangle <span data-ref="0418.g1:/ C A /">$CA$</span>, <span data-ref="0418.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>.</p>

But the square on <span data-ref="0418.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span> is equal to the squares on <span data-ref="0418.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, <span data-ref="0418.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span>, for the angle at <span data-ref="0418.g1:< C D B )">$D$</span> is right; <span class="justification">[I. 47]</span>  
and the square on <span data-ref="0418.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is equal to the squares on <span data-ref="0418.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0418.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span>; <span class="justification">[I. 47]</span>  
<p class="assertion">therefore the square on <span data-ref="0418.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span> is equal to the squares on <span data-ref="0418.g1:/ C A /">$CA$</span>, <span data-ref="0418.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> and twice the rectangle contained by <span data-ref="0418.g1:/ C A /">$CA$</span>, <span data-ref="0418.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>;</p>

<p class="assertion">so that the square on <span data-ref="0418.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span> is greater than the squares on <span data-ref="0418.g1:/ C A /">$CA$</span>, <span data-ref="0418.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> by twice the rectangle contained by <span data-ref="0418.g1:/ C A /">$CA$</span>, <span data-ref="0418.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>.</p>

<p class="qed">Therefore etc. <span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

Since in this proposition and the next we have to do with the squares on the sides of triangles, the particular form of graphic representation of areas which we have had in Book II. up to this point does not help us to visualise the results of the propositions in the same way, and only two lines of proof are possible, (1) by means of the *results* of certain earlier propositions in Book II. combined with the *result* of I. 47 and (2) by means of the procedure in Euclid's proof of I. 47 itself. The alternative proofs of II. 12, 13 after the manner of Euclid's proof of I. 47 are therefore alone worth giving.

These proofs appear in certain modern text-books (e.g. Mehler, Henrici and Treutlein, H. M. Taylor, Smith and Bryant). Smith and Bryant are not correct in saying (p. 142) that they cannot be traced further back than Lardner's Euclid (1828); they are to be found in Gregory of St. Vincent's work (published in 1647) *Opus geometricum quadraturae circuli et sectionum coni*, Book I. Pt. 2, Props. 44, 45 (pp. 31, 32).
