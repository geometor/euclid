Textual, Lexical & Historical Observations
------------------------------------------

- **Pythagorean Construction of the Regular Pentagon (Eucl. II. 11 & IV. 10, 11)**: Heath defends the testimony of Scholia IV. Nos. 2 and 4 attributing the whole of Book IV—and hence the golden-section construction of the isosceles triangle with base angles double the vertical angle (IV. 10) and the regular pentagon (IV. 11), resting on extreme and mean ratio (II. 11)—to the Pythagoreans.
- **Oenopides of Chios and Ruler-and-Compasses Restriction**: Heath explains why Oenopides (c. 470–450 B.C.) is credited by Eudemus/Proclus with the elementary constructions of Eucl. I. 12 (dropping a perpendicular, which he called *kata gnomona*) and Eucl. I. 23 (copying an angle): not because geometry was primitive, but because Oenopides formalized the canon restricting plane constructions to straightedge and compasses instead of mechanical tools like the set square (gnomon).
