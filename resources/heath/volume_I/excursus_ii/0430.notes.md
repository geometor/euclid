## Textual, Lexical & Historical Observations
- **16th-Century Logical *Pons Asinorum***: Notes the origin of the phrase in 16th-century scholastic logic (following Petrus Tartaretus / Johann Sturm) for the diagram used to find middle terms of syllogisms.
- **1925 *Punch* Allusion**: Quotes the 14 October 1925 issue of *Punch*, which helps date the composition of Heath's second-edition Excursus to late 1925.
- ***Elefuga* / *Eleufuga* (Eucl. I. 5)**:
  - Quotes Roger Bacon's *Opus Tertium*, c. vi (*"Elefuga, id est, fuga miserorum; elegia enim Graece dicitur, Latine miseria; et elegi sunt miseri"*) and Chaucer's *Troilus and Criseyde*, III, ll. 933–5 (*"Dulcarnon called is 'fleminge of wrecches'"*), where Chaucer conflates *Dulcarnon* (I. 47) with *Elefuga* (I. 5).
  - Discusses W. W. Skeat's etymology combining Greek ἔλεος ("pity/misery", punning on *Elementa*) + φυγή / Latin *fuga* ("flight"), alongside the variant *Eleufuga* in Alanus de Insulis's *Anticlaudianus* (III, c. 6) and Richard de Bury's *Philobiblon* (c. xiii, rendered "Helleflight" by J. B. Inglis in 1832).
