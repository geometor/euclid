Textual, Lexical & Historical Observations
------------------------------------------

- *The Theorem of the Bride* **(θεώρημα τῆς νύμφης) &** *Nuptial Figure* **(γαμήλιον διάγραμμα**):

  - Traces the name found in Georgius Pachymeres (1242–1310; noted by Tannery, *La Géométrie grecque*, p. 105) back to Plutarch (*De Iside et Osiride* 56, p. 373 F), Nicomachus (*Introd. Ar.* II, 24, 11), and Iamblichus (*In Nicom.*, p. 82, 20 Pistelli), connecting the $(3, 4, 5)$ right-angled triangle with Plato's Geometrical Number in *Republic* VIII, 546 B–D.
  - Also notes the Arabic adoption of "Figure of the Bride" (*shakl al-'arūs*) in Bahā' al-Dīn al-'Āmilī (Behā-ad-dīn, 1547–1622).

- *The "Bride's Chair"* (*Chaise de la petite mariée*):

  - Cites D. E. Smith (*History of Mathematics*, II, pp. 289–90) and Édouard Lucas (*Récréations Mathématiques*, II, p. 130) regarding the Hindu demonstration associated with Bhāskara's *Līlāvatī* / *Vījagaṇita* (discussed on the next page). Note that the printed text preserves the spelling ``essentialement`` in the quotation from Lucas.
