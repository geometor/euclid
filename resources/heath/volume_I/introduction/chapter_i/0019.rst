Equally remarkable are the Arabian accounts of the relation of :person:`Euclid <euclid>` and :person:`Apollonius <apollonius>`\ [#f1]_. According to them the *Elements* were originally written, not by :person:`Euclid <euclid>`, but by a man whose name was :person:`Apollonius <apollonius>`, a carpenter, who wrote the work in 15 books or sections\ [#f2]_. In the course of time some of the work was lost and the rest became disarranged, so that one of the kings at Alexandria who desired to study geometry and to master this treatise in particular first questioned about it certain learned men who visited him and then sent for :person:`Euclid <euclid>` who was at that time famous as a geometer, and asked him to revise and complete the work and reduce it to order. :person:`Euclid <euclid>` then re-wrote it in 13 books which were thereafter known by his name. (According to another version :person:`Euclid <euclid>` composed the 13 books out of commentaries which he had published on two books of :person:`Apollonius <apollonius>` on conics and out of introductory matter added to the doctrine of the five regular solids.) To the thirteen books were added two more books, the work of others (though some attribute these also to :person:`Euclid <euclid>`) which contain several things not mentioned by :person:`Apollonius <apollonius>`. According to another version Hypsicles, a pupil of :person:`Euclid <euclid>` at Alexandria, offered to the king and published Books XIV. and XV., it being also stated that Hypsicles had "discovered" the books, by which it appears to be suggested that Hypsicles had edited them from materials left by :person:`Euclid <euclid>`.

We observe here the correct statement that Books XIV. and XV. were not written by :person:`Euclid <euclid>`, but along with it the incorrect information that Hypsicles, the author of Book XIV., wrote Book XV. also.

The whole of the fable about :person:`Apollonius <apollonius>` having preceded :person:`Euclid <euclid>` and having written the *Elements* appears to have been evolved out of the preface to Book XIV. by Hypsicles, and in this way; the Book must in early times have been attributed to :person:`Euclid <euclid>`, and the inference based upon this assumption was left uncorrected afterwards when it was recognised that Hypsicles was the author.
