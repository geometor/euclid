6. *The Conics.*

:person:`Pappus <pappus>` says of this lost work: "The four books of Euclid's Conics were completed by :person:`Apollonius <apollonius>`, who added four more and gave us eight books of Conics\ [#f3]_." It is probable that Euclid's work was lost even by Pappus' time, for he goes on to speak of ":person:`Aristaeus <aristaeus>`, who wrote the *still extant* five books of *Solid Loci* connected with the conics." Speaking of the relation of Euclid's work to that of :person:`Aristaeus <aristaeus>` on conics regarded as loci, :person:`Pappus <pappus>` says in a later passage (bracketed however by :person:`Hultsch <hultsch>`) that :person:`Euclid <euclid>`, regarding :person:`Aristaeus <aristaeus>` as deserving credit for the discoveries he had already made in conics, did not (try to) anticipate him or construct anew the same system. We may no doubt conclude that the book by :person:`Aristaeus <aristaeus>` on solid loci preceded Euclid's on conics and was, at least in point of originality, more important. Though both treatises dealt with the same subject-matter, the object and the point of view were different; had they been the same, :person:`Euclid <euclid>` could scarcely have refrained, as :person:`Pappus <pappus>` says he did, from attempting to improve upon the earlier treatise. No doubt :person:`Euclid <euclid>` wrote on the general theory of conics as :person:`Apollonius <apollonius>` did, but confined himself to those properties which were necessary for the analysis of the *Solid Loci* of :person:`Aristaeus <aristaeus>`. The *Conics* of :person:`Euclid <euclid>` were evidently superseded by the treatise of :person:`Apollonius <apollonius>`.

As regards the contents of Euclid's *Conics*, the most important source of our information is :person:`Archimedes <archimedes>`, who frequently refers to propositions in conics as well known and not needing proof, adding in three cases that they are proved in the "elements of conics" or in "the conics," which expressions must clearly refer to the works of :person:`Aristaeus <aristaeus>` and :person:`Euclid <euclid>`\ [#f4]_.

:person:`Euclid <euclid>` still used the old names for the conics (sections of a right-angled, acute-angled, or obtuse-angled cone), but he was aware that an ellipse could be obtained by cutting a cone in any manner by a plane not parallel to the base (assuming the section to lie wholly between the apex of the cone and its base) and also by cutting a cylinder. This is expressly stated in a passage from the *Phaenomena* of :person:`Euclid <euclid>` about to be mentioned\ [#f5]_.
