8. *The Optics.*

This book needs no description, as it has been edited by :person:`Heiberg <heiberg>` recently\ [#f3]_, both in its genuine form and in the recension by :person:`Theon <theon-of-alexandria>`. The *Catoptrica* published by :person:`Heiberg <heiberg>` in the same volume is not genuine, and :person:`Heiberg <heiberg>` suspects that in its present form it may be Theon's. It is not even certain that :person:`Euclid <euclid>` wrote *Catoptrica* at all, as :person:`Proclus <proclus>` may easily have had Theon's work before him and inadvertently assigned it to :person:`Euclid <euclid>`\ [#f4]_.

9. Besides the above-mentioned works, :person:`Euclid <euclid>` is said to have written the *Elements of Music*\ [#f5]_ (αἱ κατὰ μουσικὴν στοιχειώσεις). Two treatises are attributed to :person:`Euclid <euclid>` in our MSS. of the *Musici*, the κατατομὴ κανόνος, *Sectio canonis* (the theory of the intervals), and the εἰσαγωγὴ ἁρμονική (introduction to harmony)\ [#f6]_. The first, resting on the Pythagorean theory of music, is mathematical, and the style and diction as well as the form of the propositions mostly agree with what we find in the *Elements*. Jan thought it genuine, especially as almost the whole of the treatise (except the preface) is quoted *in extenso*, and :person:`Euclid <euclid>` is twice mentioned by name, in the commentary on Ptolemy's *Harmonica* published by :person:`Wallis <wallis>` and attributed by him to :person:`Porphyry <porphyry>`. :person:`Tannery <tannery>` was of the opposite opinion\ [#f7]_. The latest editor, :person:`Menge <menge>`, suggests that it may be a redaction by a less competent hand from the genuine Euclidean *Elements of Music*. The second treatise is not Euclid's, but was written by :person:`Cleonides <cleonides>`, a pupil of Aristoxenus\ [#f8]_.
