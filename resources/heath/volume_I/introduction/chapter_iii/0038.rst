II. :person:`Porphyry <porphyry>`.

The :person:`Porphyry <porphyry>` here mentioned is of course the Neo-Platonist who lived about 232–304 A.D. Whether he really wrote a systematic commentary on the *Elements* is uncertain. The passages in :person:`Proclus <proclus>` which seem to make this probable are two in which he mentions him (1) as having demonstrated the necessity of the words "not on the same side" in the enunciation of I. 14\ [#f1]_, and (2) as having pointed out the necessity of understanding correctly the enunciation of I. 26, since, if the particular injunctions as to the sides of the triangles to be taken as equal are not regarded, the student may easily fall into error\ [#f2]_. These passages, showing that :person:`Porphyry <porphyry>` carefully analysed Euclid's enunciations in these cases, certainly suggest that his remarks were part of a systematic commentary. Further, the list of mathematicians in the :cite:`*Fihrist* <ibn-al-nadim:fihrist>` gives :person:`Porphyry <porphyry>` as having written "a book on the Elements." It is true that Wenrich takes this book to have been a work by :person:`Porphyry <porphyry>` mentioned by :person:`Suidas <suidas>` and :person:`Proclus <proclus>` (*Theolog. Platon.*), περὶ ἀρχῶν libri II.\ [#f3]_

There is nothing of importance in the notes attributed to :person:`Porphyry <porphyry>` by :person:`Proclus <proclus>`.

(1) Three alternative proofs of I. 20, which avoid *producing* a side of the triangle, are assigned to :person:`Heron <heron>` and :person:`Porphyry <porphyry>` without saying which belonged to which. If the first of the three was Heron's, I agree with van Pesch that it is more probable that the two others were both Porphyry's than that the second was Heron's and only the third Porphyry's. For they are similar in character, and the third uses a result obtained in the second\ [#f4]_.

(2) :person:`Porphyry <porphyry>` gave an alternative proof of I. 18 to meet a childish objection which is supposed to require the part of *AC* equal to *AB* to be cut off from *CA* and not from *AC*.

:person:`Proclus <proclus>` gives a precisely similar alternative proof of I. 6 to meet a similar supposed objection; and it may well be that, though :person:`Proclus <proclus>` mentions no name, this proof was also Porphyry's, as van Pesch suggests\ [#f5]_.

Two other references to :person:`Porphyry <porphyry>` found in :person:`Proclus <proclus>` cannot have anything to do with commentaries on the *Elements*. In the first a work called the Συμμικτά is quoted, while in the second a philosophical question is raised.
