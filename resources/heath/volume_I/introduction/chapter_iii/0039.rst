:person:`Pappus <pappus>` says at the beginning of his (commentary) on the 10th (book) of :person:`Euclid <euclid>`\ [#f1]_." Again in the :cite:`*Fihrist* <ibn-al-nadim:fihrist>` we are told that :person:`Pappus <pappus>` wrote a commentary to the tenth book of :person:`Euclid <euclid>` in two parts\ [#f2]_. Fragments of this still survive in a MS. described by :person:`Woepcke <woepcke>`\ [#f3]_, Paris. No. 952. 2 (supplément arabe de la Bibliothèque impériale), which contains a translation by Abū 'Uthmān (beginning of 10th century) of a Greek commentary on Book X. It is in two books, and there can now be no doubt that the author of the Greek commentary was :person:`Pappus <pappus>`\ [#f4]_. Again :person:`Eutocius <eutocius>`, in his note on :person:`Archimedes <archimedes>`, *On the Sphere and Cylinder* I. 13, says that :person:`Pappus <pappus>` explained in his commentary on the *Elements* how to inscribe in a circle a polygon similar to a polygon inscribed in another circle; and this would presumably come in his commentary on Book XII., just as the problem is solved in the second scholium on Eucl. XII. 1. Thus Pappus' commentary on the *Elements* must have been pretty complete, an additional confirmation of this supposition being forthcoming in the reference of :person:`Marinus <marinus>` (a pupil and follower of :person:`Proclus <proclus>`) in his preface to the *Data* to "the commentaries of :person:`Pappus <pappus>` on the book\ [#f5]_."

The actual references to :person:`Pappus <pappus>` in :person:`Proclus <proclus>` are as follows:

(1) On the Postulate (4) that all right angles are equal, :person:`Pappus <pappus>` is quoted as saying that the converse, viz. that all angles equal to a right angle are right, is not true\ [#f6]_, since the angle included between the arcs of two semicircles which are equal, and have their diameters at right angles and terminating at one point, is equal to a right angle, but is not a right angle.

(2) On the axioms :person:`Pappus <pappus>` is quoted as saying that, in addition to Euclid's axioms, others are on record as well (συναναγράφεσθαι) about unequals added to equals and equals added to unequals\ [#f7]_; these, says :person:`Proclus <proclus>`, follow from the Euclidean axioms, while others given by :person:`Pappus <pappus>` are involved by the definitions, namely those which assert that "all parts of the plane and of the straight line coincide with one another," that "a point divides a straight line, a line a surface, and a surface a solid," and that "the infinite is (obtained) in magnitudes both by addition and diminution\ [#f8]_."

.. [#f4] :person:`Woepcke <woepcke>` read the name of the author, in the title of the first book, as *B.los* (the dot representing a missing vowel). He quotes also from other MSS. (e.g. of the *Ta'rīkh al-Ḥukamā* and of the :cite:`*Fihrist* <ibn-al-nadim:fihrist>`) where he reads the name of the commentator as *B.lis*, *B.n.s* or *B.l.s*. :person:`Woepcke <woepcke>` takes this author to be Valens, and thinks it possible that he may be the same as the astrologer Vettius Valens. This :person:`Heiberg <heiberg>` (*Euklid-Studien*, pp. 169, 170) proves to be impossible, because, while one of the MSS. quoted by :person:`Woepcke <woepcke>` says that "*B.n.s*, le Roûmi" (late-Greek) was later than Claudius :person:`Ptolemy <ptolemy>` and the :cite:`*Fihrist* <ibn-al-nadim:fihrist>` says "*B.l.s*, le Roûmi" wrote a commentary on Ptolemy's *Planisphaerium*, Vettius Valens seems to have lived under Hadrian, and must therefore have been an elder contemporary of :person:`Ptolemy <ptolemy>`. But :person:`Suter <suter>` shows (:cite:`*Fihrist* <ibn-al-nadim:fihrist>`, p. 22 and p. 54, note 92) that *Bānos* is only distinguished from *Bābos* by the position of a certain dot, and *Bālos* may also easily have arisen from an original *Bābos* (there is no P in Arabic), so that :person:`Pappus <pappus>` must be the person meant. This is further confirmed by the fact that the :cite:`*Fihrist* <ibn-al-nadim:fihrist>` gives this author and Valens as the subjects of two separate paragraphs, attributing to the latter astrological works only.
