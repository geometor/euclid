Again, on I. 21, :person:`Proclus <proclus>` remarks on the paradox that straight lines may be drawn from the base to a point within a triangle which are (1) together greater than the two sides, and (2) include a less angle, provided that the straight lines may be drawn from points in the base other than its extremities. The subject of straight lines satisfying condition (1) was treated at length, with reference to a variety of cases, by :person:`Pappus <pappus>`\ [#f1]_, after a collection of "paradoxes" by Erycinus, of whom nothing more is known. :person:`Proclus <proclus>` gives Pappus' first case, and adds a rather useless proof of the possibility of drawing straight lines satisfying condition (2) *alone*, adding that "the proposition stated has been proved by me without using the parallels of the commentators\ [#f2]_." IV. :person:`Simplicius <simplicius>`.

According to the :cite:`*Fihrist* <ibn-al-nadim:fihrist>`\ [#f5]_, :person:`Simplicius <simplicius>` the Greek wrote "a commentary to the beginning of Euclid's book, which forms an introduction to geometry." And in fact this commentary on the definitions, postulates and axioms (including the postulate known as the Parallel-Axiom) is preserved in the Arabic commentary of :person:`an-Nairīzī <an-nairizi>`\ [#f6]_. On two subjects this commentary of :person:`Simplicius <simplicius>` quotes a certain ":person:`Aganis <aganis>`," the first subject being the definition of an angle, and the second the definition of parallels and the parallel-postulate. :person:`Simplicius <simplicius>` gives word for word, in a long passage placed by :person:`an-Nairīzī <an-nairizi>` after I. 29, an attempt by ":person:`Aganis <aganis>`" to prove the parallel-postulate. It starts from a definition of parallels which agrees with Geminus' view of them as given by :person:`Proclus <proclus>`\ [#f7]_, and is closely connected with the definition given by :person:`Posidonius <posidonius>`\ [#f8]_. Hence it has been assumed that ":person:`Aganis <aganis>`" is none other than :person:`Geminus <geminus>`, and the historical importance of the commentary of :person:`Simplicius <simplicius>` has been judged accordingly. But it has been recently shown by :person:`Tannery <tannery>` that the identification of ":person:`Aganis <aganis>`" with :person:`Geminus <geminus>` is practically impossible\ [#f9]_. In the translation of Besthorn-Heiberg :person:`Aganis <aganis>` is called by :person:`Simplicius <simplicius>` in one place "philosophus :person:`Aganis <aganis>`," in another "magister noster :person:`Aganis <aganis>`," in Gherard's version he is "socius :person:`Aganis <aganis>`" and "socius noster :person:`Aganis <aganis>`."
