He speaks of people who objected "even in ancient times" (iam antiquitus) to the use by geometers of this postulate. This would not have been an appropriate phrase had :person:`Geminus <geminus>` been the writer. I do not think that this difficulty can be got over by Suter's suggestion\ [#f1]_ that the passages in question may have been taken out of Heron's commentary, and that :person:`an-Nairīzī <an-nairizi>` may have forgotten to name the author; it seems clear that :person:`Simplicius <simplicius>` is the person who described ":person:`Aganis <aganis>`." Hence we are driven to suppose that :person:`Aganis <aganis>` was not :person:`Geminus <geminus>`, but some unknown contemporary of :person:`Simplicius <simplicius>`\ [#f2]_. Considerable interest will however continue to attach to the comments of :person:`Simplicius <simplicius>` so fortunately preserved.
