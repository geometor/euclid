Speaking generally, we shall not be justified in concluding that :person:`Proclus <proclus>` is stating something new of his own unless he indicates this himself in express terms.

As regards the form of Proclus' references to others by name, van Pesch notes that he very seldom mentions the particular *work* from which he is borrowing. If we leave out of account the references to Plato's dialogues, there are only the following references to books: the *Bacchae* of Philolaus\ [#f2]_, the *Symmikta* of :person:`Porphyry <porphyry>`\ [#f3]_, :person:`Archimedes <archimedes>` *On the Sphere and Cylinder*\ [#f4]_, :person:`Apollonius <apollonius>` *On the cochlias*\ [#f5]_, a book by :person:`Eudemus <eudemus>` *On the Angle*\ [#f6]_, a whole book of :person:`Posidonius <posidonius>` directed against :person:`Zeno <zeno>` of the Epicurean sect\ [#f7]_, Carpus' *Astronomy*\ [#f8]_, Eudemus' *History of Geometry*\ [#f9]_, and a tract by :person:`Ptolemy <ptolemy>` on the parallel-postulate\ [#f10]_.

Again, :person:`Proclus <proclus>` does not always indicate that he is quoting something at second-hand. He often does so, e.g. he quotes :person:`Heron <heron>` as the authority for a statement about Philippus, :person:`Eudemus <eudemus>` as attributing a certain theorem to :person:`Oenopides <oenopides>` etc.; but he says on I. 12 that ":person:`Oenopides <oenopides>` first investigated this problem, thinking it useful for astronomy" when he cannot have had Oenopides' work before him.

It has been said above that :person:`Proclus <proclus>` was in the habit of stating in his own words the substance of the things which he borrowed. We are prepared for this when we find him stating that he will select the best things from ancient commentaries and "cut short their interminable diffuseness," that he will "briefly describe" (συντόμως ἱστορῆσαι) the other proofs of I. 20 given by :person:`Heron <heron>` and :person:`Porphyry <porphyry>` and also the proofs of I. 25 by :person:`Menelaus <menelaus>` and :person:`Heron <heron>`. But the best evidence is of course to be found in the passages where he quotes works still extant, e.g. those of :person:`Plato <plato>`, :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>` and Plotinus. Examination of these passages shows great divergences from the original; even where he purports to quote textually, using the expressions ":person:`Plato <plato>` says," or "Plotinus says," he by no means quotes word for word\ [#f11]_. In fact, he seems to have had a positive distaste for quoting textually from other works. He cannot conquer this even when quoting from :person:`Euclid <euclid>`; he says in his note on I. 22, "we will follow the words of the geometer" but fails, nevertheless, to reproduce the text of :person:`Euclid <euclid>` unchanged\ [#f12]_.
