The next words about the appropriation of these terms (parabola, hyperbola and ellipse) by later writers (i.e. :person:`Apollonius <apollonius>`) to denote the conic sections are of course not due to :person:`Eudemus <eudemus>`.

Coming now to notes where :person:`Eudemus <eudemus>` is not named by :person:`Proclus <proclus>`, we may fairly conjecture, with van Pesch, that :person:`Eudemus <eudemus>` was really the authority for the statements (1) that :person:`Thales <thales>` first proved that a circle is bisected by its diameter\ [#f6]_ (though the proof by *reductio ad absurdum* which follows in :person:`Proclus <proclus>` cannot be attributed to :person:`Thales <thales>`\ [#f7]_), (2) that ":person:`Plato <plato>` made over to Leodamas the analytical method, by means of which *it is recorded* (ἱστόρηται) that the latter too made many discoveries in geometry\ [#f8]_," (3) that the theorem of I. 5 was due to :person:`Thales <thales>`, and that for equal angles he used the more archaic expression "similar" angles\ [#f9]_, (4) that :person:`Oenopides <oenopides>` first investigated the problem of I. 12, and that he called the perpendicular the *gnomonic line* (κατὰ γνώμονα)\ [#f10]_, (5) that the theorem that only three sorts of polygons can fill up the space round a point, viz. the equilateral triangle, the square and the regular hexagon, was Pythagorean\ [#f11]_. :person:`Eudemus <eudemus>` may also be the authority for Proclus' description of the two methods, referred to :person:`Plato <plato>` and :person:`Pythagoras <pythagoras>` respectively, of forming right-angled triangles in whole numbers\ [#f12]_.

We cannot attribute to :person:`Eudemus <eudemus>` the beginning of the note on I. 47 where :person:`Proclus <proclus>` says that "if we listen to those who like to recount ancient history, we may find some of them referring this theorem to :person:`Pythagoras <pythagoras>` and saying that he sacrificed an ox in honour of his discovery\ [#f13]_." As such a sacrifice was contrary to the Pythagorean tenets, and :person:`Eudemus <eudemus>` could not have been unaware of this, the story cannot rest on his authority. Moreover :person:`Proclus <proclus>` speaks as though he were not certain of the correctness of the tradition; indeed,
