Tittel is equally positive that it is Gemĭnus and suggests that Γεμῖνος is due to a *false analogy* with Ἀλεξῖνος etc. and Γεμεῖνος wrongly formed on the model of Ἀντωνεῖνος, Ἀγριππεῖνα. :person:`Geminus <geminus>`, a Stoic philosopher, born probably in the island of Rhodes, was the author of a comprehensive work on the classification of mathematics, and also wrote, about 73–67 B.C., a not less comprehensive commentary on the meteorological textbook of his teacher :person:`Posidonius <posidonius>` of Rhodes.

It is the former work in which we are specially interested here. Though :person:`Proclus <proclus>` made great use of it, he does not mention its title, unless we may suppose that, in the passage (p. 177, 24) where, after quoting from :person:`Geminus <geminus>` a classification of lines which never meet, he says, "these remarks I have selected from the φιλοκαλία of :person:`Geminus <geminus>`," φιλοκαλία is a title or an alternative title. :person:`Pappus <pappus>` however quotes a work of :person:`Geminus <geminus>` "on the classification of the mathematics" (ἐν τῷ περὶ τῆς τῶν μαθημάτων τάξεως)\ [#f1]_, while :person:`Eutocius <eutocius>` quotes from "the sixth book of the doctrine of the mathematics" (ἐν τῷ ἕκτῳ τῆς τῶν μαθημάτων θεωρίας)\ [#f2]_. :person:`Tannery <tannery>`\ [#f3]_ pointed out that the former title corresponds well enough to the long extract\ [#f4]_ which :person:`Proclus <proclus>` gives in his first prologue, and also to the fragments contained in the *Anonymi variae collectiones* published by :person:`Hultsch <hultsch>` at the end of his edition of :person:`Heron <heron>`\ [#f5]_; but it does not suit most of the other passages borrowed by :person:`Proclus <proclus>`. *The correct title* was therefore probably that given by :person:`Eutocius <eutocius>`, *The Doctrine, or Theory, of the Mathematics*; and :person:`Pappus <pappus>` probably refers to one particular portion of the work, say the first Book. If the sixth Book treated of conics, as we may conclude from :person:`Eutocius <eutocius>`, there must have been more Books to follow, because :person:`Proclus <proclus>` has preserved us details about higher curves, which must have come later. If again :person:`Geminus <geminus>` finished his work and wrote with the same fulness about the other branches of mathematics as he did about geometry, there must have been a considerable number of Books altogether. At all events it seems to have been designed to give a complete view of the whole science of mathematics, and in fact to be a sort of encyclopaedia of the subject.

I shall now indicate first the certain, and secondly the probable, obligations of :person:`Proclus <proclus>` to :person:`Geminus <geminus>`, in which task I have only to follow van Pesch, who has embodied the results of Tittel's similar inquiry also\ [#f6]_. I shall only omit the passages as regards which a case for attributing them to :person:`Geminus <geminus>` does not seem to me to have been made out.
