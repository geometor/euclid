Apollonius' *Conics*, and the curves invented by Nicomedes, Hippias and Perseus\ [#f1]_;

(16) a passage on the parallel-postulate regarded as the converse of I. 17\ [#f2]_.

Of the authors to whom :person:`Proclus <proclus>` was indebted in a less degree the most important is :person:`Apollonius <apollonius>` of Perga. Two passages allude to his *Conics*\ [#f3]_, one to a work on irrationals\ [#f4]_, and two to a treatise *On the cochlias* (apparently the cylindrical helix) by :person:`Apollonius <apollonius>`\ [#f5]_. But more important for our purpose are six references to :person:`Apollonius <apollonius>` in connexion with elementary geometry.

(1) He appears as the author of an attempt to explain the idea of a line (possessing length but no breadth) by reference to daily experience, e.g. when we tell someone to measure, merely, the length of a road or of a wall\ [#f6]_; and doubtless the similar passage showing how we may in like manner get a notion of a surface (without depth) is his also\ [#f7]_.

(2) He gave a new general definition of an angle\ [#f8]_.

(3) He tried to prove certain axioms\ [#f9]_, and :person:`Proclus <proclus>` gives his attempt to prove Axiom 1, word for word\ [#f10]_.

:person:`Proclus <proclus>` further quotes:

(4) Apollonius' solution of the problem in Eucl. I. 10, avoiding Euclid's use of I. 9\ [#f11]_,

(5) his solution of the problem in I. 11, differing only slightly from Euclid's\ [#f12]_, and

(6) his solution of the problem in I. 23\ [#f13]_.

:person:`Heiberg <heiberg>`\ [#f14]_ conjectures that :person:`Apollonius <apollonius>` departed from Euclid's method in these propositions because he objected to solving problems of a more general, by means of problems of a more particular, character. :person:`Proclus <proclus>` however considers all three solutions inferior to Euclid's; and his remarks on Apollonius' handling of these elementary matters generally suggest that he was nettled by criticisms of :person:`Euclid <euclid>` in the work containing the things which he quotes from :person:`Apollonius <apollonius>`, just as we conclude that :person:`Pappus <pappus>` was offended by the remarks of :person:`Apollonius <apollonius>` about Euclid's incomplete treatment of the "three- and four-line locus\ [#f15]_." If this was the case, :person:`Proclus <proclus>` can hardly have got his information about these things at second-hand; and there seems to be no reason to doubt that he had the actual work of :person:`Apollonius <apollonius>` before him. This work may have been the treatise mentioned by :person:`Marinus <marinus>` in the words ":person:`Apollonius <apollonius>` in his general treatise" (Ἀπολλώνιος ἐν τῇ καθόλου πραγματείᾳ)\ [#f16]_.
