Besides indicating that he paid more attention than his predecessors to questions requiring deeper study (τὸ πραγματειῶδες) and "pursued clear distinctions" (τὸ εὐδιαίρετον μεταδιώκοντας)\ [#f1]_—by which he appears to imply that his predecessors had confused the different departments of their commentaries, viz. lemmas, cases, and objections (ἐνστάσεις)\ [#f2]_—:person:`Proclus <proclus>` complains that the earlier commentators had failed to indicate the ultimate grounds or *causes* of propositions\ [#f3]_. Although it is from :person:`Geminus <geminus>` that he borrowed a passage maintaining that it is one of the proper functions of geometry to inquire into causes (τὴν αἰτίαν καὶ τὸ διὰ τί)\ [#f4]_, yet it is not likely that :person:`Geminus <geminus>` dealt with Euclid's propositions one by one; and consequently, when we find :person:`Proclus <proclus>`, on I. 8, 16, 17, 18, 32, and 47\ [#f5]_, endeavouring to explain *causes*, we have good reason to suppose that the explanations are his own.

Again, his remarks on certain things which he quotes from :person:`Pappus <pappus>` can scarcely be due to anyone else, since :person:`Pappus <pappus>` is the latest of the commentators whose works he appears to have used. Under this head come

(1) his objections to certain new axioms introduced by :person:`Pappus <pappus>`\ [#f6]_,

(2) his conjecture as to how :person:`Pappus <pappus>` came to think of his alternative proof of I. 5\ [#f7]_,

(3) an addition to Pappus' remarks about the curvilineal angle which is equal to a right angle without being one\ [#f8]_.

The defence of :person:`Geminus <geminus>` against :person:`Carpus <carpus>`, who combated his view of theorems and problems, is also probably due to :person:`Proclus <proclus>`\ [#f9]_, as well as an observation on I. 38 to the effect that I. 35—38 are really comprehended in VI. 1 as particular cases\ [#f10]_.

Lastly, we can have no hesitation in attributing to :person:`Proclus <proclus>` himself (1) the criticism of Ptolemy's attempt to prove the parallel-postulate\ [#f11]_, and (2) the other attempted proof given in the same note\ [#f12]_ (on I. 29) and assuming as an axiom that "if from one point two straight lines forming an angle be produced *ad infinitum* the distance between them when so produced *ad infinitum* exceeds any finite magnitude (i.e. length)," an assumption which purports to be the equivalent of a statement in :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>`\ [#f13]_. It is introduced by words in which the writer appears to claim originality for his proof: "To him who desires to see this proved (κατασκευαζόμενον) *let it be said by us* (λεγέσθω παρ' ἡμῶν)" etc.\ [#f14]_ Moreover, :person:`Philoponus <philoponus>`, in a note on Aristotle's :cite:`*Anal. post.* I. 10 <aristotle:anal-post:I.10>`, says that "the geometer (:person:`Euclid <euclid>`) assumes this as an axiom, but it wants a great deal of proof, insomuch that both :person:`Ptolemy <ptolemy>` and :person:`Proclus <proclus>` wrote a whole book upon it\ [#f15]_."
