**Synthesis.**

(Part I. *Construction*.)

Suppose the circle <span data-ref="0156.g1:( A B C )">$ABC$</span> and the points <span data-ref="0156.g1:D">$D$</span>, <span data-ref="0156.g1:E">$E$</span> given.

Take a rectangle contained by <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ D E /">$ED$</span> and by a certain straight line <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ F E /">$EF$</span> equal to the square on the tangent to the circle from <span data-ref="0156.g1:E">$E$</span>.

From <span data-ref="0156.g1:F">$F$</span> draw <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ F A /">$FA$</span> touching the circle in <span data-ref="0156.g1:A">$A$</span>; join <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ A E /">$ABE$</span> and then <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span>, producing <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> to meet the circle at <span data-ref="0156.g1:C">$C$</span>. Join <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>.

I say then that <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is parallel to <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>.

(Part II. *Demonstration*.)

Since, by hypothesis, the rectangle <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ F E /">$FE$</span>, <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ D E /">$ED$</span> is equal to the square on the tangent from <span data-ref="0156.g1:E">$E$</span>, which again is equal to the rectangle <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span>, <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ B E /">$EB$</span>, the rectangle <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span>, <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ B E /">$EB$</span> is equal to the rectangle <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ F E /">$FE$</span>, <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ D E /">$ED$</span>.

Therefore <span data-ref="0156.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0156.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0156.g1:D">$D$</span>, <span data-ref="0156.g1:F">$F$</span> are concyclic, whence the angle <span data-ref="0156.g1:&lt; F A E )">$FAE$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0156.g1:&lt; B D E )">$BDE$</span>.

But the angle <span data-ref="0156.g1:&lt; F A E )">$FAE$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0156.g1:&lt; A C B )">$ACB$</span> in the alternate segment; therefore the angle <span data-ref="0156.g1:&lt; A C B )">$ACB$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0156.g1:&lt; B D E )">$BDE$</span>.

Therefore <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is parallel to <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>.

In cases where a διορισμός is necessary, i.e. where a solution is only possible under certain conditions, the analysis will enable those conditions to be ascertained. Sometimes the διορισμός is stated and proved at the end of the analysis, e.g. in Archimedes, *On the Sphere and Cylinder*, II. 7; sometimes it is stated in that place and the proof postponed till after the end of the synthesis, e.g. in the solution of the problem subsidiary to *On the Sphere and Cylinder*, II. 4, preserved in Eutocius’ commentary on that proposition. The analysis should also enable us to determine the number of solutions of which the problem is susceptible.
