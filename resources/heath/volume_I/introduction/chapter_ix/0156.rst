But the rectangle :g:`AE <0156.g1:/ A E />`, :g:`EB <0156.g1:/ B E />` is given, because it is equal to the square on the tangent from :g:`E <0156.g1:E>`.

Therefore the rectangle :g:`FE <0156.g1:/ F E />`, :g:`ED <0156.g1:/ D E />` is given; and, since :g:`ED <0156.g1:/ D E />` is given, :g:`FE <0156.g1:/ F E />` is given (in length). [*Data*, 57.]

But :g:`FE <0156.g1:/ F E />` is given in position also, so that :g:`F <0156.g1:F>` is also given. [*Data*, 27.]

Now :g:`FA <0156.g1:/ F A />` is the tangent from a given point :g:`F <0156.g1:F>` to a circle :g:`ABC <0156.g1:( A B C )>` given in position; therefore :g:`FA <0156.g1:/ F A />` is given in position and magnitude. [*Data*, 90.]

And the circle :g:`ABC <0156.g1:( A B C )>` is given in position; therefore the point :g:`B <0156.g1:B>` is also given. [*Data*, 25.]

But the points :g:`D <0156.g1:D>`, :g:`E <0156.g1:E>` are also given; therefore the straight lines :g:`DB <0156.g1:/ D B />`, :g:`BE <0156.g1:/ B E />` are also given in position.

**Synthesis.**

(Part I. *Construction*.)

Suppose the circle :g:`ABC <0156.g1:( A B C )>` and the points :g:`D <0156.g1:D>`, :g:`E <0156.g1:E>` given.

Take a rectangle contained by :g:`ED <0156.g1:/ D E />` and by a certain straight line :g:`EF <0156.g1:/ F E />` equal to the square on the tangent to the circle from :g:`E <0156.g1:E>`.

From :g:`F <0156.g1:F>` draw :g:`FA <0156.g1:/ F A />` touching the circle in :g:`A <0156.g1:A>`; join :g:`ABE <0156.g1:/ A E />` and then :g:`DB <0156.g1:/ D B />`, producing :g:`DB <0156.g1:/ D B />` to meet the circle at :g:`C <0156.g1:C>`. Join :g:`AC <0156.g1:/ A C />`.

I say then that :g:`AC <0156.g1:/ A C />` is parallel to :g:`DE <0156.g1:/ D E />`.

(Part II. *Demonstration*.)

Since, by hypothesis, the rectangle :g:`FE <0156.g1:/ F E />`, :g:`ED <0156.g1:/ D E />` is equal to the square on the tangent from :g:`E <0156.g1:E>`, which again is equal to the rectangle :g:`AE <0156.g1:/ A E />`, :g:`EB <0156.g1:/ B E />`, the rectangle :g:`AE <0156.g1:/ A E />`, :g:`EB <0156.g1:/ B E />` is equal to the rectangle :g:`FE <0156.g1:/ F E />`, :g:`ED <0156.g1:/ D E />`.

Therefore :g:`A <0156.g1:A>`, :g:`B <0156.g1:B>`, :g:`D <0156.g1:D>`, :g:`F <0156.g1:F>` are concyclic, whence the angle :g:`FAE <0156.g1:< F A E )>` is equal to the angle :g:`BDE <0156.g1:< B D E )>`.

But the angle :g:`FAE <0156.g1:< F A E )>` is equal to the angle :g:`ACB <0156.g1:< A C B )>` in the alternate segment; therefore the angle :g:`ACB <0156.g1:< A C B )>` is equal to the angle :g:`BDE <0156.g1:< B D E )>`.

Therefore :g:`AC <0156.g1:/ A C />` is parallel to :g:`DE <0156.g1:/ D E />`.

In cases where a :term:`διορισμός <diorismos>` is necessary, i.e. where a solution is only possible under certain conditions, the analysis will enable those conditions to be ascertained. Sometimes the :term:`διορισμός <diorismos>` is stated and proved at the end of the analysis, e.g. in :person:`Archimedes <archimedes>`, *On the Sphere and Cylinder*, II. 7; sometimes it is stated in that place and the proof postponed till after the end of the synthesis, e.g. in the solution of the problem subsidiary to *On the Sphere and Cylinder*, II. 4, preserved in :person:`Eutocius <eutocius>`’ commentary on that proposition. The analysis should also enable us to determine the number of solutions of which the problem is susceptible.
