We now come to the alterations made by :person:`Theon <theon-of-alexandria>` in his edition of the *Elements*. I shall indicate classes into which these alterations may be divided but without details (except in cases where they affect the *mathematical content* as distinct from form or language pure and simple)\ [#f1]_.

*I. Alterations made by Theon where he found, or thought he found, mistakes in the original.*

1. Real blots in the original which :person:`Theon <theon-of-alexandria>` saw and tried to remove.

(*a*) :person:`Euclid <euclid>` has a porism (corollary) to VI. 19, the enunciation of which speaks of similar and similarly described *figures* though the proposition itself refers only to triangles, and therefore the porism should have come after VI. 20. :person:`Theon <theon-of-alexandria>` substitutes *triangle* for *figure* and proves the more general porism after VI. 20.

(*b*) In IX. 19 there is a statement which is obviously incorrect. :person:`Theon <theon-of-alexandria>` saw this and altered the proof by reducing four alternatives to two, with the result that it fails to correspond to the enunciation even with Theon's substitution of "if" for "when" in the enunciation.

(*c*) :person:`Theon <theon-of-alexandria>` omits a porism to IX. 11, although it is necessary for the proof of the succeeding proposition, apparently because, owing to an error in the text (κατὰ τὸν corrected by :person:`Heiberg <heiberg>` into ἐπὶ τὸ), he could not get out of it the right sense.

(*d*) I should also put into this category a case which :person:`Heiberg <heiberg>` classifies among those in which :person:`Theon <theon-of-alexandria>` merely fancied that he found mistakes, viz. the porism to V. 7 stating that, if four magnitudes are proportional, they are proportional inversely. :person:`Theon <theon-of-alexandria>` puts this after V. 4 with a proof, which however has no necessary connexion with V. 4 but is obvious from the definition of proportion.

(*e*) I should also put under this head XI. 1, where Euclid's argument to prove that two straight lines cannot have a common segment is altered.

2. Passages which seemed to :person:`Theon <theon-of-alexandria>` to contain blots, and which he therefore set himself to correct, though more careful consideration would have shown that Euclid's words are right or at least may be excused and offer no difficulty to an intelligent reader. Under this head come:

(*a*) an alteration in III. 24.

(*b*) a perfectly unnecessary alteration, in VI. 14, of "equiangular parallelograms" into "parallelograms having one angle equal to one angle," where :person:`Theon <theon-of-alexandria>` followed the false analogy of VI. 15.

(*c*) an omission of words in V. 26, owing to his having been misled by a wrong figure.

(*d*) an alteration of the order of XI. Deff. 27, 28.

(*e*) the substitution of "parallelepipedal solid" for "cube" in XI.

.. [#f1] Exhaustive details under all the different heads are given by :person:`Heiberg <heiberg>` (Vol. V. pp. lii—lxxv).
