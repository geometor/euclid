The total number of scholia classified by :person:`Heiberg <heiberg>` as Schol. Vat. is 138.

As regards the contents of Schol. Vat. :person:`Heiberg <heiberg>` has the following observations. The thirteen scholia to Book I. are extracts made from :person:`Proclus <proclus>` by a writer thoroughly conversant with the subject, and cleverly recast (with some additions). Their author does not seem to have had the two lacunae which our text of :person:`Proclus <proclus>` has (at the end of the note on I. 36 and the beginning of the next note, and at the beginning of the note on I. 43), for the scholia I. Nos. 125 and 137 seem to fill the gaps appropriately, at least in part. In some passages he had better readings than our MSS. have. The rest of Schol. Vat. (on Books II.—XIII.) are essentially of the same character as those on Book I., containing prolegomena, remarks on the object of the propositions, critical remarks on the text, converses, lemmas; they are, in general, exact and true to tradition. The reason of the resemblance between them and :person:`Proclus <proclus>` appears to be due to the fact that they have their origin in the commentary of :person:`Pappus <pappus>`, of which we know that :person:`Proclus <proclus>` also made use. In support of the view that :person:`Pappus <pappus>` is the source, :person:`Heiberg <heiberg>` places some of the Schol. Vat. to Book X. side by side with passages from the commentary of :person:`Pappus <pappus>` in the Arabic translation discovered by :person:`Woepcke <woepcke>`\ [#f66_1]_; he also refers to the striking confirmation afforded by the fact that XII. No. 2 contains the solution of the problem of inscribing in a given circle a polygon similar to a polygon inscribed in another circle, which problem :person:`Eutocius <eutocius>` says\ [#f66_2]_ that :person:`Pappus <pappus>` gave in his commentary on the *Elements*.

But, on the other hand, Schol. Vat. contain some things which cannot have come from :person:`Pappus <pappus>`, e.g. the allusion in X. No. 1 to :person:`Theon <theon-of-alexandria>` and irrational surfaces and solids, :person:`Theon <theon-of-alexandria>` being later than :person:`Pappus <pappus>`; III. No. 10 about porisms is more like Proclus' treatment of the subject than Pappus', though one expression recalls that of :person:`Pappus <pappus>` about *forming* (σχηματίζεσθαι) the enunciations of porisms like those of either theorems or problems.

The Schol. Vat. give us important indications as regards the text of the *Elements* as :person:`Pappus <pappus>` had it. In particular, they show that he could not have had in his text certain of the lemmas in Book X. For example, three of these are identical with what we find in Schol.
