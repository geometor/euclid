17. Abū Sa'īd Sinān b. :person:`Thābit b. Qurra <thabit-ibn-qurra>`, the son of the translator of :person:`Euclid <euclid>`, followed in his father's footsteps as geometer, astronomer and physician. He wrote an "improvement of the book of ...... on the Elements of Geometry, in which he made various additions to the original." It is natural to conjecture that *Euclid* is the name missing in this description (by Ibn abī Uṣaibi'a); Casiri has the name Aqāṭon\ [#f88_2]_. The latest editor of the *Ta'rīkh al-Ḥukamā*, however, makes the name to be Iflāṭon (= :person:`Plato <plato>`), and he refers to the statement by the :cite:`*Fihrist* <ibn-al-nadim:fihrist>` and Ibn al-Qifṭī attributing to :person:`Plato <plato>` a work on the Elements of Geometry translated by Qusṭā. It is just possible, therefore, that at the time of Qusṭā the Arabs were acquainted with a book on the Elements of Geometry translated from the Greek, which they attributed to :person:`Plato <plato>`\ [#f88_3]_. Sinān died in 943.

18. Abū Sahl Wījan (or Waijan) b. Rustam al-Kūhī (fl. 988), born at Kūh in Ṭabaristān, a distinguished geometer and astronomer, wrote, according to the :cite:`*Fihrist* <ibn-al-nadim:fihrist>`, a "Book of the Elements" after that of :person:`Euclid <euclid>`\ [#f88_4]_; the 1st and 2nd Books survive at Cairo, and a part of the 3rd Book at Berlin (5922)\ [#f88_5]_. He wrote also a number of other geometrical works: Additions to the 2nd Book of :person:`Archimedes <archimedes>` on the Sphere and Cylinder (extant at Paris, at Leiden, and in the India Office), On the finding of the side of a heptagon in a circle (India Office and Cairo), On two mean proportionals (India Office), which last may be only a part of the Additions to Archimedes' On the Sphere and Cylinder, etc.

19. Abū Naṣr Muḥ. b. Muḥ. b. Ṭarkhān b. Uzlaġ :person:`al-Fārābī <al-farabi>` (870—950) wrote a commentary on the difficulties of the introductory matter to Books I. and V.\ [#f88_6]_ This appears to survive in the Hebrew translation which is, with probability, attributed to Moses b. Tibbon\ [#f88_7]_.

20. Abū 'Alī al-Ḥasan b. al-Ḥasan b. al-Haitham (about 965—1039), known by the name :person:`Ibn al-Haitham <ibn-al-haitham>` or Abū 'Alī al-Baṣrī, was a man of great powers and knowledge, and no one of his time approached him in the field of mathematical science. He wrote several works on :person:`Euclid <euclid>` the titles of which, as translated by :person:`Woepcke <woepcke>` from Usaibi'a, are as follows\ [#f88_8]_:

   1. Commentary and abridgment of the *Elements*.
   2. Collection of the Elements of Geometry and Arithmetic, drawn from the treatises of :person:`Euclid <euclid>` and :person:`Apollonius <apollonius>`.
   3. Collection of the Elements of the Calculus deduced from the principles laid down by :person:`Euclid <euclid>` in his *Elements*.
