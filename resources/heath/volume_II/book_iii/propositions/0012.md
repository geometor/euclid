# BOOK III. PROPOSITIONS.

### PROPOSITION 1.

<p class="enunciation">To find the centre of a given circle.</p>

Let $ABC$ be the given circle;  
thus it is required to find the centre of the circle $ABC$.

Let a straight line $AB$ be drawn  
<sup>5</sup> through it at random, and let it be bisected  
at the point $D$;  
from $D$ let $DC$ be drawn at right angles  
to $AB$ and let it be drawn through to $E$;  
let $CE$ be bisected at $F$;  
<sup>10</sup> I say that $F$ is the centre of the circle  
$ABC$.

![Proposition 1](0012.g1.svg)

For suppose it is not, but, if possible,  
let $G$ be the centre,  
and let $GA$, $GD$, $GB$ be joined.

<sup>15</sup> Then, since $AD$ is equal to $DB$,  
and $DG$ is common,  
<p class="sub-clause">the two sides $AD$, $DG$ are equal to the two sides<br />
$BD$, $DG$ respectively;</p>
and the base $GA$ is equal to the base $GB$, for they are  
<sup>20</sup> radii;  
<p class="assertion">therefore the angle $ADG$ is equal to the angle $GDB$. <span class="justification">[I. 8]</span></p>

But, when a straight line set up on a straight line makes  
the adjacent angles equal to one another, each of the equal  
angles is right; <span class="justification">[I. Def. 10]</span>  
<p class="assertion"><sup>25</sup> therefore the angle $GDB$ is right.</p>
