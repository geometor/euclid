BOOK III. PROPOSITIONS.
=======================

PROPOSITION 1.
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

.. enunciation::

   To find the centre of a given circle.

| Let :g:`$ABC$ <0012.g1:( A B C )>` be the given circle;
| thus it is required to find the centre of the circle :g:`$ABC$ <0012.g1:( A B C )>`.

| Let a straight line :g:`$AB$ <0012.g1:/ A B />` be drawn
| :line:`5` through it at random, and let it be bisected
| at the point :g:`$D$ <0012.g1:D>`;
| from :g:`$D$ <0012.g1:D>` let :g:`$DC$ <0012.g1:/ D C />` be drawn at right angles
| to :g:`$AB$ <0012.g1:/ A B />` and let it be drawn through to :g:`$E$ <0012.g1:E>`;
| let :g:`$CE$ <0012.g1:/ C E />` be bisected at :g:`$F$ <0012.g1:F>`;
| :line:`10` I say that :g:`$F$ <0012.g1:F>` is the centre of the circle
| :g:`$ABC$ <0012.g1:( A B C )>`.

.. picture:: 0012.g1.svg
   :alt: Proposition 1

| For suppose it is not, but, if possible,
| let :g:`$G$ <0012.g1:G>` be the centre,
| and let :g:`$GA$ <0012.g1:/ G A />`, :g:`$GD$ <0012.g1:/ G D />`, :g:`$GB$ <0012.g1:/ G B />` be joined.

| :line:`15` Then, since :g:`$AD$ <0012.g1:/ A D />` is equal to :g:`$DB$ <0012.g1:/ D B />`,
| and :g:`$DG$ <0012.g1:/ D G />` is common,

.. subclause::

   | the two sides :g:`$AD$ <0012.g1:/ A D />`, :g:`$DG$ <0012.g1:/ D G />` are equal to the two sides
   | :g:`$BD$ <0012.g1:/ B D />`, :g:`$DG$ <0012.g1:/ D G />` respectively;

| and the base :g:`$GA$ <0012.g1:/ G A />` is equal to the base :g:`$GB$ <0012.g1:/ G B />`, for they are
| :line:`20` radii;

.. assertion:: [I. 8] <I.8>

   therefore the angle :g:`$ADG$ <0012.g1:< A D G )>` is equal to the angle :g:`$GDB$ <0012.g1:< G D B )>`.

| But, when a straight line set up on a straight line makes
| the adjacent angles equal to one another, each of the equal
| angles is right; :just:`[I. Def. 10] <I.def.10>`

.. assertion::

   :line:`25` therefore the angle :g:`$GDB$ <0012.g1:< G D B )>` is right.
