The *reductio ad absurdum* form of proof is not really necessary in this case, and it has the additional disadvantage that it requires the destruction of two hypotheses, namely that the chord is (1) outside, (2) on the circle. To prove the proposition directly, we have only to show that, if <span data-ref="0015.g1:E">$E$</span> be any point on the straight line <span data-ref="0015.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> between <span data-ref="0015.g1:A">$A$</span> and <span data-ref="0015.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0015.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> is less than the radius of the circle. This may be done by the method shown above, under I. 24, for proving what is assumed in that proposition, namely that, in the figure of the proposition, <span data-ref="0015.g1:F">$F$</span> falls below <span data-ref="0015.g1:/ E G /">$EG$</span> if <span data-ref="0015.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> is not greater than <span data-ref="0015.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span>. The assumption amounts to the following proposition, which De Morgan would make to precede I. 24: "Every straight line drawn from the vertex of a triangle to the base is less than the greater of the two sides, or than either if they be equal." The case here is that in which the two sides are equal; and, since the angle <span data-ref="0015.g1:< D A B )">$DAB$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0015.g1:< D B A )">$DBA$</span>, while the exterior angle <span data-ref="0015.g1:< D E A )">$DEA$</span> is greater than the interior and opposite angle <span data-ref="0015.g1:< D B A )">$DBA$</span>, it follows that the angle <span data-ref="0015.g1:< D E A )">$DEA$</span> is greater than the angle <span data-ref="0015.g1:< D A E )">$DAE$</span>, whence <span data-ref="0015.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> must be less than <span data-ref="0015.g1:/ D A /">$DA$</span> or <span data-ref="0015.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span>.
