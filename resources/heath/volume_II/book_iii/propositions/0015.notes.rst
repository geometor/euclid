Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
--------------------------------------------

- **Proposition 2 Proof**: By [I. 5], isosceles base angles $\angle DAE = \angle DBE$. By [I. 16], exterior angle $\angle DEB > \angle DAE$, so $\angle DEB > \angle DBE$. By [I. 19], opposite side $DB > DE$. Since $DB = DF$ (radius), $DF > DE$, contradicting that part $DF$ is greater than whole $DE$. Hence $AB$ cannot fall outside or on circumference, and must fall within.
- **Commentary**: Heath and De Morgan outline a direct proof showing $DE < \text{radius}$ for any point $E$ on the segment $AB$, avoiding the need to eliminate multiple hypotheses via *reductio ad absurdum*.
