PROPOSITION 5.
--------------

.. enunciation::

   If two circles cut one another, they will not have the same centre.

| For let the circles :g:`$ABC$ <0018.g1:( A B C )>`, :g:`$CDG$ <0018.g1:( C D G )>` cut one another at the points :g:`$B$ <0018.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0018.g1:C>`;
| I say that they will not have the same centre.

.. picture:: 0018.g1.svg
   :alt: Proposition 5 Diagram

For, if possible, let it be :g:`$E$ <0018.g1:E>`; let :g:`$EC$ <0018.g1:/ E C />` be joined, and let :g:`$EFG$ <0018.g1:/ E F G />` be drawn through at random.

Then, since the point :g:`$E$ <0018.g1:E>` is the centre of the circle :g:`$ABC$ <0018.g1:( A B C )>`,

.. assertion:: [I. Def. 15] <I.def.15>

   :g:`$EC$ <0018.g1:/ E C />` is equal to :g:`$EF$ <0018.g1:/ E F />`.

Again, since the point :g:`$E$ <0018.g1:E>` is the centre of the circle :g:`$CDG$ <0018.g1:( C D G )>`,

.. assertion::

   :g:`$EC$ <0018.g1:/ E C />` is equal to :g:`$EG$ <0018.g1:/ E G />`.

| But :g:`$EC$ <0018.g1:/ E C />` was proved equal to :g:`$EF$ <0018.g1:/ E F />` also;
| therefore :g:`$EF$ <0018.g1:/ E F />` is also equal to :g:`$EG$ <0018.g1:/ E G />`, the less to the greater: which is impossible.

.. assertion::

   | Therefore the point :g:`$E$ <0018.g1:E>` is not the centre of the circles
   | :g:`$ABC$ <0018.g1:( A B C )>`, :g:`$CDG$ <0018.g1:( C D G )>`.

.. qed::

   Therefore etc. :just:`Q. E. D.`

----

The propositions III. 5, 6 could be combined in one. It makes no difference whether the circles cut, or meet without cutting, so long as they do not coincide altogether; in either case they cannot have the same centre. The two cases are covered by the enunciation: *If the circumferences of two circles meet at a point they cannot have the same centre.* On the other hand, *If two circles have the same centre and one point in their circumferences common, they must coincide altogether.*
