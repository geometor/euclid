PROPOSITION 6.
--------------

.. enunciation::

   If two circles touch one another, they will not have the same centre.

For let the two circles :g:`ABC <0019.g1:( A B C )>`, :g:`CDE <0019.g1:( C D E )>` touch one another at the point :g:`C <0019.g1:C>`;

I say that they will not have the same centre.

.. picture:: 0019.g1.svg
   :alt: Proposition 6 Diagram

For, if possible, let it be :g:`F <0019.g1:F>`; let :g:`FC <0019.g1:/ F C />` be joined, and let :g:`FEB <0019.g1:/ F E B />` be drawn through at random.

Then, since the point :g:`F <0019.g1:F>` is the centre of the circle :g:`ABC <0019.g1:( A B C )>`,

.. assertion::

   :g:`FC <0019.g1:/ F C />` is :eq:`equal <0019.g1:/ F C / = 0019.g1:/ F B />` to :g:`FB <0019.g1:/ F B />`.

Again, since the point :g:`F <0019.g1:F>` is the centre of the circle :g:`CDE <0019.g1:( C D E )>`,

.. assertion::

   :g:`FC <0019.g1:/ F C />` is :eq:`equal <0019.g1:/ F C / = 0019.g1:/ F E />` to :g:`FE <0019.g1:/ F E />`.

But :g:`FC <0019.g1:/ F C />` was proved :eq:`equal <0019.g1:/ F C / = 0019.g1:/ F B />` to :g:`FB <0019.g1:/ F B />`; therefore :g:`FE <0019.g1:/ F E />` is also :eq:`equal <0019.g1:/ F E / = 0019.g1:/ F B />` to :g:`FB <0019.g1:/ F B />`, the less to the greater: which is impossible.

.. assertion::

   Therefore :g:`F <0019.g1:F>` is not the centre of the circles :g:`ABC <0019.g1:( A B C )>`, :g:`CDE <0019.g1:( C D E )>`.

.. qed::

   Therefore etc. :just:`Q. E. D.`

----

The English editions enunciate this proposition of circles touching *internally*, but the word (ἐντός) is a mere interpolation, which was no doubt made because Euclid's figure showed only the case of *internal* contact. The fact is that, in his usual manner, he chose for demonstration the more difficult case, and left the other case (that of *external* contact) to the intelligence of the reader. It is indeed sufficiently self-evident that circles touching externally cannot have the same centre; but Euclid's proof can really be used for this case too.

:person:`Camerer <camerer>` remarks that the proof of III. 6 seems to assume tacitly that the points $E$ and :g:`B <0019.g1:B>` cannot coincide, or that circles which touch internally at :g:`C <0019.g1:C>` cannot meet in any other point, whereas this fact is not proved by Euclid till III. 13. But no such general assumption is necessary here; it is only necessary that *one* line drawn from the assumed common centre should meet the circles in different points; and the very notion of internal contact requires that, before one circle *meets* the other on its inner side, it must have passed through points *within* the latter circle.
