### PROPOSITION 7.

<p class="enunciation">If on the diameter of a circle a point be taken which is not<br />
the centre of the circle, and from the point straight lines fall<br />
upon the circle, that will be greatest on which the centre is, the<br />
remainder of the same diameter will be least, and of the rest<br />
<sup>5</sup> the nearer to the straight line through the centre is always<br />
greater than the more remote, and only two equal straight<br />
lines will fall from the point on the circle, one on each side<br />
of the least straight line.</p>

Let $ABCD$ be a circle, and let $AD$ be a diameter of it;  
<sup>10</sup> on $AD$ let a point $F$ be taken which is not the centre of the  
circle, let $E$ be the centre of the circle,  
and from $F$ let straight lines $FB$, $FC$, $FG$ fall upon the circle  
$ABCD$;  
I say that $FA$ is greatest, $FD$ is least, and of the rest $FB$ is  
<sup>15</sup> greater than $FC$, and $FC$ than $FG$.

![Proposition 7 Diagram](0020.g1.svg)

For let $BE$, $CE$, $GE$ be joined.  
Then, since in any triangle two  
sides are greater than the remaining  
one, <span class="justification">[I. 20]</span>  
<p class="assertion"><sup>20</sup> $EB$, $EF$ are greater than $BF$.</p>

But $AE$ is equal to $BE$;  
<p class="assertion">therefore $AF$ is greater than $BF$.</p>

Again, since $BE$ is equal to $CE$,  
and $FE$ is common,  
<sup>25</sup> the two sides $BE$, $EF$ are equal to the two sides $CE$, $EF$.  
But the angle $BEF$ is also greater than the angle $CEF$;  
<p class="assertion">therefore the base $BF$ is greater than the base $CF$. <span class="justification">[I. 24]</span></p>

For the same reason  
<p class="assertion">$CF$ is also greater than $FG$.</p>

<sup>30</sup> Again, since $GF$, $FE$ are greater than $EG$,  
and $EG$ is equal to $ED$,  
<p class="assertion">$GF$, $FE$ are greater than $ED$.</p>

Let $EF$ be subtracted from each;  
<p class="assertion">therefore the remainder $GF$ is greater than the remainder<br />
<sup>35</sup> $FD$.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore $FA$ is greatest, $FD$ is least, and $FB$ is greater<br />
than $FC$, and $FC$ than $FG$.</p>
