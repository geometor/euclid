Thus <span data-ref="0023.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> is greater than the sum of <span data-ref="0023.g1:/ H F /">$HF$</span>, <span data-ref="0023.g1:/ A H /">$AH$</span>, whence, *a fortiori*, <span data-ref="0023.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> is greater than the difference of <span data-ref="0023.g1:/ H F /">$HF$</span>, <span data-ref="0023.g1:/ A H /">$AH$</span>, i.e. than <span data-ref="0023.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span>.

Heron does not give the third possible case, that, namely, where *both* perpendiculars fall on the lines produced. The fact is that, in this case, the foregoing method breaks down. Though <span data-ref="0023.g2:/ A E /">$AE$</span> be nearer to the centre than <span data-ref="0023.g2:/ A F /">$AF$</span> in the sense that <span data-ref="0023.g2:/ D G /">$DG$</span> is less than <span data-ref="0023.g2:/ D H /">$DH$</span>,

Moreover this cannot be proved by the same method as before.
For, while we can prove that
<p class="assertion"><span data-ref="0023.g2:/ G E /">$GE$</span> $>$ <span data-ref="0023.g2:/ H F /">$HF$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion"><span data-ref="0023.g2:/ G A /">$GA$</span> $>$ <span data-ref="0023.g2:/ A H /">$AH$</span>,</p>
we cannot make any inference as to the comparative length of <span data-ref="0023.g2:/ A E /">$AE$</span>, <span data-ref="0023.g2:/ A F /">$AF$</span>.

To judge by Heron's corresponding note to III. 8, he would, to prove this case, practically prove III. 35 first, i.e. prove that, if <span data-ref="0023.g2:/ E A /">$EA$</span> be produced to <span data-ref="0023.g2:K">$K$</span> and <span data-ref="0023.g2:/ F A /">$FA$</span> to <span data-ref="0023.g2:L">$L$</span>,
<p class="assertion">rect. <span data-ref="0023.g2:/ F A /">$FA$</span>, <span data-ref="0023.g2:/ A L /">$AL$</span> $=$ rect. <span data-ref="0023.g2:/ E A /">$EA$</span>, <span data-ref="0023.g2:/ A K /">$AK$</span>,</p>
from which he would infer that, since <span data-ref="0023.g2:/ A K /">$AK$</span> $>$ <span data-ref="0023.g2:/ A L /">$AL$</span> by the first case,
<p class="assertion"><span data-ref="0023.g2:/ A E /">$AE$</span> $<$ <span data-ref="0023.g2:/ A F /">$AF$</span>.</p>

An excellent moral can, I think, be drawn from the note of Heron. Having the appearance of supplementing, or giving an alternative for, Euclid's proposition, it cannot be said to do more than confuse the subject. Nor was it necessary to find a new proof for the case where the two lines which are compared are on *opposite* sides of the diameter, since Euclid shows that for each line from the point to the circumference on one side of the diameter there is another of the same length equally inclined to it on the other side.
