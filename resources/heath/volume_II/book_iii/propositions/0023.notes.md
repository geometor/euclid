## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Breakdown of Heron's Criterion in Case 3**: When both perpendiculars $DG$ and $DH$ fall on the produced segments $EA, FA$ (i.e., when $AE$ and $AF$ are inclined toward the nearer end $C$ of the diameter $CB$), having $DG < DH$ means the entire chord $EK$ is greater than $FL$, so the shorter segment $AE$ is actually *less* than $AF$. As Heath remarks, resolving this via Heron's approach requires anticipating the intersecting chords theorem (III. 35: $\text{rect. } FA, AL = \text{rect. } EA, AK$).
- **Proposition 8 Enunciation**: Begins at the bottom of the page and introduces the distinction between lines falling on the *concave circumference* (κοίλη περιφέρεια) and the *convex circumference* (κυρτὴ περιφέρεια).
