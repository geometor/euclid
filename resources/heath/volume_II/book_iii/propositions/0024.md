title: "Book III, Proposition 8 (Enunciation cont. and Proof)"

*the nearer to that through the centre is always greater than the more remote, but, of the straight lines falling on the convex circumference, that between the point and the diameter is least, while of the rest the nearer to the least is always less than the more remote, and only two equal straight lines will fall on the circle from the point, one on each side of the least.*

Let <span data-ref="0024.g1:( A B C )">$ABC$</span> be a circle, and let a point <span data-ref="0024.g1:D">$D$</span> be taken outside <span data-ref="0024.g1:( A B C )">$ABC$</span>; let there be drawn through from it straight lines <span data-ref="0024.g1:/ D A /">$DA$</span>, <span data-ref="0024.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>, <span data-ref="0024.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span>, <span data-ref="0024.g1:/ D C /">$DC$</span>, and let <span data-ref="0024.g1:/ D A /">$DA$</span> be through the centre;
I say that, of the straight lines falling on the concave circumference <span data-ref="0024.g1:( A E F C )">$AEFC$</span>, the straight line <span data-ref="0024.g1:/ D A /">$DA$</span> through the centre is greatest,
while <span data-ref="0024.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> is greater than <span data-ref="0024.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span> and <span data-ref="0024.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span> than <span data-ref="0024.g1:/ D C /">$DC$</span>;
but, of the straight lines falling on the convex circumference <span data-ref="0024.g1:( H L K G )">$HLKG$</span>, the straight line <span data-ref="0024.g1:/ D G /">$DG$</span> between the point and the diameter <span data-ref="0024.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span> is least; and the nearer to the least <span data-ref="0024.g1:/ D G /">$DG$</span> is always less than the more remote, namely <span data-ref="0024.g1:/ D K /">$DK$</span> than <span data-ref="0024.g1:/ D L /">$DL$</span>, and <span data-ref="0024.g1:/ D L /">$DL$</span> than <span data-ref="0024.g1:/ D H /">$DH$</span>.

For let the centre of the circle <span data-ref="0024.g1:( A B C )">$ABC$</span> be taken [III. 1], and let it be <span data-ref="0024.g1:M">$M$</span>; let <span data-ref="0024.g1:/ M E /">$ME$</span>, <span data-ref="0024.g1:/ M F /">$MF$</span>, <span data-ref="0024.g1:/ M C /">$MC$</span>, <span data-ref="0024.g1:/ M K /">$MK$</span>, <span data-ref="0024.g1:/ M L /">$ML$</span>, <span data-ref="0024.g1:/ M H /">$MH$</span> be joined.

Then, since <span data-ref="0024.g1:/ A M /">$AM$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0024.g1:/ E M /">$EM$</span>,
let <span data-ref="0024.g1:/ M D /">$MD$</span> be added to each;
therefore <span data-ref="0024.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0024.g1:/ E M /">$EM$</span>, <span data-ref="0024.g1:/ M D /">$MD$</span>.
But <span data-ref="0024.g1:/ E M /">$EM$</span>, <span data-ref="0024.g1:/ M D /">$MD$</span> are greater than <span data-ref="0024.g1:/ E D /">$ED$</span>; [I. 20]
therefore <span data-ref="0024.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> is also greater than <span data-ref="0024.g1:/ E D /">$ED$</span>.

Again, since <span data-ref="0024.g1:/ M E /">$ME$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0024.g1:/ M F /">$MF$</span>,
and <span data-ref="0024.g1:/ M D /">$MD$</span> is common,
therefore <span data-ref="0024.g1:/ E M /">$EM$</span>, <span data-ref="0024.g1:/ M D /">$MD$</span> are equal to <span data-ref="0024.g1:/ F M /">$FM$</span>, <span data-ref="0024.g1:/ M D /">$MD$</span>;
and the angle <span data-ref="0024.g1:< E M D )">$EMD$</span> is greater than the angle <span data-ref="0024.g1:< F M D )">$FMD$</span>;
therefore the base <span data-ref="0024.g1:/ E D /">$ED$</span> is greater than the base <span data-ref="0024.g1:/ F D /">$FD$</span>. [I. 24]

Similarly we can prove that <span data-ref="0024.g1:/ F D /">$FD$</span> is greater than <span data-ref="0024.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>;
therefore <span data-ref="0024.g1:/ D A /">$DA$</span> is greatest, while <span data-ref="0024.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> is greater than <span data-ref="0024.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span>, and <span data-ref="0024.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span> than <span data-ref="0024.g1:/ D C /">$DC$</span>.
