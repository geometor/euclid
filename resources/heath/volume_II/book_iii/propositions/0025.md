Next, since $MK$, $KD$ are greater than $MD$, <span class="justification">[I. 20]</span>  
and $MG$ is equal to $MK$,  
therefore the remainder $KD$ is greater than the remainder $GD$,  
<p class="assertion">so that $GD$ is less than $KD$.</p>

And, since on $MD$, one of the sides of the triangle $MLD$, two straight lines $MK$, $KD$ were constructed meeting within the triangle,  
therefore $MK$, $KD$ are less than $ML$, $LD$; <span class="justification">[I. 21]</span>  
and $MK$ is equal to $ML$;  
<p class="assertion">therefore the remainder $DK$ is less than the remainder $DL$.</p>

Similarly we can prove that $DL$ is also less than $DH$;  
<p class="assertion">therefore $DG$ is least, while $DK$ is less than $DL$, and<br />
$DL$ than $DH$.</p>

I say also that only two equal straight lines will fall from the point $D$ on the circle, one on each side of the least $DG$.

On the straight line $MD$, and at the point $M$ on it, let the angle $DMB$ be constructed equal to the angle $KMD$, and let $DB$ be joined.

Then, since $MK$ is equal to $MB$,  
and $MD$ is common,  
<p class="sub-clause">the two sides $KM$, $MD$ are equal to the two sides $BM$, $MD$ respectively;</p>
and the angle $KMD$ is equal to the angle $BMD$;  
<p class="assertion">therefore the base $DK$ is equal to the base $DB$. <span class="justification">[I. 4]</span></p>

I say that no other straight line equal to the straight line $DK$ will fall on the circle from the point $D$.

For, if possible, let a straight line so fall, and let it be $DN$.  
Then, since $DK$ is equal to $DN$,  
while $DK$ is equal to $DB$,  
<p class="assertion">$DB$ is also equal to $DN$,</p>
that is, the nearer to the least $DG$ equal to the more remote: which was proved impossible.

<p class="assertion">Therefore no more than two equal straight lines will fall<br />
on the circle $ABC$ from the point $D$, one on each side of<br />
$DG$ the least.</p>

<p class="qed">Therefore etc.</p>
