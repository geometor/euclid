This demonstration is claimed by Heron (see an-Nairīzī, ed. Curtze, pp. 120—1). It is incomplete because it assumes that the point <span data-ref="0030.g1:K">$K$</span> which is taken as the centre of the circle <span data-ref="0030.g1:( A B C )">$ABC$</span> is *within* the circle <span data-ref="0030.g1:( D E F )">$DEF$</span>. It can however be completed by means of III. 8 and the corresponding proposition with reference to a point *on* the circumference of a circle which was enunciated in the note on III. 8. For (1) if the point <span data-ref="0030.g1:K">$K$</span> is *on* the circumference of the circle <span data-ref="0030.g1:( D E F )">$DEF$</span>, we obtain a contradiction of the latter proposition which asserts that only *two* equal straight lines can be drawn from <span data-ref="0030.g1:K">$K$</span> to the circumference of the circle <span data-ref="0030.g1:( D E F )">$DEF$</span>; (2) if the point <span data-ref="0030.g1:K">$K$</span> is *outside* the circle <span data-ref="0030.g1:( D E F )">$DEF$</span>, we obtain a contradiction of the corresponding part of III. 8.

Euclid's proof contains an unproved assumption, namely that the lines bisecting <span data-ref="0029.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span>, <span data-ref="0029.g1:/ B H /">$BH$</span> at right angles *will* meet in a point <span data-ref="0029.g1:P">$P$</span>. For a discussion of this assumption see note on IV. 5.

### PROPOSITION 11.

*If two circles touch one another internally, and their centres be taken, the straight line joining their centres[^25_2], if it be also produced, will fall on the point of contact[^25_3] of the circles.*

For let the two circles <span data-ref="0030.g2:( A B C )">$ABC$</span>, <span data-ref="0030.g2:( A D E )">$ADE$</span> touch one another internally at the point <span data-ref="0030.g2:A">$A$</span>, and let the centre <span data-ref="0030.g2:F">$F$</span> of the circle <span data-ref="0030.g2:( A B C )">$ABC$</span>, and the centre <span data-ref="0030.g2:G">$G$</span> of <span data-ref="0030.g2:( A D E )">$ADE$</span>, be taken;

I say that the straight line joined from <span data-ref="0030.g2:G">$G$</span> to <span data-ref="0030.g2:F">$F$</span> and produced will fall on <span data-ref="0030.g2:A">$A$</span>.

![Diagram for Proposition 11 showing circles ABC and ADE touching internally at A](0030.g2.svg)

For suppose it does not, but, if possible, let it fall as <span data-ref="0030.g2:/ F G H /">$FGH$</span>, and let <span data-ref="0030.g2:/ A F /">$AF$</span>, <span data-ref="0030.g2:/ A G /">$AG$</span> be joined.

Then, since <span data-ref="0030.g2:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0030.g2:/ G F /">$GF$</span> are greater than <span data-ref="0030.g2:/ F A /">$FA$</span>, that is, than <span data-ref="0030.g2:/ F H /">$FH$</span>,
let <span data-ref="0030.g2:/ F G /">$FG$</span> be subtracted from each;
therefore the remainder <span data-ref="0030.g2:/ A G /">$AG$</span> is greater than the remainder <span data-ref="0030.g2:/ G H /">$GH$</span>.
