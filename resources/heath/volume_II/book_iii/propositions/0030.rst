But $K$ is also the centre of the circle :g:`ABC <0030.g1:( A B C )>`.

Therefore two circles cutting one another have the same centre :g:`K <0030.g1:K>`: which is impossible. :just:`[III. 5] <III.5>`

.. assertion::

   Therefore a circle does not cut a circle at more points than two.

.. qed::

   :just:`Q. E. D."`

This demonstration is claimed by :person:`Heron <heron>` (see :person:`an-Nairīzī <an-nairizi>`, ed. Curtze, pp. 120—1). It is incomplete because it assumes that the point :g:`K <0030.g1:K>` which is taken as the centre of the circle :g:`ABC <0030.g1:( A B C )>` is *within* the circle :g:`DEF <0030.g1:( D E F )>`. It can however be completed by means of III. 8 and the corresponding proposition with reference to a point *on* the circumference of a circle which was enunciated in the note on III. 8. For (1) if the point :g:`K <0030.g1:K>` is *on* the circumference of the circle :g:`DEF <0030.g1:( D E F )>`, we obtain a contradiction of the latter proposition which asserts that only *two* equal straight lines can be drawn from :g:`K <0030.g1:K>` to the circumference of the circle :g:`DEF <0030.g1:( D E F )>`; (2) if the point :g:`K <0030.g1:K>` is *outside* the circle :g:`DEF <0030.g1:( D E F )>`, we obtain a contradiction of the corresponding part of III. 8.

Euclid's proof contains an unproved assumption, namely that the lines bisecting :g:`BG <0029.g1:/ B G />`, :g:`BH <0029.g1:/ B H />` at right angles *will* meet in a point :g:`P <0029.g1:P>`. For a discussion of this assumption see note on IV. 5.

PROPOSITION 11.
---------------

.. enunciation::

   If two circles touch one another internally, and their centres be taken, the straight line joining their centres,\ [#f1]_ if it be also produced, will fall on the point of contact\ [#f2]_ of the circles.

For let the two circles :g:`ABC <0030.g2:( A B C )>`, :g:`ADE <0030.g2:( A D E )>` touch one another internally at the point :g:`A <0030.g2:A>`, and let the centre :g:`F <0030.g2:F>` of the circle :g:`ABC <0030.g2:( A B C )>`, and the centre :g:`G <0030.g2:G>` of :g:`ADE <0030.g2:( A D E )>`, be taken;

I say that the straight line joined from :g:`G <0030.g2:G>` to :g:`F <0030.g2:F>` and produced will fall on :g:`A <0030.g2:A>`.

.. picture:: 0030.g2.svg
   :alt: Diagram for Proposition 11 showing circles ABC and ADE touching internally at A

For suppose it does not, but, if possible, let it fall as :g:`FGH <0030.g2:/ F G H />`, and let :g:`AF <0030.g2:/ A F />`, :g:`AG <0030.g2:/ A G />` be joined.

Then, since :g:`AG <0030.g2:/ A G />`, :g:`GF <0030.g2:/ G F />` are greater than :g:`FA <0030.g2:/ F A />`, that is, than :g:`FH <0030.g2:/ F H />`,

.. subclause::

   let :g:`FG <0030.g2:/ F G />` be subtracted from each;

.. assertion::

   therefore the remainder :g:`AG <0030.g2:/ A G />` is greater than the remainder :g:`GH <0030.g2:/ G H />`.
