(*b*) <span data-ref="0032.g2:A">$A$</span> may lie outside the line <span data-ref="0032.g2:[ F G ]">$FG$</span> altogether, in which case <span data-ref="0032.g2:/ F G /">$FG$</span> produced beyond <span data-ref="0032.g2:G">$G$</span> must, in consequence of result 2 above, either

(i) meet the circles in a point common to both, or

(ii) meet the circles in two points, of which that which is on the inner circle is nearer to <span data-ref="0030.g2:G">$G$</span> than the other is.

(*a*) is then proved to be impossible by means of the fact that the radius of the inner circle is less than the radius of the outer.

(*b*) (ii) is Euclid's case; and his proof holds equally of (*b*) (i), the hypothesis, namely, that <span data-ref="0030.g2:D">$D$</span> and <span data-ref="0030.g2:H">$H$</span> in the figure coincide.

Thus all alternative hypotheses are successively shown to be impossible, and the proposition is completely established.

I think, however, that this procedure may be somewhat shortened in the following manner.

In order to make Euclid's proof absolutely conclusive we have only (1) to take care to produce <span data-ref="0030.g2:/ F G /">$FG$</span> beyond <span data-ref="0030.g2:G">$G$</span>, the centre of the "inner" circle, and then (2) to prove that the point in which <span data-ref="0030.g2:/ F G /">$FG$</span> so produced meets the "inner" circle is *not further* from <span data-ref="0030.g2:G">$G$</span> than is the point in which it meets the other circle. Euclid's proof is equally valid whether the first point is nearer to <span data-ref="0030.g2:G">$G$</span> than the second or the first point and the second coincide.

If <span data-ref="0032.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span> produced beyond <span data-ref="0032.g1:G">$G$</span> does not pass through <span data-ref="0032.g1:A">$A$</span>, there are two

conceivable hypotheses: (*a*) <span data-ref="0032.g1:A">$A$</span> may lie on <span data-ref="0032.g1:/ G F /">$GF$</span> produced beyond <span data-ref="0032.g1:F">$F$</span>, or (*b*) <span data-ref="0032.g2:A">$A$</span> may be outside <span data-ref="0032.g2:[ F G ]">$FG$</span> produced either way. In either case, if <span data-ref="0032.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span> produced meets the "inner" circle in <span data-ref="0032.g1:D">$D$</span> and the other in <span data-ref="0032.g1:H">$H$</span>, and if <span data-ref="0032.g1:/ G D /">$GD$</span> is greater than <span data-ref="0032.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span>, then the "inner" circle must cut the "outer" circle at some point between <span data-ref="0032.g1:A">$A$</span> and <span data-ref="0032.g1:D">$D$</span>, say <span data-ref="0032.g1:X">$X$</span>.

But if two circles have a common point <span data-ref="0032.g1:X">$X$</span> lying on one side of the line of centres, they must have another corresponding point on the other side of the line of centres. This is clear from III. 7, 8; for the point is determined by drawing from <span data-ref="0032.g1:F">$F$</span> and <span data-ref="0032.g1:G">$G$</span>, on the opposite side to that where <span data-ref="0032.g1:X">$X$</span> is, straight lines <span data-ref="0032.g1:/ F Y /">$FY$</span>, <span data-ref="0032.g1:/ G Y /">$GY$</span> making with <span data-ref="0032.g1:/ F D /">$FD$</span> angles equal to the angles <span data-ref="0032.g1:< D F X )">$DFX$</span>, <span data-ref="0032.g1:< D G X )">$DGX$</span> respectively.

Hence the two circles will have at least three points common: which is impossible.

Therefore <span data-ref="0032.g1:/ G D /">$GD$</span> cannot be greater than <span data-ref="0032.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span>; accordingly <span data-ref="0032.g1:/ G D /">$GD$</span> must be either equal to, or less than, <span data-ref="0032.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span>, and Euclid's proof is valid.

The particular hypothesis in which <span data-ref="0032.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span> is supposed to be in the same straight line with <span data-ref="0032.g1:A">$A$</span> but <span data-ref="0032.g1:G">$G$</span> is on the side of <span data-ref="0032.g1:F">$F$</span> away from <span data-ref="0032.g1:A">$A$</span> is easily disposed of, and would in any case have been left to the reader by Euclid.

For <span data-ref="0032.g1:/ G D /">$GD$</span> is either equal to or less than <span data-ref="0032.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span>.

Therefore <span data-ref="0032.g1:/ G D /">$GD$</span> is less than <span data-ref="0032.g1:/ F H /">$FH$</span>, and therefore less than <span data-ref="0032.g1:/ F A /">$FA$</span>.

But <span data-ref="0032.g1:/ G D /">$GD$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0032.g1:/ G A /">$GA$</span>, and therefore greater than <span data-ref="0032.g1:/ F A /">$FA$</span>: which is impossible.
