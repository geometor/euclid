I say that the straight line joined from <span data-ref="0034.g1:F">$F$</span> to <span data-ref="0034.g1:G">$G$</span> will pass through the point of contact at <span data-ref="0034.g1:A">$A$</span>.

![Diagram for Proposition 12 showing two circles ABC and ADE touching externally at A](0034.g1.svg)

For suppose it does not, <sup>10</sup> but, if possible, let it pass as <span data-ref="0034.g1:/ F C D G /">$FCDG$</span>, and let <span data-ref="0034.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span>, <span data-ref="0034.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span> be joined.

Then, since the point <span data-ref="0034.g1:F">$F$</span> is the centre of the circle <span data-ref="0034.g1:( A B C )">$ABC$</span>,
<sup>15</sup> <span data-ref="0034.g1:/ F A /">$FA$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0034.g1:/ F C /">$FC$</span>.

Again, since the point <span data-ref="0034.g1:G">$G$</span> is the centre of the circle <span data-ref="0034.g1:( A D E )">$ADE$</span>,
<span data-ref="0034.g1:/ G A /">$GA$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0034.g1:/ G D /">$GD$</span>.

But <span data-ref="0034.g1:/ F A /">$FA$</span> was also proved equal to <span data-ref="0034.g1:/ F C /">$FC$</span>;
<sup>20</sup> therefore <span data-ref="0034.g1:/ F A /">$FA$</span>, <span data-ref="0034.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span> are equal to <span data-ref="0034.g1:/ F C /">$FC$</span>, <span data-ref="0034.g1:/ G D /">$GD$</span>,
so that the whole <span data-ref="0034.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span> is greater than <span data-ref="0034.g1:/ F A /">$FA$</span>, <span data-ref="0034.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>;
but it is also less [I. 20]: which is impossible.

Therefore the straight line joined from <span data-ref="0034.g1:F">$F$</span> to <span data-ref="0034.g1:G">$G$</span> will not fail to pass[^28_23] through the point of contact at <span data-ref="0034.g1:A">$A$</span>;
<sup>25</sup> therefore it will pass through it.

Therefore etc.

Q. E. D.]

[^28_23]: **will not fail to pass.** The Greek has the double negative, οὐκ ἄρα ἡ...εὐθεῖα...οὐκ ἐλεύσεται, literally "the straight line...will not *not*-pass...."

Heron says on III. 11: "Euclid in proposition 11 has supposed the two circles to touch internally, made his proposition deal with this case and proved what was sought in it. *But I will show how it is to be proved if the contact is external.*" He then gives substantially the proof and figure of III. 12. It seems clear that neither Heron nor an-Nairīzī had III. 12 in this place.

Campanus and the Arabic edition of Naṣīraddīn aṭ-Ṭūsī have nothing more of III. 12 than the following addition to III. 11. "In the case of external contact the two lines <span data-ref="0034.g1:/ A G /">$ae$</span> and <span data-ref="0034.g1:/ A F /">$eb$</span> will be greater than <span data-ref="0034.g1:/ F G /">$ab$</span>, whence <span data-ref="0034.g1:/ G D /">$ad$</span> and <span data-ref="0034.g1:/ F C /">$cb$</span> will be greater than the whole <span data-ref="0034.g1:/ F G /">$ab$</span>, which is false." (The points <span data-ref="0034.g1:G">$a$</span>, <span data-ref="0034.g1:F">$b$</span>, <span data-ref="0034.g1:C">$c$</span>, <span data-ref="0034.g1:D">$d$</span>, <span data-ref="0034.g1:A">$e$</span> correspond respectively to <span data-ref="0034.g1:G">$G$</span>, <span data-ref="0034.g1:F">$F$</span>, <span data-ref="0034.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0034.g1:D">$D$</span>, <span data-ref="0034.g1:A">$A$</span> in the above figure.) It is most probable that Theon or some other editor added Heron's proof in his edition and made Prop. 12 out of it (an-Nairīzī, ed. Curtze, pp. 121—2). An-Nairīzī and Campanus, conformably with what has been said, number Prop. 13 of Heiberg's text Prop. 12, and so on through the Book.

What was said in the note on the last proposition applies, *mutatis mutandis*, to this. Camerer proceeds in the same manner as before; and we may use the same alternative argument in this case also.
