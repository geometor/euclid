*do not meet at any other point*, Mr Taylor uses the questionable corollary. Therefore in any case his alternative procedure does not seem preferable to Euclid's.

The alternative to Eucl. III. 11—13 which finds most favour in modern continental text-books (e.g. Legendre, Baltzer, Henrici and Treutlein, Veronese, Ingrami, Enriques and Amaldi) connects the number, position and nature of the coincidences between points on two circles with the relation in which the distance between their centres stands to the length of their radii. Enriques and Amaldi, whose treatment of the different cases is typical, give the following propositions (Veronese gives them in the converse form).

1. *If the distance between the centres of two circles is greater than the sum of the radii, the two circles have no point common and are external to one another.*

Let <span data-ref="0036.g1:O">$O$</span>, <span data-ref="0036.g1:O'">$O'$</span> be the centres of the circles (which we will call "the circles <span data-ref="0036.g1:( O A )">$O$</span>, <span data-ref="0036.g1:( O' A' )">$O'$</span>"), <span data-ref="0036.g1:/ O A /">$r$</span>, <span data-ref="0036.g1:/ O' A' /">$r'$</span> their radii respectively.

Since then <span data-ref="0036.g1:/ O O' /">$OO'$</span> $>$ <span data-ref="0036.g1:/ O A /">$r$</span> $+$ <span data-ref="0036.g1:/ O' A' /">$r'$</span>, *a fortiori* <span data-ref="0036.g1:/ O O' /">$OO'$</span> $>$ <span data-ref="0036.g1:/ O A /">$r$</span>, and <span data-ref="0036.g1:O'">$O'$</span> is therefore exterior to the circle <span data-ref="0036.g1:( O A )">$O$</span>.

Next, the circumference of the circle <span data-ref="0036.g1:( O A )">$O$</span> intersects <span data-ref="0036.g1:/ O O' /">$OO'$</span> in a point <span data-ref="0036.g1:A">$A$</span>, and since <span data-ref="0036.g1:/ O O' /">$OO'$</span> $>$ <span data-ref="0036.g1:/ O A /">$r$</span> $+$ <span data-ref="0036.g1:/ O' A' /">$r'$</span>, <span data-ref="0036.g1:/ A O' /">$AO'$</span> $>$ <span data-ref="0036.g1:/ O' A' /">$r'$</span>, and <span data-ref="0036.g1:A">$A$</span> is external to the circle <span data-ref="0036.g1:( O' A' )">$O'$</span>.

![Commentary diagram for two external circles](0036.g1.svg)

But <span data-ref="0036.g1:/ O' A /">$O'A$</span> is less than any straight line, as <span data-ref="0036.g1:/ O' B /">$O'B$</span>, drawn to the circumference of the circle <span data-ref="0036.g1:( O A )">$O$</span> [III. 8]; hence all points, as <span data-ref="0036.g1:B">$B$</span>, on the circumference of the circle <span data-ref="0036.g1:( O A )">$O$</span> are external to the circle <span data-ref="0036.g1:( O' A' )">$O'$</span>.

Lastly, if <span data-ref="0036.g1:C">$C$</span> be any point internal to the circle <span data-ref="0036.g1:( O A )">$O$</span>, the sum of <span data-ref="0036.g1:/ O C /">$OC$</span>, <span data-ref="0036.g1:/ O' C /">$O'C$</span> is greater than <span data-ref="0036.g1:/ O' O /">$O'O$</span>, and *a fortiori* greater than <span data-ref="0036.g1:/ O A /">$r$</span> $+$ <span data-ref="0036.g1:/ O' A' /">$r'$</span>.

But <span data-ref="0036.g1:/ O C /">$OC$</span> is less than <span data-ref="0036.g1:/ O A /">$r$</span>: therefore <span data-ref="0036.g1:/ O' C /">$O'C$</span> is greater than <span data-ref="0036.g1:/ O' A' /">$r'$</span>, or <span data-ref="0036.g1:C">$C$</span> is external to <span data-ref="0036.g1:( O A )">$O$</span>.

Similarly we prove that any point on or within the circumference of the circle <span data-ref="0036.g1:( O' A' )">$O'$</span> is external to the circle <span data-ref="0036.g1:( O A )">$O$</span>.

2. *If the distance between the centres of two unequal circles is less than the difference of the radii, the two circumferences have no common point and the lesser circle is entirely within the greater.*

Let <span data-ref="0036.g2:O">$O$</span>, <span data-ref="0036.g2:O'">$O'$</span> be the centres of the two circles, <span data-ref="0036.g2:/ O A /">$r$</span>, <span data-ref="0036.g2:/ O' A' /">$r'$</span> their radii respectively (<span data-ref="0036.g2:/ O A /">$r$</span> $<$ <span data-ref="0036.g2:/ O' A' /">$r'$</span>).

Since <span data-ref="0036.g2:/ O O' /">$OO'$</span> $<$ <span data-ref="0036.g2:/ O' A' /">$r'$</span> $-$ <span data-ref="0036.g2:/ O A /">$r$</span>, *a fortiori* <span data-ref="0036.g2:/ O O' /">$OO'$</span> $<$ <span data-ref="0036.g2:/ O' A' /">$r'$</span>, so that <span data-ref="0036.g2:O">$O$</span> is internal to the circle <span data-ref="0036.g2:( O' A' )">$O'$</span>.

![Commentary diagram for one circle entirely within another](0036.g2.svg)

If <span data-ref="0036.g2:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0036.g2:A'">$A'$</span> be the points in which the straight line <span data-ref="0036.g2:[ O' O ]">$O'O$</span> intersects respectively the circumferences of the circles <span data-ref="0036.g2:( O A )">$O$</span>, <span data-ref="0036.g2:( O' A' )">$O'$</span>,
<p class="assertion"><span data-ref="0036.g2:/ O' O /">$O'O$</span> is less than <span data-ref="0036.g2:/ O' A' /">$O'A'$</span> $-$ <span data-ref="0036.g2:/ O A /">$OA$</span>,</p>
so that <span data-ref="0036.g2:/ O' O /">$O'O$</span> $+$ <span data-ref="0036.g2:/ O A /">$OA$</span>, or <span data-ref="0036.g2:/ O' A /">$O'A$</span>, is less than <span data-ref="0036.g2:/ O' A' /">$O'A'$</span>,
and therefore <span data-ref="0036.g2:A">$A$</span> is internal to the circle <span data-ref="0036.g2:( O' A' )">$O'$</span>.

But, of all the straight lines from <span data-ref="0036.g2:O'">$O'$</span> to the circumference of the circle <span data-ref="0036.g2:( O A )">$O$</span>, <span data-ref="0036.g2:/ O' A /">$O'A$</span> passing through the centre <span data-ref="0036.g2:O">$O$</span> is the greatest [III. 7];
whence all the points of the circumference of <span data-ref="0036.g2:( O A )">$O$</span> are internal to the circle <span data-ref="0036.g2:( O' A' )">$O'$</span>.

A similar argument to the preceding will show that all points within the circle <span data-ref="0036.g2:( O A )">$O$</span> are internal to the circle <span data-ref="0036.g2:( O' A' )">$O'$</span>.
