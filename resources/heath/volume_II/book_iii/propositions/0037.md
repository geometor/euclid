3. *If the distance between the centres of two circles is equal to the sum of the radii, the two circumferences have one point common and one only, and that point is on the line of centres. Each circle is external to the other.*

Let <span data-ref="0037.g1:O">$O$</span>, <span data-ref="0037.g1:O'">$O'$</span> be the centres, <span data-ref="0037.g1:/ O A /">$r$</span>, <span data-ref="0037.g1:/ O' A /">$r'$</span> the radii of the circles, so that <span data-ref="0037.g1:/ O O' /">$OO'$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0037.g1:/ O A /">$r$</span> $+$ <span data-ref="0037.g1:/ O' A /">$r'$</span>.

![Commentary diagram for two circles touching externally](0037.g1.svg)

Thus <span data-ref="0037.g1:/ O O' /">$OO'$</span> is greater than <span data-ref="0037.g1:/ O A /">$r$</span>, so that <span data-ref="0037.g1:O'">$O'$</span> is external to the circle <span data-ref="0037.g1:( O A )">$O$</span>, and the circumference of the circle <span data-ref="0037.g1:( O A )">$O$</span> cuts <span data-ref="0037.g1:/ O O' /">$OO'$</span> in a point <span data-ref="0037.g1:A">$A$</span>.

And, since <span data-ref="0037.g1:/ O O' /">$OO'$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0037.g1:/ O A /">$r$</span> $+$ <span data-ref="0037.g1:/ O' A /">$r'$</span>, and <span data-ref="0037.g1:/ O A /">$OA$</span> to <span data-ref="0037.g1:/ O A /">$r$</span>, it follows that <span data-ref="0037.g1:/ O' A /">$O'A$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0037.g1:/ O' A /">$r'$</span>, so that <span data-ref="0037.g1:A">$A$</span> belongs also to the circumference of the circle <span data-ref="0037.g1:( O' A )">$O'$</span>.

The proof that all other points on, and all points within, the circumference of the circle <span data-ref="0037.g1:( O A )">$O$</span> are external to the circle <span data-ref="0037.g1:( O' A )">$O'$</span> follows the similar proof of prop. 1 above. And similarly all points (except <span data-ref="0037.g1:A">$A$</span>) on, and all points within, the circumference of the circle <span data-ref="0037.g1:( O' A )">$O'$</span> are external to the circle <span data-ref="0037.g1:( O A )">$O$</span>.

The two circles, having one common point only, *touch* at that point, which lies, as shown, on the line of centres. And, since the circles are external to one another, they touch *externally*.

4. *If the distance between the centres of two unequal circles is equal to the difference between the radii, the two circumferences have one point and one only in common, and that point lies on the line of centres. The lesser circle is within the other.*

The proof is that of prop. 2 above, *mutatis mutandis*.

The circles here touch *internally* at the point on the line of centres.

5. *If the distance between the centres of two circles is less than the sum, and greater than the difference, of the radii, the two circumferences have two common points symmetrically situated with respect to the line of centres but not lying on that line.*

Let <span data-ref="0037.g2:O">$O$</span>, <span data-ref="0037.g2:O'">$O'$</span> be the centres of the two circles, <span data-ref="0037.g2:/ O M /">$r$</span>, <span data-ref="0037.g2:/ O' A /">$r'$</span> their radii, <span data-ref="0037.g2:/ O' A /">$r'$</span> being the greater, so that
<p class="assertion"><span data-ref="0037.g2:/ O' A /">$r'$</span> $-$ <span data-ref="0037.g2:/ O M /">$r$</span> $<$ <span data-ref="0037.g2:/ O O' /">$OO'$</span> $<$ <span data-ref="0037.g2:/ O M /">$r$</span> $+$ <span data-ref="0037.g2:/ O' A /">$r'$</span>.</p>

It follows that in any case <span data-ref="0037.g2:/ O O' /">$OO'$</span> $+$ <span data-ref="0037.g2:/ O M /">$r$</span> $>$ <span data-ref="0037.g2:/ O' A /">$r'$</span>, so that, if <span data-ref="0037.g2:/ O M /">$OM$</span> be taken on <span data-ref="0037.g2:[ O' O ]">$O'O$</span> produced equal to <span data-ref="0037.g2:/ O M /">$r$</span> (so that <span data-ref="0037.g2:M">$M$</span> is on the circumference of the circle <span data-ref="0037.g2:( O M )">$O$</span>), <span data-ref="0037.g2:M">$M$</span> is external to the circle <span data-ref="0037.g2:( O' A )">$O'$</span>.

We have to use the same Postulate as in Eucl. I. 1 that

*An arc of a circle which has one extremity within and the other without a given circle has one point common with the latter and only one*; from which it follows, if we consider two such arcs making a complete circumference, that, *if a circumference of a circle passes through one point internal to, and one point external to a given circle, it cuts the latter circle in two points.*

![Commentary diagram for intersecting circles, Case 1](0037.g2.svg)

We have then to prove that the circle <span data-ref="0037.g2:( O M )">$O$</span>, besides having one point <span data-ref="0037.g2:M">$M$</span> of its circumference external to the circle <span data-ref="0037.g2:( O' A )">$O'$</span>, has one other point of its circumference (<span data-ref="0037.g2:L">$L$</span>) internal to the latter circle.
