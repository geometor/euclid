Three cases have to be distinguished according as <span data-ref="0038.g1:/ O O' /">$OO'$</span> is greater than, equal to, or less than, the radius <span data-ref="0038.g1:/ O M /">$r$</span> of the lesser circle.

![Commentary diagram for intersecting circles, Case 2](0038.g1.svg)

(1) <span data-ref="0037.g2:/ O O' /">$OO'$</span> $>$ <span data-ref="0037.g2:/ O M /">$r$</span>. (See the preceding figure.)
Measure <span data-ref="0037.g2:/ O L /">$OL$</span> along <span data-ref="0037.g2:/ O O' /">$OO'$</span> equal to <span data-ref="0037.g2:/ O M /">$r$</span>, so that <span data-ref="0037.g2:L">$L$</span> lies on the circumference of the circle <span data-ref="0037.g2:( O M )">$O$</span>.

Then, since <span data-ref="0037.g2:/ O O' /">$OO'$</span> $<$ <span data-ref="0037.g2:/ O M /">$r$</span> $+$ <span data-ref="0037.g2:/ O' A /">$r'$</span>, <span data-ref="0037.g2:/ L O' /">$O'L$</span> will be less than <span data-ref="0037.g2:/ O' A /">$r'$</span>, so that <span data-ref="0037.g2:L">$L$</span> is within the circle <span data-ref="0037.g2:( O' A )">$O'$</span>.

(2) <span data-ref="0038.g1:/ O O' /">$OO'$</span> $=$ <span data-ref="0038.g1:/ O M /">$r$</span>.
In this case the circumference of the circle <span data-ref="0038.g1:( O M )">$O$</span> passes through <span data-ref="0038.g1:O'">$O'$</span>, or <span data-ref="0038.g1:L">$L$</span> coincides with <span data-ref="0038.g1:O'">$O'$</span>.

(3) <span data-ref="0038.g2:/ O O' /">$OO'$</span> $<$ <span data-ref="0038.g2:/ O M /">$r$</span>.
If we measure <span data-ref="0038.g2:/ O L /">$OL$</span> along <span data-ref="0038.g2:[ O O' ]">$OO'$</span> equal to <span data-ref="0038.g2:/ O M /">$r$</span>, the point <span data-ref="0038.g2:L">$L$</span> will lie on the circumference of the circle <span data-ref="0038.g2:( O M )">$O$</span>.

![Commentary diagram for intersecting circles, Case 3](0038.g2.svg)

Then <span data-ref="0038.g2:/ O' L /">$O'L$</span> $=$ <span data-ref="0038.g2:/ O M /">$r$</span> $-$ <span data-ref="0038.g2:/ O O' /">$OO'$</span>,
so that <span data-ref="0038.g2:/ O' L /">$O'L$</span> $<$ <span data-ref="0038.g2:/ O M /">$r$</span>, and *a fortiori* <span data-ref="0038.g2:/ O' L /">$O'L$</span> $<$ <span data-ref="0038.g2:/ O' A /">$r'$</span>, so that <span data-ref="0038.g2:L">$L$</span> lies within the circle <span data-ref="0038.g2:( O' A )">$O'$</span>.

Thus, in all three cases, since the circumference of <span data-ref="0038.g2:( O M )">$O$</span> passes through one point (<span data-ref="0038.g2:M">$M$</span>) external to, and one point (<span data-ref="0038.g2:L">$L$</span>) internal to, the circle <span data-ref="0038.g2:( O' A )">$O'$</span>, the two circumferences intersect in two points <span data-ref="0038.g2:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0038.g2:B">$B$</span> [Post.].

And <span data-ref="0038.g2:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0038.g2:B">$B$</span> cannot lie on the line of centres <span data-ref="0038.g2:[ O O' ]">$OO'$</span>, since this straight line intersects the circle <span data-ref="0038.g2:( O M )">$O$</span> in <span data-ref="0038.g2:L">$L$</span>, <span data-ref="0038.g2:M">$M$</span> only, and of these points one is inside, the other outside, the circle <span data-ref="0038.g2:( O' A )">$O'$</span>.

Since <span data-ref="0038.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is a common chord of both circles, the straight line bisecting it at right angles passes through both centres, i.e. is identical with <span data-ref="0038.g2:[ O O' ]">$OO'$</span>.

And again by means of III. 7, 8 we prove that all points except <span data-ref="0038.g2:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0038.g2:B">$B$</span> on the arc <span data-ref="0038.g2:< A L B )">$ALB$</span> lie within the circle <span data-ref="0038.g2:( O' A )">$O'$</span>, and all points except <span data-ref="0038.g2:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0038.g2:B">$B$</span> on the arc <span data-ref="0038.g2:< A M B )">$AMB$</span> outside that circle; and so on.

## PROPOSITION 13.

<p class="enunciation">A circle does not touch a circle at more points than one,<br />
whether it touch it internally or externally.</p>

For, if possible, let the circle <span data-ref="0038.g3:( A B D C )">$ABDC$</span> touch the circle
<span data-ref="0038.g3:( E B F D )">$EBFD$</span>, first internally, at more
<sup>5</sup> points than one, namely <span data-ref="0038.g3:D">$D$</span>, <span data-ref="0038.g3:B">$B$</span>.

![Proposition 13 diagram](0038.g3.svg)

Let the centre <span data-ref="0038.g3:G">$G$</span> of the circle
<span data-ref="0038.g3:( A B D C )">$ABDC$</span>, and the centre <span data-ref="0038.g3:H">$H$</span> of
<span data-ref="0038.g3:( E B F D )">$EBFD$</span>, be taken.

Therefore the straight line
<sup>10</sup> joined from <span data-ref="0038.g3:G">$G$</span> to <span data-ref="0038.g3:H">$H$</span> will fall on
<span data-ref="0038.g3:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0038.g3:D">$D$</span>. <span class="justification">[III. 11]</span>

Let it so fall, as <span data-ref="0038.g3:/ B G H D /">$BGHD$</span>.

Then, since the point <span data-ref="0038.g3:G">$G$</span> is
the centre of the circle <span data-ref="0038.g3:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span>,

<p class="assertion"><sup>15</sup> <span data-ref="0038.g3:/ B G /">$BG$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0038.g3:/ G D /">$GD$</span>;</p>
