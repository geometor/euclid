Three cases have to be distinguished according as :g:`$OO'$ <0038.g1:/ O O' />` is greater than, equal to, or less than, the radius :g:`$r$ <0038.g1:/ O M />` of the lesser circle.

.. picture:: 0038.g1.svg
   :alt: Commentary diagram for intersecting circles, Case 2

(1) :g:`$OO'$ <0037.g2:/ O O' />` $>$ :g:`$r$ <0037.g2:/ O M />`. (See the preceding figure.)

Measure :g:`$OL$ <0037.g2:/ O L />` along :g:`$OO'$ <0037.g2:/ O O' />` equal to :g:`$r$ <0037.g2:/ O M />`, so that :g:`$L$ <0037.g2:L>` lies on the circumference of the circle :g:`$O$ <0037.g2:( O M )>`.

Then, since :g:`$OO'$ <0037.g2:/ O O' />` $<$ :g:`$r$ <0037.g2:/ O M />` $+$ :g:`$r'$ <0037.g2:/ O' A />`, :g:`$O'L$ <0037.g2:/ L O' />` will be less than :g:`$r'$ <0037.g2:/ O' A />`, so that :g:`$L$ <0037.g2:L>` is within the circle :g:`$O'$ <0037.g2:( O' A )>`.

(2) :g:`$OO'$ <0038.g1:/ O O' />` $=$ :g:`$r$ <0038.g1:/ O M />`.

In this case the circumference of the circle :g:`$O$ <0038.g1:( O M )>` passes through :g:`$O'$ <0038.g1:O'>`, or :g:`$L$ <0038.g1:L>` coincides with :g:`$O'$ <0038.g1:O'>`.

(3) :g:`$OO'$ <0038.g2:/ O O' />` $<$ :g:`$r$ <0038.g2:/ O M />`.

If we measure :g:`$OL$ <0038.g2:/ O L />` along :g:`$OO'$ <0038.g2:[ O O' ]>` equal to :g:`$r$ <0038.g2:/ O M />`, the point :g:`$L$ <0038.g2:L>` will lie on the circumference of the circle :g:`$O$ <0038.g2:( O M )>`.

.. picture:: 0038.g2.svg
   :alt: Commentary diagram for intersecting circles, Case 3

Then :g:`$O'L$ <0038.g2:/ O' L />` $=$ :g:`$r$ <0038.g2:/ O M />` $-$ :g:`$OO'$ <0038.g2:/ O O' />`,
so that :g:`$O'L$ <0038.g2:/ O' L />` $<$ :g:`$r$ <0038.g2:/ O M />`, and :term:`*a fortiori* <a-fortiori>` :g:`$O'L$ <0038.g2:/ O' L />` $<$ :g:`$r'$ <0038.g2:/ O' A />`, so that :g:`$L$ <0038.g2:L>` lies within the circle :g:`$O'$ <0038.g2:( O' A )>`.

Thus, in all three cases, since the circumference of :g:`$O$ <0038.g2:( O M )>` passes through one point (:g:`$M$ <0038.g2:M>`) external to, and one point (:g:`$L$ <0038.g2:L>`) internal to, the circle :g:`$O'$ <0038.g2:( O' A )>`, the two circumferences intersect in two points :g:`$A$ <0038.g2:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0038.g2:B>` [Post.].

And :g:`$A$ <0038.g2:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0038.g2:B>` cannot lie on the line of centres :g:`$OO'$ <0038.g2:[ O O' ]>`, since this straight line intersects the circle :g:`$O$ <0038.g2:( O M )>` in :g:`$L$ <0038.g2:L>`, :g:`$M$ <0038.g2:M>` only, and of these points one is inside, the other outside, the circle :g:`$O'$ <0038.g2:( O' A )>`.

Since :g:`$AB$ <0038.g2:/ A B />` is a common chord of both circles, the straight line bisecting it at right angles passes through both centres, i.e. is identical with :g:`$OO'$ <0038.g2:[ O O' ]>`.

And again by means of III. 7, 8 we prove that all points except :g:`$A$ <0038.g2:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0038.g2:B>` on the arc :g:`$ALB$ <0038.g2:< A L B )>` lie within the circle :g:`$O'$ <0038.g2:( O' A )>`, and all points except :g:`$A$ <0038.g2:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0038.g2:B>` on the arc :g:`$AMB$ <0038.g2:< A M B )>` outside that circle; and so on.

PROPOSITION 13.
---------------

.. enunciation::

   A circle does not touch a circle at more points than one, whether it touch it internally or externally.

For, if possible, let the circle :g:`$ABDC$ <0038.g3:( A B D C )>`\ [#f1]_ touch the circle
:g:`$EBFD$ <0038.g3:( E B F D )>`, first internally, at more
points than one, namely :g:`$D$ <0038.g3:D>`, :g:`$B$ <0038.g3:B>`.

.. picture:: 0038.g3.svg
   :alt: Proposition 13 diagram

Let the centre :g:`$G$ <0038.g3:G>` of the circle
:g:`$ABDC$ <0038.g3:( A B D C )>`, and the centre :g:`$H$ <0038.g3:H>` of
:g:`$EBFD$ <0038.g3:( E B F D )>`, be taken.

Therefore the straight line
joined from :g:`$G$ <0038.g3:G>` to :g:`$H$ <0038.g3:H>` will fall on
:g:`$B$ <0038.g3:B>`, :g:`$D$ <0038.g3:D>`. :just:`[III. 11] <III.11>`

Let it so fall, as :g:`$BGHD$ <0038.g3:/ B G H D />`.

Then, since the point :g:`$G$ <0038.g3:G>` is
the centre of the circle :g:`$ABCD$ <0038.g3:( A B C D )>`,
