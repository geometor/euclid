and let it be <span data-ref="0041.g1:E">$E$</span>; from <span data-ref="0041.g1:E">$E$</span> let <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ E G /">$EG$</span> be drawn perpendicular to <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, and let <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span>, <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ E C /">$EC$</span> be joined.

![Proposition 14 diagram](0041.g1.svg)

Then, since a straight line <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> through the centre cuts a straight line <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> not through the centre at right angles, it also bisects it. [III. 3]

Therefore <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ F B /">$FB$</span>;
therefore <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is double of <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span>.

For the same reason
<span data-ref="0041.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> is also double of <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ C G /">$CG$</span>;
and <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>;
therefore <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span> is also equal to <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ C G /">$CG$</span>.

And, since <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ E C /">$EC$</span>,
the square on <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> is also equal to the square on <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ E C /">$EC$</span>.

But the squares on <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span>, <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> are equal to the square on <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span>, for the angle at <span data-ref="0041.g1:< E F A )">$F$</span> is right;
and the squares on <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ E G /">$EG$</span>, <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ G C /">$GC$</span> are equal to the square on <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ E C /">$EC$</span>, for the angle at <span data-ref="0041.g1:< E G C )">$G$</span> is right; [I. 47]
therefore the squares on <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span>, <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ F E /">$FE$</span> are equal to the squares on <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ C G /">$CG$</span>, <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ G E /">$GE$</span>,
of which the square on <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span> is equal to the square on <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ C G /">$CG$</span>, for <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ C G /">$CG$</span>;
therefore the square on <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ F E /">$FE$</span> which remains is equal to the square on <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ E G /">$EG$</span>,
therefore <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ E G /">$EG$</span>.

But in a circle straight lines are said to be equally distant from the centre when the perpendiculars drawn to them from the centre are equal; [III. Def. 4]
therefore <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> are equally distant from the centre.

Next, let the straight lines <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> be equally distant from the centre; that is, let <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> be equal to <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ E G /">$EG$</span>.

I say that <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is also equal to <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>.

For, with the same construction, we can prove, similarly, that <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is double of <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span>, and <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> of <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ C G /">$CG$</span>.

And, since <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ C E /">$CE$</span>,
the square on <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> is equal to the square on <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ C E /">$CE$</span>.

But the squares on <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ F A /">$FA$</span> are equal to the square on <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span>, and the squares on <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ E G /">$EG$</span>, <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ G C /">$GC$</span> equal to the square on <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ C E /">$CE$</span>. [I. 47]
