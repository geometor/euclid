Then, since a straight line $EF$ through
the centre cuts a straight line $AB$ not through
the centre at right angles, it also bisects it. <span class="justification">[III. 3]</span>

<p class="assertion">Therefore $AF$ is equal to $FB$;<br />
therefore $AB$ is double of $AF$.</p>

For the same reason

<p class="assertion">$CD$ is also double of $CG$;</p>

and $AB$ is equal to $CD$;

<p class="assertion">therefore $AF$ is also equal to $CG$.</p>

And, since $AE$ is equal to $EC$,

<p class="assertion">the square on $AE$ is also equal to the square on $EC$.</p>

But the squares on $AF, EF$ are equal to the square on $AE$,
for the angle at $F$ is right;
and the squares on $EG, GC$ are equal to the square on $EC$,
for the angle at $G$ is right; <span class="justification">[I. 47]</span>

<p class="assertion">therefore the squares on $AF, FE$ are equal to the<br />
squares on $CG, GE$,</p>

of which the square on $AF$ is equal to the square on $CG$,
for $AF$ is equal to $CG$;

<p class="assertion">therefore the square on $FE$ which remains is equal to<br />
the square on $EG$,</p>

<p class="assertion">therefore $EF$ is equal to $EG$.</p>

But in a circle straight lines are said to be equally distant
from the centre when the perpendiculars drawn to them from
the centre are equal; <span class="justification">[III. Def. 4]</span>

<p class="assertion">therefore $AB, CD$ are equally distant from the centre.</p>

Next, let the straight lines $AB, CD$ be equally distant
from the centre; that is, let $EF$ be equal to $EG$.

I say that $AB$ is also equal to $CD$.

For, with the same construction, we can prove, similarly,
that $AB$ is double of $AF$, and $CD$ of $CG$.

And, since $AE$ is equal to $CE$,

<p class="assertion">the square on $AE$ is equal to the square on $CE$.</p>

But the squares on $EF, FA$ are equal to the square on $AE$,
and the squares on $EG, GC$ equal to the square on $CE$. <span class="justification">[I. 47]</span>
