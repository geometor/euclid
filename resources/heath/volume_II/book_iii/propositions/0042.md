Therefore the squares on <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ F A /">$FA$</span> are equal to the squares on <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ E G /">$EG$</span>, <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ G C /">$GC$</span>,
of which the square on <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> is equal to the square on <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ E G /">$EG$</span>, for <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ E G /">$EG$</span>;
therefore the square on <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span> which remains is equal to the square on <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ C G /">$CG$</span>;
therefore <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ C G /">$CG$</span>.

And <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is double of <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span>, and <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> double of <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ C G /">$CG$</span>;
therefore <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0041.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>.

<p class="assertion">therefore $AB$ is equal to $CD$.</p>

<p class="qed">Therefore etc. <span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

Heron (an-Nairīzī, pp. 125—7) has an elaborate addition to this proposition in which he proves, first by *reductio ad absurdum*, and then directly, that the centre of the circle falls between the two chords.

## PROPOSITION 15.

<p class="enunciation">Of straight lines in a circle the diameter is greatest,<br />
and of the rest the nearer to the centre is always greater than<br />
the more remote.</p>

Let <span data-ref="0042.g1:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span> be a circle, let <span data-ref="0042.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> be its diameter and <span data-ref="0042.g1:E">$E$</span> the centre; and let <span data-ref="0042.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> be nearer to the diameter <span data-ref="0042.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>[^37_5], and <span data-ref="0042.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span> more remote;
I say that <span data-ref="0042.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> is greatest and <span data-ref="0042.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> greater than <span data-ref="0042.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span>.

![Diagram for Proposition 15](0042.g1.svg)

For from the centre <span data-ref="0042.g1:E">$E$</span> let <span data-ref="0042.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span>, <span data-ref="0042.g1:/ E K /">$EK$</span> be drawn perpendicular to <span data-ref="0042.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, <span data-ref="0042.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span>.

Then, since <span data-ref="0042.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is nearer to the centre and <span data-ref="0042.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span> more remote, <span data-ref="0042.g1:/ E K /">$EK$</span> is greater than <span data-ref="0042.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span>. [III. Def. 5]

Let <span data-ref="0042.g1:/ E L /">$EL$</span> be made equal to <span data-ref="0042.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span>, through <span data-ref="0042.g1:L">$L$</span> let <span data-ref="0042.g1:/ L M /">$LM$</span> be drawn at right angles to <span data-ref="0042.g1:/ E K /">$EK$</span> and carried through to <span data-ref="0042.g1:N">$N$</span>, and let <span data-ref="0042.g1:/ M E /">$ME$</span>, <span data-ref="0042.g1:/ E N /">$EN$</span>, <span data-ref="0042.g1:/ F E /">$FE$</span>, <span data-ref="0042.g1:/ E G /">$EG$</span> be joined.

Then, since <span data-ref="0042.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0042.g1:/ E L /">$EL$</span>,
<span data-ref="0042.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is also equal to <span data-ref="0042.g1:/ M N /">$MN$</span>. [III. 14]

Again, since <span data-ref="0042.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0042.g1:/ E M /">$EM$</span>, and <span data-ref="0042.g1:/ E D /">$ED$</span> to <span data-ref="0042.g1:/ E N /">$EN$</span>,
<span data-ref="0042.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0042.g1:/ M E /">$ME$</span>, <span data-ref="0042.g1:/ E N /">$EN$</span>.
