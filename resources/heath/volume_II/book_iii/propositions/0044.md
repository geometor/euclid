I say that the straight line drawn from <span data-ref="0044.g1:A">$A$</span> at right angles to <span data-ref="0044.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> from its extremity will fall outside the circle.

![Diagram for Proposition 16](0044.g1.svg)

For suppose it does not, but, if possible, let it fall within as <span data-ref="0044.g1:/ C A /">$CA$</span>, and let <span data-ref="0044.g1:/ D C /">$DC$</span> be joined.

Since <span data-ref="0044.g1:/ D A /">$DA$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0044.g1:/ D C /">$DC$</span>,
the angle <span data-ref="0044.g1:< D A C )">$DAC$</span> is also equal to the angle <span data-ref="0044.g1:< A C D )">$ACD$</span>. [I. 5]

But the angle <span data-ref="0044.g1:< D A C )">$DAC$</span> is right;
therefore the angle <span data-ref="0044.g1:< A C D )">$ACD$</span> is also right:
thus, in the triangle <span data-ref="0044.g1:< A C D >">$ACD$</span>, the two angles <span data-ref="0044.g1:< D A C )">$DAC$</span>, <span data-ref="0044.g1:< A C D )">$ACD$</span> are equal to two right angles: which is impossible. [I. 17]

Therefore the straight line drawn from the point <span data-ref="0044.g1:A">$A$</span> at right angles to <span data-ref="0044.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span> will not fall within the circle.

Similarly we can prove that neither will it fall on the circumference;
therefore it will fall outside.

Let it fall as <span data-ref="0044.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span>;
I say next that into the space between the straight line <span data-ref="0044.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> and the circumference <span data-ref="0044.g1:( C H A )">$CHA$</span> another straight line cannot be interposed.

For, if possible, let another straight line be so interposed, as <span data-ref="0044.g1:/ F A /">$FA$</span>, and let <span data-ref="0044.g1:/ D G /">$DG$</span> be drawn from the point <span data-ref="0044.g1:D">$D$</span> perpendicular to <span data-ref="0044.g1:/ F A /">$FA$</span>.

Then, since the angle <span data-ref="0044.g1:< A G D )">$AGD$</span> is right,
and the angle <span data-ref="0044.g1:< D A G )">$DAG$</span> is less than a right angle,
<span data-ref="0044.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> is greater than <span data-ref="0044.g1:/ D G /">$DG$</span>. [I. 19]

But <span data-ref="0044.g1:/ D A /">$DA$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0044.g1:/ D H /">$DH$</span>;
therefore <span data-ref="0044.g1:/ D H /">$DH$</span> is greater than <span data-ref="0044.g1:/ D G /">$DG$</span>, the less than the greater: which is impossible.

Therefore another straight line cannot be interposed into the space between the straight line and the circumference.

I say further that the angle of the semicircle contained by the straight line <span data-ref="0044.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span> and the circumference <span data-ref="0044.g1:( C H A )">$CHA$</span> is greater than any acute rectilineal angle,
