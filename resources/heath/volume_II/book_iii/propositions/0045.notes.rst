Transcription Notes: Folio 0045 (Page 39)
=========================================

Summary & Folio Continuity
--------------------------

- **Folio**: ``0045`` (Page 39)
- **Content**: Conclusion of the proof of Book III, Proposition 16, its Porism, and the beginning of Heath's extensive historical commentary on the "angle of a semicircle" and the "horn-like" or *cornicular* angle (κερατοειδὴς γωνία).
- **Continuity**: Continues mid-sentence from Folio ``0044`` (Page 38) ("$CHA$, and any rectilineal angle less...") and breaks mid-word at the bottom of the page ("like that of the semi-"), continuing onto Folio ``0046`` (Page 40) ("circle, or by a straight line and a concave circumference...").

Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
--------------------------------------------

- **Punctuation Before Porism**: Note the dash after the period at the end of the main proof (``straight line $AE$.—``) preceding ``PORISM.``.
- **Greek Terms**:

  - ``οὐ παρεμπεσεῖται`` ("will not fall in between", translated as "cannot be interposed") in the apparatus note on line 4.
  - ``κερατοειδὴς γωνία`` (*horn-like* or *cornicular* angle), used frequently by Proclus though absent by name in Euclid.

- **Historical Context**: Heath conjectures that Euclid included the "angle of a semicircle" and the "remaining angle" (along with the definition of the angle of a segment, III. Def. 7) not for subsequent deductive use in the *Elements*, but because the topic was actively debated among Greek geometers of his era—analogous to unused definitions like rhombus and rhomboid in Book I.
