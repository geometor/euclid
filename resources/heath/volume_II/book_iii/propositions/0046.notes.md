## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Non-Archimedean Magnitude Paradox (Proclus, pp. 121–122)**: Proclus points out the conflict between III. 16 and the Archimedean/Eudoxean axiom of magnitudes (V. Def. 4): if cornicular and rectilineal angles were homogeneous magnitudes having a ratio, a multiple of the cornicular angle would exceed a rectilineal angle, contradicting III. 16.
- **Democritus Conjecture**: Heath proposes emending the corrupt title in Diogenes Laertius IX. 47 from `περὶ διαφορῆς γνώμονος` (or `γνώμης`) `ἢ περὶ ψαύσιος κύκλου καὶ σφαίρης` to `περὶ διαφορῆς γωνίης ἢ περὶ ψαύσιος κύκλου καὶ σφαίρης` (*On a difference in an angle or on contact with a circle and a sphere*), comparing it with Proclus p. 50, 4 (`πῶς δὲ γωνιῶν διαφορὰς λέγομεν καὶ αὐξήσεις αὐτῶν... πῶς δὲ τὰς ἁφὰς τῶν κύκλων ἢ τῶν εὐθειῶν`).
- **Democritus's Cone Paradox & Archimedes' *Method***: Connects Democritus's interest in tangency and cornicular angles with his dilemma on parallel sections of a cone (Plutarch, *On Common Notions*, XXXIX. 3) and Heiberg's 1906 discovery of Archimedes' *Method* crediting Democritus with first stating (without proof) the volume of the cone and pyramid.
