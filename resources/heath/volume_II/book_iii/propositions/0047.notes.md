## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Campanus's Continuity Paradox**: Campanus observed that a secant rotating into a tangent jumps from making an acute angle *less* than the angle of a semicircle to a right angle *greater* than the angle of a semicircle without ever passing through equality, challenging the principle of continuity and Euclid X. 1 unless curved and rectilineal angles are denied univocal status (*univoce* vs. *simpliciter*).
- **Cardano (1501–1576)**: Formulated the paradox of two quantities where one continually increases (angles of contact of smaller and smaller circles) and the other continually diminishes (acute rectilineal angles), yet the first always remains less than the second.
- **Peletier (1557) vs. Clavius (1607)**: Peletier argued that the "angle of contact" is not an angle or a quantity at all (*not*-angles, *not*-quantities, *nothings*), that all angles of semicircles are equal to a right angle, and that the essence of an angle lies in *cutting* (*inclination*) rather than contact (*immersion*).
