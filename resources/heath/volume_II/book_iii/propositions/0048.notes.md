## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Clavius's Defense of Non-Archimedean Quantities**: Clavius maintained that angles of contact are genuine quantities divisible by intermediate arcs, escaping violation of X. 1 by treating them as heterogeneous to rectilineal angles, and remarked that Euclid would not have "sweated so much" (*desudasset*) over a nothing.
- **Vieta (1540–1603) and Galileo (1564–1642)**: Treated the circle as a plane figure (or limit of an inscribed polygon) with an infinite number of sides, so that a tangent coincides with an infinitesimal side and forms no angle.
- **John Wallis (1616–1703) vs. Vincent Leotaud (1595–1672)**: In *De angulo contactus et semicirculi tractatus* (1656) and *A defense of the treatise of the angle of contact* (1684/1685, replying to Leotaud's *Cyclomathia* of 1663), Wallis resolved the controversy by distinguishing inclination/initial tendency towards separation (*Angulus (seu gradus divaricationis) Distantia non est sed Inceptivus distantiae*)—where the tangent lies on the curve `ἀκλινῶς` (without inclination)—from *degree of curvature* (*gradus curvitatis*), replacing Clavius's *angulus contactus* with a homogeneous measure of curvature.
