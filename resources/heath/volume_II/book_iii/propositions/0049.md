The words usually given as a part of the corollary "and that a straight line touches a circle at one point only, since in fact the straight line meeting it in two points was proved to fall within it" are omitted by Heiberg as being an undoubted addition of Theon's. It was Simson who added the further remark that "it is evident that there can be but one straight line which touches the circle at the same point."

## PROPOSITION 17.

<p class="enunciation">From a given point to draw a straight line touching a<br />
given circle.</p>

Let <span data-ref="0049.g1:A">$A$</span> be the given point, and <span data-ref="0049.g1:( B C D )">$BCD$</span> the given circle; thus it is required to draw from the point <span data-ref="0049.g1:A">$A$</span> a straight line touching the circle <span data-ref="0049.g1:( B C D )">$BCD$</span>.

For let the centre <span data-ref="0049.g1:E">$E$</span> of the circle be taken; [III. 1]
let <span data-ref="0049.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> be joined, and with centre <span data-ref="0049.g1:E">$E$</span> and distance <span data-ref="0049.g1:/ E A /">$EA$</span> let the circle <span data-ref="0049.g1:( A F G )">$AFG$</span> be described;
from <span data-ref="0049.g1:D">$D$</span> let <span data-ref="0049.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span> be drawn at right angles to <span data-ref="0049.g1:/ E A /">$EA$</span>,
and let <span data-ref="0049.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, <span data-ref="0049.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> be joined;
I say that <span data-ref="0049.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> has been drawn from the point <span data-ref="0049.g1:A">$A$</span> touching the circle <span data-ref="0049.g1:( B C D )">$BCD$</span>.

![Diagram for Proposition 17](0049.g1.svg)

For, since <span data-ref="0049.g1:E">$E$</span> is the centre of the circles <span data-ref="0049.g1:( B C D )">$BCD$</span>, <span data-ref="0049.g1:( A F G )">$AFG$</span>,
<span data-ref="0049.g1:/ E A /">$EA$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0049.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, and <span data-ref="0049.g1:/ E D /">$ED$</span> to <span data-ref="0049.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span>;
therefore the two sides <span data-ref="0049.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span>, <span data-ref="0049.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span> are equal to the two sides <span data-ref="0049.g1:/ F E /">$FE$</span>, <span data-ref="0049.g1:/ E D /">$ED$</span>:
and they contain a common angle, the angle at <span data-ref="0049.g1:E">$E$</span>;
therefore the base <span data-ref="0049.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span> is equal to the base <span data-ref="0049.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>,
and the triangle <span data-ref="0049.g1:< D E F >">$DEF$</span> is equal to the triangle <span data-ref="0049.g1:< B E A >">$BEA$</span>,
and the remaining angles to the remaining angles; [I. 4]
therefore the angle <span data-ref="0049.g1:< E D F )">$EDF$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0049.g1:< E B A )">$EBA$</span>.

But the angle <span data-ref="0049.g1:< E D F )">$EDF$</span> is right;
therefore the angle <span data-ref="0049.g1:< E B A )">$EBA$</span> is also right.

Now <span data-ref="0049.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span> is a radius;
and the straight line drawn at right angles to the diameter of a circle, from its extremity, touches the circle; [III. 16, Por.]
therefore <span data-ref="0049.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> touches the circle <span data-ref="0049.g1:( B C D )">$BCD$</span>.

Therefore from the given point <span data-ref="0049.g1:A">$A$</span> the straight line <span data-ref="0049.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> has been drawn touching the circle <span data-ref="0049.g1:( B C D )">$BCD$</span>.
