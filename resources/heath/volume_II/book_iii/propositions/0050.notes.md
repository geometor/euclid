## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Heron's Corollary to III. 17**: Heath cites Heron (preserved in an-Nairīzī, ed. Curtze, p. 130) as explicitly stating that two tangents can be drawn from an external point, that they are equal in length, and that they are equally inclined to the line joining the external point to the centre.
- **Modern Construction via III. 31**: Heath contrasts Euclid's concentric-circle construction in III. 17 with the standard modern textbook construction using the circle on $AE$ as diameter (which depends on III. 31).
- **Reductio Proof of III. 18**: Euclid proves that the radius $FC$ to the point of contact $C$ is perpendicular to the tangent $DE$ by assuming another line $FG \perp DE$ (cutting the circle at $B$), which makes $\angle FGC$ right, $\angle FCG$ acute (I. 17), $FC > FG$ (I. 19), and hence $FB = FC > FG$, the part greater than the whole.
- **Cross-Page Footnote Reference**: The lexical note on p. 45 (`3. the tangent, ἡ ἐφαπτομένη.`) refers to line 3 of the enunciation of Proposition 18 (`[^45_3]`).
