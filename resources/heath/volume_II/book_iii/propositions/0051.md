**3. the tangent**, $\text{ἡ ἐφαπτομένη}$.

---

Just as III. 3 contains two *partial* converses of the Porism to III. 1, so the present proposition and the next give two partial converses of the corollary to III. 16. We may show their relation thus: suppose three things, (1) a tangent at a point of a circle, (2) a straight line drawn from the centre to the point of contact, (3) right angles made at the point of contact [with (1) or (2) as the case may be]. Then the corollary to III. 16 asserts that (2) and (3) together give (1), III. 18 that (1) and (2) give (3), and III. 19 that (1) and (3) give (2), i.e. that the straight line drawn from the point of contact at right angles to the tangent passes through the centre.

---

## PROPOSITION 19.

<p class="enunciation">If a straight line touch a circle, and from the point of<br />
contact a straight line be drawn at right angles to the tangent,<br />
the centre of the circle will be on the straight line so drawn.</p>

For let a straight line <span data-ref="0051.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> touch the circle <span data-ref="0051.g1:( A B C )">$ABC$</span> at the point <span data-ref="0051.g1:C">$C$</span>, and from <span data-ref="0051.g1:C">$C$</span> let <span data-ref="0051.g1:/ C A /">$CA$</span> be drawn at right angles to <span data-ref="0051.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>;
I say that the centre of the circle is on <span data-ref="0051.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>.

![Diagram for Proposition 19](0051.g1.svg)

For suppose it is not, but, if possible, let <span data-ref="0051.g1:F">$F$</span> be the centre, and let <span data-ref="0051.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span> be joined.

Since a straight line <span data-ref="0051.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> touches the circle <span data-ref="0051.g1:( A B C )">$ABC$</span>,
and <span data-ref="0051.g1:/ F C /">$FC$</span> has been joined from the centre to the point of contact,
<span data-ref="0051.g1:/ F C /">$FC$</span> is perpendicular to <span data-ref="0051.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>; [III. 18]
therefore the angle <span data-ref="0051.g1:< F C E )">$FCE$</span> is right.

But the angle <span data-ref="0051.g1:< A C E )">$ACE$</span> is also right;
therefore the angle <span data-ref="0051.g1:< F C E )">$FCE$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0051.g1:< A C E )">$ACE$</span>,
the less to the greater: which is impossible.

Therefore <span data-ref="0051.g1:F">$F$</span> is not the centre of the circle <span data-ref="0051.g1:( A B C )">$ABC$</span>.

Similarly we can prove that neither is any other point except a point on <span data-ref="0051.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>.

<p class="qed">Therefore etc. <span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>
