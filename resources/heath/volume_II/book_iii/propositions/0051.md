**3. the tangent**, $\text{ἡ ἐφαπτομένη}$.

---

Just as III. 3 contains two *partial* converses of the Porism to III. 1, so the present proposition and the next give two partial converses of the corollary to III. 16. We may show their relation thus: suppose three things, (1) a tangent at a point of a circle, (2) a straight line drawn from the centre to the point of contact, (3) right angles made at the point of contact [with (1) or (2) as the case may be]. Then the corollary to III. 16 asserts that (2) and (3) together give (1), III. 18 that (1) and (2) give (3), and III. 19 that (1) and (3) give (2), i.e. that the straight line drawn from the point of contact at right angles to the tangent passes through the centre.

---

## PROPOSITION 19.

<p class="enunciation">If a straight line touch a circle, and from the point of<br />
contact a straight line be drawn at right angles to the tangent,<br />
the centre of the circle will be on the straight line so drawn.</p>

For let a straight line $DE$ touch the circle $ABC$ at the
point $C$, and from $C$ let $CA$ be
drawn at right angles to $DE$;
I say that the centre of the circle
is on $AC$.

![Diagram for Proposition 19](0051.g1.svg)

For suppose it is not, but, if
possible, let $F$ be the centre,
and let $CF$ be joined.

Since a straight line $DE$ touches
the circle $ABC$,
and $FC$ has been joined from the
centre to the point of contact,

<p class="assertion">$FC$ is perpendicular to $DE$; <span class="justification">[III. 18]</span><br />
therefore the angle $FCE$ is right.</p>

But the angle $ACE$ is also right;

<p class="assertion">therefore the angle $FCE$ is equal to the angle $ACE$,<br />
the less to the greater: which is impossible.</p>

Therefore $F$ is not the centre of the circle $ABC$.

Similarly we can prove that neither is any other point
except a point on $AC$.

<p class="qed">Therefore etc. <span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>
