### PROPOSITION 20.

*In a circle the angle at the centre is double of the angle at the circumference, when the angles have the same circumference as base.*

Let <span data-ref="0052.g1:( A B C )">$ABC$</span> be a circle, let the angle <span data-ref="0052.g1:< B E C )">$BEC$</span> be an angle <sup>5</sup> at its centre, and the angle <span data-ref="0052.g1:< B A C )">$BAC$</span> an angle at the circumference, and let them have the same circumference <span data-ref="0052.g1:( B C )">$BC$</span> as base;
I say that the angle <span data-ref="0052.g1:< B E C )">$BEC$</span> is double of <sup>10</sup> the angle <span data-ref="0052.g1:< B A C )">$BAC$</span>.

![Diagram for Proposition 20](0052.g1.svg)

For let <span data-ref="0052.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> be joined and drawn through to <span data-ref="0052.g1:F">$F$</span>.

Then, since <span data-ref="0052.g1:/ E A /">$EA$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0052.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span>,
the angle <span data-ref="0052.g1:< E A B )">$EAB$</span> is also equal to the <sup>15</sup> angle <span data-ref="0052.g1:< E B A )">$EBA$</span>; [I. 5]
therefore the angles <span data-ref="0052.g1:< E A B )">$EAB$</span>, <span data-ref="0052.g1:< E B A )">$EBA$</span> are double of the angle <span data-ref="0052.g1:< E A B )">$EAB$</span>.

But the angle <span data-ref="0052.g1:< B E F )">$BEF$</span> is equal to the angles <span data-ref="0052.g1:< E A B )">$EAB$</span>, <span data-ref="0052.g1:< E B A )">$EBA$</span>; [I. 32]
therefore the angle <span data-ref="0052.g1:< B E F )">$BEF$</span> is also double of the angle <sup>20</sup> <span data-ref="0052.g1:< E A B )">$EAB$</span>.

For the same reason
the angle <span data-ref="0052.g1:< F E C )">$FEC$</span> is also double of the angle <span data-ref="0052.g1:< E A C )">$EAC$</span>.

Therefore the whole angle <span data-ref="0052.g1:< B E C )">$BEC$</span> is double of the whole angle <span data-ref="0052.g1:< B A C )">$BAC$</span>.
