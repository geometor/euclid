Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
--------------------------------------------

- **Technical Term** ``κλάω`` **/** ``κεκλάσθαι`` ("**to inflect**"): Heath explains ``κεκλάσθω δὴ πάλιν`` (used elliptically without ``εὐθεῖα``) as the technical Greek geometrical term for drawing a broken line from two points meeting at a point on a curve or straight line ("inflecting"), citing Aristotle, *Analytica Posteriora* I. 10, 76b9.
- **Heron's Decomposition of Reflex Central Angles**: Because classical Greek geometry restricted rectilineal angles to be strictly less than two right angles ($180^\circ$), Heron could not speak of a single "angle greater than two right angles" at the centre $D$. Instead, by producing $BD$ and $CD$ to $F$ and $G$, he decomposed the reflex region around $D$ into three convex Euclidean angles ($\angle BDG$, $\angle GDF$, $\angle FDC$) that "fill up the four right angles" together with $\angle BDC$, proving that their sum is double of any angle $\angle BEC$ in the minor segment $BEC$.
