## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Heron's Proof of III. 20 (Extended), III. 21, and III. 22**: Using $\triangle CDE$ and $\triangle BDE$, Heron shows $\angle GDE + \angle FDE = 2\angle BEC$. Since $\angle FDG = \angle BDC$ (vertical angles by I. 15), the sum of the three angles $\angle BDG + \angle GDF + \angle FDC$ equals $\angle GDE + \angle FDE = 2\angle BEC$. Adding $\angle BDC = 2\angle BAC$ gives $4\text{ right angles} = 2(\angle BAC + \angle BEC)$, whence $\angle BAC + \angle BEC = 2\text{ right angles}$ (III. 22).
- **Greek Conception of Angle (Exclusion of Straight and Reflex Angles)**: Heath cites Euclid's definition of an angle as an inclination (`κλίσις`), Proclus' "four-sided triangle" (`τετράπλευρον τρίγωνον`), Zenodorus' "hollow-angled" (`κοιλogώνιον`), Charles Lutwidge Dodgson (*Euclid and his Modern Rivals*, p. 160), and Proclus' commentary on I. 13 (`ἐὰν γωνίας ποιῇ`, "*if it make angles*") to prove that neither a straight angle nor a re-entrant/reflex angle was considered an angle in Greek geometry.
