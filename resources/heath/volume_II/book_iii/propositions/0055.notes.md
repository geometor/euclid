## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Proclus on Rectilineal and Solid Angles**: Heath completes the quotation from Proclus (ed. Friedlein, p. 292, 13–20), explicitly stating that every rectilineal angle is less than two right angles, just as every solid angle is less than four right angles (cf. XI. 21). Heath also previews his note on VI. 33, arguing against the common belief that VI. 33 tacitly extends the definition of an angle.
- **Implicit Use of Distributive Property for Doubles (V. 1 and V. 5)**: Heath notes Todhunter's and Clavius' observation that adding/subtracting doubles in III. 20 ($2B \pm 2D = 2(B \pm D)$) is a special case of Book V, Propositions 1 and 5, though immediately evident from Common Notions 2 and 3 without Book V's general theory of magnitudes.
- **Scope Restriction in Proposition 21**: Because III. 20 in Euclid's formulation requires a convex central angle $\angle BFD < 2\text{ right angles}$, Euclid's proof of III. 21 strictly applies only when segment $BAED$ is greater than a semicircle.
