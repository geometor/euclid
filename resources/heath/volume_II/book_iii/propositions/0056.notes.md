## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Alternative Proof of III. 21 (Segment $\le$ Semicircle)**:
  - By drawing diameter $AFC$ through centre $F$ and joining $CE$, the angles $\angle BAD$ and $\angle BED$ are decomposed into $(\angle BAC + \angle CAD)$ and $(\angle BEC + \angle CED)$, where each pair lies in a segment ($BADC$ and $CBED$) greater than a semicircle, reducing the case to the first case without requiring two intersecting diameters.
- **Converse of III. 21 & Anticipation of IV. 5**:
  - Heath notes that although the problem of circumscribing a circle about a given triangle is formally stated in IV. 5, Euclid's construction in III. 9 already demonstrates how to pass a circle through three non-collinear points.
- **Serenus of Antinoeia (*De sectione coni*, Props. 52, 53)**:
  - Serenus uses the auxiliary circle $AHB$ centred at the midpoint $C$ of the arc $ACB$ with radius $CA = CB$ to fold the chord $AD$ onto $DE$ ($AD$ produced along $BD$ to $E$), converting the sum of two chords $AD + DB$ into a single chord $BE$ of the outer circle.
