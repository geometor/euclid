## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Greek Terminology (`οὐ συσταθήσεται`, `[^53_1]`)**:
  - Heath notes that `cannot be constructed` translates `οὐ συσταθήσεται`, the exact same verb (`συνίστασθαι`) used in Book I, Proposition 7 for constructing two triangles on the same base on the same side with equal conterminous sides.
- **Logical Interdependence of III. 10, III. 23, and III. 24 (Simson's Critique)**:
  - In III. 23, Euclid tacitly assumes that one of the two segments $ACB, ADB$ on the common chord $AB$ lies entirely inside the other, whereas in III. 24 he explicitly invokes III. 10 (two circles cannot cut at more than two points) to rule out the "awry" (`παραλλάξει`) case $CGD$.
  - Simson moves the appeal to III. 10 into III. 23 (where it justifies drawing one segment inside the other) and replaces "unequal" by "not coinciding with one another" in III. 23, thereby reducing III. 24 to an immediate corollary of superposition and III. 23.
- **Clavius on Opposite Sides**:
  - Clavius remarks that rotating one segment around the line $AB$ shows that similar unequal segments cannot exist on opposite sides of $AB$ either, though Euclid specifies "on the same side" (`ἐπὶ τὰ αὐτὰ μέρη`) for direct application of I. 16.
