:person:`Clavius <clavius>` and the other early editors point out that, while the words “on the same side” in the enunciation are necessary for Euclid's proof, it is equally true that neither can there be two similar and unequal segments on *opposite* sides of the same straight line; this is at once made clear by causing one of the segments to revolve round the base till it is on the same side with the other.

:person:`Simson <simson>` observes with reason that, while Euclid in the following proposition, III. 24, thinks it necessary to dispose of the hypothesis that, if two similar segments on equal bases are applied to one another with the bases coincident, the segments cannot cut in any other point than the extremities of the base (since otherwise two circles would cut one another in more points than two), this remark is an equally necessary preliminary to III. 23, in order that we may be justified in drawing the segments as being one inside the other. :person:`Simson <simson>` accordingly begins his proof of III. 23 thus:

“Then, because the circle :g:`$ACB$ <0058.g2:( A C B )>` cuts the circle :g:`$ADB$ <0058.g2:( A D B )>` in the two points :g:`$A$ <0058.g2:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0058.g2:B>`, they cannot cut one another in any other point:
One of the segments must therefore fall within the other.
Let :g:`$ACB$ <0058.g2:( A C B )>` fall within :g:`$ADB$ <0058.g2:( A D B )>` and draw the straight line :g:`$ACD$ <0058.g2:/ A C D />`, etc.”

:person:`Simson <simson>` has also substituted “not coinciding with one another” for “unequal” in Euclid's enunciation.

Then in III. 24 :person:`Simson <simson>` leaves out the words referring to the hypothesis that the segment :g:`$AEB$ <0060.g1:( A E B )>` when applied to the other :g:`$CFD$ <0060.g1:( C F D )>` may be “otherwise placed as :g:`$CGD$ <0060.g1:( C G D )>`”; in fact, after stating that :g:`$AB$ <0060.g1:/ A B />` must coincide with :g:`$CD$ <0060.g1:/ C D />`, he merely adds words quoting the result of III. 23: “Therefore, the straight line :g:`$AB$ <0060.g1:/ A B />` coinciding with :g:`$CD$ <0060.g1:/ C D />`, the segment :g:`$AEB$ <0060.g1:( A E B )>` must coincide with the segment :g:`$CFD$ <0060.g1:( C F D )>`, and is therefore equal to it.”

PROPOSITION 24.
---------------

.. enunciation::

   Similar segments of circles on equal straight lines are equal to one another.
