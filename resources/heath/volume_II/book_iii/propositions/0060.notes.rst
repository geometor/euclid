Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
--------------------------------------------

- **Greek Terminology** (``παραλλάξει``, ``[#f54_1]_``):

  - In line 15, ``fall awry`` translates ``παραλλάξει`` (future indicative of ``παραλλάσσω``), matching the superposition terminology of Book I, Proposition 8. It signifies an oblique or partially overlapping displacement where the arc neither lies wholly inside nor wholly outside the target arc.

- **Punctuation and Logical Scope in III. 24**:

  - Heath explains his editorial decision to place a semicolon after ``or outside it;`` (where Heiberg's Greek text has a comma), making it clear that the clause ``and a circle cuts a circle at more points than two: which is impossible [III. 10]`` refutes only the third hypothesis (``παραλλάξει, ὡς τὸ ΓΗΔ``), whereas the first two hypotheses ("within it, or outside it") are refuted tacitly by III. 23.
