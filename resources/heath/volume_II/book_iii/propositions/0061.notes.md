## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Trichotomy by Angle Comparison ($\angle ABD \gtreqless \angle BAD$)**:
  - Rather than bisecting the chord $AB$ at right angles to meet the perpendicular bisector $BD$ of $AC$, Euclid constructs $\angle BAE = \angle ABD$ at $A$ on $BA$ so that $AE$ meets $BD$ (or $BD$ produced) at $E$, immediately yielding $EA = EB$ by I. 6.
  - Because $D$ is the midpoint of $AC$ and $BD \perp AC$, the condition $\angle ABD \gtreqless \angle BAD$ is equivalent to $AD \gtreqless BD$, which directly determines whether the centre $E$ falls outside the segment ($ABC < \text{semicircle}$), on the base at $D$ ($ABC = \text{semicircle}$), or inside the segment ($ABC > \text{semicircle}$).
- **Appeal to III. 9**:
  - Once $AE = EB = EC$ is established, Euclid cites III. 9 (if from a point within a circle more than two equal straight lines fall on the circumference, that point is the centre) to guarantee that the circle described with centre $E$ and radius $EA$ coincides with the given arc $ABC$ rather than merely passing through the three discrete points $A, B, C$.
