Then, since the angle :g:`$ABE$ <0061.g1:< A B E )>` is equal to
the angle :g:`$BAE$ <0061.g1:< B A E )>`,
the straight line :g:`$EB$ <0061.g1:/ E B />` is also equal to
:g:`$EA$ <0061.g1:/ E A />`. :just:`[I. 6] <I.6>`

And, since :g:`$AD$ <0061.g1:/ A D />` is equal to :g:`$DC$ <0061.g1:/ D C />`,
and :g:`$DE$ <0061.g1:/ D E />` is common,
the two sides :g:`$AD$ <0061.g1:/ A D />`, :g:`$DE$ <0061.g1:/ D E />` are equal to the two sides :g:`$CD$ <0061.g1:/ C D />`, :g:`$DE$ <0061.g1:/ D E />`
respectively;
and the angle :g:`$ADE$ <0061.g1:< A D E )>` is equal to the angle :g:`$CDE$ <0061.g1:< C D E )>`, for each is
right;

.. assertion::

   therefore the base :g:`$AE$ <0061.g1:/ A E />` is equal to the base :g:`$CE$ <0061.g1:/ C E />`.

But :g:`$AE$ <0061.g1:/ A E />` was proved equal to :g:`$BE$ <0061.g1:/ B E />`;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$BE$ <0061.g1:/ B E />` is also equal to :g:`$CE$ <0061.g1:/ C E />`;

therefore the three straight lines :g:`$AE$ <0061.g1:/ A E />`, :g:`$EB$ <0061.g1:/ E B />`, :g:`$EC$ <0061.g1:/ E C />` are equal to
one another.

Therefore the circle drawn with centre :g:`$E$ <0061.g1:E>` and distance
one of the straight lines :g:`$AE$ <0061.g1:/ A E />`, :g:`$EB$ <0061.g1:/ E B />`, :g:`$EC$ <0061.g1:/ E C />` will also pass through
the remaining points and will have been completed. :just:`[III. 9] <III.9>`

Therefore, given a segment of a circle, the complete circle
has been described.

And it is manifest that the segment :g:`$ABC$ <0061.g1:( A B C )>` is less than a
semicircle, because the centre :g:`$E$ <0061.g1:E>` happens to be outside it.
