Let <span data-ref="0063.g1:( A B C )">$ABC$</span>, <span data-ref="0063.g1:( D E F )">$DEF$</span> be equal circles, and in them let there be equal angles, namely at the centres the angles <span data-ref="0063.g1:< B G C )">$BGC$</span>, <span data-ref="0063.g1:< E H F )">$EHF$</span>, and at the circumferences the angles <span data-ref="0063.g1:< B A C )">$BAC$</span>, <span data-ref="0063.g1:< E D F )">$EDF$</span>;
I say that the circumference <span data-ref="0063.g1:( B K C )">$BKC$</span> is equal to the circumference <span data-ref="0063.g1:( E L F )">$ELF$</span>.

![Diagram](0063.g1.svg)

For let <span data-ref="0063.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, <span data-ref="0063.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> be joined.

Now, since the circles <span data-ref="0063.g1:( A B C )">$ABC$</span>, <span data-ref="0063.g1:( D E F )">$DEF$</span> are equal,
the radii are equal.

Thus the two straight lines <span data-ref="0063.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span>, <span data-ref="0063.g1:/ G C /">$GC$</span> are equal to the two straight lines <span data-ref="0063.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span>, <span data-ref="0063.g1:/ H F /">$HF$</span>;
and the angle at <span data-ref="0063.g1:G">$G$</span> is equal to the angle at <span data-ref="0063.g1:H">$H$</span>;
therefore the base <span data-ref="0063.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is equal to the base <span data-ref="0063.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>. [I. 4]

And, since the angle at <span data-ref="0063.g1:A">$A$</span> is equal to the angle at <span data-ref="0063.g1:D">$D$</span>,
the segment <span data-ref="0063.g1:( B A C )">$BAC$</span> is similar to the segment <span data-ref="0063.g1:( E D F )">$EDF$</span>; [III. Def. 11]
and they are upon equal straight lines.

But similar segments of circles on equal straight lines are equal to one another; [III. 24]
therefore the segment <span data-ref="0063.g1:( B A C )">$BAC$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0063.g1:( E D F )">$EDF$</span>.

But the whole circle <span data-ref="0063.g1:( A B C )">$ABC$</span> is also equal to the whole circle <span data-ref="0063.g1:( D E F )">$DEF$</span>;
therefore the circumference <span data-ref="0063.g1:( B K C )">$BKC$</span> which remains is equal to the circumference <span data-ref="0063.g1:( E L F )">$ELF$</span>.

But the whole circle $ABC$ is also equal to the whole circle
$DEF$;
therefore the circumference $BKC$ which remains is equal to
the circumference $ELF$.

<p class="qed">Therefore etc. <span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

As in III. 21, if Euclid's proof is to cover all cases, it requires us to take cognisance of "angles at the centre" which are equal to or greater than two right angles. Otherwise we must deal separately with the cases where the angle at the circumference is equal to or greater than a right angle. The case of an *obtuse* angle at the circumference can of course be reduced by means of III. 22 to the case of an acute angle at the circumference; and, in case the angle at the circumference is right, it is readily proved, by drawing the radii to the vertex of the angle and to the other extremities of the lines containing it, that the latter two radii are in a straight line, whence they make equal bases in the two circles as in Euclid's proof.
