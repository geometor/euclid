## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Structure of Proof**: Euclid proves that equal central angles ($\angle BGC = \angle EHF$) and equal inscribed angles ($\angle BAC = \angle EDF$) in equal circles stand on equal arcs ($BKC = ELF$) by:
  1. Using SAS congruence (`[I. 4]`) on $\triangle BGC$ and $\triangle EHF$ to show the chords $BC$ and $EF$ are equal.
  2. Noting that segments $BAC$ and $EDF$ are similar (`[III. Def. 11]`) and stand on equal straight lines $BC, EF$, so by `[III. 24]` the segments $BAC$ and $EDF$ are equal.
  3. Subtracting the equal segments from the equal whole circles $ABC, DEF$ to conclude that the remaining circumferences $BKC$ and $ELF$ are equal.
- **Heath's Case Analysis**: Heath notes that unless "angles at the centre" equal to or greater than two right angles (straight and reflex angles) are admitted (as in III. 21), separate cases are needed when the inscribed angle is right or obtuse. An obtuse inscribed angle reduces to the acute case via III. 22 (opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral), and a right inscribed angle implies the central radii form a straight line (diameter).
