Lardner has another way of dealing with the right angle or obtuse angle at the circumference. In either case, he says, "bisect them, and the halves of them are equal, and it can be proved, as above, that the arcs upon which these halves stand are equal, whence it follows that the arcs on which the given angles stand are equal."

---

## PROPOSITION 27.

<p class="enunciation">In equal circles angles standing on equal circumferences<br />
are equal to one another, whether they stand at the centres or<br />
at the circumferences.</p>

For in equal circles <span data-ref="0064.g1:( A B C )">$ABC$</span>, <span data-ref="0064.g1:( D E F )">$DEF$</span>, on equal circumferences
<span data-ref="0064.g1:( B C )">$BC$</span>, <span data-ref="0064.g1:( E F )">$EF$</span>, let the angles <span data-ref="0064.g1:< B G C )">$BGC$</span>, <span data-ref="0064.g1:< E H F )">$EHF$</span> stand at the centres <span data-ref="0064.g1:G">$G$</span>,
<span data-ref="0064.g1:H">$H$</span>, and the angles <span data-ref="0064.g1:< B A C )">$BAC$</span>, <span data-ref="0064.g1:< E D F )">$EDF$</span> at the circumferences;
I say that the angle <span data-ref="0064.g1:< B G C )">$BGC$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0064.g1:< E H F )">$EHF$</span>,
and the angle <span data-ref="0064.g1:< B A C )">$BAC$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0064.g1:< E D F )">$EDF$</span>.

![Diagram](0064.g1.svg)

For, if the angle <span data-ref="0064.g1:< B G C )">$BGC$</span> is unequal to the angle <span data-ref="0064.g1:< E H F )">$EHF$</span>,

<p class="assertion">one of them is greater.</p>

Let the angle <span data-ref="0064.g1:< B G C )">$BGC$</span> be greater: and on the straight line <span data-ref="0064.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span>,
and at the point <span data-ref="0064.g1:G">$G$</span> on it, let the angle <span data-ref="0064.g1:< B G K )">$BGK$</span> be constructed
equal to the angle <span data-ref="0064.g1:< E H F )">$EHF$</span>. <span class="justification">[I. 23]</span>

Now equal angles stand on equal circumferences, when
they are at the centres; <span class="justification">[III. 26]</span>

<p class="assertion">therefore the circumference <span data-ref="0064.g1:( B K )">$BK$</span> is equal to the circum-<br />
ference <span data-ref="0064.g1:( E F )">$EF$</span>.</p>

But <span data-ref="0064.g1:( E F )">$EF$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0064.g1:( B C )">$BC$</span>;

<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0064.g1:( B K )">$BK$</span> is also equal to <span data-ref="0064.g1:( B C )">$BC$</span>, the less to the<br />
greater: which is impossible.</p>

Therefore the angle <span data-ref="0064.g1:< B G C )">$BGC$</span> is not unequal to the angle
<span data-ref="0064.g1:< E H F )">$EHF$</span>;

<p class="assertion">therefore it is equal to it.</p>
