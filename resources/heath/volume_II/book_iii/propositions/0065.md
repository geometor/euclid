## PROPOSITION 28.

<p class="enunciation">In equal circles equal straight lines cut off equal circum-<br />
ferences, the greater equal to the greater and the less to the<br />
less.</p>

Let $ABC, DEF$ be equal circles, and in the circles let
$AB, DE$ be equal straight lines cutting off $ACB, DFE$ as
greater circumferences and $AGB, DHE$ as lesser;
I say that the greater circumference $ACB$ is equal to the
greater circumference $DFE$, and the less circumference $AGB$
to $DHE$.

![Diagram](0065.g1.svg)

For let the centres $K, L$ of the circles be taken, and let
$AK, KB, DL, LE$ be joined.

Now, since the circles are equal,

<p class="assertion">the radii are also equal;<br />
therefore the two sides $AK, KB$ are equal to the two</p>
sides $DL, LE$;
and the base $AB$ is equal to the base $DE$;

<p class="assertion">therefore the angle $AKB$ is equal to the angle $DLE$. <span class="justification">[I. 8]</span></p>

But equal angles stand on equal circumferences, when
they are at the centres; <span class="justification">[III. 26]</span>

<p class="assertion">therefore the circumference $AGB$ is equal to $DHE$.</p>
