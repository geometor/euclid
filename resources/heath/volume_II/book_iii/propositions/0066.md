---

Euclid's proof does not in terms cover the particular case in which the chord in one circle passes through its centre; but indeed this was scarcely worth giving, as the proof can easily be supplied. Since the chord in one circle passes through its centre, the chord in the second circle must also be a diameter of that circle, for equal circles are those which have equal diameters, and all other chords in any circle are less than its diameter [III. 15]; hence the segments cut off in each circle are semicircles, and these must be equal because the circles are equal.

## PROPOSITION 29.

<p class="enunciation">In equal circles equal circumferences are subtended by equal<br />
straight lines.</p>

Let <span data-ref="0066.g1:( A B C )">$ABC$</span>, <span data-ref="0066.g1:( D E F )">$DEF$</span> be equal circles, and in them let equal
circumferences <span data-ref="0066.g1:( B G C )">$BGC$</span>, <span data-ref="0066.g1:( E H F )">$EHF$</span> be cut off; and let the straight
lines <span data-ref="0066.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, <span data-ref="0066.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> be joined;
I say that <span data-ref="0066.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0066.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>.

![Diagram](0066.g1.svg)

For let the centres of the circles be taken, and let them
be <span data-ref="0066.g1:K">$K$</span>, <span data-ref="0066.g1:L">$L$</span>; let <span data-ref="0066.g1:/ B K /">$BK$</span>, <span data-ref="0066.g1:/ K C /">$KC$</span>, <span data-ref="0066.g1:/ E L /">$EL$</span>, <span data-ref="0066.g1:/ L F /">$LF$</span> be joined.

Now, since the circumference <span data-ref="0066.g1:( B G C )">$BGC$</span> is equal to the
circumference <span data-ref="0066.g1:( E H F )">$EHF$</span>,

<p class="assertion">the angle <span data-ref="0066.g1:< B K C )">$BKC$</span> is also equal to the angle <span data-ref="0066.g1:< E L F )">$ELF$</span>. <span class="justification">[III. 27]</span></p>

And, since the circles <span data-ref="0066.g1:( A B C )">$ABC$</span>, <span data-ref="0066.g1:( D E F )">$DEF$</span> are equal,

<p class="assertion">the radii are also equal;</p>

therefore the two sides <span data-ref="0066.g1:/ B K /">$BK$</span>, <span data-ref="0066.g1:/ K C /">$KC$</span> are equal to the two sides
<span data-ref="0066.g1:/ E L /">$EL$</span>, <span data-ref="0066.g1:/ L F /">$LF$</span>; and they contain equal angles;

<p class="assertion">therefore the base <span data-ref="0066.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is equal to the base <span data-ref="0066.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>. <span class="justification">[I. 4]</span></p>

<p class="qed">Therefore etc.</p>
