## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Diameter Case in III. 28**: Heath points out that if the chord in one circle passes through the centre (is a diameter), the equal chord in the equal circle must also be a diameter by `[III. Def. 1]` (equal circles have equal diameters) and `[III. 15]` (the diameter is the greatest chord), so both cut off equal semicircles.
- **Proposition 29 Proof**: Converse of III. 28. From $\text{arc } BGC = \text{arc } EHF$, `[III. 27]` gives $\angle BKC = \angle ELF$, and SAS congruence (`[I. 4]`) gives $BC = EF$.
