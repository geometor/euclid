The particular case of this converse of III. 28 in which the given arcs are arcs of semicircles is even easier than the corresponding case of III. 28 itself.

The propositions III. 26—29 are of course equally true if the *same* circle is taken instead of *two equal* circles.

---

### PROPOSITION 30.

*To bisect a given circumference.*

Let <span data-ref="0067.g1:( A D B )">$ADB$</span> be the given circumference;
thus it is required to bisect the circumference <span data-ref="0067.g1:( A D B )">$ADB$</span>.

Let <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> be joined and bisected at <span data-ref="0067.g1:C">$C$</span>; from the point <span data-ref="0067.g1:C">$C$</span> let <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> be drawn at right angles to the straight line <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, and let <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> be joined.

![Diagram](0067.g1.svg)

Then, since <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span>, and <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> is common,
the two sides <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> are equal to the two sides <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>;
and the angle <span data-ref="0067.g1:< A C D )">$ACD$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0067.g1:< B C D )">$BCD$</span>, for each is right;
therefore the base <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> is equal to the base <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span>. [I. 4]

But equal straight lines cut off equal circumferences, the greater equal to the greater, and the less to the less; [III. 28]
and each of the circumferences <span data-ref="0067.g1:( A D )">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0067.g1:( D B )">$DB$</span> is less than a semicircle;
therefore the circumference <span data-ref="0067.g1:( A D )">$AD$</span> is equal to the circumference <span data-ref="0067.g1:( D B )">$DB$</span>.

Therefore the given circumference has been bisected at the point <span data-ref="0067.g1:D">$D$</span>.

Q. E. F.

### PROPOSITION 31.

*In a circle the angle in the semicircle is right, that in a greater segment less than a right angle, and that in a less segment greater than a right angle; and further the angle of the greater segment is greater than a right angle, and the angle of the less segment less than a right angle.*
