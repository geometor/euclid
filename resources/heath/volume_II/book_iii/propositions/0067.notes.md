## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Application of III. 26–29 to a Single Circle**: Heath explicitly notes what Euclid assumes tacitly in III. 30 and throughout the *Elements*: that III. 26–29 hold equally within the *same* circle as in *two equal* circles.
- **Proposition 30 Proof**: To bisect arc $ADB$, Euclid bisects chord $AB$ at $C$ (`[I. 10]`), erects perpendicular $CD \perp AB$ (`[I. 11]`), shows $AD = DB$ by SAS (`[I. 4]`), and notes that since $CD$ is the perpendicular bisector of $AB$, it passes through the centre (III. 1 Por.), so each of the arcs $AD, DB$ is less than a semicircle; hence by `[III. 28]` (applied within a single circle) the lesser arcs $AD$ and $DB$ are equal.
