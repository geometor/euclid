title: "Proposition 31 (Continued)"

Let $ABCD$ be a circle, let $BC$ be its diameter, and $E$ its
centre, and let $BA$, $AC$, $AD$, $DC$
be joined;
I say that the angle $BAC$ in the
semicircle $BAC$ is right,
the angle $ABC$ in the segment $ABC$
greater than the semicircle is less
than a right angle,
and the angle $ADC$ in the segment
$ADC$ less than the semicircle is
greater than a right angle.

![Diagram](0068.g1.svg)

Let $AE$ be joined, and let $BA$
be carried through to $F$.

Then, since $BE$ is equal to $EA$,
<p class="assertion">the angle $ABE$ is also equal to the angle $BAE$. <span class="justification">[I. 5]</span></p>

Again, since $CE$ is equal to $EA$,
<p class="assertion">the angle $ACE$ is also equal to the angle $CAE$. <span class="justification">[I. 5]</span></p>

Therefore the whole angle $BAC$ is equal to the two angles
$ABC$, $ACB$.

But the angle $FAC$ exterior to the triangle $ABC$ is also
equal to the two angles $ABC$, $ACB$; <span class="justification">[I. 32]</span>
<p class="assertion">therefore the angle $BAC$ is also equal to the angle $FAC$;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore each is right; <span class="justification">[I. Def. 10]</span></p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the angle $BAC$ in the semicircle $BAC$ is right.</p>

Next, since in the triangle $ABC$ the two angles $ABC$,
$BAC$ are less than two right angles, <span class="justification">[I. 17]</span>
and the angle $BAC$ is a right angle,
<p class="assertion">the angle $ABC$ is less than a right angle;</p>
and it is the angle in the segment $ABC$ greater than the
semicircle.

Next, since $ABCD$ is a quadrilateral in a circle,
and the opposite angles of quadrilaterals in circles are equal
to two right angles, <span class="justification">[III. 22]</span>
while the angle $ABC$ is less than a right angle,
therefore the angle $ADC$ which remains is greater than a
right angle;
and it is the angle in the segment $ADC$ less than the semi-
circle.
