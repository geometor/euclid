## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Thales' Theorem (Angle in a Semicircle)**: Joining the vertex $A$ to the centre $E$ divides $\triangle ABC$ into two isosceles triangles $\triangle EAB$ and $\triangle EAC$. By `[I. 5]`, $\angle EBA = \angle EAB$ and $\angle ECA = \angle EAC$, so $\angle BAC = \angle ABC + \angle ACB$. Producing $BA$ to $F$, the exterior angle $\angle FAC$ also equals $\angle ABC + \angle ACB$ by `[I. 32]`, whence $\angle BAC = \angle FAC$, making each a right angle by `[I. Def. 10]`.
- **Angles in Greater and Less Segments**:
  - Since $\angle BAC$ is right, `[I. 17]` (any two angles of a triangle are together less than two right angles) implies $\angle ABC$ (the angle in the greater segment $ABC$) is acute.
  - Since $ABCD$ is a cyclic quadrilateral, `[III. 22]` implies $\angle ABC + \angle ADC = 2\text{ right angles}$, whence $\angle ADC$ (the angle in the less segment $ADC$) is obtuse.
