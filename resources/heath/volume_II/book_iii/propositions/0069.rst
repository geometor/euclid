I say further that the angle of the greater segment, namely
that contained by the circumference :g:`$ABC$ <0068.g1:( A B C )>` and the straight
line :g:`$AC$ <0068.g1:/ A C />`, is greater than a right angle;
and the angle of the less segment, namely that contained by
the circumference :g:`$ADC$ <0068.g1:( A D C )>` and the straight line :g:`$AC$ <0068.g1:/ A C />`, is less than
a right angle.

This is at once manifest.
For, since the angle contained by the straight lines :g:`$BA$ <0068.g1:/ B A />`, :g:`$AC$ <0068.g1:/ A C />`
is right,

.. assertion::

   | the angle contained by the circumference :g:`$ABC$ <0068.g1:( A B C )>` and the
   | straight line :g:`$AC$ <0068.g1:/ A C />` is greater than a right angle.

Again, since the angle contained by the straight lines
:g:`$AC$ <0068.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$AF$ <0068.g1:/ A F />` is right,

.. assertion::

   | the angle contained by the straight line :g:`$CA$ <0068.g1:/ C A />` and the
   | circumference :g:`$ADC$ <0068.g1:( A D C )>` is less than a right angle.

.. qed::

   Therefore etc. :just:`Q. E. D.`

----

As already stated, this proposition is immediately deducible from III. 20 if that theorem is extended so as to include the case where the segment is equal to or less than a semicircle, and where consequently the "angle at the centre" is equal to two right angles or greater than two right angles respectively.

There are indications in :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>` that the proof of the first part of the theorem in use before Euclid's time proceeded on different lines. Two passages of :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>` refer to the proposition that the angle in a semicircle is a right angle. The first passage is :cite:`*Anal. Post.* II. 11, 94 a 28 <aristotle:anal-post:ii-11-94-a-28>`: "Why is the angle in a semicircle a right angle? Or what makes it a right angle? (τίνος ὄντος ὀρθή;) Suppose :g:`$A$ <0069.g1:A>` to be a right angle, :g:`$B$ <0069.g1:B>` half of two right angles, :g:`$C$ <0069.g1:C>` the angle in a semicircle. Then :g:`$B$ <0069.g1:B>` is the cause of :g:`$A$ <0069.g1:A>`, the right angle, being an attribute of :g:`$C$ <0069.g1:C>`, the angle in the semicircle. For :g:`$B$ <0069.g1:B>` is equal to :g:`$A$ <0069.g1:A>`, and :g:`$C$ <0069.g1:C>` to :g:`$B$ <0069.g1:B>`; for :g:`$C$ <0069.g1:C>` is half of two right angles. Therefore it is in virtue of :g:`$B$ <0069.g1:B>` being half of two right angles that :g:`$A$ <0069.g1:A>` is an attribute of :g:`$C$ <0069.g1:C>`; and the latter means the fact that the angle in a semicircle is right." Now this passage by itself would be consistent with a proof like Euclid's or the alternative interpolated proof next to be mentioned. But the second passage throws a different light on the subject. This is :cite:`*Metaph.* 1051 a 26 <aristotle:met:1051-a-26>`: "Why is the angle in a semicircle a right angle invariably (καθόλου)?
