For let <span data-ref="0072.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span> be drawn from <span data-ref="0072.g1:B">$B$</span> at right angles to <span data-ref="0072.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>,
let a point <span data-ref="0072.g1:C">$C$</span> be taken at random on the circumference <span data-ref="0072.g1:( B D )">$BD$</span>,
and let <span data-ref="0072.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0072.g1:/ D C /">$DC$</span>, <span data-ref="0072.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span> be joined.

Then, since a straight line <span data-ref="0072.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> touches the circle <span data-ref="0072.g1:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span> at <span data-ref="0072.g1:B">$B$</span>,
and <span data-ref="0072.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span> has been drawn from the point of contact at right angles to the tangent,
the centre of the circle <span data-ref="0072.g1:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span> is on <span data-ref="0072.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span>. [III. 19]

Therefore <span data-ref="0072.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span> is a diameter of the circle <span data-ref="0072.g1:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span>;
therefore the angle <span data-ref="0072.g1:< A D B )">$ADB$</span>, being an angle in a semicircle, is right. [III. 31]

Therefore the remaining angles <span data-ref="0072.g1:< B A D )">$BAD$</span>, <span data-ref="0072.g1:< A B D )">$ABD$</span> are equal to one right angle. [I. 32]

But the angle <span data-ref="0072.g1:< A B F )">$ABF$</span> is also right;
therefore the angle <span data-ref="0072.g1:< A B F )">$ABF$</span> is equal to the angles <span data-ref="0072.g1:< B A D )">$BAD$</span>, <span data-ref="0072.g1:< A B D )">$ABD$</span>.

Let the angle <span data-ref="0072.g1:< A B D )">$ABD$</span> be subtracted from each;
therefore the angle <span data-ref="0072.g1:< D B F )">$DBF$</span> which remains is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0072.g1:< B A D )">$BAD$</span> in the alternate segment of the circle.

Next, since <span data-ref="0072.g1:< A B C D >">$ABCD$</span> is a quadrilateral in a circle,
its opposite angles are equal to two right angles. [III. 22]

But the angles <span data-ref="0072.g1:< D B F )">$DBF$</span>, <span data-ref="0072.g1:< D B E )">$DBE$</span> are also equal to two right angles;
therefore the angles <span data-ref="0072.g1:< D B F )">$DBF$</span>, <span data-ref="0072.g1:< D B E )">$DBE$</span> are equal to the angles <span data-ref="0072.g1:< B A D )">$BAD$</span>, <span data-ref="0072.g1:< B C D )">$BCD$</span>,
of which the angle <span data-ref="0072.g1:< B A D )">$BAD$</span> was proved equal to the angle <span data-ref="0072.g1:< D B F )">$DBF$</span>;
therefore the angle <span data-ref="0072.g1:< D B E )">$DBE$</span> which remains is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0072.g1:< D C B )">$DCB$</span> in the alternate segment <span data-ref="0072.g1:( D C B )">$DCB$</span> of the circle.

Therefore etc.

Q. E. D.

---

The converse of this theorem is true, namely that, *If a straight line drawn through one extremity of a chord of a circle make with that chord angles equal respectively to the angles in the alternate segments of the circle, the straight line so drawn touches the circle.*
