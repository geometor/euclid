For let :g:`BA <0072.g1:/ B A />` be drawn from :g:`B <0072.g1:B>` at
right angles to :g:`EF <0072.g1:/ E F />`,
let a point :g:`C <0072.g1:C>` be taken at random on
the circumference :g:`BD <0072.g1:( B D )>`,
and let :g:`AD <0072.g1:/ A D />`, :g:`DC <0072.g1:/ D C />`, :g:`CB <0072.g1:/ C B />` be joined.

Then, since a straight line :g:`EF <0072.g1:/ E F />`
touches the circle :g:`ABCD <0072.g1:( A B C D )>` at :g:`B <0072.g1:B>`,
and :g:`BA <0072.g1:/ B A />` has been drawn from the point
of contact at right angles to the tangent,
the centre of the circle :g:`ABCD <0072.g1:( A B C D )>` is on :g:`BA <0072.g1:/ B A />`. :just:`[III. 19] <III.19>`

Therefore :g:`BA <0072.g1:/ B A />` is a diameter of the circle :g:`ABCD <0072.g1:( A B C D )>`;
therefore the angle :g:`ADB <0072.g1:< A D B )>`, being an angle in a semicircle,
is right. :just:`[III. 31] <III.31>`

Therefore the remaining angles :g:`BAD <0072.g1:< B A D )>`, :g:`ABD <0072.g1:< A B D )>` are equal to
one right angle. :just:`[I. 32] <I.32>`

But the angle :g:`ABF <0072.g1:< A B F )>` is also right;
therefore the angle :g:`ABF <0072.g1:< A B F )>` is equal to the angles :g:`BAD <0072.g1:< B A D )>`, :g:`ABD <0072.g1:< A B D )>`.

Let the angle :g:`ABD <0072.g1:< A B D )>` be subtracted from each;
therefore the angle :g:`DBF <0072.g1:< D B F )>` which remains is :eq:`equal <0072.g1:< D B F ) = 0072.g1:< B A D )>` to the angle
:g:`BAD <0072.g1:< B A D )>` in the alternate segment of the circle.

Next, since :g:`ABCD <0072.g1:< A B C D >>` is a quadrilateral in a circle,
its opposite angles are equal to two right angles. :just:`[III. 22] <III.22>`

But the angles :g:`DBF <0072.g1:< D B F )>`, :g:`DBE <0072.g1:< D B E )>` are also equal to two right
angles;
therefore the angles :g:`DBF <0072.g1:< D B F )>`, :g:`DBE <0072.g1:< D B E )>` are equal to the angles
:g:`BAD <0072.g1:< B A D )>`, :g:`BCD <0072.g1:< B C D )>`,

.. subclause::

   of which the angle :g:`BAD <0072.g1:< B A D )>` was proved :eq:`equal <0072.g1:< B A D ) = 0072.g1:< D B F )>` to the angle :g:`DBF <0072.g1:< D B F )>`;

therefore the angle :g:`DBE <0072.g1:< D B E )>` which remains is :eq:`equal <0072.g1:< D B E ) = 0072.g1:< D C B )>` to the
angle :g:`DCB <0072.g1:< D C B )>` in the alternate segment :g:`DCB <0072.g1:( D C B )>` of the circle.

.. qed::

   Therefore etc. :just:`Q. E. D.`

----

The converse of this theorem is true, namely that, *If a straight line drawn through one extremity of a chord of a circle make with that chord angles equal respectively to the angles in the alternate segments of the circle, the straight line so drawn touches the circle.*
