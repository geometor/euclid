Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
--------------------------------------------

- **Direct Proof of Converse of III. 32 (Clavius)**: Shows that if $\angle FBD = \angle BAD$, adding $\angle ABD$ gives $\angle ABF = \angle ABD + \angle BAD = 1\text{ right angle}$ (since $\angle BDA$ is in a semicircle), so $EF \perp BA$ and touches the circle by III. 16 Porism.
- **Pappus's Consequence**: Cites Pappus IV, p. 196 and VII, p. 826 on a line through the point of contact of two tangent circles cutting off similar segments.
- **Proposition 33 (Case 1: Acute Angle)**: Divides the problem into three cases according as the given angle $C$ is acute, right, or obtuse.
