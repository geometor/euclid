let <span data-ref="0074.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> be bisected at <span data-ref="0074.g1:F">$F$</span>, and with centre <span data-ref="0074.g1:F">$F$</span> and distance either <span data-ref="0074.g1:/ F A /">$FA$</span> or <span data-ref="0074.g1:/ F B /">$FB$</span> let the circle <span data-ref="0074.g1:( A E B )">$AEB$</span> be described.

Therefore the straight line <span data-ref="0074.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> touches the circle <span data-ref="0074.g1:( A B E )">$ABE$</span>, because the angle at <span data-ref="0074.g1:< A )">$A$</span> is right. [III. 16, Por.]

And the angle <span data-ref="0074.g1:< B A D )">$BAD$</span> is equal to the angle in the segment <span data-ref="0074.g1:( A E B )">$AEB$</span>, for the latter too is itself a right angle, being an angle in a semicircle. [III. 31]

But the angle <span data-ref="0074.g1:< B A D )">$BAD$</span> is also equal to the angle at <span data-ref="0074.g1:< C )">$C$</span>.

Therefore the angle <span data-ref="0074.g1:< A E B )">$AEB$</span> is also equal to the angle at <span data-ref="0074.g1:< C )">$C$</span>.

Therefore again the segment <span data-ref="0074.g1:( A E B )">$AEB$</span> of a circle has been described on <span data-ref="0074.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> admitting an angle equal to the angle at <span data-ref="0074.g1:< C )">$C$</span>.

Next, let the angle at <span data-ref="0075.g1:< C )">$C$</span> be obtuse;

let <span data-ref="0075.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> be drawn at right angles to <span data-ref="0075.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, let <span data-ref="0075.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> be again bisected at <span data-ref="0075.g1:F">$F$</span>, let <span data-ref="0075.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span> be drawn at right angles to <span data-ref="0075.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, and let <span data-ref="0075.g1:/ G B /">$GB$</span> be joined.

Then, since <span data-ref="0075.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span> is again equal to <span data-ref="0075.g1:/ F B /">$FB$</span>,
and <span data-ref="0075.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span> is common,
the two sides <span data-ref="0075.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span>, <span data-ref="0075.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span> are equal to the two sides <span data-ref="0075.g1:/ B F /">$BF$</span>, <span data-ref="0075.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span>;
and the angle <span data-ref="0075.g1:< A F G )">$AFG$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0075.g1:< B F G )">$BFG$</span>;
therefore the base <span data-ref="0075.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span> is equal to the base <span data-ref="0075.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span>. [I. 4]

Therefore the circle described with centre <span data-ref="0075.g1:G">$G$</span> and distance <span data-ref="0075.g1:/ G A /">$GA$</span> will pass through <span data-ref="0075.g1:B">$B$</span> also; let it so pass, as <span data-ref="0075.g1:( A E B )">$AEB$</span>.

Now, since <span data-ref="0075.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> is drawn at right angles to the diameter <span data-ref="0075.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> from its extremity,
<span data-ref="0075.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> touches the circle <span data-ref="0075.g1:( A E B )">$AEB$</span>. [III. 16, Por.]

And <span data-ref="0075.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> has been drawn across from the point of contact at <span data-ref="0075.g1:A">$A$</span>;
therefore the angle <span data-ref="0075.g1:< B A D )">$BAD$</span> is equal to the angle constructed in the alternate segment <span data-ref="0075.g1:( A H B )">$AHB$</span> of the circle. [III. 32]
