Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
--------------------------------------------

- **Commentary on III. 33**:

  - Notes Robert Simson's combination of the first (acute) and third (obtuse) cases, which share the identical construction and proof.
  - Explains why Euclid constructs the tangent $AD$ in the second (right-angle) case only to invoke III. 31 instead of III. 32: in III. 32, Euclid did not treat separately the case where the chord is perpendicular to the tangent (which would itself rely on III. 31).
  - Notes the tacit assumption that the perpendiculars $AE$ (to $AD$) and $FG$ (to $AB$) intersect (since $AD$ and $AB$ meet at an angle other than a right angle).

- **Proposition 34 Opening**: Introduces the problem of cutting off a segment from a given circle admitting an angle equal to a given rectilineal angle $D$, using the tangent $EF$ at $B$ and constructing $\angle FBC = \angle D$ (I. 23).
