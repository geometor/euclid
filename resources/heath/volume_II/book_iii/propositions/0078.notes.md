## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Geometric Application of Book II (`[II. 5]`)**: By dropping perpendiculars $FG, FH$ from the centre $F$ to the chords $AC, DB$, Euclid bisects $AC$ at $G$ (`[III. 3]`) and applies II. 5 to obtain $\text{rect.}(AE, EC) + EG^2 = GC^2$. Adding $GF^2$ to both sides and applying the Pythagorean theorem (`[I. 47]`) yields $\text{rect.}(AE, EC) + FE^2 = FC^2 = FB^2$.
- **Symmetry Argument (`For the same reason, also`)**: Rather than repeating the steps with midpoint $H$ and radius $FD$, Euclid invokes symmetry to conclude $\text{rect.}(DE, EB) + FE^2 = FB^2$, whence $\text{rect.}(AE, EC) = \text{rect.}(DE, EB)$.
- **Omission of `Q. E. D.`**: In Heath's text of III. 35 on page 72, the proof ends with `Therefore etc.` without the usual `Q. E. D.` tag at the right margin.
