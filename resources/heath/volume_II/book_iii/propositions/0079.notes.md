## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Multiplicity of Cases in Euclid**: Heath critiques Simson's conjecture that Theon excised two intermediate cases (which appear in the Campanus/Arabic tradition), noting that Euclid consistently states only the simplest and most general cases to avoid excessive case subdivision while acknowledging case distinctions exist.
- **Secant-Secant Theorem (Corollary to III. 36)**: Heath notes that Clavius and Simson append the external secant-secant theorem as a corollary to III. 36, and that in modern geometry III. 35 and the secant-secant corollary can be unified into a single power-of-a-point statement.
- **Todhunter's Isosceles Triangle Lemma**: Heath highlights Todhunter's remark that the core of both III. 35 and III. 36 is the property of an isosceles triangle ($FAC$ or $EAC$) with a line drawn from the vertex to a point on the base (II. 5) or base produced (II. 6) combined with I. 47.
- **Converse Concyclicity Theorem**: If $AB, CD$ intersect at $O$ such that $\text{rect.}(AO, OB) = \text{rect.}(CO, OD)$, then $A, B, C, D$ are concyclic, proved by *reductio ad absurdum* using the circumcircle of $\triangle ABC$.
