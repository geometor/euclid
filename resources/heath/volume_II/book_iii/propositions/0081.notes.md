## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Completion of Case 2 of III. 36**: Using II. 6 on the bisected chord $AC$ with external extension $CD$ gives $\text{rect.}(AD, DC) + FC^2 = FD^2$. Adding $FE^2$ and applying I. 47 twice (first to $\triangle EFC$ and $\triangle EFD$, then to $\triangle EBD$) establishes $\text{rect.}(AD, DC) = DB^2$.
- **Greek Attitude Toward Limiting Cases**: Heath contrasts modern treatments (such as H. M. Taylor's *Euclid*, p. 253, which proves the two-secant theorem first and derives the tangent-secant theorem by taking the limiting position as the two intersection points $E, B$ coalesce) with the classical Greek method (both in Euclid and in Apollonius's *Conics*), which always provides an independent synthetic demonstration for the tangent (limiting) case rather than inferring it by continuity.
