<p class="enunciation">the circle and the straight line intercepted on it outside<br />
between the point and the convex circumference be equal to<br />
the square on the straight line which falls on the circle, the<br />
straight line which falls on it will touch the circle.</p>

For let a point <span data-ref="0082.g1:D">$D$</span> be taken outside the circle <span data-ref="0082.g1:( A B C )">$ABC$</span>;
from <span data-ref="0082.g1:D">$D$</span> let the two straight lines <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ D C A /">$DCA$</span>, <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> fall on the circle <span data-ref="0082.g1:( A C B )">$ACB$</span>;
let <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ D C A /">$DCA$</span> cut the circle and <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> fall on it; and let the rectangle <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ D C /">$DC$</span> be equal to the square on <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span>.

![Proposition 37 diagram](0082.g1.svg)

I say that <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> touches the circle <span data-ref="0082.g1:( A B C )">$ABC$</span>.

For let <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> be drawn touching <span data-ref="0082.g1:( A B C )">$ABC$</span>; let the centre of the circle <span data-ref="0082.g1:( A B C )">$ABC$</span> be taken, and let it be <span data-ref="0082.g1:F">$F$</span>; let <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ F E /">$FE$</span>, <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ F B /">$FB$</span>, <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ F D /">$FD$</span> be joined.

Thus the angle <span data-ref="0082.g1:< F E D )">$FED$</span> is right. [III. 18]

Now, since <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> touches the circle <span data-ref="0082.g1:( A B C )">$ABC$</span>, and <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ D C A /">$DCA$</span> cuts it, the rectangle <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ D C /">$DC$</span> is equal to the square on <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>. [III. 36]

But the rectangle <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ D C /">$DC$</span> was also equal to the square on <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span>;
therefore the square on <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> is equal to the square on <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span>;
therefore <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span>.

And <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ F E /">$FE$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ F B /">$FB$</span>;
therefore the two sides <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>, <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> are equal to the two sides <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span>, <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ B F /">$BF$</span>;
and <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ F D /">$FD$</span> is the common base of the triangles;
therefore the angle <span data-ref="0082.g1:< D E F )">$DEF$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0082.g1:< D B F )">$DBF$</span>. [I. 8]

But the angle <span data-ref="0082.g1:< D E F )">$DEF$</span> is right;
therefore the angle <span data-ref="0082.g1:< D B F )">$DBF$</span> is also right.

And <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ F B /">$FB$</span> produced is a diameter;
and the straight line drawn at right angles to the diameter of a circle, from its extremity, touches the circle; [III. 16, Por.]
therefore <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> touches the circle.

Similarly this can be proved to be the case even if the centre be on <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>.

<p class="qed">Therefore etc. <span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>
