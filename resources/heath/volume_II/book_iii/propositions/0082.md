<p class="enunciation">the circle and the straight line intercepted on it outside<br />
between the point and the convex circumference be equal to<br />
the square on the straight line which falls on the circle, the<br />
straight line which falls on it will touch the circle.</p>

For let a point $D$ be taken outside the circle $ABC$;
from $D$ let the two straight lines
$DCA$, $DB$ fall on the circle $ACB$;
let $DCA$ cut the circle and $DB$
fall on it; and let the rectangle $AD$,
$DC$ be equal to the square on $DB$.

![Proposition 37 diagram](0082.g1.svg)

I say that $DB$ touches the circle
$ABC$.

For let $DE$ be drawn touching
$ABC$; let the centre of the circle $ABC$ be taken, and let it
be $F$; let $FE$, $FB$, $FD$ be joined.

Thus the angle $FED$ is right. <span class="justification">[III. 18]</span>

Now, since $DE$ touches the circle $ABC$, and $DCA$ cuts it,
the rectangle $AD$, $DC$ is equal to the square on $DE$. <span class="justification">[III. 36]</span>

But the rectangle $AD$, $DC$ was also equal to the square
on $DB$;
therefore the square on $DE$ is equal to the square on $DB$;
<p class="assertion">therefore $DE$ is equal to $DB$.</p>

And $FE$ is equal to $FB$;
therefore the two sides $DE$, $EF$ are equal to the two sides
$DB$, $BF$;
and $FD$ is the common base of the triangles;
<p class="assertion">therefore the angle $DEF$ is equal to the angle $DBF$. <span class="justification">[I. 8]</span></p>

But the angle $DEF$ is right;
<p class="assertion">therefore the angle $DBF$ is also right.</p>

And $FB$ produced is a diameter;
and the straight line drawn at right angles to the diameter
of a circle, from its extremity, touches the circle; <span class="justification">[III. 16, Por.]</span>
<p class="assertion">therefore $DB$ touches the circle.</p>

Similarly this can be proved to be the case even if the
centre be on $AC$.

<p class="qed">Therefore etc. <span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>
