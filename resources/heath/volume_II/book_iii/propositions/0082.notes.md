## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Converse of the Tangent-Secant Theorem (III. 37)**: To prove that $DB$ touches the circle $ABC$ when $\text{rect.}(AD, DC) = DB^2$, Euclid draws a tangent $DE$ from $D$ (implicitly by `[III. 17]`), uses `[III. 36]` to show $DE^2 = \text{rect.}(AD, DC) = DB^2$, whence $DE = DB$, and then proves $\triangle DEF \cong \triangle DBF$ by `[I. 8]` (SSS), concluding that $\angle DBF = \angle DEF$ is a right angle and hence $DB$ is tangent by `[III. 16, Por.]`.
- **Case Remark (`Similarly this can be proved...`)**: Rather than writing out two separate cases as in III. 36, Euclid simply adds the remark that the same proof applies even if the centre $F$ lies on $AC$, since III. 36 has already been proved for both cases.
- **Analogy with I. 48 (De Morgan)**: Heath begins his note citing Augustus De Morgan's observation that drawing the auxiliary tangent $DE$ on the other side of $DF$ is an "apparent avoidance of indirect demonstration" completely analogous to Euclid's proof of the converse of the Pythagorean theorem (I. 48).
