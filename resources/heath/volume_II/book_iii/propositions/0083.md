He drew the straight line from $D$ passing through the centre, and then (without drawing a second tangent) proved by the aid of II. 6 that the square on $DF$ is equal to the sum of the squares on $DB$, $BF$; whence (by I. 48) the angle $DBF$ is a right angle. But this proof uses I. 48, the very proposition to which De Morgan's original remark relates.

The undisguised indirect proof is easy. If $DB$ does not touch the circle, it must cut it if produced, and it follows that the square on $DB$ must be equal to the rectangle contained by $DB$ and a longer line: which is absurd.
