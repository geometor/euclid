## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Campanus's Proof of III. 37**: Heath notes that Campanus avoided drawing the auxiliary tangent $DE$ by drawing the secant through $D$ and the centre $F$, using II. 6 to show $DF^2 = DB^2 + BF^2$, and then invoking I. 48 to conclude that $\angle DBF$ is right—which merely shifts the disguised indirect proof back to I. 48.
- **Direct *Reductio ad Absurdum***: Heath gives the two-sentence undisguised indirect proof: if $DB$ does not touch the circle, it must cut the circle again when produced, so by III. 36 (or its secant corollary) $DB^2$ would equal the rectangle contained by $DB$ and a longer segment, which is impossible.
