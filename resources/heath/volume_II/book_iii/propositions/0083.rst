the opposite side of :g:`$DF$ <0082.g1:/ D F />` from :g:`$DB$ <0082.g1:/ D B />`. The case is similar to the *apparently* direct proof which :person:`Campanus <campanus>` gave. He drew the straight line from :g:`$D$ <0082.g1:D>` passing through the centre, and then (without drawing a second tangent) proved by the aid of II. 6 that the square on :g:`$DF$ <0082.g1:/ D F />` is equal to the sum of the squares on :g:`$DB$ <0082.g1:/ D B />`, :g:`$BF$ <0082.g1:/ B F />`; whence (by I. 48) the angle :g:`$DBF$ <0082.g1:< D B F )>` is a right angle. But this proof uses I. 48, the very proposition to which :person:`De Morgan <de-morgan>`'s original remark relates.

The undisguised indirect proof is easy. If :g:`$DB$ <0082.g1:/ D B />` does not touch the circle, it must cut it if produced, and it follows that the square on :g:`$DB$ <0082.g1:/ D B />` must be equal to the rectangle contained by :g:`$DB$ <0082.g1:/ D B />` and a longer line: which is absurd.
