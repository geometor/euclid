# Transcription Notes: Folio 0090 (Page 84)

## Summary & Folio Continuity
- **Folio**: `0090` (Page 84, Volume II)
- **Content**: Continuation and conclusion of the proof of Book IV, Proposition 3 (lines 20–42), two apparatus notes on lines 10 and 22, and the first part of Heath's commentary on IV. 3.
- **Continuity**: Continues Proposition 3 from Folio `0089` (Page 83). Heath's commentary ends mid-sentence at the bottom of the page ("If the three pairs of parallel tangents be drawn and produced far enough,") and continues onto Folio `0091` (Page 85).

## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Quadrilateral Angle Sum (Line 22)**: Euclid justifies that the four angles of quadrilateral $AMBK$ are equal to four right angles by noting that "$AMBK$ is in fact divisible ($\kappa\alpha\grave{\iota}\ \delta\iota\alpha\iota\rho\varepsilon\tilde{\iota}\tau\alpha\iota$, literally 'is actually divided') into two triangles."
- **Intersection of the Three Tangents**: Heath supplies the proof omitted by Euclid that the three tangents $LAM, MBN, NCL$ actually meet to form a triangle: each central angle $\angle AKB, \angle BKC, \angle CKA$ is supplementary to an angle of $\triangle DEF$ and hence is less than two right angles, so the tangents at the endpoints of each arc meet on the side of that arc.
- **Alternative Construction (Peletarius and Borelli)**: Inscribing an equiangular triangle first (by IV. 2) and drawing tangents parallel to its sides yields two circumscribed triangles (and, when all three pairs of parallel tangents are drawn, an equiangular hexagon).
