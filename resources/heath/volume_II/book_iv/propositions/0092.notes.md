## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Greek Idiom for Radius (`[^86_1]`)**: Heath highlights Euclid's shorthand Greek expression $\kappa\alpha\grave{\iota}\ \delta\iota\alpha\sigma\tau\acute{\eta}\mu\alpha\tau\iota\ \dot{\varepsilon}\nu\grave{\iota}\ \tau\tilde{\omega}\nu\ \text{E, Z, H}$ ("and with distance one of the [points] $E, F, G$") and even more explicitly in IV. 13 $\delta\iota\alpha\sigma\tau\acute{\eta}\mu\alpha\tau\iota\ \dot{\varepsilon}\nu\grave{\iota}\ \tau\tilde{\omega}\nu\ \text{H, }\Theta\text{, K, }\Lambda\text{, M }\sigma\eta\mu\varepsilon\acute{\iota}\omega\nu$, which Heiberg characterizes as *Graecam locutionem satis miram et negligentem* ("a rather strange and careless Greek expression") but retains due to manuscript authority.
- **Concurrency of Angle Bisectors**: Heath notes that the proof immediately implies the concurrency of the three internal angle bisectors of a triangle at the incentre $D$.
- **General Problem of Tritangent Circles**: Heath generalizes IV. 4 to describing a circle tangent to three lines not all concurrent and at most two parallel: (1) two parallel lines cut by a transversal yield two equal circles; (2) three lines forming a triangle yield four circles (one inscribed and three escribed).
