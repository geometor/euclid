Hence we have the inscribed circle, and three escribed circles (one opposite each angle of the triangle), i.e. four circles in all, satisfying the conditions of the problem.

It may perhaps not be inappropriate to give at this point Heron's elegant proof of the formula for the area of a triangle in terms of the sides, which we usually write thus:

$$\Delta = \sqrt{s(s - a)(s - b)(s - c)},$$

although it requires the theory of proportions and uses some ungeometrical expressions, e.g. the product of two areas and the "side" of such a product, where of course the areas are so many square units of length. The proof is given in the *Metrica*, I. 8, and in the *Dioptra*, 30 (Heron, Vol. III., Teubner, 1903, pp. 20—24 and pp. 280—4, or Heron, ed. Hultsch, pp. 235—7).

Suppose the sides of the triangle <span data-ref="0093.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> to be given in length.

Inscribe the circle <span data-ref="0093.g1:( D E F )">$DEF$</span>, and let <span data-ref="0093.g1:G">$G$</span> be its centre.

![Heron's proof diagram](0093.g1.svg)

Join <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span>, <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ C G /">$CG$</span>, <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ D G /">$DG$</span>, <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ E G /">$EG$</span>, <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span>.

Then

$$\begin{aligned}
BC \cdot EG &= 2 \cdot \Delta BGC, \\
CA \cdot FG &= 2 \cdot \Delta ACG, \\
AB \cdot DG &= 2 \cdot \Delta ABG.
\end{aligned}$$

Therefore, by addition,

$$p \cdot EG = 2 \cdot \Delta ABC,$$

where $p$ is the perimeter.

Produce <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span> to <span data-ref="0093.g1:H">$H$</span>, so that <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ B H /">$BH$</span> = <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>.

Then, since <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> = <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span>, <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> = <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span>, <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ F C /">$FC$</span> = <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ C E /">$CE$</span>,

$$CH = \tfrac{1}{2}p.$$

Hence

$$CH \cdot EG = \Delta ABC.$$
