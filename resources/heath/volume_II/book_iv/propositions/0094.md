But <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ C H /">$CH$</span> $\cdot$ <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ E G /">$EG$</span> is the "side" of the product $CH^2 \cdot EG^2$, that is $\sqrt{CH^2 \cdot EG^2}$;
therefore

$$(\Delta ABC)^2 = CH^2 \cdot EG^2.$$

Draw <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ G L /">$GL$</span> at right angles to <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ C G /">$CG$</span>, and <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ B L /">$BL$</span> at right angles to <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span>, meeting at <span data-ref="0093.g1:L">$L$</span>. Join <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ C L /">$CL$</span>.

Then, since each of the angles <span data-ref="0093.g1:< C G L )">$CGL$</span>, <span data-ref="0093.g1:< C B L )">$CBL$</span> is right, <span data-ref="0093.g1:< C G B L >">$CGBL$</span> is a quadrilateral in a circle.

Therefore the angles <span data-ref="0093.g1:< C G B )">$CGB$</span>, <span data-ref="0093.g1:< C L B )">$CLB$</span> are equal to two right angles.

Now the angles <span data-ref="0093.g1:< C G B )">$CGB$</span>, <span data-ref="0093.g1:< A G D )">$AGD$</span> are equal to two right angles, since <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span>, <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ C G /">$CG$</span> bisect the angles at <span data-ref="0093.g1:G">$G$</span>, and the angles <span data-ref="0093.g1:< C G B )">$CGB$</span>, <span data-ref="0093.g1:< A G D )">$AGD$</span> are equal to the angles <span data-ref="0093.g1:< A G C )">$AGC$</span>, <span data-ref="0093.g1:< D G B )">$DGB$</span>, while the sum of all four is equal to four right angles.

Therefore the angles <span data-ref="0093.g1:< A G D )">$AGD$</span>, <span data-ref="0093.g1:< C L B )">$CLB$</span> are equal.

So are the right angles <span data-ref="0093.g1:< A D G )">$ADG$</span>, <span data-ref="0093.g1:< C B L )">$CBL$</span>.

Therefore the triangles <span data-ref="0093.g1:< A G D >">$AGD$</span>, <span data-ref="0093.g1:< C L B >">$CLB$</span> are similar.

Hence

$$\begin{aligned}
BC : BL &= AD : DG \\
&= BH : EG,
\end{aligned}$$

and, alternately,

$$\begin{aligned}
CB : BH &= BL : EG \\
&= BK : KE,
\end{aligned}$$

whence, *componendo*,

$$CH : HB = BE : EK.$$

It follows that

$$\begin{aligned}
CH^2 : CH \cdot HB &= BE \cdot EC : CE \cdot EK \\
&= BE \cdot EC : EG^2
\end{aligned}$$

Therefore

$$\begin{aligned}
(\Delta ABC)^2 = CH^2 \cdot EG^2 &= CH \cdot HB \cdot CE \cdot EB \\
&= \tfrac{1}{2}p\,(\tfrac{1}{2}p - BC)(\tfrac{1}{2}p - AB)(\tfrac{1}{2}p - AC).
\end{aligned}$$

### PROPOSITION 5.

<p class="enunciation">About a given triangle to circumscribe a circle.</p>

Let <span data-ref="0094.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> be the given triangle;
thus it is required to circumscribe a circle about the given triangle <span data-ref="0094.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span>.

![Proposition 5 diagrams](0094.g1.svg)

Let the straight lines <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> be bisected at the points <span data-ref="0094.g1:D">$D$</span>, <span data-ref="0094.g1:E">$E$</span> [I. 10], and from the points <span data-ref="0094.g1:D">$D$</span>, <span data-ref="0094.g1:E">$E$</span> let <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span>, <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> be drawn at right angles to <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>;
they will then meet within the triangle <span data-ref="0094.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span>, or on the straight line <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, or outside <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>.
