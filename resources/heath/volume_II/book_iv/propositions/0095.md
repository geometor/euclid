First let them meet within at <span data-ref="0094.g1:F">$F$</span>, and let <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ F B /">$FB$</span>, <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ F C /">$FC$</span>, <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ F A /">$FA$</span> be joined.

Then, since <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span>,
and <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span> is common and at right angles,
therefore the base <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span> is equal to the base <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ F B /">$FB$</span>. [I. 4]

Similarly we can prove that
<span data-ref="0094.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span> is also equal to <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span>;
so that <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ F B /">$FB$</span> is also equal to <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ F C /">$FC$</span>;
therefore the three straight lines <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ F A /">$FA$</span>, <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ F B /">$FB$</span>, <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ F C /">$FC$</span> are equal to one another.

Therefore the circle described with centre <span data-ref="0094.g1:F">$F$</span> and distance one of the straight lines <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ F A /">$FA$</span>, <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ F B /">$FB$</span>, <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ F C /">$FC$</span> will pass also through the remaining points, and the circle will have been circumscribed about the triangle <span data-ref="0094.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span>.

Let it be circumscribed, as <span data-ref="0094.g1:( A B C )">$ABC$</span>.

Next, let <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span>, <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> meet on the straight line <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> at <span data-ref="0094.g1:F">$F$</span>, as is the case in the second figure; and let <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span> be joined.

Then, similarly, we shall prove that the point <span data-ref="0094.g1:F">$F$</span> is the centre of the circle circumscribed about the triangle <span data-ref="0094.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span>.

Again, let <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span>, <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> meet outside the triangle <span data-ref="0094.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> at <span data-ref="0094.g1:F">$F$</span>, as is the case in the third figure, and let <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span>, <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ B F /">$BF$</span>, <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span> be joined.

Then again, since <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span>,
and <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span> is common and at right angles,
therefore the base <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span> is equal to the base <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ F B /">$FB$</span>. [I. 4]

Similarly we can prove that
<span data-ref="0094.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span> is also equal to <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span>;
so that <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ B F /">$BF$</span> is also equal to <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ F C /">$FC$</span>;
therefore the circle described with centre <span data-ref="0094.g1:F">$F$</span> and distance one of the straight lines <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ F A /">$FA$</span>, <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ F B /">$FB$</span>, <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ F C /">$FC$</span> will pass also through the remaining points, and will have been circumscribed about the triangle <span data-ref="0094.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span>.

Therefore about the given triangle a circle has been
circumscribed.
<p class="qed"><span class="justification">Q. E. F.</span></p>

Q. E. F.
