when the centre falls on the straight line <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, the angle <span data-ref="0094.g1:< B A C )">$BAC$</span>, being in a semicircle, is right;
and when the centre of the circle falls outside the triangle, the angle <span data-ref="0094.g1:< B A C )">$BAC$</span>, being in a segment less than the semicircle, is greater than a right angle. [III. 31]

---

Simson points out that Euclid does not prove that <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span>, <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> will meet, and he inserts in the text the following argument to supply the omission.

"<span data-ref="0094.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span>, <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> produced meet one another. For, if they do not meet, they are parallel, wherefore <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, which are at right angles to them, are parallel [or, he should have added, in a straight line]: which is absurd."

This assumes, of course, that straight lines which are at right angles to two parallels are themselves parallel; but this is an obvious deduction from I. 28.

On the assumption that <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span>, <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> will meet Todhunter has this note: "It has been proposed to show this in the following way: join <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>; then the angles <span data-ref="0094.g1:< E D F )">$EDF$</span> and <span data-ref="0094.g1:< D E F )">$DEF$</span> are together less than the angles <span data-ref="0094.g1:< A D F )">$ADF$</span> and <span data-ref="0094.g1:< A E F )">$AEF$</span>, that is, they are together less than two right angles; and therefore <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span> and <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> will meet, by Axiom 12 [Post. 5]. This assumes that <span data-ref="0094.g1:< A D E )">$ADE$</span> and <span data-ref="0094.g1:< A E D )">$AED$</span> are *acute* angles; it may, however, be easily shown that <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> is parallel to <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, so that the triangle <span data-ref="0094.g1:< A D E >">$ADE$</span> is equiangular to the triangle <span data-ref="0094.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span>; and we must therefore select the two sides <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> and <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> such that <span data-ref="0094.g1:< A B C )">$ABC$</span> and <span data-ref="0094.g1:< A C B )">$ACB$</span> may be acute angles."

This is, however, unsatisfactory. Euclid makes no such selection in III. 9 and III. 10, where the same assumption is tacitly made; and it is unnecessary, because it is easy to prove that the straight lines <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span>, <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> meet in *all* cases, by considering the different possibilities separately and drawing a separate figure for each case.

Simson thinks that Euclid's demonstration had been spoiled by some unskilful hand both because of the omission to prove that the perpendicular bisectors meet, and because "without any reason he divides the proposition into three cases, whereas one and the same construction and demonstration serves for them all, as Campanus has observed." However, up to the usual words $\ὅ\pi\varepsilon\rho\ \ἔ\delta\varepsilon\iota\ \pi\omicron\iota\tilde{\eta}\sigma\alpha\iota$ there seems to be no doubt about the text. Heiberg suggests that Euclid gave separately the case where <span data-ref="0094.g1:F">$F$</span> falls on <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> because, in that case, only <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span> needs to be drawn and not <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ B F /">$BF$</span>, <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span> as well.

The addition, though given in Simson and the text-books as a "corollary," has no heading $\pi\acute{\omicron}\rho\iota\sigma\mu\alpha$ in the best MSS.; it is an explanation like that which is contained in the penultimate paragraph of III. 25.

The Greek text has a further addition, which is rejected by Heiberg as not genuine, "So that, further, when the given angle happens to be less than a right angle, <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span>, <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> will fall within the triangle, when it is right, on <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, and, when it is greater than a right angle, outside <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>. (being) what it was required to do." Simson had already observed that the text here is vitiated "where mention is made of a given angle, though there neither is, nor can be, anything in the proposition relating to a given angle."
