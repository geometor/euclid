Moreover, in this case, the problem *may* be solved in the same way as that of IV. 2, i.e. by simply inscribing a triangle equiangular to one of the triangles into which the quadrilateral is divided by either diagonal, and then drawing on the side corresponding to the diagonal as base another triangle equiangular to the other triangle contained in the quadrilateral. Then <span data-ref="0098.g1:< A B' C D' >">$AB'CD'$</span> is also *equiangular* to the given quadrilateral, but not of the same *form*. Hence the problem is indeterminate in the case of the general quadrilateral. It is equally so if the given quadrilateral is a rectangle; and it is determinate only when the given quadrilateral is a *square*.

### PROPOSITION 7.

*About a given circle to circumscribe a square.*

Let <span data-ref="0098.g2:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span> be the given circle;
thus it is required to circumscribe a square about the circle <span data-ref="0098.g2:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span>.

Let two diameters <span data-ref="0098.g2:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0098.g2:/ B D /">$BD$</span> of the circle <span data-ref="0098.g2:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span> be drawn at right angles to one another, and through the points <span data-ref="0098.g2:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0098.g2:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0098.g2:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0098.g2:D">$D$</span> let <span data-ref="0098.g2:/ F G /">$FG$</span>, <span data-ref="0098.g2:/ G H /">$GH$</span>, <span data-ref="0098.g2:/ H K /">$HK$</span>, <span data-ref="0098.g2:/ K F /">$KF$</span> be drawn touching the circle <span data-ref="0098.g2:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span>. [III. 16, Por.]

![Proposition IV. 7 Diagram](0098.g2.svg)

Then, since <span data-ref="0098.g2:/ F G /">$FG$</span> touches the circle <span data-ref="0098.g2:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span>,
and <span data-ref="0098.g2:/ E A /">$EA$</span> has been joined from the centre <span data-ref="0098.g2:E">$E$</span> to the point of contact at <span data-ref="0098.g2:A">$A$</span>,
therefore the angles at <span data-ref="0098.g2:A">$A$</span> are right. [III. 18]

For the same reason
the angles at the points <span data-ref="0098.g2:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0098.g2:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0098.g2:D">$D$</span> are also right.

Now, since the angle <span data-ref="0098.g2:< A E B )">$AEB$</span> is right,
and the angle <span data-ref="0098.g2:< E B G )">$EBG$</span> is also right,
therefore <span data-ref="0098.g2:/ G H /">$GH$</span> is parallel to <span data-ref="0098.g2:/ A C /">$AC$</span>. [I. 28]
