---

<p class="assertion">therefore the five angles $DAC, ACE, ECD, CDB, BDA$<br />
are equal to one another.</p>

But equal angles stand on equal circumferences; <span class="justification">[III. 26]</span>
<p class="assertion">therefore the five circumferences $AB, BC, CD, DE, EA$ are<br />
equal to one another.</p>

But equal circumferences are subtended by equal straight
lines; <span class="justification">[III. 29]</span>
<p class="assertion">therefore the five straight lines $AB, BC, CD, DE, EA$ are<br />
equal to one another;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the pentagon $ABCDE$ is equilateral.</p>

I say next that it is also equiangular.

For, since the circumference $AB$ is equal to the circum-
ference $DE$, let $BCD$ be added to each;
<p class="assertion">therefore the whole circumference $ABCD$ is equal to the<br />
whole circumference $EDCB$.</p>

And the angle $AED$ stands on the circumference $ABCD$,
and the angle $BAE$ on the circumference $EDCB$;
<p class="assertion">therefore the angle $BAE$ is also equal to the angle $AED$. <span class="justification">[III. 27]</span></p>

For the same reason
each of the angles $ABC, BCD, CDE$ is also equal to each
of the angles $BAE, AED$;
<p class="assertion">therefore the pentagon $ABCDE$ is equiangular.</p>

But it was also proved equilateral;
<p class="qed">therefore in the given circle an equilateral and equi-<br />
angular pentagon has been inscribed. <span class="justification">Q. E. F.</span></p>

---

De Morgan remarks that "the method of IV. 11 is not so natural as making a direct use of the angle obtained in the last." On the other hand, if we look at the figure and notice that it shows the whole of the *pentagram-star* except one line (that connecting $B$ and $E$), I think we shall conclude that the method is nearer to that used by the Pythagoreans, and therefore of much more historical interest.

Another method would of course be to use IV. 10 to describe a *decagon* in the circle, and then to join any vertex to the next alternate one, the latter to the next alternate one, and so on.
