Mr H. M. Taylor gives "a complete geometrical construction for inscribing a regular decagon or pentagon in a given circle," as follows.

"Find $O$ the centre.

Draw two diameters $AOC, BOD$ at right
angles to one another.

Bisect $OD$ in $E$.

Draw $AE$ and cut off $EF$ equal to $OE$.

Place round the circle ten chords equal
to $AF$.

![Taylor's decagon and pentagon construction](0108.g1.svg)

These chords will be the sides of a regular
decagon. Draw the chords joining five alternate
vertices of the decagon; they will be the sides
of a regular pentagon."

The construction is of course only a com-
bination of those in II. 11 and IV. 1; and the
proof would have to follow that in IV. 10.

### PROPOSITION 12.

<p class="enunciation">About a given circle to circumscribe an equilateral and<br />
equiangular pentagon.</p>

Let $ABCDE$ be the given circle;
thus it is required to circumscribe an equilateral and equi-
angular pentagon about the circle
$ABCDE$.

Let $A, B, C, D, E$ be conceived to
be the angular points of the inscribed
pentagon, so that the circumferences
$AB, BC, CD, DE, EA$ are equal;
<span class="justification">[IV. 11]</span>
through $A, B, C, D, E$ let $GH, HK,$
$KL, LM, MG$ be drawn touching the
circle;
<span class="justification">[III. 16, Por.]</span>

![Proposition 12 diagram](0108.g2.svg)

let the centre $F$ of the circle $ABCDE$ be taken <span class="justification">[III. 1]</span>, and
let $FB, FK, FC, FL, FD$ be joined.

Then, since the straight line $KL$ touches the circle $ABCDE$
at $C$,
and $FC$ has been joined from the centre $F$ to the point of
contact at $C$,
<p class="assertion">therefore $FC$ is perpendicular to $KL$; <span class="justification">[III. 18]</span></p>
<p class="assertion">therefore each of the angles at $C$ is right.</p>

For the same reason
<p class="assertion">the angles at the points $B, D$ are also right.</p>
