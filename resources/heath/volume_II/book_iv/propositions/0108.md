Mr H. M. Taylor gives "a complete geometrical construction for inscribing a regular decagon or pentagon in a given circle," as follows.

"Find <span data-ref="0108.g1:O">$O$</span> the centre.

Draw two diameters <span data-ref="0108.g1:/ A O C /">$AOC$</span>, <span data-ref="0108.g1:/ B O D /">$BOD$</span> at right angles to one another.

Bisect <span data-ref="0108.g1:/ O D /">$OD$</span> in <span data-ref="0108.g1:E">$E$</span>.

Draw <span data-ref="0108.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> and cut off <span data-ref="0108.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> equal to <span data-ref="0108.g1:/ O E /">$OE$</span>.

Place round the circle ten chords equal to <span data-ref="0108.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span>.

![Taylor's decagon and pentagon construction](0108.g1.svg)

These chords will be the sides of a regular decagon. Draw the chords joining five alternate vertices of the decagon; they will be the sides of a regular pentagon."

The construction is of course only a combination of those in II. 11 and IV. 1; and the proof would have to follow that in IV. 10.

## PROPOSITION 12.

*About a given circle to circumscribe an equilateral and equiangular pentagon.*

Let <span data-ref="0108.g2:( A B C D E )">$ABCDE$</span> be the given circle;
thus it is required to circumscribe an equilateral and equiangular pentagon about the circle <span data-ref="0108.g2:( A B C D E )">$ABCDE$</span>.

Let <span data-ref="0108.g2:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0108.g2:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0108.g2:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0108.g2:D">$D$</span>, <span data-ref="0108.g2:E">$E$</span> be conceived to be the angular points of the inscribed pentagon, so that the circumferences <span data-ref="0108.g2:( A B )">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0108.g2:( B C )">$BC$</span>, <span data-ref="0108.g2:( C D )">$CD$</span>, <span data-ref="0108.g2:( D E )">$DE$</span>, <span data-ref="0108.g2:( E A )">$EA$</span> are equal; [IV. 11]

![Proposition 12 diagram](0108.g2.svg)

through <span data-ref="0108.g2:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0108.g2:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0108.g2:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0108.g2:D">$D$</span>, <span data-ref="0108.g2:E">$E$</span> let <span data-ref="0108.g2:/ G H /">$GH$</span>, <span data-ref="0108.g2:/ H K /">$HK$</span>, <span data-ref="0108.g2:/ K L /">$KL$</span>, <span data-ref="0108.g2:/ L M /">$LM$</span>, <span data-ref="0108.g2:/ M G /">$MG$</span> be drawn touching the circle; [III. 16, Por.]
let the centre <span data-ref="0108.g2:F">$F$</span> of the circle <span data-ref="0108.g2:( A B C D E )">$ABCDE$</span> be taken [III. 1], and let <span data-ref="0108.g2:/ F B /">$FB$</span>, <span data-ref="0108.g2:/ F K /">$FK$</span>, <span data-ref="0108.g2:/ F C /">$FC$</span>, <span data-ref="0108.g2:/ F L /">$FL$</span>, <span data-ref="0108.g2:/ F D /">$FD$</span> be joined.

Then, since the straight line <span data-ref="0108.g2:/ K L /">$KL$</span> touches the circle <span data-ref="0108.g2:( A B C D E )">$ABCDE$</span> at <span data-ref="0108.g2:C">$C$</span>,
and <span data-ref="0108.g2:/ F C /">$FC$</span> has been joined from the centre <span data-ref="0108.g2:F">$F$</span> to the point of contact at <span data-ref="0108.g2:C">$C$</span>,
therefore <span data-ref="0108.g2:/ F C /">$FC$</span> is perpendicular to <span data-ref="0108.g2:/ K L /">$KL$</span>; [III. 18]
therefore each of the angles at <span data-ref="0108.g2:< K C L )">$C$</span> is right.

For the same reason
the angles at the points <span data-ref="0108.g2:< F B K )">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0108.g2:< F D L )">$D$</span> are also right.
