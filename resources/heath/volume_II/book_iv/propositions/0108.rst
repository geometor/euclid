Mr H. M. Taylor gives "a complete geometrical construction for inscribing a regular decagon or pentagon in a given circle," as follows.

"Find :g:`$O$ <0108.g1:O>` the centre.

Draw two diameters :g:`$AOC$ <0108.g1:/ A O C />`, :g:`$BOD$ <0108.g1:/ B O D />` at right
angles to one another.

Bisect :g:`$OD$ <0108.g1:/ O D />` in :g:`$E$ <0108.g1:E>`.

Draw :g:`$AE$ <0108.g1:/ A E />` and cut off :g:`$EF$ <0108.g1:/ E F />` equal to :g:`$OE$ <0108.g1:/ O E />`.

Place round the circle ten chords equal
to :g:`$AF$ <0108.g1:/ A F />`.

.. picture:: 0108.g1.svg
   :alt: Taylor's decagon and pentagon construction

These chords will be the sides of a regular
decagon. Draw the chords joining five alternate
vertices of the decagon; they will be the sides
of a regular pentagon."

The construction is of course only a combination
of those in II. 11 and IV. 1; and the
proof would have to follow that in IV. 10.

PROPOSITION 12.
---------------

.. enunciation::

   About a given circle to circumscribe an equilateral and equiangular pentagon.

Let :g:`$ABCDE$ <0108.g2:( A B C D E )>` be the given circle;
thus it is required to circumscribe an equilateral and equiangular
pentagon about the circle
:g:`$ABCDE$ <0108.g2:( A B C D E )>`.

Let :g:`$A$ <0108.g2:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0108.g2:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0108.g2:C>`, :g:`$D$ <0108.g2:D>`, :g:`$E$ <0108.g2:E>` be conceived to
be the angular points of the inscribed
pentagon, so that the circumferences
:g:`$AB$ <0108.g2:( A B )>`, :g:`$BC$ <0108.g2:( B C )>`, :g:`$CD$ <0108.g2:( C D )>`, :g:`$DE$ <0108.g2:( D E )>`, :g:`$EA$ <0108.g2:( E A )>` are equal;
:just:`[IV. 11] <IV.11>`
through :g:`$A$ <0108.g2:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0108.g2:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0108.g2:C>`, :g:`$D$ <0108.g2:D>`, :g:`$E$ <0108.g2:E>` let :g:`$GH$ <0108.g2:/ G H />`, :g:`$HK$ <0108.g2:/ H K />`,
:g:`$KL$ <0108.g2:/ K L />`, :g:`$LM$ <0108.g2:/ L M />`, :g:`$MG$ <0108.g2:/ M G />` be drawn touching the
circle;
:just:`[III. 16, Por.]`

.. picture:: 0108.g2.svg
   :alt: Proposition 12 diagram

let the centre :g:`$F$ <0108.g2:F>` of the circle :g:`$ABCDE$ <0108.g2:( A B C D E )>` be taken :just:`[III. 1] <III.1>`, and
let :g:`$FB$ <0108.g2:/ F B />`, :g:`$FK$ <0108.g2:/ F K />`, :g:`$FC$ <0108.g2:/ F C />`, :g:`$FL$ <0108.g2:/ F L />`, :g:`$FD$ <0108.g2:/ F D />` be joined.

Then, since the straight line :g:`$KL$ <0108.g2:/ K L />` touches the circle :g:`$ABCDE$ <0108.g2:( A B C D E )>`
at :g:`$C$ <0108.g2:C>`,
and :g:`$FC$ <0108.g2:/ F C />` has been joined from the centre :g:`$F$ <0108.g2:F>` to the point of
contact at :g:`$C$ <0108.g2:C>`,

.. assertion:: [III. 18] <III.18>

   therefore :g:`$FC$ <0108.g2:/ F C />` is perpendicular to :g:`$KL$ <0108.g2:/ K L />`;

.. assertion::

   therefore each of the angles at :g:`$C$ <0108.g2:< K C L )>` is right.

For the same reason

.. assertion::

   the angles at the points :g:`$B$ <0108.g2:< F B K )>`, :g:`$D$ <0108.g2:< F D L )>` are also right.
