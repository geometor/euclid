And, since the angle $FCK$ is right,
<p class="assertion">therefore the square on $FK$ is equal to the squares on $FC, CK$.</p>

For the same reason <span class="justification">[I. 47]</span>
<p class="assertion">the square on $FK$ is also equal to the squares on $FB, BK$;</p>
so that the squares on $FC, CK$ are equal to the squares
on $FB, BK$,
<p class="sub-clause">of which the square on $FC$ is equal to the square on $FB$;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the square on $CK$ which remains is equal to the<br />
square on $BK$.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore $BK$ is equal to $CK$.</p>

And, since $FB$ is equal to $FC$,
and $FK$ common,
the two sides $BF, FK$ are equal to the two sides $CF, FK$;
and the base $BK$ equal to the base $CK$;
<p class="assertion">therefore the angle $BFK$ is equal to the angle $KFC$, <span class="justification">[I. 8]</span><br />
and the angle $BKF$ to the angle $FKC$.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore the angle $BFC$ is double of the angle $KFC$,<br />
and the angle $BKC$ of the angle $FKC$.</p>

For the same reason
<p class="assertion">the angle $CFD$ is also double of the angle $CFL$,<br />
and the angle $DLC$ of the angle $FLC$.</p>

Now, since the circumference $BC$ is equal to $CD$,
<p class="assertion">the angle $BFC$ is also equal to the angle $CFD$. <span class="justification">[III. 27]</span></p>

And the angle $BFC$ is double of the angle $KFC$, and the
angle $DFC$ of the angle $LFC$;
<p class="assertion">therefore the angle $KFC$ is also equal to the angle $LFC$.</p>

But the angle $FCK$ is also equal to the angle $FCL$;
therefore $FKC, FLC$ are two triangles having two angles
equal to two angles and one side equal to one side, namely
$FC$ which is common to them;
therefore they will also have the remaining sides equal to the
remaining sides, and the remaining angle to the remaining
angle; <span class="justification">[I. 26]</span>
<p class="assertion">therefore the straight line $KC$ is equal to $CL$,<br />
and the angle $FKC$ to the angle $FLC$.</p>

And, since $KC$ is equal to $CL$,
<p class="assertion">therefore $KL$ is double of $KC$.</p>
