## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Congruence of Right Triangles**: Since Euclid does not have a dedicated "hypotenuse-leg" congruence theorem in Book I, he explicitly uses the Pythagorean theorem [I. 47] on $\triangle FCK$ and $\triangle FBK$ to deduce $CK^2 = BK^2$ and hence $BK = CK$, reducing the congruence of $\triangle BFK$ and $\triangle CFK$ to side-side-side [I. 8].
- **Symmetry of Tangent Sides**: Pairs of triangles meeting at a tangent vertex ($\triangle BFK, \triangle CFK$) establish $\angle BKC = 2 \angle FKC$, while pairs of triangles meeting at a point of contact ($\triangle FKC, \triangle FLC$) establish $KC = CL$ and $\angle FKC = \angle FLC$.
