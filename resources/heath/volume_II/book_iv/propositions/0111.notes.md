## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Two-Stage Congruence Strategy**: Rather than bisecting all five angles independently and proving concurrency, Euclid bisects two adjacent angles ($BCD, CDE$) to locate $F$, joins $FB, FA, FE$, and uses SAS congruence ([I. 4]) on $\triangle BCF \cong \triangle DCF$ together with the equiangularity of the pentagon ($\angle ABC = \angle CDE = 2\angle CDF = 2\angle CBF$) to deduce that $BF, FA, FE$ bisect the remaining angles, then uses AAS congruence ([I. 26]) to show equality of the perpendiculars from $F$ to the five sides.
