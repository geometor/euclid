Again, since the point <span data-ref="0113.g1:D">$D$</span> is the centre of the circle <span data-ref="0113.g1:( G C H )">$GCH$</span>,
<p class="assertion"><span data-ref="0113.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0113.g1:/ G D /">$DG$</span>.</p>

But <span data-ref="0113.g1:/ G E /">$GE$</span> was proved equal to <span data-ref="0113.g1:/ G D /">$GD$</span>;
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0113.g1:/ G E /">$GE$</span> is also equal to <span data-ref="0113.g1:/ D E /">$ED$</span>;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the triangle <span data-ref="0113.g1:< E G D >">$EGD$</span> is equilateral;</p>
and therefore its three angles <span data-ref="0113.g1:< E G D )">$EGD$</span>, <span data-ref="0113.g1:< G D E )">$GDE$</span>, <span data-ref="0113.g1:< D E G )">$DEG$</span> are equal
to one another, inasmuch as, in isosceles triangles, the angles
at the base are equal to one another. <span class="justification">[I. 5]</span>

And the three angles of the triangle are equal to two
right angles; <span class="justification">[I. 32]</span>
<p class="assertion">therefore the angle <span data-ref="0113.g1:< E G D )">$EGD$</span> is one-third of two right angles.</p>

Similarly, the angle <span data-ref="0113.g1:< D G C )">$DGC$</span> can also be proved to be one-
third of two right angles.

And, since the straight line <span data-ref="0113.g1:/ G C /">$CG$</span> standing on <span data-ref="0113.g1:/ B E /">$EB$</span> makes
the adjacent angles <span data-ref="0113.g1:< E G C )">$EGC$</span>, <span data-ref="0113.g1:< C G B )">$CGB$</span> equal to two right angles,
<p class="assertion">therefore the remaining angle <span data-ref="0113.g1:< C G B )">$CGB$</span> is also one-third of two<br />
right angles.</p>

Therefore the angles <span data-ref="0113.g1:< E G D )">$EGD$</span>, <span data-ref="0113.g1:< D G C )">$DGC$</span>, <span data-ref="0113.g1:< C G B )">$CGB$</span> are equal to one
another;
so that the angles vertical to them, the angles <span data-ref="0113.g1:< B G A )">$BGA$</span>, <span data-ref="0113.g1:< A G F )">$AGF$</span>,
<span data-ref="0113.g1:< F G E )">$FGE$</span> are equal. <span class="justification">[I. 15]</span>

<p class="assertion">Therefore the six angles <span data-ref="0113.g1:< E G D )">$EGD$</span>, <span data-ref="0113.g1:< D G C )">$DGC$</span>, <span data-ref="0113.g1:< C G B )">$CGB$</span>, <span data-ref="0113.g1:< B G A )">$BGA$</span>, <span data-ref="0113.g1:< A G F )">$AGF$</span>,<br />
<span data-ref="0113.g1:< F G E )">$FGE$</span> are equal to one another.</p>

But equal angles stand on equal circumferences; <span class="justification">[III. 26]</span>
<p class="assertion">therefore the six circumferences <span data-ref="0113.g1:( A B )">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0113.g1:( B C )">$BC$</span>, <span data-ref="0113.g1:( C D )">$CD$</span>, <span data-ref="0113.g1:( D E )">$DE$</span>, <span data-ref="0113.g1:( E F )">$EF$</span>, <span data-ref="0113.g1:( F A )">$FA$</span><br />
are equal to one another.</p>

And equal circumferences are subtended by equal straight
lines; <span class="justification">[III. 29]</span>
<p class="assertion">therefore the six straight lines are equal to one another;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the hexagon <span data-ref="0113.g1:< A B C D E F >">$ABCDEF$</span> is equilateral.</p>

I say next that it is also equiangular.

For, since the circumference <span data-ref="0113.g1:( F A )">$FA$</span> is equal to the circum-
ference <span data-ref="0113.g1:( D E )">$ED$</span>,
<p class="sub-clause">let the circumference <span data-ref="0113.g1:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span> be added to each;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the whole <span data-ref="0113.g1:( F A B C D )">$FABCD$</span> is equal to the whole<br />
<span data-ref="0113.g1:( E D C B A )">$EDCBA$</span>;</p>
