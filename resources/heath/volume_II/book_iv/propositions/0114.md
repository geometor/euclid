Again, since the point $D$ is the centre of the circle $GCH$,
<p class="assertion">$DE$ is equal to $DG$.</p>

But $GE$ was proved equal to $GD$;
<p class="assertion">therefore $GE$ is also equal to $ED$;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the triangle $EGD$ is equilateral;</p>
and therefore its three angles $EGD, GDE, DEG$ are equal
to one another, inasmuch as, in isosceles triangles, the angles
at the base are equal to one another. <span class="justification">[I. 5]</span>

And the three angles of the triangle are equal to two
right angles; <span class="justification">[I. 32]</span>
<p class="assertion">therefore the angle $EGD$ is one-third of two right angles.</p>

Similarly, the angle $DGC$ can also be proved to be one-
third of two right angles.

And, since the straight line $CG$ standing on $EB$ makes
the adjacent angles $EGC, CGB$ equal to two right angles,
<p class="assertion">therefore the remaining angle $CGB$ is also one-third of two<br />
right angles.</p>

Therefore the angles $EGD, DGC, CGB$ are equal to one
another;
so that the angles vertical to them, the angles $BGA, AGF,$
$FGE$ are equal. <span class="justification">[I. 15]</span>

<p class="assertion">Therefore the six angles $EGD, DGC, CGB, BGA, AGF,$<br />
$FGE$ are equal to one another.</p>

But equal angles stand on equal circumferences; <span class="justification">[III. 26]</span>
<p class="assertion">therefore the six circumferences $AB, BC, CD, DE, EF, FA$<br />
are equal to one another.</p>

And equal circumferences are subtended by equal straight
lines; <span class="justification">[III. 29]</span>
<p class="assertion">therefore the six straight lines are equal to one another;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the hexagon $ABCDEF$ is equilateral.</p>

I say next that it is also equiangular.

For, since the circumference $FA$ is equal to the circum-
ference $ED$,
<p class="sub-clause">let the circumference $ABCD$ be added to each;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the whole $FABCD$ is equal to the whole<br />
$EDCBA$;</p>
