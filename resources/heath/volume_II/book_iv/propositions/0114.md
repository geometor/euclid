Again, since the point <span data-ref="0113.g1:D">$D$</span> is the centre of the circle <span data-ref="0113.g1:( G C H )">$GCH$</span>,
<span data-ref="0113.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0113.g1:/ G D /">$DG$</span>.

But <span data-ref="0113.g1:/ G E /">$GE$</span> was proved equal to <span data-ref="0113.g1:/ G D /">$GD$</span>;
therefore <span data-ref="0113.g1:/ G E /">$GE$</span> is also equal to <span data-ref="0113.g1:/ D E /">$ED$</span>;
therefore the triangle <span data-ref="0113.g1:< E G D >">$EGD$</span> is equilateral;
and therefore its three angles <span data-ref="0113.g1:< E G D )">$EGD$</span>, <span data-ref="0113.g1:< G D E )">$GDE$</span>, <span data-ref="0113.g1:< D E G )">$DEG$</span> are equal to one another, inasmuch as, in isosceles triangles, the angles at the base are equal to one another. [I. 5]

And the three angles of the triangle are equal to two right angles; [I. 32]
therefore the angle <span data-ref="0113.g1:< E G D )">$EGD$</span> is one-third of two right angles.

Similarly, the angle <span data-ref="0113.g1:< D G C )">$DGC$</span> can also be proved to be one-third of two right angles.

And, since the straight line <span data-ref="0113.g1:/ G C /">$CG$</span> standing on <span data-ref="0113.g1:/ B E /">$EB$</span> makes the adjacent angles <span data-ref="0113.g1:< E G C )">$EGC$</span>, <span data-ref="0113.g1:< C G B )">$CGB$</span> equal to two right angles,
therefore the remaining angle <span data-ref="0113.g1:< C G B )">$CGB$</span> is also one-third of two right angles.

Therefore the angles <span data-ref="0113.g1:< E G D )">$EGD$</span>, <span data-ref="0113.g1:< D G C )">$DGC$</span>, <span data-ref="0113.g1:< C G B )">$CGB$</span> are equal to one another;
so that the angles vertical to them, the angles <span data-ref="0113.g1:< B G A )">$BGA$</span>, <span data-ref="0113.g1:< A G F )">$AGF$</span>, <span data-ref="0113.g1:< F G E )">$FGE$</span> are equal. [I. 15]

Therefore the six angles <span data-ref="0113.g1:< E G D )">$EGD$</span>, <span data-ref="0113.g1:< D G C )">$DGC$</span>, <span data-ref="0113.g1:< C G B )">$CGB$</span>, <span data-ref="0113.g1:< B G A )">$BGA$</span>, <span data-ref="0113.g1:< A G F )">$AGF$</span>, <span data-ref="0113.g1:< F G E )">$FGE$</span> are equal to one another.

But equal angles stand on equal circumferences; [III. 26]
therefore the six circumferences <span data-ref="0113.g1:( A B )">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0113.g1:( B C )">$BC$</span>, <span data-ref="0113.g1:( C D )">$CD$</span>, <span data-ref="0113.g1:( D E )">$DE$</span>, <span data-ref="0113.g1:( E F )">$EF$</span>, <span data-ref="0113.g1:( F A )">$FA$</span> are equal to one another.

And equal circumferences are subtended by equal straight lines; [III. 29]
therefore the six straight lines are equal to one another;
therefore the hexagon <span data-ref="0113.g1:< A B C D E F >">$ABCDEF$</span> is equilateral.

I say next that it is also equiangular.
