Again, since the point :g:`$D$ <0113.g1:D>` is the centre of the circle :g:`$GCH$ <0113.g1:( G C H )>`,

.. assertion::

   :g:`$DE$ <0113.g1:/ D E />` is equal to :g:`$DG$ <0113.g1:/ G D />`.

But :g:`$GE$ <0113.g1:/ G E />` was proved equal to :g:`$GD$ <0113.g1:/ G D />`;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$GE$ <0113.g1:/ G E />` is also equal to :g:`$ED$ <0113.g1:/ D E />`;

.. assertion::

   therefore the triangle :g:`$EGD$ <0113.g1:< E G D >>` is equilateral;

and therefore its three angles :g:`$EGD$ <0113.g1:< E G D )>`, :g:`$GDE$ <0113.g1:< G D E )>`, :g:`$DEG$ <0113.g1:< D E G )>` are equal
to one another, inasmuch as, in isosceles triangles, the angles
at the base are equal to one another. :just:`[I. 5] <I.5>`

And the three angles of the triangle are equal to two
right angles; :just:`[I. 32] <I.32>`

.. assertion::

   therefore the angle :g:`$EGD$ <0113.g1:< E G D )>` is one-third of two right angles.

Similarly, the angle :g:`$DGC$ <0113.g1:< D G C )>` can also be proved to be one-
third of two right angles.

And, since the straight line :g:`$CG$ <0113.g1:/ G C />` standing on :g:`$EB$ <0113.g1:/ B E />` makes
the adjacent angles :g:`$EGC$ <0113.g1:< E G C )>`, :g:`$CGB$ <0113.g1:< C G B )>` equal to two right angles,

.. assertion::

   | therefore the remaining angle :g:`$CGB$ <0113.g1:< C G B )>` is also one-third of two
   | right angles.

Therefore the angles :g:`$EGD$ <0113.g1:< E G D )>`, :g:`$DGC$ <0113.g1:< D G C )>`, :g:`$CGB$ <0113.g1:< C G B )>` are equal to one
another;
so that the angles vertical to them, the angles :g:`$BGA$ <0113.g1:< B G A )>`, :g:`$AGF$ <0113.g1:< A G F )>`,
:g:`$FGE$ <0113.g1:< F G E )>` are equal. :just:`[I. 15] <I.15>`

.. assertion::

   | Therefore the six angles :g:`$EGD$ <0113.g1:< E G D )>`, :g:`$DGC$ <0113.g1:< D G C )>`, :g:`$CGB$ <0113.g1:< C G B )>`, :g:`$BGA$ <0113.g1:< B G A )>`, :g:`$AGF$ <0113.g1:< A G F )>`,
   | :g:`$FGE$ <0113.g1:< F G E )>` are equal to one another.

But equal angles stand on equal circumferences; :just:`[III. 26] <III.26>`

.. assertion::

   | therefore the six circumferences :g:`$AB$ <0113.g1:( A B )>`, :g:`$BC$ <0113.g1:( B C )>`, :g:`$CD$ <0113.g1:( C D )>`, :g:`$DE$ <0113.g1:( D E )>`, :g:`$EF$ <0113.g1:( E F )>`, :g:`$FA$ <0113.g1:( F A )>`
   | are equal to one another.

And equal circumferences are subtended by equal straight
lines; :just:`[III. 29] <III.29>`

.. assertion::

   therefore the six straight lines are equal to one another;

.. assertion::

   therefore the hexagon :g:`$ABCDEF$ <0113.g1:< A B C D E F >>` is equilateral.

I say next that it is also equiangular.

For, since the circumference :g:`$FA$ <0113.g1:( F A )>` is equal to the circum-
ference :g:`$ED$ <0113.g1:( D E )>`,

.. subclause::

   let the circumference :g:`$ABCD$ <0113.g1:( A B C D )>` be added to each;

.. assertion::

   | therefore the whole :g:`$FABCD$ <0113.g1:( F A B C D )>` is equal to the whole
   | :g:`$EDCBA$ <0113.g1:( E D C B A )>`;
