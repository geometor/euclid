### PROPOSITION 16.

<p class="enunciation">In a given circle to inscribe a fifteen-angled figure which<br />
shall be both equilateral and equiangular.</p>

Let <span data-ref="0116.g1:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span> be the given circle;
thus it is required to inscribe in the circle <span data-ref="0116.g1:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span> a fifteen-angled figure which shall be both equilateral and equiangular.

In the circle <span data-ref="0116.g1:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span> let there be inscribed a side <span data-ref="0116.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> of the equilateral triangle inscribed in it, and a side <span data-ref="0116.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> of an equilateral pentagon;
therefore, of the equal segments of which there are fifteen in the circle <span data-ref="0116.g1:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span>, there will be five in the circumference <span data-ref="0116.g1:( A B C )">$ABC$</span> which is one-third of the circle, and there will be three in the circumference <span data-ref="0116.g1:( A B )">$AB$</span> which is one-fifth of the circle;

![Book IV Proposition 16: Fifteen-angled figure inscribed in circle ABCD using triangle side AC and pentagon side AB](0116.g1.svg)

therefore in the remainder <span data-ref="0116.g1:( B C )">$BC$</span> there will be two of the equal segments.

Let <span data-ref="0116.g1:( B C )">$BC$</span> be bisected at <span data-ref="0116.g1:E">$E$</span>; [III. 30]
therefore each of the circumferences <span data-ref="0116.g1:( B E )">$BE$</span>, <span data-ref="0116.g1:( E C )">$EC$</span> is a fifteenth of the circle <span data-ref="0116.g1:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span>.

If therefore we join <span data-ref="0116.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span>, <span data-ref="0116.g1:/ E C /">$EC$</span> and fit into the circle <span data-ref="0116.g1:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span> straight lines equal to them and in contiguity, a fifteen-angled figure which is both equilateral and equiangular will have been inscribed in it.

And, in like manner as in the case of the pentagon, if
through the points of division on the circle we draw
tangents to the circle, there will be circumscribed about the
circle a fifteen-angled figure which is equilateral and equi-
angular.

And further, by proofs similar to those in the case of the
pentagon, we can both inscribe a circle in the given fifteen-
angled figure and circumscribe one about it.
