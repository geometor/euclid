## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Number-Theoretic Arc Subtraction ($\frac{1}{3} - \frac{1}{5} = \frac{2}{15}$)**: Proposition 16 combines the regular triangle (IV. 2) and regular pentagon (IV. 11) inscribed from a common vertex $A$: since arc $ABC = \frac{5}{15}$ of the circumference and arc $AB = \frac{3}{15}$, their difference $BC = \frac{2}{15}$, which is then bisected at $E$ by [III. 30] to yield a $\frac{1}{15}$ arc ($BE = EC$).
- **Absence of Formal Proof of Equiangularity**: Here Euclid treats the equality of sides and angles of the resulting 15-gon as immediate once chords equal to $BE, EC$ are fitted in contiguity around the circle (via IV. 1).
