## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **"Circle" as Circumference**: Heath points out that in IV. 16 ("of the equal segments of which there are fifteen in the circle $ABCD$... which is one-third of the circle"), Euclid uses κύκλος in the sense of circumference (περιφέρεια), as in III. 10.
- **Astronomical Motivation (Obliquity of the Ecliptic = $\frac{360^\circ}{15} = 24^\circ$)**: Citing Proclus (p. 269) and Theon of Smyrna (pp. 198–9, ed. Hiller, quoting Dercyllides and Eudemus's ἀστρολογίαι), Heath notes that the side of the regular 15-gon subtends $24^\circ$, the standard pre-Eratosthenes Greek value for the obliquity of the ecliptic (later refined by Eratosthenes to $\frac{11}{83} \times 180^\circ \approx 23^\circ\ 51'\ 20''$, adopted by Ptolemy).
- **Constructible Regular Polygons in Euclid**: Heath summarizes the scope of Book IV together with iterated arc bisection ([III. 30]): Euclidean methods construct all regular polygons with $3 \cdot 2^n, 4 \cdot 2^n, 5 \cdot 2^n, 15 \cdot 2^n$ sides ($n \ge 0$).
