For let <span data-ref="0392.g1:C">$C$</span>, the side of <span data-ref="0392.g1:A">$A$</span>, be taken, and let <span data-ref="0392.g1:C">$C$</span> by multiplying<br />
itself make <span data-ref="0392.g1:D">$D$</span>.

<p class="assertion">It is then manifest that <span data-ref="0392.g1:C">$C$</span> by multiplying<br />
<span data-ref="0392.g1:D">$D$</span> has made <span data-ref="0392.g1:A">$A$</span>.</p>

![Proposition 3 Diagram](0392.g1.svg)

<p class="sub-clause">Now, since <span data-ref="0392.g1:C">$C$</span> by multiplying itself has<br />
made <span data-ref="0392.g1:D">$D$</span>,</p>

<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0392.g1:C">$C$</span> measures <span data-ref="0392.g1:D">$D$</span> according to the units in itself.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">But further the unit also measures <span data-ref="0392.g1:C">$C$</span> according to the units<br />
in it;</p>

<p class="assertion">therefore, as the unit is to <span data-ref="0392.g1:C">$C$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0392.g1:C">$C$</span> to <span data-ref="0392.g1:D">$D$</span>. <span class="justification">[VII. Def. 20]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">Again, since <span data-ref="0392.g1:C">$C$</span> by multiplying <span data-ref="0392.g1:D">$D$</span> has made <span data-ref="0392.g1:A">$A$</span>,</p>

<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0392.g1:D">$D$</span> measures <span data-ref="0392.g1:A">$A$</span> according to the units in <span data-ref="0392.g1:C">$C$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">But the unit also measures <span data-ref="0392.g1:C">$C$</span> according to the units in it;</p>

<p class="assertion">therefore, as the unit is to <span data-ref="0392.g1:C">$C$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0392.g1:D">$D$</span> to <span data-ref="0392.g1:A">$A$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">But, as the unit is to <span data-ref="0392.g1:C">$C$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0392.g1:C">$C$</span> to <span data-ref="0392.g1:D">$D$</span>;</p>

<p class="assertion">therefore also, as the unit is to <span data-ref="0392.g1:C">$C$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0392.g1:C">$C$</span> to <span data-ref="0392.g1:D">$D$</span>, and <span data-ref="0392.g1:D">$D$</span> to <span data-ref="0392.g1:A">$A$</span>.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore between the unit and the number <span data-ref="0392.g1:A">$A$</span> two mean<br />
proportional numbers <span data-ref="0392.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0392.g1:D">$D$</span> have fallen in continued proportion.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">Again, since <span data-ref="0392.g1:A">$A$</span> by multiplying itself has made <span data-ref="0392.g1:B">$B$</span>,</p>

<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0392.g1:A">$A$</span> measures <span data-ref="0392.g1:B">$B$</span> according to the units in itself.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">But the unit also measures <span data-ref="0392.g1:A">$A$</span> according to the units in it;</p>

<p class="assertion">therefore, as the unit is to <span data-ref="0392.g1:A">$A$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0392.g1:A">$A$</span> to <span data-ref="0392.g1:B">$B$</span>. <span class="justification">[VII. Def. 20]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But between the unit and <span data-ref="0392.g1:A">$A$</span> two mean proportional numbers<br />
have fallen;</p>

<p class="assertion">therefore two mean proportional numbers will also fall between<br />
<span data-ref="0392.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0392.g1:B">$B$</span>. <span class="justification">[VIII. 8]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But, if two mean proportional numbers fall between two<br />
numbers, and the first be cube, the second will also be cube.<br />
<span class="justification">[VIII. 23]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">And <span data-ref="0392.g1:A">$A$</span> is cube;</p>

<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0392.g1:B">$B$</span> is also cube.</p>

<p class="qed"><span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

---

The product of $a^3$ into itself, or $a^3 \cdot a^3$, is a cube.<br />
For
$$1 : a = a : a^2 = a^2 : a^3.$$
Therefore between $1$ and $a^3$ there are two mean proportionals.<br />
Also
$$1 : a^3 = a^3 : a^3 \cdot a^3.$$
Therefore two mean proportionals fall between $a^3$ and $a^3 \cdot a^3$. <span class="justification">[VIII. 8]</span><br />
(It is true that VIII. 8 is only enunciated of two pairs of numbers, but the<br />
proof is equally valid if one number of one pair is unity.)<br />
And $a^3$ is a cube number:<br />
therefore $a^3 \cdot a^3$ is also cube. <span class="justification">[VIII. 23]</span>
