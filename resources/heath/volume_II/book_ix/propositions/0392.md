For let <span data-ref="0392.g1:C">$C$</span>, the side of <span data-ref="0392.g1:A">$A$</span>, be taken, and let <span data-ref="0392.g1:C">$C$</span> by multiplying itself make <span data-ref="0392.g1:D">$D$</span>.

It is then manifest that <span data-ref="0392.g1:C">$C$</span> by multiplying <span data-ref="0392.g1:D">$D$</span> has made <span data-ref="0392.g1:A">$A$</span>.

![Proposition 3 Diagram](0392.g1.svg)

Now, since <span data-ref="0392.g1:C">$C$</span> by multiplying itself has made <span data-ref="0392.g1:D">$D$</span>, therefore <span data-ref="0392.g1:C">$C$</span> measures <span data-ref="0392.g1:D">$D$</span> according to the units in itself.

But further the unit also measures <span data-ref="0392.g1:C">$C$</span> according to the units in it; therefore, as the unit is to <span data-ref="0392.g1:C">$C$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0392.g1:C">$C$</span> to <span data-ref="0392.g1:D">$D$</span>. [VII. Def. 20]

Again, since <span data-ref="0392.g1:C">$C$</span> by multiplying <span data-ref="0392.g1:D">$D$</span> has made <span data-ref="0392.g1:A">$A$</span>, therefore <span data-ref="0392.g1:D">$D$</span> measures <span data-ref="0392.g1:A">$A$</span> according to the units in <span data-ref="0392.g1:C">$C$</span>.

But the unit also measures <span data-ref="0392.g1:C">$C$</span> according to the units in it; therefore, as the unit is to <span data-ref="0392.g1:C">$C$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0392.g1:D">$D$</span> to <span data-ref="0392.g1:A">$A$</span>.

But, as the unit is to <span data-ref="0392.g1:C">$C$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0392.g1:C">$C$</span> to <span data-ref="0392.g1:D">$D$</span>; therefore also, as the unit is to <span data-ref="0392.g1:C">$C$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0392.g1:C">$C$</span> to <span data-ref="0392.g1:D">$D$</span>, and <span data-ref="0392.g1:D">$D$</span> to <span data-ref="0392.g1:A">$A$</span>.

Therefore between the unit and the number <span data-ref="0392.g1:A">$A$</span> two mean proportional numbers <span data-ref="0392.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0392.g1:D">$D$</span> have fallen in continued proportion.

Again, since <span data-ref="0392.g1:A">$A$</span> by multiplying itself has made <span data-ref="0392.g1:B">$B$</span>, therefore <span data-ref="0392.g1:A">$A$</span> measures <span data-ref="0392.g1:B">$B$</span> according to the units in itself.

But the unit also measures <span data-ref="0392.g1:A">$A$</span> according to the units in it; therefore, as the unit is to <span data-ref="0392.g1:A">$A$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0392.g1:A">$A$</span> to <span data-ref="0392.g1:B">$B$</span>. [VII. Def. 20]

But between the unit and <span data-ref="0392.g1:A">$A$</span> two mean proportional numbers have fallen; therefore two mean proportional numbers will also fall between <span data-ref="0392.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0392.g1:B">$B$</span>. [VIII. 8]

But, if two mean proportional numbers fall between two numbers, and the first be cube, the second will also be cube. [VIII. 23]

And <span data-ref="0392.g1:A">$A$</span> is cube;  
therefore <span data-ref="0392.g1:B">$B$</span> is also cube.

**Q. E. D.**

---

The product of $a^3$ into itself, or $a^3 \cdot a^3$, is a cube.  
For
$$1 : a = a : a^2 = a^2 : a^3.$$
Therefore between $1$ and $a^3$ there are two mean proportionals.  
Also
$$1 : a^3 = a^3 : a^3 \cdot a^3.$$
Therefore two mean proportionals fall between $a^3$ and $a^3 \cdot a^3$. [VIII. 8]  
(It is true that VIII. 8 is only enunciated of two pairs of numbers, but the proof is equally valid if one number of one pair is unity.)  
And $a^3$ is a cube number:  
therefore $a^3 \cdot a^3$ is also cube. [VIII. 23]
