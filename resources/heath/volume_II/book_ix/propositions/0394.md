Now, since <span data-ref="0393.g2:A">$A$</span> by multiplying itself has made <span data-ref="0393.g2:D">$D$</span>, and by multiplying <span data-ref="0393.g2:B">$B$</span> has made <span data-ref="0393.g2:C">$C$</span>, therefore, as <span data-ref="0393.g2:A">$A$</span> is to <span data-ref="0393.g2:B">$B$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0393.g2:D">$D$</span> to <span data-ref="0393.g2:C">$C$</span>. [VII. 17]

And since <span data-ref="0393.g2:D">$D$</span>, <span data-ref="0393.g2:C">$C$</span> are cube, they are similar solid numbers.

Therefore two mean proportional numbers fall between <span data-ref="0393.g2:D">$D$</span>, <span data-ref="0393.g2:C">$C$</span>. [VIII. 19]

And, as <span data-ref="0393.g2:D">$D$</span> is to <span data-ref="0393.g2:C">$C$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0393.g2:A">$A$</span> to <span data-ref="0393.g2:B">$B$</span>; therefore two mean proportional numbers fall between <span data-ref="0393.g2:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0393.g2:B">$B$</span> also. [VIII. 8]

And <span data-ref="0393.g2:A">$A$</span> is cube;  
therefore <span data-ref="0393.g2:B">$B$</span> is also cube. [VIII. 23]

If the product $a^3b$ is a cube number, $b$ is cube.  
By IX. 3, the product $a^3 \cdot a^3$ is a cube.  
And
$$a^3 \cdot a^3 : a^3b = a^3 : b. \hfill \text{[VII. 17]}$$
The first two terms are cubes, and therefore "similar solids"; therefore there are two mean proportionals between them. [VIII. 19]  
Therefore there are two mean proportionals between $a^3$, $b$. [VIII. 8]  
And $a^3$ is a cube:  
therefore $b$ is a cube number. [VIII. 23]

### PROPOSITION 6.

*If a number by multiplying itself make a cube number, it will itself also be cube.*

For let the number <span data-ref="0394.g1:A">$A$</span> by multiplying itself make the cube number <span data-ref="0394.g1:B">$B$</span>;  
I say that <span data-ref="0394.g1:A">$A$</span> is also cube.

![Proposition 6 Diagram](0394.g1.svg)

For let <span data-ref="0394.g1:A">$A$</span> by multiplying <span data-ref="0394.g1:B">$B$</span> make <span data-ref="0394.g1:C">$C$</span>.

Since, then, <span data-ref="0394.g1:A">$A$</span> by multiplying itself has made <span data-ref="0394.g1:B">$B$</span>, and by multiplying <span data-ref="0394.g1:B">$B$</span> has made <span data-ref="0394.g1:C">$C$</span>, therefore <span data-ref="0394.g1:C">$C$</span> is cube.

And, since <span data-ref="0394.g1:A">$A$</span> by multiplying itself has made <span data-ref="0394.g1:B">$B$</span>, therefore <span data-ref="0394.g1:A">$A$</span> measures <span data-ref="0394.g1:B">$B$</span> according to the units in itself.

But the unit also measures <span data-ref="0394.g1:A">$A$</span> according to the units in it.  
Therefore, as the unit is to <span data-ref="0394.g1:A">$A$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0394.g1:A">$A$</span> to <span data-ref="0394.g1:B">$B$</span>. [VII. Def. 20]

And, since <span data-ref="0394.g1:A">$A$</span> by multiplying <span data-ref="0394.g1:B">$B$</span> has made <span data-ref="0394.g1:C">$C$</span>, therefore <span data-ref="0394.g1:B">$B$</span> measures <span data-ref="0394.g1:C">$C$</span> according to the units in <span data-ref="0394.g1:A">$A$</span>.

But the unit also measures <span data-ref="0394.g1:A">$A$</span> according to the units in it.
