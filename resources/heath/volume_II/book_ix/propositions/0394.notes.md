## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Proposition 5 Conclusion**: The ratio $D : C = A : B$ is exploited; since $D$ and $C$ are cubes, two mean proportionals fall between them (VIII. 19), hence also between $A$ and $B$ (VIII. 8). Because $A$ is a cube, $B$ must be cube (VIII. 23).
- **Proposition 6 Formulation**: If $A^2 = B$ is cube, then $A$ is cube. Euclid forms $C = A \times B = A^3$, which is cube by definition of cube numbers. He then sets up proportions from unity: $1 : A = A : B$ and $1 : A = B : C$.
- **Heath's Algebraic Summary**: Heath clarifies the reduction: $a^3 \cdot a^3 : a^3b = a^3 : b$, showing the elegance of Euclid's method.
