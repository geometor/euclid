<p class="assertion">Therefore, as the unit is to <span data-ref="0394.g1:A">$A$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0394.g1:B">$B$</span> to <span data-ref="0394.g1:C">$C$</span>. <span class="justification">[VII. Def. 20]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But, as the unit is to <span data-ref="0394.g1:A">$A$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0394.g1:A">$A$</span> to <span data-ref="0394.g1:B">$B$</span>;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore also, as <span data-ref="0394.g1:A">$A$</span> is to <span data-ref="0394.g1:B">$B$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0394.g1:B">$B$</span> to <span data-ref="0394.g1:C">$C$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">And, since <span data-ref="0394.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0394.g1:C">$C$</span> are cube,</p>
<p class="assertion">they are similar solid numbers.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore there are two mean proportional numbers<br />
between <span data-ref="0394.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0394.g1:C">$C$</span>. <span class="justification">[VIII. 19]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">And, as <span data-ref="0394.g1:B">$B$</span> is to <span data-ref="0394.g1:C">$C$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0394.g1:A">$A$</span> to <span data-ref="0394.g1:B">$B$</span>.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore there are two mean proportional numbers<br />
between <span data-ref="0394.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0394.g1:B">$B$</span> also. <span class="justification">[VIII. 8]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">And <span data-ref="0394.g1:B">$B$</span> is cube;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0394.g1:A">$A$</span> is also cube. <span class="justification">[cf. VIII. 23]</span></p>

<p class="qed"><span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

---

If $a^2$ is a cube number, $a$ is also a cube.<br />
For
$$1 : a = a : a^2 = a^2 : a^3.$$
Now $a^2$, $a^3$ are both cubes, and therefore "similar solids"; therefore there<br />
are two mean proportionals between them. <span class="justification">[VIII. 19]</span><br />
Therefore there are two mean proportionals between $a$, $a^2$. <span class="justification">[VIII. 8]</span><br />
And $a^2$ is a cube:<br />
therefore $a$ is also a cube number. <span class="justification">[VIII. 23]</span>

It will be noticed that the last step is not an exact quotation of the result<br />
of VIII. 23, because it is there the *first* of four terms which is known to be a<br />
cube, and the *last* which is proved to be a cube; here the case is reversed.<br />
But there is no difficulty. Without inverting the proportions, we have only<br />
to refer to VIII. 21 which proves that $a$, $a^2$, having two mean proportionals<br />
between them, are two similar solid numbers; whence, since $a^2$ is a cube,<br />
$a$ is also a cube.

### PROPOSITION 7.

<p class="enunciation">If a composite number by multiplying any number make<br />
some number, the product will be solid.</p>

For let the composite number <span data-ref="0395.g1:A">$A$</span> by multiplying any number<br />
<span data-ref="0395.g1:B">$B$</span> make <span data-ref="0395.g1:C">$C$</span>;<br />
I say that <span data-ref="0395.g1:C">$C$</span> is solid.

![Proposition 7 Diagram](0395.g1.svg)

<p class="sub-clause">For, since <span data-ref="0395.g1:A">$A$</span> is composite,</p>
<p class="assertion">it will be measured by some<br />
number. <span class="justification">[VII. Def. 13]</span></p>

Let it be measured by <span data-ref="0395.g1:D">$D$</span>;<br />
and, as many times as <span data-ref="0395.g1:D">$D$</span> measures <span data-ref="0395.g1:A">$A$</span>, so many units let there<br />
be in <span data-ref="0395.g1:E">$E$</span>.
