## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Critical Scholarly Note on VIII. 23**: Heath explains why the citation in Proposition 6 is marked `[cf. VIII. 23]`: in VIII. 23, the *first* term of four numbers in continued proportion is known to be a cube, and the *fourth* is proved to be cube; in Proposition 6, the order is reversed (the fourth is cube, proving the first is cube). Heath demonstrates that VIII. 21 justifies the step directly since $a, a^2$ are similar solid numbers.
- **Proposition 7 Setup**: A composite number $A$ is factored as $D \times E$ (VII. Def. 13 & 15), so $C = A \times B = (D \times E) \times B$, showing $C$ is a solid number with sides $D, E, B$.
