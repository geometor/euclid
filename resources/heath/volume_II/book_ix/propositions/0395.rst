.. assertion:: [VII. Def. 20] <VII.def.20>

   Therefore, as the unit is to :g:`$A$ <0394.g1:A>`, so is :g:`$B$ <0394.g1:B>` to :g:`$C$ <0394.g1:C>`.

.. subclause::

   But, as the unit is to :g:`$A$ <0394.g1:A>`, so is :g:`$A$ <0394.g1:A>` to :g:`$B$ <0394.g1:B>`;

.. assertion::

   therefore also, as :g:`$A$ <0394.g1:A>` is to :g:`$B$ <0394.g1:B>`, so is :g:`$B$ <0394.g1:B>` to :g:`$C$ <0394.g1:C>`.

.. subclause::

   And, since :g:`$B$ <0394.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0394.g1:C>` are cube,

.. assertion::

   they are similar solid numbers.

.. assertion::

   Therefore there are two mean proportional numbers between :g:`$B$ <0394.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0394.g1:C>`. :just:`[VIII. 19] <VIII.19>`

.. subclause::

   And, as :g:`$B$ <0394.g1:B>` is to :g:`$C$ <0394.g1:C>`, so is :g:`$A$ <0394.g1:A>` to :g:`$B$ <0394.g1:B>`.

.. assertion::

   Therefore there are two mean proportional numbers between :g:`$A$ <0394.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0394.g1:B>` also. :just:`[VIII. 8] <VIII.8>`

.. subclause::

   And :g:`$B$ <0394.g1:B>` is cube;

.. assertion:: [cf. VIII. 23]

   therefore :g:`$A$ <0394.g1:A>` is also cube.

.. qed::

   :just:`Q. E. D.`

----

If $a^2$ is a cube number, $a$ is also a cube.

For

$$1 : a = a : a^2 = a^2 : a^3.$$

Now $a^2$, $a^3$ are both cubes, and therefore "similar solids"; therefore there are two mean proportionals between them. :just:`[VIII. 19] <VIII.19>`

Therefore there are two mean proportionals between $a$, $a^2$. :just:`[VIII. 8] <VIII.8>`

And $a^2$ is a cube: therefore $a$ is also a cube number. :just:`[VIII. 23] <VIII.23>`

It will be noticed that the last step is not an exact quotation of the result of VIII. 23, because it is there the *first* of four terms which is known to be a cube, and the *last* which is proved to be a cube; here the case is reversed. But there is no difficulty. Without inverting the proportions, we have only to refer to VIII. 21 which proves that $a$, $a^2$, having two mean proportionals between them, are two similar solid numbers; whence, since $a^2$ is a cube, $a$ is also a cube.

----

PROPOSITION 7.
--------------

.. enunciation::

   If a composite number by multiplying any number make some number, the product will be solid.

For let the composite number :g:`$A$ <0395.g1:A>` by multiplying any number :g:`$B$ <0395.g1:B>` make :g:`$C$ <0395.g1:C>`;

I say that :g:`$C$ <0395.g1:C>` is solid.

.. picture:: 0395.g1.svg
   :alt: Proposition 7 Diagram

.. subclause::

   For, since :g:`$A$ <0395.g1:A>` is composite,

.. assertion::

   it will be measured by some number. :just:`[VII. Def. 13] <VII.def.13>`

Let it be measured by :g:`$D$ <0395.g1:D>`; and, as many times as :g:`$D$ <0395.g1:D>` measures :g:`$A$ <0395.g1:A>`, so many units let there be in :g:`$E$ <0395.g1:E>`.
