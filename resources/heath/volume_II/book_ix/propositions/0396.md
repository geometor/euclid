<p class="sub-clause">Since then <span data-ref="0395.g1:D">$D$</span> measures <span data-ref="0395.g1:A">$A$</span> according to the units in <span data-ref="0395.g1:E">$E$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0395.g1:E">$E$</span> by multiplying <span data-ref="0395.g1:D">$D$</span> has made <span data-ref="0395.g1:A">$A$</span>. <span class="justification">[VII. Def. 15]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">And, since <span data-ref="0395.g1:A">$A$</span> by multiplying <span data-ref="0395.g1:B">$B$</span> has made <span data-ref="0395.g1:C">$C$</span>,<br />
and <span data-ref="0395.g1:A">$A$</span> is the product of <span data-ref="0395.g1:D">$D$</span>, <span data-ref="0395.g1:E">$E$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the product of <span data-ref="0395.g1:D">$D$</span>, <span data-ref="0395.g1:E">$E$</span> by multiplying <span data-ref="0395.g1:B">$B$</span> has made <span data-ref="0395.g1:C">$C$</span>.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore <span data-ref="0395.g1:C">$C$</span> is solid, and <span data-ref="0395.g1:D">$D$</span>, <span data-ref="0395.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0395.g1:B">$B$</span> are its sides.</p>

<p class="qed"><span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

---

Since a composite number is the product of two factors, the result of<br />
multiplying it by another number is to produce a number which is the<br />
product of three factors, i.e. a "solid number."

### PROPOSITION 8.

<p class="enunciation">If as many numbers as we please beginning from an unit be<br />
in continued proportion, the third from the unit will be square,<br />
as will also those which successively leave out one; the fourth<br />
will be cube, as will also all those which leave out two; and the<br />
seventh will be at once cube and square, as will also those which<br />
leave out five.</p>

Let there be as many numbers as we please, <span data-ref="0396.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0396.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0396.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0396.g1:D">$D$</span>,<br />
<span data-ref="0396.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0396.g1:F">$F$</span>, beginning from an unit and in con-<br />
tinued proportion;<br />
I say that <span data-ref="0396.g1:B">$B$</span>, the third from the unit, is<br />
square, as are also all those which leave<br />
out one; <span data-ref="0396.g1:C">$C$</span>, the fourth, is cube, as are<br />
also all those which leave out two; and<br />
<span data-ref="0396.g1:F">$F$</span>, the seventh, is at once cube and<br />
square, as are also all those which leave out five.

![Proposition 8 Diagram](0396.g1.svg)

<p class="sub-clause">For since, as the unit is to <span data-ref="0396.g1:A">$A$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0396.g1:A">$A$</span> to <span data-ref="0396.g1:B">$B$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the unit measures the number <span data-ref="0396.g1:A">$A$</span> the same number<br />
of times that <span data-ref="0396.g1:A">$A$</span> measures <span data-ref="0396.g1:B">$B$</span>. <span class="justification">[VII. Def. 20]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But the unit measures the number <span data-ref="0396.g1:A">$A$</span> according to the<br />
units in it;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0396.g1:A">$A$</span> also measures <span data-ref="0396.g1:B">$B$</span> according to the units in <span data-ref="0396.g1:A">$A$</span>.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore <span data-ref="0396.g1:A">$A$</span> by multiplying itself has made <span data-ref="0396.g1:B">$B$</span>;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0396.g1:B">$B$</span> is square.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">And, since <span data-ref="0396.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0396.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0396.g1:D">$D$</span> are in continued proportion, and <span data-ref="0396.g1:B">$B$</span> is<br />
square,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0396.g1:D">$D$</span> is also square. <span class="justification">[VIII. 22]</span></p>
