Since then <span data-ref="0395.g1:D">$D$</span> measures <span data-ref="0395.g1:A">$A$</span> according to the units in <span data-ref="0395.g1:E">$E$</span>, therefore <span data-ref="0395.g1:E">$E$</span> by multiplying <span data-ref="0395.g1:D">$D$</span> has made <span data-ref="0395.g1:A">$A$</span>. [VII. Def. 15]

And, since <span data-ref="0395.g1:A">$A$</span> by multiplying <span data-ref="0395.g1:B">$B$</span> has made <span data-ref="0395.g1:C">$C$</span>, and <span data-ref="0395.g1:A">$A$</span> is the product of <span data-ref="0395.g1:D">$D$</span>, <span data-ref="0395.g1:E">$E$</span>, therefore the product of <span data-ref="0395.g1:D">$D$</span>, <span data-ref="0395.g1:E">$E$</span> by multiplying <span data-ref="0395.g1:B">$B$</span> has made <span data-ref="0395.g1:C">$C$</span>.

Therefore <span data-ref="0395.g1:C">$C$</span> is solid, and <span data-ref="0395.g1:D">$D$</span>, <span data-ref="0395.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0395.g1:B">$B$</span> are its sides.

**Q. E. D.**

---

Since a composite number is the product of two factors, the result of multiplying it by another number is to produce a number which is the product of three factors, i.e. a "solid number."

### PROPOSITION 8.

*If as many numbers as we please beginning from an unit be in continued proportion, the third from the unit will be square, as will also those which successively leave out one; the fourth will be cube, as will also all those which leave out two; and the seventh will be at once cube and square, as will also those which leave out five.*

Let there be as many numbers as we please, <span data-ref="0396.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0396.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0396.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0396.g1:D">$D$</span>, <span data-ref="0396.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0396.g1:F">$F$</span>, beginning from an unit and in continued proportion;  
I say that <span data-ref="0396.g1:B">$B$</span>, the third from the unit, is square, as are also all those which leave out one; <span data-ref="0396.g1:C">$C$</span>, the fourth, is cube, as are also all those which leave out two; and <span data-ref="0396.g1:F">$F$</span>, the seventh, is at once cube and square, as are also all those which leave out five.

![Proposition 8 Diagram](0396.g1.svg)

For since, as the unit is to <span data-ref="0396.g1:A">$A$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0396.g1:A">$A$</span> to <span data-ref="0396.g1:B">$B$</span>, therefore the unit measures the number <span data-ref="0396.g1:A">$A$</span> the same number of times that <span data-ref="0396.g1:A">$A$</span> measures <span data-ref="0396.g1:B">$B$</span>. [VII. Def. 20]

But the unit measures the number <span data-ref="0396.g1:A">$A$</span> according to the units in it; therefore <span data-ref="0396.g1:A">$A$</span> also measures <span data-ref="0396.g1:B">$B$</span> according to the units in <span data-ref="0396.g1:A">$A$</span>.

Therefore <span data-ref="0396.g1:A">$A$</span> by multiplying itself has made <span data-ref="0396.g1:B">$B$</span>;  
therefore <span data-ref="0396.g1:B">$B$</span> is square.

And, since <span data-ref="0396.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0396.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0396.g1:D">$D$</span> are in continued proportion, and <span data-ref="0396.g1:B">$B$</span> is square, therefore <span data-ref="0396.g1:D">$D$</span> is also square. [VIII. 22]
