## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Proposition 7 Conclusion**: The proof finishes cleanly with the definition of solid numbers: $C = D \cdot E \cdot B$.
- **Proposition 8 Geometric Sequence Theorem**: Proposition 8 investigates a geometric progression starting from unity: $1, A, B, C, D, E, F, \dots$ ($1, a, a^2, a^3, a^4, a^5, a^6, \dots$).
  - 3rd from unity ($B = a^2$) and leaving out one ($D = a^4, F = a^6, \dots$) are square.
  - 4th from unity ($C = a^3$) and leaving out two ($F = a^6, \dots$) are cube.
  - 7th from unity ($F = a^6$) and leaving out five are both square and cube ($a^6 = (a^3)^2 = (a^2)^3$).
- **Application of VII. Def. 20 and VIII. 22**: Euclid demonstrates that $B = A^2$ is square, and since $B, C, D$ are in continued proportion, $D$ is also square by VIII. 22.
