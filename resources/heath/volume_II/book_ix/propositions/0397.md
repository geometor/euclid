For the same reason  
$F$ is also square.  
Similarly we can prove that all those which leave out one are square.

I say next that $C,$ the fourth from the unit, is cube, as are also all those which leave out two.

For since, as the unit is to $A,$ so is $B$ to $C,$  
therefore the unit measures the number $A$ the same number of times that $B$ measures $C.$

But the unit measures the number $A$ according to the units in $A;$  
therefore $B$ also measures $C$ according to the units in $A.$

Therefore $A$ by multiplying $B$ has made $C.$

Since then $A$ by multiplying itself has made $B,$ and by multiplying $B$ has made $C,$  
therefore $C$ is cube.

And, since $C, D, E, F$ are in continued proportion, and $C$ is cube,  
therefore $F$ is also cube. [VIII. 23]

But it was also proved square;  
therefore the seventh from the unit is both cube and square.

Similarly we can prove that all the numbers which leave out five are also both cube and square.

If $1, a, a_2, a_3, \dots$ be a geometrical progression, then $a_2, a_4, a_6, \dots$ are squares;  
$a_3, a_6, a_9, \dots$ are cubes;  
$a_6, a_{12}, \dots$ are both squares and cubes.

Since
$$\begin{aligned}
1 : a &= a : a_2, \\
a_2 &= a^2.
\end{aligned}$$
And, since $a_2, a_3, a_4$ are in geometrical progression and $a_2\ (= a^2)$ is a square,
$$a_4\text{ is a square. \hfill [VIII. 22]}$$
Similarly $a_6, a_8, \dots$ are squares.

Next,
$$\begin{aligned}
1 : a &= a_2 : a_3 \\
&= a^2 : a_3,
\end{aligned}$$
whence $a_3 = a^3$, a cube number.

And, since $a_3, a_4, a_5, a_6$ are in geometrical progression, and $a_3$ is a cube,
$$a_6\text{ is a cube. \hfill [VIII. 23]}$$
