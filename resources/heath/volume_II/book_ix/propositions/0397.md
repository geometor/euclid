For the same reason  
<span data-ref="0396.g1:F">$F$</span> is also square.  
Similarly we can prove that all those which leave out one are square.

I say next that <span data-ref="0396.g1:C">$C$</span>, the fourth from the unit, is cube, as are also all those which leave out two.

For since, as the unit is to <span data-ref="0396.g1:A">$A$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0396.g1:B">$B$</span> to <span data-ref="0396.g1:C">$C$</span>,  
therefore the unit measures the number <span data-ref="0396.g1:A">$A$</span> the same number of times that <span data-ref="0396.g1:B">$B$</span> measures <span data-ref="0396.g1:C">$C$</span>.

But the unit measures the number <span data-ref="0396.g1:A">$A$</span> according to the units in <span data-ref="0396.g1:A">$A$</span>;  
therefore <span data-ref="0396.g1:B">$B$</span> also measures <span data-ref="0396.g1:C">$C$</span> according to the units in <span data-ref="0396.g1:A">$A$</span>.

Therefore <span data-ref="0396.g1:A">$A$</span> by multiplying <span data-ref="0396.g1:B">$B$</span> has made <span data-ref="0396.g1:C">$C$</span>.

Since then <span data-ref="0396.g1:A">$A$</span> by multiplying itself has made <span data-ref="0396.g1:B">$B$</span>, and by multiplying <span data-ref="0396.g1:B">$B$</span> has made <span data-ref="0396.g1:C">$C$</span>,  
therefore <span data-ref="0396.g1:C">$C$</span> is cube.

And, since <span data-ref="0396.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0396.g1:D">$D$</span>, <span data-ref="0396.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0396.g1:F">$F$</span> are in continued proportion, and <span data-ref="0396.g1:C">$C$</span> is cube,  
therefore <span data-ref="0396.g1:F">$F$</span> is also cube. [VIII. 23]

But it was also proved square;  
therefore the seventh from the unit is both cube and square.

Similarly we can prove that all the numbers which leave out five are also both cube and square.

<p align="right">Q. E. D.</p>

If $1, a, a_2, a_3, \dots$ be a geometrical progression, then $a_2, a_4, a_6, \dots$ are squares;  
$a_3, a_6, a_9, \dots$ are cubes;  
$a_6, a_{12}, \dots$ are both squares and cubes.

Since
$$\begin{aligned}
1 : a &= a : a_2, \\
a_2 &= a^2.
\end{aligned}$$
And, since $a_2, a_3, a_4$ are in geometrical progression and $a_2\ (= a^2)$ is a square,
$$a_4\text{ is a square. \hfill [VIII. 22]}$$
Similarly $a_6, a_8, \dots$ are squares.

Next,
$$\begin{aligned}
1 : a &= a_2 : a_3 \\
&= a^2 : a_3,
\end{aligned}$$
whence $a_3 = a^3$, a cube number.

And, since $a_3, a_4, a_5, a_6$ are in geometrical progression, and $a_3$ is a cube,
$$a_6\text{ is a cube. \hfill [VIII. 23]}$$
