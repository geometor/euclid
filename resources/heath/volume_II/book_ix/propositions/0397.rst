:title: Proposition 8 (continuation)

.. subclause::

   For the same reason

.. assertion::

   :g:`F <0396.g1:F>` is also square.

.. assertion::

   Similarly we can prove that all those which leave out one are square.

I say next that :g:`C <0396.g1:C>`, the fourth from the unit, is cube, as are also all those which leave out two.

.. subclause::

   For since, as the unit is to :g:`A <0396.g1:A>`, so is :g:`B <0396.g1:B>` to :g:`C <0396.g1:C>`,

.. assertion::

   therefore the unit measures the number :g:`A <0396.g1:A>` the same number of times that :g:`B <0396.g1:B>` measures :g:`C <0396.g1:C>`.

.. subclause::

   But the unit measures the number :g:`A <0396.g1:A>` according to the units in :g:`A <0396.g1:A>`;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`B <0396.g1:B>` also measures :g:`C <0396.g1:C>` according to the units in :g:`A <0396.g1:A>`.

.. assertion::

   Therefore :g:`A <0396.g1:A>` by multiplying :g:`B <0396.g1:B>` has made :g:`C <0396.g1:C>`.

.. subclause::

   Since then :g:`A <0396.g1:A>` by multiplying itself has made :g:`B <0396.g1:B>`, and by multiplying :g:`B <0396.g1:B>` has made :g:`C <0396.g1:C>`,

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`C <0396.g1:C>` is cube.

.. subclause::

   And, since :g:`C <0396.g1:C>`, :g:`D <0396.g1:D>`, :g:`E <0396.g1:E>`, :g:`F <0396.g1:F>` are in continued proportion, and :g:`C <0396.g1:C>` is cube,

.. assertion:: [VIII. 23] <VIII.23>

   therefore :g:`F <0396.g1:F>` is also cube.

.. subclause::

   But it was also proved square;

.. assertion::

   therefore the seventh from the unit is both cube and square.

.. assertion::

   Similarly we can prove that all the numbers which leave out five are also both cube and square.

.. qed::

   :just:`Q. E. D.`

----

If $1, a, a_2, a_3, \dots$ be a geometrical progression, then $a_2, a_4, a_6, \dots$ are squares; $a_3, a_6, a_9, \dots$ are cubes; $a_6, a_{12}, \dots$ are both squares and cubes.

Since

$$\begin{aligned}
1 : a &= a : a_2, \\
a_2 &= a^2.
\end{aligned}$$

And, since $a_2, a_3, a_4$ are in geometrical progression and $a_2\ (= a^2)$ is a square,

$$a_4\text{ is a square. \hfill [VIII. 22]}$$

Similarly $a_6, a_8, \dots$ are squares.

Next,

$$\begin{aligned}
1 : a &= a_2 : a_3 \\
&= a^2 : a_3,
\end{aligned}$$

whence $a_3 = a^3$, a cube number.

And, since $a_3, a_4, a_5, a_6$ are in geometrical progression, and $a_3$ is a cube,

$$a_6\text{ is a cube. \hfill [VIII. 23]}$$
