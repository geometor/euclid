<p class="assertion">Similarly we can prove that neither is any other of the<br />
numbers cube except the fourth from the unit and those which<br />
leave out two.</p>

<p class="qed"><span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

---

If $1, a, a_2, a_3, a_4, \dots$ be a geometrical progression, then (1), if $a$ is not a<br />
square, none of the terms will be square except $a_2, a_4, a_6, \dots$ ;<br />
and (2), if $a$ is not a cube, none of the terms will be cube except $a_3, a_6, a_9, \dots$.

With reference to the first part of the proof, viz. that which proves that, if<br />
$a_3$ is a square, $a$ must be a square, Heiberg remarks that the words which<br />
I have bracketed are perhaps spurious ; for it is easier to use VIII. 24 than<br />
the converse of VIII. 26, and a use of VIII. 24 would correspond better to the<br />
use of VIII. 25 in the second part relating to cubes. I agree in this view and<br />
have bracketed the words accordingly. (See however note, p. 383, on<br />
converses of VIII. 26, 27 given by Heron.) If this change be made, the<br />
proof runs as follows.

(1) If possible, let $a_3$ be square.<br />
Now
$$a_2 : a_3 = a : a_2.$$
But $a_2$ is a square. <span class="justification">[ix. 8]</span><br />
Therefore $a$ is to $a_2$ in the ratio of a square to a square.<br />
And $a_2$ is square ;<br />
therefore $a$ is square <span class="justification">[VIII. 24]</span>: which is impossible.

(2) If possible, let $a_4$ be a cube.<br />
Now
$$a_3 : a_4 = a_2 : a_3.$$
And $a_3$ is a cube. <span class="justification">[ix. 8]</span><br />
Therefore $a_2$ is to $a_3$ in the ratio of a cube to a cube.<br />
And $a_3$ is a cube :<br />
therefore $a_2$ is a cube. <span class="justification">[VIII. 25]</span><br />
But, since
$$\begin{aligned}
1 : a &= a : a_2, \\
a_2 &= a^2.
\end{aligned}$$
And, since $a^2$ is a cube,<br />
$a$ must be a cube <span class="justification">[ix. 6]</span>: which is impossible.

The propositions VIII. 24, 25 are here not quoted in their exact form in<br />
that the first and second squares, or cubes, change places. But there is no<br />
difficulty, since the method by which the theorems are proved shows that<br />
either inference is equally correct.

### PROPOSITION 11.

<p class="enunciation">If as many numbers as we please beginning from an unit be<br />
in continued proportion, the less measures the greater according<br />
to some one of the numbers which have place among the propor-<br />
tional numbers.</p>
