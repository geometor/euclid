Let there be as many numbers as we please, <span data-ref="0402.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0402.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0402.g1:D">$D$</span>, <span data-ref="0402.g1:E">$E$</span>,<br />
beginning from the unit <span data-ref="0402.g1:A">$A$</span> and in con-<br />
tinued proportion ;<br />
I say that <span data-ref="0402.g1:B">$B$</span>, the least of the numbers <span data-ref="0402.g1:B">$B$</span>,<br />
<span data-ref="0402.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0402.g1:D">$D$</span>, <span data-ref="0402.g1:E">$E$</span>, measures <span data-ref="0402.g1:E">$E$</span> according to some<br />
one of the numbers <span data-ref="0402.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0402.g1:D">$D$</span>.

![Proposition 11 Diagram](0402.g1.svg)

<p class="sub-clause">For since, as the unit <span data-ref="0402.g1:A">$A$</span> is to <span data-ref="0402.g1:B">$B$</span>, so<br />
is <span data-ref="0402.g1:D">$D$</span> to <span data-ref="0402.g1:E">$E$</span>,</p>

<p class="assertion">therefore the unit <span data-ref="0402.g1:A">$A$</span> measures the number <span data-ref="0402.g1:B">$B$</span> the same number<br />
of times as <span data-ref="0402.g1:D">$D$</span> measures <span data-ref="0402.g1:E">$E$</span>;</p>

<p class="assertion">therefore, alternately, the unit <span data-ref="0402.g1:A">$A$</span> measures <span data-ref="0402.g1:D">$D$</span> the same number<br />
of times as <span data-ref="0402.g1:B">$B$</span> measures <span data-ref="0402.g1:E">$E$</span>. <span class="justification">[VII. 15]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But the unit <span data-ref="0402.g1:A">$A$</span> measures <span data-ref="0402.g1:D">$D$</span> according to the units in it;</p>

<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0402.g1:B">$B$</span> also measures <span data-ref="0402.g1:E">$E$</span> according to the units in <span data-ref="0402.g1:D">$D$</span>;</p>

<p class="assertion">so that <span data-ref="0402.g1:B">$B$</span> the less measures <span data-ref="0402.g1:E">$E$</span> the greater according to some<br />
number of those which have place among the proportional<br />
numbers.—</p>

<p class="assertion">PORISM. And it is manifest that, whatever place the<br />
measuring number has, reckoned from the unit, the same<br />
place also has the number according to which it measures,<br />
reckoned from the number measured, in the direction of the<br />
number before it.—</p>

<p class="qed"><span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

The proposition and the porism together assert that, if $1, a, a_2, \dots a_n$ be a<br />
geometrical progression, $a_r$ measures $a_n$ and gives the quotient $a_{n-r}\ (r < n)$.

Euclid only proves that $a_n = a \cdot a_{n-1}$, as follows.
$$1 : a = a_{n-1} : a_n.$$
Therefore $1$ measures $a$ the same number of times as $a_{n-1}$ measures $a_n.$<br />
Hence $1$ measures $a_{n-1}$ the same number of times as $a$ measures $a_n;$ <span class="justification">[VII. 15]</span><br />
that is,
$$a_n = a \cdot a_{n-1}.$$

We can supply the proof of the porism as follows.
$$\begin{aligned}
1 : a &= a_r : a_{r+1}, \\
a : a_2 &= a_{r+1} : a_{r+2}, \\
&\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots \\
a_{n-r-1} : a_{n-r} &= a_{n-1} : a_n,
\end{aligned}$$
whence, *ex aequali*,
$$1 : a_{n-r} = a_r : a_n.\text{ \hfill [VII. 14]}$$

It follows, by the same argument as before, that
$$a_n = a_r \cdot a_{n-r}.$$

With our notation, we have the theorem of indices that
$$a^{m+n} = a^m \cdot a^n.$$
