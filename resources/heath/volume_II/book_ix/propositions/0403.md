### PROPOSITION 12.

<p class="enunciation">If as many numbers as we please beginning from an unit be<br />
in continued proportion, by however many prime numbers the<br />
last is measured, the next to the unit will also be measured by<br />
the same.</p>

Let there be as many numbers as we please, <span data-ref="0403.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0403.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0403.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0403.g1:D">$D$</span>,<br />
beginning from an unit, and in continued proportion;<br />
I say that, by however many prime numbers <span data-ref="0403.g1:D">$D$</span> is measured,<br />
<span data-ref="0403.g1:A">$A$</span> will also be measured by the same.

![Proposition 12 Diagram](0403.g1.svg)

For let <span data-ref="0403.g1:D">$D$</span> be measured by any prime number <span data-ref="0403.g1:E">$E$</span>;<br />
I say that <span data-ref="0403.g1:E">$E$</span> measures <span data-ref="0403.g1:A">$A$</span>.

For suppose it does not;

<p class="sub-clause">now <span data-ref="0403.g1:E">$E$</span> is prime, and any prime number is prime to any which<br />
it does not measure; <span class="justification">[VII. 29]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0403.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0403.g1:A">$A$</span> are prime to one another.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">And, since <span data-ref="0403.g1:E">$E$</span> measures <span data-ref="0403.g1:D">$D$</span>, let it measure it according to <span data-ref="0403.g1:F">$F$</span>,</p>

<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0403.g1:E">$E$</span> by multiplying <span data-ref="0403.g1:F">$F$</span> has made <span data-ref="0403.g1:D">$D$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">Again, since <span data-ref="0403.g1:A">$A$</span> measures <span data-ref="0403.g1:D">$D$</span> according to the units in <span data-ref="0403.g1:C">$C$</span>,<br />
<span class="justification">[IX. 11 and Por.]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0403.g1:A">$A$</span> by multiplying <span data-ref="0403.g1:C">$C$</span> has made <span data-ref="0403.g1:D">$D$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">But, further, <span data-ref="0403.g1:E">$E$</span> has also by multiplying <span data-ref="0403.g1:F">$F$</span> made <span data-ref="0403.g1:D">$D$</span>;</p>

<p class="assertion">therefore the product of <span data-ref="0403.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0403.g1:C">$C$</span> is equal to the product of <span data-ref="0403.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0403.g1:F">$F$</span>.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore, as <span data-ref="0403.g1:A">$A$</span> is to <span data-ref="0403.g1:E">$E$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0403.g1:F">$F$</span> to <span data-ref="0403.g1:C">$C$</span>. <span class="justification">[VII. 19]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But <span data-ref="0403.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0403.g1:E">$E$</span> are prime,<br />
primes are also least, <span class="justification">[VII. 21]</span><br />
and the least measure those which have the same ratio the<br />
same number of times, the antecedent the antecedent and the<br />
consequent the consequent ; <span class="justification">[VII. 20]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0403.g1:E">$E$</span> measures <span data-ref="0403.g1:C">$C$</span>.</p>

Let it measure it according to <span data-ref="0403.g1:G">$G$</span>;

<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0403.g1:E">$E$</span> by multiplying <span data-ref="0403.g1:G">$G$</span> has made <span data-ref="0403.g1:C">$C$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">But, further, by the theorem before this,<br />
<span data-ref="0403.g1:A">$A$</span> has also by multiplying <span data-ref="0403.g1:B">$B$</span> made <span data-ref="0403.g1:C">$C$</span>. <span class="justification">[IX. 11 and Por.]</span></p>
