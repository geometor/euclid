For, if possible, suppose that $p$ does not measure $a$; then, $p$ being prime,<br />
$p, a$ are prime to one another. <span class="justification">[VII. 29]</span>

Suppose
$$a_n = m \cdot p.$$

Now
$$a_n = a \cdot a_{n-1}. \qquad \text{[IX. 11]}$$

Therefore
$$a \cdot a_{n-1} = m \cdot p,$$
and
$$a : p = m : a_{n-1}. \qquad \text{[VII. 19]}$$

Hence, $a, p$ being prime to one another,
$$p \text{ measures } a_{n-1}. \qquad \text{[VII. 20, 21]}$$

By a repetition of the same process, we can prove that $p$ measures $a_{n-2}$<br />
and therefore $a_{n-3}$, and so on, and finally that $p$ measures $a$.

But, by hypothesis, $p$ does not measure $a$: which is impossible.

Hence $p, a$ are not prime to one another:<br />
therefore they have some common factor. <span class="justification">[VII. Def. 14]</span>

But $p$ is the only number which measures $p$;<br />
therefore $p$ measures $a$.

Heiberg remarks that, as, in the ἔκθεσις, Euclid sets himself to prove that<br />
<span data-ref="0403.g1:E">$E$</span> measures <span data-ref="0403.g1:A">$A$</span>, the words bracketed above are unnecessary and therefore<br />
perhaps interpolated.

### PROPOSITION 13.

<p class="enunciation">If as many numbers as we please beginning from an unit be<br />
in continued proportion, and the number after the unit be prime,<br />
the greatest will not be measured by any except those which have<br />
a place among the proportional numbers.</p>

Let there be as many numbers as we please, <span data-ref="0405.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0405.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0405.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0405.g1:D">$D$</span>,<br />
beginning from an unit and in continued proportion, and let <span data-ref="0405.g1:A">$A$</span>,<br />
the number after the unit, be prime;<br />
I say that <span data-ref="0405.g1:D">$D$</span>, the greatest of them, will not be measured by any<br />
other number except <span data-ref="0405.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0405.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0405.g1:C">$C$</span>.

![Proposition 13 diagram](0405.g1.svg)

For, if possible, let it be measured by <span data-ref="0405.g1:E">$E$</span>, and let <span data-ref="0405.g1:E">$E$</span> not be<br />
the same with any of the numbers <span data-ref="0405.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0405.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0405.g1:C">$C$</span>.

<p class="assertion">It is then manifest that <span data-ref="0405.g1:E">$E$</span> is not prime.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">For, if <span data-ref="0405.g1:E">$E$</span> is prime and measures <span data-ref="0405.g1:D">$D$</span>,<br />
it will also measure <span data-ref="0405.g1:A">$A$</span> <span class="justification">[IX. 12]</span>, which is prime, though it is not<br />
the same with it:</p>

<p class="assertion">which is impossible.</p>
