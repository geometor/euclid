| For, if possible, suppose that $p$ does not measure $a$; then, $p$ being prime,
| $p, a$ are prime to one another. :just:`[VII. 29] <VII.29>`

Suppose

$$a_n = m \cdot p.$$

Now

$$a_n = a \cdot a_{n-1}. \qquad \text{[IX. 11]}$$

Therefore

$$a \cdot a_{n-1} = m \cdot p,$$

and

$$a : p = m : a_{n-1}. \qquad \text{[VII. 19]}$$

Hence, $a, p$ being prime to one another,

$$p \text{ measures } a_{n-1}. \qquad \text{[VII. 20, 21]}$$

| By a repetition of the same process, we can prove that $p$ measures $a_{n-2}$
| and therefore $a_{n-3}$, and so on, and finally that $p$ measures $a$.

But, by hypothesis, $p$ does not measure $a$: which is impossible.

| Hence $p, a$ are not prime to one another:
| therefore they have some common factor. :just:`[VII. Def. 14] <VII.def.14>`

| But $p$ is the only number which measures $p$;
| therefore $p$ measures $a$.

| Heiberg remarks that, as, in the ἔκθεσις, Euclid sets himself to prove that
| :g:`$E$ <0403.g1:E>` measures :g:`$A$ <0403.g1:A>`, the words bracketed above are unnecessary and therefore
| perhaps interpolated.

PROPOSITION 13.
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

.. enunciation::

   If as many numbers as we please beginning from an unit be in continued proportion, and the number after the unit be prime, the greatest will not be measured by any except those which have a place among the proportional numbers.

| Let there be as many numbers as we please, :g:`$A$ <0405.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0405.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0405.g1:C>`, :g:`$D$ <0405.g1:D>`,
| beginning from an unit and in continued proportion, and let :g:`$A$ <0405.g1:A>`,
| the number after the unit, be prime;
| I say that :g:`$D$ <0405.g1:D>`, the greatest of them, will not be measured by any
| other number except :g:`$A$ <0405.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0405.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0405.g1:C>`.

.. picture:: 0405.g1.svg
   :alt: Proposition 13 diagram

| For, if possible, let it be measured by :g:`$E$ <0405.g1:E>`, and let :g:`$E$ <0405.g1:E>` not be
| the same with any of the numbers :g:`$A$ <0405.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0405.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0405.g1:C>`.

.. assertion::

   It is then manifest that :g:`$E$ <0405.g1:E>` is not prime.

.. subclause::

   | For, if :g:`$E$ <0405.g1:E>` is prime and measures :g:`$D$ <0405.g1:D>`,
   | it will also measure :g:`$A$ <0405.g1:A>` :just:`[IX. 12] <IX.12>`, which is prime, though it is not
   | the same with it:

.. assertion::

   which is impossible.
