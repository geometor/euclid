| I say that :g:`$A$ <0409.g1:A>` will not be measured by any other prime number
| except :g:`$B$ <0409.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0409.g1:C>`, :g:`$D$ <0409.g1:D>`.

| For, if possible, let it be measured by the prime number
| :g:`$E$ <0409.g1:E>`, and let :g:`$E$ <0409.g1:E>` not be the same with any one of the numbers
| :g:`$B$ <0409.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0409.g1:C>`, :g:`$D$ <0409.g1:D>`.

.. picture:: 0409.g1.svg
   :alt: Proposition 14 diagram

.. subclause::

   | Now, since :g:`$E$ <0409.g1:E>` measures :g:`$A$ <0409.g1:A>`, let it measure it according
   | to :g:`$F$ <0409.g1:F>` ;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$E$ <0409.g1:E>` by multiplying :g:`$F$ <0409.g1:F>` has made :g:`$A$ <0409.g1:A>`.

.. subclause::

   And :g:`$A$ <0409.g1:A>` is measured by the prime numbers :g:`$B$ <0409.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0409.g1:C>`, :g:`$D$ <0409.g1:D>`.

.. subclause::

   | But, if two numbers by multiplying one another make some
   | number, and any prime number measure the product, it will
   | also measure one of the original numbers ; :just:`[VII. 30] <VII.30>`

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$B$ <0409.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0409.g1:C>`, :g:`$D$ <0409.g1:D>` will measure one of the numbers :g:`$E$ <0409.g1:E>`, :g:`$F$ <0409.g1:F>`.

.. subclause::

   | Now they will not measure :g:`$E$ <0409.g1:E>` ;
   | for :g:`$E$ <0409.g1:E>` is prime and not the same with any one of the numbers
   | :g:`$B$ <0409.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0409.g1:C>`, :g:`$D$ <0409.g1:D>`.

.. assertion::

   | Therefore they will measure :g:`$F$ <0409.g1:F>`, which is less than :g:`$A$ <0409.g1:A>` :
   | which is impossible, for :g:`$A$ <0409.g1:A>` is by hypothesis the least number
   | measured by :g:`$B$ <0409.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0409.g1:C>`, :g:`$D$ <0409.g1:D>`.

.. assertion::

   | Therefore no prime number will measure :g:`$A$ <0409.g1:A>` except
   | :g:`$B$ <0409.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0409.g1:C>`, :g:`$D$ <0409.g1:D>`.

.. qed::

   :just:`Q. E. D.`

----

| In other words, a number can be resolved into prime factors in only
| one way.

| Let $a$ be the least number measured by each of the prime numbers
| $b, c, d, \dots k$.

If possible, suppose that $a$ has a prime factor $p$ different from $b, c, d, \dots k$.

Let

$$a = p \cdot m.$$

Now $b, c, d, \dots k$, measuring $a$, must measure one of the two factors $p, m$. :just:`[VII. 30] <VII.30>`

| They do not, by hypothesis, measure $p$ ;
| therefore they must measure $m$, a number less than $a$ :
| which is contrary to the hypothesis.

Therefore $a$ has no prime factors except $b, c, d, \dots k$.
