<p class="assertion">Similarly we can prove that <span data-ref="0410.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0410.g1:C">$C$</span> added together are<br />
prime to <span data-ref="0410.g1:A">$A$</span>.</p>

I say next that <span data-ref="0410.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0410.g1:C">$C$</span> added together are also prime to <span data-ref="0410.g1:B">$B$</span>.

<p class="sub-clause">For, since <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span> is prime to each of the numbers <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>, <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">the square on <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span> is also prime to the product of <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>, <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>. <span class="justification">[VII. 24, 25]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But the squares on <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>, <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> together with twice the pro-<br />
duct of <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>, <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> are equal to the square on <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span> ; <span class="justification">[II. 4]</span></p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the squares on <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>, <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> together with twice the<br />
product of <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>, <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> are prime to the product of <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>, <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>.</p>

<p class="assertion">*Separando*, the squares on <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>, <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> together with once<br />
the product of <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>, <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> are prime to the product of <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>, <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore, *separando* again, the squares on <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>, <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> are<br />
prime to the product of <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>, <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">And the square on <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> is <span data-ref="0410.g1:A">$A$</span>,<br />
the product of <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>, <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> is <span data-ref="0410.g1:B">$B$</span>,<br />
and the square on <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> is <span data-ref="0410.g1:C">$C$</span>.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore <span data-ref="0410.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0410.g1:C">$C$</span> added together are prime to <span data-ref="0410.g1:B">$B$</span>.</p>

<p class="qed"><span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

If *a*, *b*, *c* be a geometrical progression in the least terms which have a<br />
given common ratio, (*b* + *c*), (*c* + *a*), (*a* + *b*) are respectively prime to *a*, *b*, *c*.

Let *α* : *β* be the common ratio in its lowest terms, so that the geometrical<br />
progression is
$$\alpha^2,\quad \alpha\beta,\quad \beta^2. \qquad \text{[VIII. 2]}$$

Now, *α*, *β* being prime to one another,<br />
*α* + *β* is prime to both *α* and *β*. <span class="justification">[VII. 28]</span>

Therefore (*α* + *β*), *α* are both prime to *β*.

Hence (*α* + *β*) *α* is prime to *β*, <span class="justification">[VII. 24]</span><br />
and therefore to *β*² ; <span class="justification">[VII. 25]</span><br />
i.e. *α*² + *αβ* is prime to *β*²,<br />
or *a* + *b* is prime to *c*.

Similarly, *αβ* + *β*² is prime to *α*²,<br />
or *b* + *c* is prime to *a*.

Lastly, *α* + *β* being prime to both *α* and *β*,<br />
(*α* + *β*)² is prime to *αβ*, <span class="justification">[VII. 24, 25]</span><br />
or *α*² + *β*² + 2*αβ* is prime to *αβ* :<br />
whence *α*² + *β*² is prime to *αβ*.

The latter inference, made in two steps, may be proved by *reductio ad<br />
absurdum* as Commandinus proves it.

If *α*² + *β*² is not prime to *αβ*, let *x* measure them ;<br />
therefore *x* measures *α*² + *β*² + 2*αβ* as well as *αβ* ;<br />
hence *α*² + *β*² + 2*αβ* and *αβ* are not prime to one another, which is contrary<br />
to the hypothesis.
