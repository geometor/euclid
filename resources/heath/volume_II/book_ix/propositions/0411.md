Similarly we can prove that <span data-ref="0410.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0410.g1:C">$C$</span> added together are prime to <span data-ref="0410.g1:A">$A$</span>.

I say next that <span data-ref="0410.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0410.g1:C">$C$</span> added together are also prime to <span data-ref="0410.g1:B">$B$</span>.

For, since <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span> is prime to each of the numbers <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>, <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, the square on <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span> is also prime to the product of <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>, <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>. [VII. 24, 25]

But the squares on <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>, <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> together with twice the product of <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>, <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> are equal to the square on <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span>; [II. 4] therefore the squares on <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>, <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> together with twice the product of <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>, <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> are prime to the product of <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>, <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>.

_Separando_, the squares on <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>, <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> together with once the product of <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>, <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> are prime to the product of <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>, <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>.

Therefore, _separando_ again, the squares on <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>, <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> are prime to the product of <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>, <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>.

And the square on <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> is <span data-ref="0410.g1:A">$A$</span>, the product of <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>, <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> is <span data-ref="0410.g1:B">$B$</span>, and the square on <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> is <span data-ref="0410.g1:C">$C$</span>.

Therefore <span data-ref="0410.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0410.g1:C">$C$</span> added together are prime to <span data-ref="0410.g1:B">$B$</span>.

<p align="right">Q. E. D.</p>

If _a_, _b_, _c_ be a geometrical progression in the least terms which have a given common ratio, (_b_ + _c_), (_c_ + _a_), (_a_ + _b_) are respectively prime to _a_, _b_, _c_.

Let _α_ : _β_ be the common ratio in its lowest terms, so that the geometrical progression is
$$\alpha^2,\quad \alpha\beta,\quad \beta^2. \qquad \text{[VIII. 2]}$$

Now, _α_, _β_ being prime to one another,
_α_ + _β_ is prime to both _α_ and _β_. [VII. 28]

Therefore (_α_ + _β_), _α_ are both prime to _β_.

Hence (_α_ + _β_) _α_ is prime to _β_, [VII. 24]
and therefore to _β_²; [VII. 25]
i.e. _α_² + _αβ_ is prime to _β_²,
or _a_ + _b_ is prime to _c_.

Similarly, _αβ_ + _β_² is prime to _α_²,
or _b_ + _c_ is prime to _a_.

Lastly, _α_ + _β_ being prime to both _α_ and _β_,
(_α_ + _β_)² is prime to _αβ_, [VII. 24, 25]
or _α_² + _β_² + 2_αβ_ is prime to _αβ_;
whence _α_² + _β_² is prime to _αβ_.

The latter inference, made in two steps, may be proved by _reductio ad absurdum_ as Commandinus proves it.

If _α_² + _β_² is not prime to _αβ_, let _x_ measure them; therefore _x_ measures _α_² + _β_² + 2_αβ_ as well as _αβ_; hence _α_² + _β_² + 2_αβ_ and _αβ_ are not prime to one another, which is contrary to the hypothesis.
