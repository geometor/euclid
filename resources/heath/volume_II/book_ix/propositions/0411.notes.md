# Transcription Notes: Folio 0411 (Page 405)

## Summary & Folio Continuity
- **Page Position**: Folio 0411 corresponds to page 405 of Heath's *Elements*, Volume II, Book IX.
- **Content**:
  - Concludes the Greek proof of **Proposition 15** (begun on page 404 / folio 0410).
  - Demonstrates that $(B + C)$ is prime to $A$, and $(A + C)$ is prime to $B$.
  - Concludes with Q. E. D.
  - Followed by Heath's mathematical commentary on Proposition 15, providing modern algebraic notation for the geometric progression in lowest terms $\alpha^2, \alpha\beta, \beta^2$ and Commandinus's *reductio ad absurdum* lemma.
- **Continuity**:
  - Follows folio 0410 (page 404), which introduced Proposition 15 and established the construction of $DE, EF$ and the first part of the proof.
  - Precedes folio 0412 (page 406), where Proposition 16 begins.

## Textual / Lexical / Mathematical Observations
- **Technical Vocabulary**:
  - *Separando* (*διελόντι*): Used twice in arithmetic operation on ratios and sums of squares, reducing $(DE^2 + EF^2 + 2 DE \cdot EF)$ coprime to $DE \cdot EF$ first to $(DE^2 + EF^2 + DE \cdot EF)$ coprime to $DE \cdot EF$, and then to $(DE^2 + EF^2)$ coprime to $DE \cdot EF$.
- **Citations in Margins**:
  - `[VII. 24, 25]`: If two numbers be prime to any number, their product will be prime to the same; powers of primes are prime to each other.
  - `[II. 4]`: Algebraic identity $(x + y)^2 = x^2 + y^2 + 2xy$ applied geometrically to segments $DE, EF$.
  - `[VIII. 2]`: Construction of continued proportion in least terms.
  - `[VII. 28]`: If two numbers be prime to one another, their sum will be prime to each.
- **Notation**:
  - Heath uses Greek letters $\alpha, \beta$ for the common ratio in lowest terms, distinguishing them from the progression terms $a, b, c$. In the 1908 edition, the Greek alpha font closely mirrors an italic $a$, but context confirms $\alpha : \beta$.
