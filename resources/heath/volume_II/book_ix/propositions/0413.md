<p class="sub-clause">But <span data-ref="0412.g2:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0412.g2:D">$D$</span> are prime,<br />

and the least numbers measure those which have the same<br />
ratio the same number of times, the antecedent the antecedent<br />
and the consequent the consequent. <span class="justification">[VII. 20]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore <span data-ref="0412.g2:A">$A$</span> measures <span data-ref="0412.g2:B">$B$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">And, as <span data-ref="0412.g2:A">$A$</span> is to <span data-ref="0412.g2:B">$B$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0412.g2:B">$B$</span> to <span data-ref="0412.g2:C">$C$</span>.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore <span data-ref="0412.g2:B">$B$</span> also measures <span data-ref="0412.g2:C">$C$</span> ;<br />
so that <span data-ref="0412.g2:A">$A$</span> also measures <span data-ref="0412.g2:C">$C$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">And since, as <span data-ref="0412.g2:B">$B$</span> is to <span data-ref="0412.g2:C">$C$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0412.g2:C">$C$</span> to <span data-ref="0412.g2:D">$D$</span>,<br />
and <span data-ref="0412.g2:B">$B$</span> measures <span data-ref="0412.g2:C">$C$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0412.g2:C">$C$</span> also measures <span data-ref="0412.g2:D">$D$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">But <span data-ref="0412.g2:A">$A$</span> measured <span data-ref="0412.g2:C">$C$</span> ;</p>
<p class="assertion">so that <span data-ref="0412.g2:A">$A$</span> also measures <span data-ref="0412.g2:D">$D$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">But it also measures itself ;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0412.g2:A">$A$</span> measures <span data-ref="0412.g2:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0412.g2:D">$D$</span> which are prime to one another :<br />

If $a, a_2, a_3, \dots a_n$ be a geometrical progression, and $a, a_n$ are prime to one<br />
another, then $a, a_2, a_n$ can have no integral fourth proportional.

For, if possible, let
$$a : a_2 = a_n : x.$$

Therefore
$$a : a_n = a_2 : x,$$
and hence <span class="justification">[VII. 20, 21]</span> *a* measures $a_2$.

Therefore $a_2$ measures $a_3$, <span class="justification">[VII. Def. 20]</span><br />
and hence *a* measures $a_3$, and therefore also ultimately $a_n$.

Thus $a, a_n$ are both measured by *a* : which is contrary to the hypothesis.

### PROPOSITION 18.

<p class="enunciation">Given two numbers, to investigate whether it is possible to<br />
find a third proportional to them.</p>

Let <span data-ref="0414.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0414.g1:B">$B$</span> be the given two numbers, and let it be required<br />
to investigate whether it is possible to find a third proportional<br />
to them.

<p class="sub-clause">Now <span data-ref="0414.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0414.g1:B">$B$</span> are either prime to one another or not.</p>

<p class="assertion">And, if they are prime to one another, it has been proved<br />
that it is impossible to find a third proportional to them. <span class="justification">[IX. 16]</span></p>
