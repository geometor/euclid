Next, let <span data-ref="0414.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0414.g1:B">$B$</span> not be prime to one another,<br />
and let <span data-ref="0414.g1:B">$B$</span> by multiplying itself make <span data-ref="0414.g1:C">$C$</span>.

Then <span data-ref="0414.g1:A">$A$</span> either measures <span data-ref="0414.g1:C">$C$</span> or does not measure it.

![Proposition 18](0414.g1.svg)

<p class="sub-clause">First, let it measure it according to <span data-ref="0414.g1:D">$D$</span> ;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0414.g1:A">$A$</span> by multiplying <span data-ref="0414.g1:D">$D$</span> has made <span data-ref="0414.g1:C">$C$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">But, further, <span data-ref="0414.g1:B">$B$</span> has also by multiplying itself made <span data-ref="0414.g1:C">$C$</span> ;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the product of <span data-ref="0414.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0414.g1:D">$D$</span> is equal to the square on <span data-ref="0414.g1:B">$B$</span>.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore, as <span data-ref="0414.g1:A">$A$</span> is to <span data-ref="0414.g1:B">$B$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0414.g1:B">$B$</span> to <span data-ref="0414.g1:D">$D$</span> ; <span class="justification">[VII. 19]</span><br />
therefore a third proportional number <span data-ref="0414.g1:D">$D$</span> has been found to<br />
<span data-ref="0414.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0414.g1:B">$B$</span>.</p>

Next, let <span data-ref="0414.g1:A">$A$</span> not measure <span data-ref="0414.g1:C">$C$</span> ;<br />
I say that it is impossible to find a third proportional number<br />
to <span data-ref="0414.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0414.g1:B">$B$</span>.

For, if possible, let <span data-ref="0414.g1:D">$D$</span>, such third proportional, have been<br />
found.

<p class="assertion">Therefore the product of <span data-ref="0414.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0414.g1:D">$D$</span> is equal to the square on <span data-ref="0414.g1:B">$B$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">But the square on <span data-ref="0414.g1:B">$B$</span> is <span data-ref="0414.g1:C">$C$</span> ;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the product of <span data-ref="0414.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0414.g1:D">$D$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0414.g1:C">$C$</span>.</p>

<p class="assertion">Hence <span data-ref="0414.g1:A">$A$</span> by multiplying <span data-ref="0414.g1:D">$D$</span> has made <span data-ref="0414.g1:C">$C$</span> ;<br />
therefore <span data-ref="0414.g1:A">$A$</span> measures <span data-ref="0414.g1:C">$C$</span> according to <span data-ref="0414.g1:D">$D$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">But, by hypothesis, it also does not measure it :</p>
<p class="assertion">which is absurd.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore it is not possible to find a third proportional<br />
number to <span data-ref="0414.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0414.g1:B">$B$</span> when <span data-ref="0414.g1:A">$A$</span> does not measure <span data-ref="0414.g1:C">$C$</span>.</p>

<p class="qed"><span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

Given two numbers *a*, *b*, to find the condition that they may have an<br />
integral third proportional.

(1) *a*, *b* must not be prime to one another. <span class="justification">[IX. 16]</span>

(2) *a* must measure *b*².

For, if *a*, *b*, *c* be in continued proportion,
$$ac = b^2.$$

Therefore *a* measures *b*².

Condition (1) is included in condition (2) since, if $b^2 = ma$, *a* and *b* cannot<br />
be prime to one another.

The result is of course easily seen if the three terms in continued<br />
proportion be written
$$a,\quad a\frac{b}{a},\quad a\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^2.$$
