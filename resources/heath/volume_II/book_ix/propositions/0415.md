### PROPOSITION 19.

_Given three numbers, to investigate when it is possible to find a fourth proportional to them._

Let <span data-ref="0415.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0415.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0415.g1:C">$C$</span> be the given three numbers, and let it be required to investigate when it is possible to find a fourth proportional to them.

![Proposition 19](0415.g1.svg)

Now either they are not in continued proportion, and the extremes of them are prime to one another;
or they are in continued proportion, and the extremes of them are not prime to one another;
or they are not in continued proportion, nor are the extremes of them prime to one another;
or they are in continued proportion, and the extremes of them are prime to one another.

If then <span data-ref="0415.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0415.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0415.g1:C">$C$</span> are in continued proportion, and the extremes of them <span data-ref="0415.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0415.g1:C">$C$</span> are prime to one another, it has been proved that it is impossible to find a fourth proportional number to them. [IX. 17]

†Next, let <span data-ref="0415.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0415.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0415.g1:C">$C$</span> not be in continued proportion, the extremes being again prime to one another;
I say that in this case also it is impossible to find a fourth proportional to them.

For, if possible, let <span data-ref="0415.g1:D">$D$</span> have been found, so that, as <span data-ref="0415.g1:A">$A$</span> is to <span data-ref="0415.g1:B">$B$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0415.g1:C">$C$</span> to <span data-ref="0415.g1:D">$D$</span>,
and let it be contrived that, as <span data-ref="0415.g1:B">$B$</span> is to <span data-ref="0415.g1:C">$C$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0415.g1:D">$D$</span> to <span data-ref="0415.g1:E">$E$</span>.

Now, since, as <span data-ref="0415.g1:A">$A$</span> is to <span data-ref="0415.g1:B">$B$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0415.g1:C">$C$</span> to <span data-ref="0415.g1:D">$D$</span>,
and, as <span data-ref="0415.g1:B">$B$</span> is to <span data-ref="0415.g1:C">$C$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0415.g1:D">$D$</span> to <span data-ref="0415.g1:E">$E$</span>,
therefore, _ex aequali_, as <span data-ref="0415.g1:A">$A$</span> is to <span data-ref="0415.g1:C">$C$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0415.g1:C">$C$</span> to <span data-ref="0415.g1:E">$E$</span>. [VII. 14]

But <span data-ref="0415.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0415.g1:C">$C$</span> are prime, primes are also least, [VII. 21] and the least numbers measure those which have the same ratio, the antecedent the antecedent and the consequent the consequent. [VII. 20]

Therefore <span data-ref="0415.g1:A">$A$</span> measures <span data-ref="0415.g1:C">$C$</span> as antecedent antecedent.
