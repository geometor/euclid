### PROPOSITION 19.

<p class="enunciation">Given three numbers, to investigate when it is possible to<br />
find a fourth proportional to them.</p>

Let <span data-ref="0415.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0415.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0415.g1:C">$C$</span> be the given three numbers, and let it be<br />
required to investigate when it is<br />
possible to find a fourth proportional<br />
to them.

![Proposition 19](0415.g1.svg)

Now either they are not in con-<br />
tinued proportion, and the extremes<br />
of them are prime to one another ;<br />
or they are in continued proportion, and the extremes of them<br />
are not prime to one another ;<br />
or they are not in continued proportion, nor are the extremes<br />
of them prime to one another ;<br />
or they are in continued proportion, and the extremes of them<br />
are prime to one another.

<p class="sub-clause">If then <span data-ref="0415.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0415.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0415.g1:C">$C$</span> are in continued proportion, and the<br />
extremes of them <span data-ref="0415.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0415.g1:C">$C$</span> are prime to one another,</p>
<p class="assertion">it has been proved that it is impossible to find a fourth pro-<br />
portional number to them. <span class="justification">[IX. 17]</span></p>

†Next, let <span data-ref="0415.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0415.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0415.g1:C">$C$</span> not be in continued proportion, the<br />
extremes being again prime to one another ;<br />
I say that in this case also it is impossible to find a fourth<br />
proportional to them.

For, if possible, let <span data-ref="0415.g1:D">$D$</span> have been found, so that,<br />
as <span data-ref="0415.g1:A">$A$</span> is to <span data-ref="0415.g1:B">$B$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0415.g1:C">$C$</span> to <span data-ref="0415.g1:D">$D$</span>,<br />
and let it be contrived that, as <span data-ref="0415.g1:B">$B$</span> is to <span data-ref="0415.g1:C">$C$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0415.g1:D">$D$</span> to <span data-ref="0415.g1:E">$E$</span>.

<p class="sub-clause">Now, since, as <span data-ref="0415.g1:A">$A$</span> is to <span data-ref="0415.g1:B">$B$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0415.g1:C">$C$</span> to <span data-ref="0415.g1:D">$D$</span>,<br />
and, as <span data-ref="0415.g1:B">$B$</span> is to <span data-ref="0415.g1:C">$C$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0415.g1:D">$D$</span> to <span data-ref="0415.g1:E">$E$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore, <em>ex aequali</em>, as <span data-ref="0415.g1:A">$A$</span> is to <span data-ref="0415.g1:C">$C$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0415.g1:C">$C$</span> to <span data-ref="0415.g1:E">$E$</span>. <span class="justification">[VII. 14]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But <span data-ref="0415.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0415.g1:C">$C$</span> are prime,<br />
primes are also least, <span class="justification">[VII. 21]</span><br />
and the least numbers measure those which have the same<br />
ratio, the antecedent the antecedent and the consequent the<br />
consequent. <span class="justification">[VII. 20]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore <span data-ref="0415.g1:A">$A$</span> measures <span data-ref="0415.g1:C">$C$</span> as antecedent antecedent.</p>
