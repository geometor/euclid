PROPOSITION 19.
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

.. enunciation::

   Given three numbers, to investigate when it is possible to find a fourth proportional to them.

| Let :g:`$A$ <0415.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0415.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0415.g1:C>` be the given three numbers, and let it be
| required to investigate when it is
| possible to find a fourth proportional
| to them.

.. picture:: 0415.g1.svg
   :alt: Proposition 19

| Now either they are not in con-
| tinued proportion, and the extremes
| of them are prime to one another ;
| or they are in continued proportion, and the extremes of them
| are not prime to one another ;
| or they are not in continued proportion, nor are the extremes
| of them prime to one another ;
| or they are in continued proportion, and the extremes of them
| are prime to one another.

.. subclause::

   | If then :g:`$A$ <0415.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0415.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0415.g1:C>` are in continued proportion, and the
   | extremes of them :g:`$A$ <0415.g1:A>`, :g:`$C$ <0415.g1:C>` are prime to one another,

.. assertion::

   | it has been proved that it is impossible to find a fourth pro-
   | portional number to them. :just:`[IX. 17] <IX.17>`

| †Next, let :g:`$A$ <0415.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0415.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0415.g1:C>` not be in continued proportion, the
| extremes being again prime to one another ;
| I say that in this case also it is impossible to find a fourth
| proportional to them.

| For, if possible, let :g:`$D$ <0415.g1:D>` have been found, so that,
| as :g:`$A$ <0415.g1:A>` is to :g:`$B$ <0415.g1:B>`, so is :g:`$C$ <0415.g1:C>` to :g:`$D$ <0415.g1:D>`,
| and let it be contrived that, as :g:`$B$ <0415.g1:B>` is to :g:`$C$ <0415.g1:C>`, so is :g:`$D$ <0415.g1:D>` to :g:`$E$ <0415.g1:E>`.

.. subclause::

   | Now, since, as :g:`$A$ <0415.g1:A>` is to :g:`$B$ <0415.g1:B>`, so is :g:`$C$ <0415.g1:C>` to :g:`$D$ <0415.g1:D>`,
   | and, as :g:`$B$ <0415.g1:B>` is to :g:`$C$ <0415.g1:C>`, so is :g:`$D$ <0415.g1:D>` to :g:`$E$ <0415.g1:E>`,

.. assertion:: [VII. 14] <VII.14>

   therefore, *ex aequali*, as :g:`$A$ <0415.g1:A>` is to :g:`$C$ <0415.g1:C>`, so is :g:`$C$ <0415.g1:C>` to :g:`$E$ <0415.g1:E>`.

.. subclause::

   | But :g:`$A$ <0415.g1:A>`, :g:`$C$ <0415.g1:C>` are prime,
   | primes are also least, :just:`[VII. 21] <VII.21>`
   | and the least numbers measure those which have the same
   | ratio, the antecedent the antecedent and the consequent the
   | consequent. :just:`[VII. 20] <VII.20>`

.. assertion::

   Therefore :g:`$A$ <0415.g1:A>` measures :g:`$C$ <0415.g1:C>` as antecedent antecedent.
