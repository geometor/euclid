## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Text Structure**: Contains three propositions across transitions. Proposition 22 concludes its subtraction-of-units proof; Proposition 23 is presented in full with a textual footnote on Greek idiom; Proposition 24 begins at the bottom of the folio.
- **Mathematical Significance**: 
  - IX. 22: The sum of an even number of odd numbers is even, established by subtracting 1 from each odd number, summing the resulting even numbers (by IX. 21), and adding back the even multitude of units (again by IX. 21).
  - IX. 23: The sum of an odd number of odd numbers is odd, reduced by separating the last odd number into an even part and a unit, applying IX. 22 and IX. 21, and concluding by VII. Def. 7.
  - IX. 24: Difference of two even numbers is even.
- **Footnote / Editorial Note**: Footnote `[^414_1]` (labeled "3." in the printed text, referencing line 3 of the proposition) notes Euclid's Greek phrasing: *ἔστωσαν γὰρ ὁποσοιοῦν ἀριθμοὶ περισσοί, ὧν τὸ πλῆθος ἔστω περισσόν*.
