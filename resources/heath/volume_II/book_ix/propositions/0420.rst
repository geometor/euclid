PROPOSITION 23.
---------------

.. enunciation::

   If as many odd numbers as we please be added together, and their multitude be odd, the whole will also be odd.

| For let as many odd numbers as we please, :g:`$AB$ <0420.g2:/ A B />`, :g:`$BC$ <0420.g2:/ B C />`, :g:`$CD$ <0420.g2:/ C D />`,
| the multitude of which is odd,\ [#f414_1]_
| be added together ;
| I say that the whole :g:`$AD$ <0420.g2:/ A D />` is
| also odd.

.. picture:: 0420.g2.svg
   :alt: Book IX, Proposition 23

Let the unit :g:`$DE$ <0420.g2:/ D E />` be subtracted from :g:`$CD$ <0420.g2:/ C D />` ;

.. assertion:: [VII. Def. 7] <VII.def.7>

   therefore the remainder :g:`$CE$ <0420.g2:/ C E />` is even.

.. subclause:: [IX. 22] <IX.22>

   But :g:`$CA$ <0420.g2:/ A C />` is also even ;

.. assertion:: [IX. 21] <IX.21>

   therefore the whole :g:`$AE$ <0420.g2:/ A E />` is also even.

.. subclause::

   And :g:`$DE$ <0420.g2:/ D E />` is an unit.

.. assertion:: [VII. Def. 7] <VII.def.7>

   Therefore :g:`$AD$ <0420.g2:/ A D />` is odd.

.. qed::

   :just:`Q. E. D.`

.. [#f414_1] 3. Literally “let there be as many numbers as we please, of which *let the multitude be odd*.” This form, natural in Greek, is awkward in English.

PROPOSITION 24.
---------------

.. enunciation::

   If from an even number an even number be subtracted, the remainder will be even.

| For from the even number :g:`$AB$ <0420.g3:/ A B />` let the even number :g:`$BC$ <0420.g3:/ B C />`
| be subtracted :
| I say that the remainder :g:`$CA$ <0420.g3:/ A C />` is even.

.. picture:: 0420.g3.svg
   :alt: Book IX, Proposition 24

   | For, since :g:`$AB$ <0420.g3:/ A B />` is even, it has a half
   | part. :just:`[VII. Def. 6] <VII.def.6>`
