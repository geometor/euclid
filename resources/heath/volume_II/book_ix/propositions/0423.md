### PROPOSITION 30.

*If an odd number measure an even number, it will also measure the half of it.*

For let the odd number <span data-ref="0423.g1:A">$A$</span> measure the even number <span data-ref="0423.g1:B">$B$</span>;  
I say that it will also measure the half of it.

![Book IX, Proposition 30](0423.g1.svg)

For, since <span data-ref="0423.g1:A">$A$</span> measures <span data-ref="0423.g1:B">$B$</span>,  
let it measure it according to <span data-ref="0423.g1:C">$C$</span>;  
I say that <span data-ref="0423.g1:C">$C$</span> is not odd.  
For, if possible, let it be so.  
Then, since <span data-ref="0423.g1:A">$A$</span> measures <span data-ref="0423.g1:B">$B$</span> according to <span data-ref="0423.g1:C">$C$</span>,  
therefore <span data-ref="0423.g1:A">$A$</span> by multiplying <span data-ref="0423.g1:C">$C$</span> has made <span data-ref="0423.g1:B">$B$</span>.  
Therefore <span data-ref="0423.g1:B">$B$</span> is made up of odd numbers the multitude of which is odd.  
Therefore <span data-ref="0423.g1:B">$B$</span> is odd: [IX. 23]  
which is absurd, for by hypothesis it is even.  
Therefore <span data-ref="0423.g1:C">$C$</span> is not odd;  
therefore <span data-ref="0423.g1:C">$C$</span> is even.  
Thus <span data-ref="0423.g1:A">$A$</span> measures <span data-ref="0423.g1:B">$B$</span> an even number of times.  
For this reason then it also measures the half of it.

<p align="right">Q. E. D.</p>

### PROPOSITION 31.

*If an odd number be prime to any number, it will also be prime to the double of it.*

For let the odd number <span data-ref="0423.g2:A">$A$</span> be prime to any number <span data-ref="0423.g2:B">$B$</span>,  
and let <span data-ref="0423.g2:C">$C$</span> be double of <span data-ref="0423.g2:B">$B$</span>;  
I say that <span data-ref="0423.g2:A">$A$</span> is prime to <span data-ref="0423.g2:C">$C$</span>.

![Book IX, Proposition 31](0423.g2.svg)

For, if they are not prime to one another, some number will measure them.
