Let a number measure them, and let it be <span data-ref="0423.g2:D">$D$</span>.

<p class="sub-clause">Now <span data-ref="0423.g2:A">$A$</span> is odd;</p>

<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0423.g2:D">$D$</span> is also odd.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">And since <span data-ref="0423.g2:D">$D$</span> which is odd measures <span data-ref="0423.g2:C">$C$</span>,<br />
and <span data-ref="0423.g2:C">$C$</span> is even,</p>

<p class="assertion">therefore [<span data-ref="0423.g2:D">$D$</span>] will measure the half of <span data-ref="0423.g2:C">$C$</span> also. <span class="justification">[IX. 30]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But <span data-ref="0423.g2:B">$B$</span> is half of <span data-ref="0423.g2:C">$C$</span>;</p>

<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0423.g2:D">$D$</span> measures <span data-ref="0423.g2:B">$B$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">But it also measures <span data-ref="0423.g2:A">$A$</span>;</p>

<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0423.g2:D">$D$</span> measures <span data-ref="0423.g2:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0423.g2:B">$B$</span> which are prime to one another:<br />
which is impossible.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore <span data-ref="0423.g2:A">$A$</span> cannot but be prime to <span data-ref="0423.g2:C">$C$</span>.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore <span data-ref="0423.g2:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0423.g2:C">$C$</span> are prime to one another.</p>

<p class="qed"><span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

### PROPOSITION 32.

<p class="enunciation">Each of the numbers which are continually doubled beginning<br />
from a dyad is even-times even only.</p>

For let as many numbers as we please, <span data-ref="0424.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0424.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0424.g1:D">$D$</span>, have been<br />
continually doubled beginning<br />
from the dyad <span data-ref="0424.g1:A">$A$</span>;<br />
I say that <span data-ref="0424.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0424.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0424.g1:D">$D$</span> are even-<br />
times even only.

![Book IX, Proposition 32](0424.g1.svg)

<p class="assertion">Now that each of the<br />
numbers <span data-ref="0424.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0424.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0424.g1:D">$D$</span> is even-times even is manifest; for it is<br />
doubled from a dyad.</p>

I say that it is also even-times even only.

For let an unit be set out.

<p class="sub-clause">Since then as many numbers as we please beginning from<br />
an unit are in continued proportion,<br />
and the number <span data-ref="0424.g1:A">$A$</span> after the unit is prime,</p>

<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0424.g1:D">$D$</span>, the greatest of the numbers <span data-ref="0424.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0424.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0424.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0424.g1:D">$D$</span>, will not<br />
be measured by any other number except <span data-ref="0424.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0424.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0424.g1:C">$C$</span>. <span class="justification">[IX. 13]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">And each of the numbers <span data-ref="0424.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0424.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0424.g1:C">$C$</span> is even;</p>

<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0424.g1:D">$D$</span> is even-times even only. <span class="justification">[VII. Def. 8]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">Similarly we can prove that each of the numbers <span data-ref="0424.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0424.g1:C">$C$</span> is<br />
even-times even only.</p>

<p class="qed"><span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>
