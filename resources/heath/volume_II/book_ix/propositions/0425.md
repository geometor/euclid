See the notes on VII. Deff. 8 to 11 for a discussion of the difficulties<br />
shown by Iamblichus to be involved by the Euclidean definitions of “even-<br />
times even,” “even-times odd” and “odd-times even.”

---

### PROPOSITION 33.

<p class="enunciation">If a number have its half odd, it is even-times odd only.</p>

For let the number <span data-ref="0425.g1:A">$A$</span> have its half odd;<br />
I say that <span data-ref="0425.g1:A">$A$</span> is even-times odd only.

![Proposition 33 Diagram](0425.g1.svg)

<p class="assertion">Now that it is even-times odd is<br />
manifest; for the half of it, being odd,<br />
measures it an even number of times. <span class="justification">[VII. Def. 9]</span></p>

I say next that it is also even-times odd only.

<p class="sub-clause">For, if <span data-ref="0425.g1:A">$A$</span> is even-times even also,</p>

<p class="assertion">it will be measured by an even number according to an even<br />
number; <span class="justification">[VII. Def. 8]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">so that the half of it will also be measured by an even number<br />
though it is odd:<br />
which is absurd.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore <span data-ref="0425.g1:A">$A$</span> is even-times odd only.</p>

<p class="qed"><span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

---

### PROPOSITION 34.

<p class="enunciation">If a number neither be one of those which are continually<br />
doubled from a dyad, nor have its half odd, it is both even-<br />
times even and even-times odd.</p>

For let the number <span data-ref="0425.g2:A">$A$</span> neither be one of those doubled<br />
from a dyad, nor have its half odd;<br />
I say that <span data-ref="0425.g2:A">$A$</span> is both even-times even<br />
and even-times odd.

![Proposition 34 Diagram](0425.g2.svg)

<p class="assertion">Now that <span data-ref="0425.g2:A">$A$</span> is even-times even is manifest;<br />
for it has not its half odd. <span class="justification">[VII. Def. 8]</span></p>

I say next that it is also even-times odd.

<p class="sub-clause">For, if we bisect <span data-ref="0425.g2:A">$A$</span>, then bisect its half, and do this con-<br />
tinually, we shall come upon some odd number which will<br />
measure <span data-ref="0425.g2:A">$A$</span> according to an even number.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">For, if not, we shall come upon a dyad,<br />
and <span data-ref="0425.g2:A">$A$</span> will be among those which are doubled from a dyad:<br />
which is contrary to the hypothesis.</p>
