This proposition is perhaps the most interesting in the arithmetical Books,<br />
since it gives a method, and a very elegant one, of summing any series of<br />
*terms in geometrical progression*.

Let $a_1, a_2, a_3, \dots a_n, a_{n+1}$ be a series of terms in geometrical progression.<br />
Then Euclid's proposition proves that
$$(a_{n+1} - a_1) : (a_1 + a_2 + \dots + a_n) = (a_2 - a_1) : a_1.$$

For clearness' sake we will on this occasion use the fractional notation of<br />
algebra to represent proportions.

Euclid's method then comes to this.

Since
$$\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n} = \frac{a_n}{a_{n-1}} = \dots = \frac{a_2}{a_1},$$
we have, *separando*,
$$\frac{a_{n+1} - a_n}{a_n} = \frac{a_n - a_{n-1}}{a_{n-1}} = \dots = \frac{a_3 - a_2}{a_2} = \frac{a_2 - a_1}{a_1},$$
whence, since, as one of the antecedents is to one of the consequents, so is<br />
the sum of all the antecedents to the sum of all the consequents, <span class="justification">[VII. 12]</span>
$$\frac{a_{n+1} - a_1}{a_n + a_{n-1} + \dots + a_1} = \frac{a_2 - a_1}{a_1},$$
which gives $a_1 + a_2 + \dots + a_n$, or $S_n$.

If, to compare the result with that arrived at in algebraical text-books, we<br />
write the series in the form
$$a,\ ar,\ ar^2, \dots ar^{n-1} \quad (n\text{ terms}),$$
we have
$$\frac{ar^n - a}{S_n} = \frac{ar - a}{a},$$
or
$$S_n = \frac{a(r^n - 1)}{r - 1}.$$

### PROPOSITION 36.

<p class="enunciation">If as many numbers as we please beginning from an unit<br />
be set out continuously in double proportion, until the sum of all<br />
becomes prime, and if the sum multiplied into the last make<br />
some number, the product will be perfect.</p>

For let as many numbers as we please, <span data-ref="0428.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0428.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0428.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0428.g1:D">$D$</span>,<br />
beginning from an unit be set out in double proportion, until<br />
the sum of all becomes prime,<br />
let <span data-ref="0428.g1:E">$E$</span> be equal to the sum, and let <span data-ref="0428.g1:E">$E$</span> by multiplying <span data-ref="0428.g1:D">$D$</span><br />
make <span data-ref="0428.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span>;<br />
I say that <span data-ref="0428.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span> is perfect.

For, however many <span data-ref="0428.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0428.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0428.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0428.g1:D">$D$</span> are in multitude, let so<br />
many <span data-ref="0428.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0428.g1:/ H K /">$HK$</span>, <span data-ref="0428.g1:L">$L$</span>, <span data-ref="0428.g1:M">$M$</span> be taken in double proportion beginning<br />
from <span data-ref="0428.g1:E">$E$</span>;

<p class="assertion">therefore, *ex aequali*, as <span data-ref="0428.g1:A">$A$</span> is to <span data-ref="0428.g1:D">$D$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0428.g1:E">$E$</span> to <span data-ref="0428.g1:M">$M$</span>. <span class="justification">[VII. 14]</span></p>
