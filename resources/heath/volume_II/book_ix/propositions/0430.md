If the sum of any number of terms of the series
$$1, 2, 2^2, \dots 2^{n-1}$$
be prime, and the said sum be multiplied by the last term, the product will be<br />
a “perfect” number, i.e. equal to the sum of all its factors.

Let $1 + 2 + 2^2 + \dots + 2^{n-1} (= S_n)$ be prime ;<br />
then shall $S_n \cdot 2^{n-1}$ be “perfect.”

Take $(n - 1)$ terms of the series
$$S_n, 2S_n, 2^2 S_n, \dots 2^{n-2} S_n.$$
These are then terms proportional to the terms
$$2, 2^2, 2^3, \dots 2^{n-1}.$$
Therefore, *ex aequali*,
$$2 : 2^{n-1} = S_n : 2^{n-2} S_n, \tag{VII. 14}$$
or
$$2 \cdot 2^{n-2} S_n = 2^{n-1} \cdot S_n. \tag{VII. 19}$$
(This is of course obvious algebraically, but Euclid’s notation requires him to<br />
prove it.)

Now, by IX. 35, we can sum the series $S_n + 2S_n + \dots + 2^{n-2} S_n$, and
$$(2S_n - S_n) : S_n = (2^{n-1} S_n - S_n) : (S_n + 2S_n + \dots + 2^{n-2} S_n).$$
Therefore
$$S_n + 2S_n + 2^2 S_n + \dots + 2^{n-2} S_n = 2^{n-1} S_n - S_n,$$
or
$$\begin{aligned}
2^{n-1} S_n &= S_n + 2S_n + 2^2 S_n + \dots + 2^{n-2} S_n + S_n \\
&= S_n + 2S_n + \dots + 2^{n-2} S_n + (1 + 2 + 2^2 + \dots + 2^{n-1}),
\end{aligned}$$
and $2^{n-1} S_n$ is measured by every term of the right hand expression.

It is now necessary to prove that $2^{n-1} S_n$ cannot have any factor except<br />
those terms.

Suppose, if possible, that it has a factor $x$ different from all of them,<br />
and let
$$2^{n-1} S_n = x \cdot m.$$
Therefore
$$S_n : m = x : 2^{n-1}. \tag{VII. 19}$$

Now $2^{n-1}$ can only be measured by the preceding terms of the series
$$1, 2, 2^2, \dots 2^{n-1}, \tag{IX. 13}$$
and $x$ is different from all of these ;<br />
therefore $x$ does not measure $2^{n-1}$,<br />
so that $S_n$ does not measure $m$. <span class="justification">[VII. Def. 20]</span>

And $S_n$ is prime ; therefore it is prime to $m$. <span class="justification">[VII. 29]</span>

It follows <span class="justification">[VII. 20, 21]</span> that
$$m\text{ measures }2^{n-1}.$$
