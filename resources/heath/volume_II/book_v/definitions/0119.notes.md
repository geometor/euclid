# Transcription Notes: Folio 0119 (Page 113)

## Summary & Folio Continuity
- **Folio**: `0119` (Page 113)
- **Section**: Book V, Introductory Note (concluded) & Definitions 1–2
- **Content**: Concludes the Introductory Note to Book V (explaining why Books I–IV avoid proportion by using area-application in I. 44, and why Euclid treats proportion twice, in Book V for general magnitudes and in Book VII for numbers) and presents the enunciations of Book V Definitions 1 (*part*) and 2 (*multiple*).
- **Continuity**: Continues from Folio `0118` (Page 112) and flows onto Folio `0120` (Page 114) beginning with Definition 3.

## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Aristotelian & Ancient Citations**:
  - Aristotle, *Anal. post.* I. 7, 75 b 4 (on the non-transferability of arithmetical proofs to magnitudes that are not numbers).
  - Aristotle, *Anal. post.* I. 5, 74 a 17 (on the historical transition from separate proofs of alternation for numbers, lines, solids, and times to a single general demonstration).
  - Euclid X. 5 ("Commensurable magnitudes have to one another the ratio which a number has to a number").
  - Pappus, *Collection* VII. p. 678 (on Euclid's fairness and deference to his predecessors' work).
- **Definitions**:
  - **Def. 1**: Defines **part** (μέρος) as an aliquot part / submultiple ("when it measures the greater").
  - **Def. 2**: Defines **multiple** (πολλαπλάσιον) as the reciprocal relation ("when it is measured by the less").
