DEFINITION 3.
-------------

**Λόγος ἐστὶ δύο μεγεθῶν ὁμογενῶν ἡ κατὰ πηλικότητα ποιὰ σχέσις.**

The best explanation of the definitions of *ratio* and *proportion* that I have seen is that of :person:`De Morgan <de-morgan>`, which will be found in the articles under those titles in the :cite:`*Penny Cyclopaedia* <de-morgan:penny-cyclopaedia>`, Vol. XIX. (1841); and in the following notes I shall draw largely from these articles. Very valuable also are the notes on the definitions of Book V. given by :person:`Hankel <hankel>` (fragment on Euclid published as an appendix to his work :cite:`*Zur Geschichte der Mathematik in Alterthum und Mittelalter* <hankel:geschichte>`, 1874).

There has been controversy as to what is the proper translation of the word :term:`πηλικότης <pelikotes>` in the definition. :term:`σχέσις <schesis>` κατὰ :term:`πηλικότητα <pelikotes>` has generally been translated "relation in respect of *quantity*." Upon this :person:`De Morgan <de-morgan>` remarks that it makes nonsense of the definition; "for magnitude has hardly a different meaning from quantity, and a relation of magnitudes with respect to quantity may give a clear idea to those who want a word to convey a notion of architecture with respect to building or of battles with respect to fighting, and to no others." The true interpretation :person:`De Morgan <de-morgan>`, following :person:`Wallis <wallis>` and Gregory, takes to be *quantuplicity*, referring to the number of times one magnitude is contained in the other. For, he says, we cannot describe magnitude in language without quantuplicitative reference to other magnitude; hence he supposes that the definition simply conveys the fact that the mode of expressing quantity in terms of quantity is entirely based upon the notion of quantuplicity or that relation of which we take cognizance when we find how many times one is contained in the other. While all the rest of :person:`De Morgan <de-morgan>`'s observations on the definition are admirable, it seems to me that on this question of the proper translation of :term:`πηλικότης <pelikotes>` he is in error. He supports his view mainly by reference (1) to the definition of a compounded ratio usually given as the 5th definition of Book VI., which speaks of the πηλικότητες of two ratios being multiplied together, and (2) to the comments of :person:`Eutocius <eutocius>` and a scholiast on this definition. :person:`Eutocius <eutocius>` says namely (:person:`Archimedes <archimedes>`, ed. :person:`Heiberg <heiberg>`, III. p. 120) that "the term :term:`πηλικότης <pelikotes>` is evidently used of the number from which the given ratio is called, as (among others) :person:`Nicomachus <nicomachus>` says in his first book on music and :person:`Heron <heron>` in his commentary on the Introduction to Arithmetic." But it now appears certain that this definition is an interpolation; it is never used, it is not found in :person:`Campanus <campanus>`, and :person:`Peyrard <peyrard>`'s MS. only has it in the margin. At the same time it is clear that, if the definition is admitted at all, any commentator would be obliged to explain it in the way that :person:`Eutocius <eutocius>` does, whether the explanation was consistent with the proper meaning of :term:`πηλικότης <pelikotes>` or not. Hence we must look elsewhere for the meaning of πηλίκος and :term:`πηλικότης <pelikotes>`. If we do this, I think we shall find no case in which the words have the sense attributed to them by :person:`De Morgan <de-morgan>`. The real meaning of πηλίκος is *how great*. It is so used by :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>`, e.g. in :cite:`*Eth. Nic.* V. 10, 1134 b 11 <aristotle:eth-nic:v-10-1134-b-11>`, where he speaks of a man's child being as it were a part of him so long as he is of a certain age (ἕως ἂν ᾖ πηλίκον). Hoche) distinguishes πηλίκος as referring to *magnitude*, while ποσός refers to *multitude*.
