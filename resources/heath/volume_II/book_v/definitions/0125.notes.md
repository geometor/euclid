## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Greek Approximations to Incommensurables**:
  - Plato (*Republic* 546 C) and the Pythagoreans: $\frac{7}{5} = 1.4$ as a rational approximation to $\sqrt{2}$.
  - Theon of Smyrna's system of *side-* and *diagonal-numbers* (cross-referenced to Heath's note on Book II, Props. 9 and 10).
  - Archimedes (*Measurement of a Circle*): rational bounds $\frac{1351}{780} > \sqrt{3} > \frac{265}{153}$ and $3\frac{1}{7} > \pi > 3\frac{10}{71}$.
  - Sexagesimal approximations in Ptolemy's *Syntaxis* (σύνταξις) and Theon of Alexandria's commentary (extracting $\sqrt{4500}$): Ptolemy's value for $\sqrt{3}$ is $1^\circ\, 43'\, 55''\, 23''' = 1 + \frac{43}{60} + \frac{55}{60^2} + \frac{23}{60^3} = \frac{103}{60} + \frac{55}{60^2} + \frac{23}{60^3} \approx 1.7320509$.
- **Interpolated Definitions of Proportion**:
  - Discusses the post-Theonic interpolation between Def. 3 and Def. 4 found in two Theonic MSS. and Campanus (and in the margin of MS. P): ἀναλογία δὲ ἡ τῶν λόγων ταὐτότης ("Proportion is the sameness of ratios"), comparing Aristotle, *Eth. Nic.* V. 6, 1131a31, and the similar interpolation after Def. 7 using ὁμοιότης ("similarity").
