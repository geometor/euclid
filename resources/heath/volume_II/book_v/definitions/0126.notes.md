## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Greek Text of Definition 4**:
  - Λόγον ἔχειν πρὸς ἄλληλα μεγέθη λέγεται, ἃ δύναται πολλαπλασιαζόμενα ἀλλήλων ὑπερέχειν.
  - Heath emphasizes that Definition 4 not only requires magnitudes in a ratio to be homogeneous (of the same species, excluding infinitesimals and infinite magnitudes) but specifically extends Def. 3 to include *incommensurable* finite magnitudes.
- **Greek Text of Definition 5**:
  - Note on printed Greek text: in the fourth line of the Greek block of Definition 5, Heath's typesetter prints `ἢ ἅμα ὑπερέχῃ ἢ ἅμα ἴσα ἢ ἅμα ἐλλείπῃ` (omitting the subjunctive copula `ᾖ` after `ἴσα` which appears in Heiberg's critical edition). This has been transcribed verbatim as printed on the folio.
  - Heath explains his translation choices for `καθ᾽ ὁποιονοῦν πολλαπλασιασμὸν` ("according to any multiplication whatever") and `ἅμα` ("alike" rather than "simultaneously").
- **Aristotle's *Topics* VIII. 3, 158b29 & Anthyphairesis**:
  - Discusses Aristotle's reference to `ἀνταναίρεσις` and Alexander of Aphrodisias's gloss `ἀνθυφαίρεσις` (the process of reciprocal subtraction, cf. Book X, Prop. 2) in relation to Heiberg's view (*Mathematisches zu Aristoteles*, p. 22).
