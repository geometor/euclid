Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
--------------------------------------------

- **Etymology of ἀνθυφαίρεσις and ἀνταναίρεσις**:

  - Analyzes ``ὑφαίρεσις`` / ``ἀναίρεσις`` ("removal", "taking away", "destruction") with the prefix ``ἀντὶ`` ("answering to", "corresponding with", "alternating with"), citing Waitz's commentary on Aristotle's *Organon* (*Topics* VIII. 3).
  -Note that later scholarship (notably Oskar Becker, 1933) reconstructed a rigorous pre-Eudoxian theory of proportion based on *anthyphairesis* (continued-fraction / Euclidean algorithm equivalence), whereas Heath here argues from Waitz's reading that it referred to taking away corresponding aliquot parts (fractions) and that V. Def. 5 itself must belong to Eudoxus.

- **Renaissance and Early Modern Reception of V. Def. 5**:

  - Surveys criticisms from Campanus, Petrus Ramus (1515–72), Galileo, André Tacquet, and Thomas Simpson (*Elements of Geometry*, London, 1800), referencing Camerer's Excursus (*Euclidis Elementa*, Vol. II, 1825).
  - Summarizes Isaac Barrow's defense of V. Def. 5 in *Lectiones Cantabrigienses* (Lecture VII of 1666), including the Latin quotation *"quamvis non temere nec imprudenter at certis de causis iustis illis et idoneis"* and the contrast between V. Def. 5 (general magnitudes, including incommensurables) and VII. Def. 20 (commensurable numbers).
