Transcription Notes: Folio 0128 (Page 122)
==========================================

Summary & Folio Continuity
--------------------------

- **Folio 0128 (Page 122)** continues Heath's commentary on **Book V, Definition 5**, introducing Augustus De Morgan's celebrated "colonnade and railings" illustration of Eudoxian equimultiple distribution.
- **Preceding Folio Continuity** (``0127.rst``, **p. 121**): Continues mid-sentence from ``"To come now to the merits of the case, the best defence and explanation"`` at the bottom of page 121 with ``"of the definition that I have seen is that given by De Morgan."``
- **Following Folio Continuity** (``0129.rst``, **p. 123**): Ends mid-sentence at the bottom of page 122 (``"...the second railing is pushed forward by $\frac{2}{1000}C$, the third by $\frac{3}{1000}C$, and so on, so that"``), continuing on page 123 with ``"the railings after the thousandth are pushed forward by more than $C$..."``.

Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
--------------------------------------------

- **De Morgan's Colonnade and Railing Analogy**:

  - Quoted/adapted from Augustus De Morgan's *Connexion of Number and Magnitude: An Attempt to Explain the Fifth Book of Euclid* (1836) / *Penny Cyclopaedia* ("Proportion").
  - Columns at multiples $mC$ ($1, 2, \dots, 9$ in the diagram) and railings at multiples $nR$ ($1, 2, \dots, 18$ in the diagram) measured from a common bounding wall visually represent the relative ordering of all equimultiples $mC$ and $nR$.

- **Typographical Note**:

  - In the sixth line from the bottom of the page (``"when the position of *any* given railing can be found"``), the right leg of the letter ``n`` in ``given`` is slightly worn in the impression, resembling ``giver``; it is transcribed as ``given``.
