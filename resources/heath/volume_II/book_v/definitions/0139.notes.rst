Transcription Notes: Folio 0139 (Page 133)
==========================================

Summary & Folio Continuity
--------------------------

- **Preceding Folio** (``0138.rst``): Ends mid-sentence introducing De Morgan's remarks on compound ratio (``...which not only give a very clear view of what is meant by it but at the same time``).
- **Current Folio** (``0139.rst``): Completes the commentary on **Definitions 9, 10**, quoting De Morgan's operational view of ratio, parenthetically noting the fourth-proportional existence assumption (proved for lines in VI. 12 and assumed in V. 18), discussing Archimedes' term **συνημμένος**, Hippocrates of Chios's reduction of the duplication of the cube to two mean proportionals, the grammatical distinction between **διπλασίων** (duplicate) and **διπλάσιος** (double), the comparative syntax with **ἤπερ**, and the requirement that the four magnitudes in Def. 10 be in *continued* proportion (**κατὰ τὸ συνεχές**).
- **Succeeding Folio** (``0140.rst``): Begins **Definitions 11–14**.

Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
--------------------------------------------

- **Existence of a Fourth Proportional**: Heath highlights a crucial foundational point in Book V: the existence of a magnitude $A$ such that $A:B = P:Q$ for arbitrary magnitudes $P, Q, B$ is not proved in Book V (only constructed for straight lines in VI. 12), which renders the Greek proof of V. 18 open to objection.
- **Hippocrates & Two Mean Proportionals**: If $a:x = x:y = y:b$, then $a:b$ is the triplicate ratio of $a:x$, so $a^3:x^3 = a:b$.
- **Lexical Distinctions**:

  - **συνημμένος** (from **συνάπτω**): used by Archimedes and later writers for *compounded* ratio.
  - **διπλασίων / τριπλασίων** (*duplicate / triplicate* ratio) vs. **διπλάσιος / τριπλάσιος λόγος** (*double / triple* ratio, 2:1, 3:1).
  - **διπλασίονα (τριπλασίονα) λόγον ἔχειν ἤπερ πρὸς τὸ δεύτερον**: comparative construction with **ἤπερ** (analogous to **μείζονα** or **ἐλάσσονα**), or with the genitive of comparison **τοῦ, ὃν ἔχει...**, explained via Hultsch's Latin rendering *duplo maior*.
  - **κατὰ τὸ συνεχές**: *in continued proportion* (unstated but required in Def. 10).
