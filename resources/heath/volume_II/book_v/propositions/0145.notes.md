## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **De Morgan's Characterization of Book V. 1–6**: Heath quotes Augustus De Morgan's *Connexion of Number and Magnitude* on V. 1–6 as "simple propositions of concrete arithmetic, covered in language which makes them unintelligible to modern ears."
- **Heath's Algebraic Convention for Book V**: Heath establishes his notation for the Book V commentary: lowercase letters $a, b, c, \dots$ for magnitudes (to avoid confusion with Euclid's capital letters for line segments) and lowercase $m, n, p, \dots$ for positive integers (multipliers).
- **Proposition 2 (Addition of Multiples)**: Begins the proof that if $AB = mC$, $DE = mF$, $BG = nC$, and $EH = nF$, then $AG = AB + BG$ is the same multiple of $C$ that $DH = DE + EH$ is of $F$.
