To find the corresponding formula for the result of this proposition, we may suppose $a$ to be the "second" magnitude and $b$ the "fourth." If now the "first" magnitude is $ma$, the "third" is, by hypothesis, $mb$; and, if the "fifth" magnitude is $na$, the "sixth" is $nb$. The proposition then asserts that $ma + na$ is the same multiple of $a$ that $mb + nb$ is of $b$.

More generally, if $pa$, $qa \dots$ and $pb$, $qb \dots$ be any further equimultiples of $a$, $b$ respectively, $ma + na + pa + qa + \dots$ is the same multiple of $a$ that $mb + nb + pb + qb + \dots$ is of $b$. This extension is stated in Simson's corollary to V. 2 thus:

"From this it is plain that, if any number of magnitudes <span data-ref="0145.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0145.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span>, $GH$ be multiples of another <span data-ref="0145.g1:C">$C$</span>; and as many <span data-ref="0145.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>, $EK$, $KL$ be the same multiples of <span data-ref="0145.g1:F">$F$</span>, each of each; the whole of the first, viz. $AH$, is the same multiple of <span data-ref="0145.g1:C">$C$</span> that the whole of the last, viz. $DL$, is of <span data-ref="0145.g1:F">$F$</span>."

The course of the proof, which separates $m$ into its units and also $n$ into its units, practically tells us that the multiple of $a$ arrived at by adding the *two* multiples is the $(m + n)$th multiple; or practically we are shown that

$$ma + na = (m + n)a,$$

or, more generally, that

$$ma + na + pa + \dots = (m + n + p + \dots)a.$$

### PROPOSITION 3.

<p class="enunciation">If a first magnitude be the same multiple of a second<br />
that a third is of a fourth, and if equimultiples be taken<br />
of the first and third, then also *ex aequali* the magnitudes<br />
taken will be equimultiples respectively, the one of the second<br />
and the other of the fourth.</p>

Let a first magnitude <span data-ref="0147.g1:A">$A$</span> be the same multiple of a second <span data-ref="0147.g1:B">$B$</span> that a third <span data-ref="0147.g1:C">$C$</span> is of a fourth <span data-ref="0147.g1:D">$D$</span>, and let equimultiples <span data-ref="0147.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, <span data-ref="0147.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span> be taken of <span data-ref="0147.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0147.g1:C">$C$</span>;

I say that <span data-ref="0147.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> is the same multiple of <span data-ref="0147.g1:B">$B$</span> that <span data-ref="0147.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span> is of <span data-ref="0147.g1:D">$D$</span>.

For, since <span data-ref="0147.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> is the same multiple of <span data-ref="0147.g1:A">$A$</span> that <span data-ref="0147.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span> is of <span data-ref="0147.g1:C">$C$</span>, therefore, as many magnitudes as there are in <span data-ref="0147.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> equal to <span data-ref="0147.g1:A">$A$</span>, so many also are there in <span data-ref="0147.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span> equal to <span data-ref="0147.g1:C">$C$</span>.
