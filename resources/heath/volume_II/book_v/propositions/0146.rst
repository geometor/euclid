.. assertion::

   therefore, as many as there are in the whole :g:`$AG$ <0145.g1:/ A G />` equal to :g:`$C$ <0145.g1:C>`, so many also are there in the whole :g:`$DH$ <0145.g1:/ D H />` equal to :g:`$F$ <0145.g1:F>`.

.. assertion::

   Therefore, whatever multiple :g:`$AG$ <0145.g1:/ A G />` is of :g:`$C$ <0145.g1:C>`, that multiple also is :g:`$DH$ <0145.g1:/ D H />` of :g:`$F$ <0145.g1:F>`.

.. assertion::

   Therefore the sum of the first and fifth, :g:`$AG$ <0145.g1:/ A G />`, is the same multiple of the second, :g:`$C$ <0145.g1:C>`, that the sum of the third and sixth, :g:`$DH$ <0145.g1:/ D H />`, is of the fourth, :g:`$F$ <0145.g1:F>`.

.. qed::

   Therefore etc. :just:`Q. E. D.`

----

To find the corresponding formula for the result of this proposition, we may suppose $a$ to be the "second" magnitude and $b$ the "fourth." If now the "first" magnitude is $ma$, the "third" is, by hypothesis, $mb$; and, if the "fifth" magnitude is $na$, the "sixth" is $nb$. The proposition then asserts that $ma + na$ is the same multiple of $a$ that $mb + nb$ is of $b$.

More generally, if $pa$, $qa \dots$ and $pb$, $qb \dots$ be any further equimultiples of $a$, $b$ respectively, $ma + na + pa + qa + \dots$ is the same multiple of $a$ that $mb + nb + pb + qb + \dots$ is of $b$. This extension is stated in :person:`Simson <simson>`'s corollary to V. 2 thus:

"From this it is plain that, if any number of magnitudes :g:`$AB$ <0145.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$BG$ <0145.g1:/ B G />`, $GH$ be multiples of another :g:`$C$ <0145.g1:C>`; and as many :g:`$DE$ <0145.g1:/ D E />`, $EK$, $KL$ be the same multiples of :g:`$F$ <0145.g1:F>`, each of each; the whole of the first, viz. $AH$, is the same multiple of :g:`$C$ <0145.g1:C>` that the whole of the last, viz. $DL$, is of :g:`$F$ <0145.g1:F>`."

The course of the proof, which separates $m$ into its units and also $n$ into its units, practically tells us that the multiple of $a$ arrived at by adding the *two* multiples is the $(m + n)$th multiple; or practically we are shown that

$$ma + na = (m + n)a,$$

or, more generally, that

$$ma + na + pa + \dots = (m + n + p + \dots)a.$$

PROPOSITION 3.
--------------

.. enunciation::

   If a first magnitude be the same multiple of a second that a third is of a fourth, and if equimultiples be taken of the first and third, then also :term:`*ex aequali* <ex-aequali>` the magnitudes taken will be equimultiples respectively, the one of the second and the other of the fourth.

Let a first magnitude :g:`$A$ <0147.g1:A>` be the same multiple of a second :g:`$B$ <0147.g1:B>` that a third :g:`$C$ <0147.g1:C>` is of a fourth :g:`$D$ <0147.g1:D>`, and let equimultiples :g:`$EF$ <0147.g1:/ E F />`, :g:`$GH$ <0147.g1:/ G H />` be taken of :g:`$A$ <0147.g1:A>`, :g:`$C$ <0147.g1:C>`;

I say that :g:`$EF$ <0147.g1:/ E F />` is the same multiple of :g:`$B$ <0147.g1:B>` that :g:`$GH$ <0147.g1:/ G H />` is of :g:`$D$ <0147.g1:D>`.

For, since :g:`$EF$ <0147.g1:/ E F />` is the same multiple of :g:`$A$ <0147.g1:A>` that :g:`$GH$ <0147.g1:/ G H />` is of :g:`$C$ <0147.g1:C>`,

.. assertion::

   therefore, as many magnitudes as there are in :g:`$EF$ <0147.g1:/ E F />` equal to :g:`$A$ <0147.g1:A>`, so many also are there in :g:`$GH$ <0147.g1:/ G H />` equal to :g:`$C$ <0147.g1:C>`.
