Let <span data-ref="0147.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> be divided into the magnitudes <span data-ref="0147.g1:/ E K /">$EK$</span>, <span data-ref="0147.g1:/ K F /">$KF$</span> equal  
to <span data-ref="0147.g1:A">$A$</span>, and <span data-ref="0147.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span> into the magnitudes <span data-ref="0147.g1:/ G L /">$GL$</span>, <span data-ref="0147.g1:/ L H /">$LH$</span> equal to <span data-ref="0147.g1:C">$C$</span>;  
then the multitude of the magnitudes <span data-ref="0147.g1:/ E K /">$EK$</span>, <span data-ref="0147.g1:/ K F /">$KF$</span> will be equal  
to the multitude of the magnitudes <span data-ref="0147.g1:/ G L /">$GL$</span>, <span data-ref="0147.g1:/ L H /">$LH$</span>.

And, since <span data-ref="0147.g1:A">$A$</span> is the same multiple of <span data-ref="0147.g1:B">$B$</span> that <span data-ref="0147.g1:C">$C$</span> is of <span data-ref="0147.g1:D">$D$</span>,
<p class="sub-clause">while <span data-ref="0147.g1:/ E K /">$EK$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0147.g1:A">$A$</span>, and <span data-ref="0147.g1:/ G L /">$GL$</span> to <span data-ref="0147.g1:C">$C$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0147.g1:/ E K /">$EK$</span> is the same multiple of <span data-ref="0147.g1:B">$B$</span> that <span data-ref="0147.g1:/ G L /">$GL$</span> is of <span data-ref="0147.g1:D">$D$</span>.</p>

For the same reason
<p class="assertion"><span data-ref="0147.g1:/ K F /">$KF$</span> is the same multiple of <span data-ref="0147.g1:B">$B$</span> that <span data-ref="0147.g1:/ L H /">$LH$</span> is of <span data-ref="0147.g1:D">$D$</span>.</p>

Since, then, a first magnitude <span data-ref="0147.g1:/ E K /">$EK$</span> is the same multiple  
of a second <span data-ref="0147.g1:B">$B$</span> that a third <span data-ref="0147.g1:/ G L /">$GL$</span> is of a fourth <span data-ref="0147.g1:D">$D$</span>,  
and a fifth <span data-ref="0147.g1:/ K F /">$KF$</span> is also the same multiple of the second <span data-ref="0147.g1:B">$B$</span> that  
a sixth <span data-ref="0147.g1:/ L H /">$LH$</span> is of the fourth <span data-ref="0147.g1:D">$D$</span>,
<p class="assertion">therefore the sum of the first and fifth, <span data-ref="0147.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, is also the same<br />
multiple of the second <span data-ref="0147.g1:B">$B$</span> that the sum of the third and sixth,<br />
<span data-ref="0147.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span>, is of the fourth <span data-ref="0147.g1:D">$D$</span>. <span class="justification">[V. 2]</span></p>

<p class="qed">Therefore etc.<span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

Heiberg remarks of the use of *ex aequali* in the enunciation of this proposition that, strictly speaking, it has no reference to the definition (17) of a *ratio ex aequali*. But the uses of the expression here and in the definition are, I think, sufficiently parallel, as may be seen thus. The proposition asserts that, if

$$na, nb \text{ are equimultiples of } a, b,$$

and if

$$m.na, m.nb \text{ are equimultiples of } na, nb,$$

then $m.na$ is the same multiple of $a$ that $m.nb$ is of $b$. Clearly the proposition can be extended by taking further equimultiples of the last equimultiples and so on; and we can prove that

$$p.q\dots m.na \text{ is the same multiple of } a \text{ that } p.q\dots m.nb \text{ is of } b,$$

where the series of numbers $p.q\dots m.n$ is exactly the same in both expressions;
and *ex aequali* ($\delta\iota\text{'} \text{ }\ἴ\sigma\omicron\upsilon$) expresses the fact that the equimultiples are *at the same distance* from $a$, $b$ in the series $na$, $m.na \dots$ and $nb$, $m.nb \dots$ respectively.
