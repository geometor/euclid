## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Associativity of Integer Multipliers ($m \cdot (na) = (mn)a$)**: Proposition 3 reduces a multiple of a multiple ($EF = m \cdot A$ where $A = n \cdot B$) to a sum of $m$ multiples each equal to $n \cdot B$, and then invokes Proposition 2. Notice that Euclid's diagram and proof represent $EF$ as split into two parts ($EK, KF$, i.e., $m=2$), relying implicitly on finite induction (or Simson's extension of V. 2) when $m > 2$.
- **Etymology and Meaning of $\delta\iota\text{'} \text{ }\ἴ\sigma\omicron\upsilon$ (*ex aequali*)**: Heath defends the parallel between the use of $\delta\iota\text{'} \text{ }\ἴ\sigma\omicron\upsilon$ here and in Book V Def. 17: literally "at an equal [interval/distance]," indicating that $p \cdot q \dots m \cdot na$ and $p \cdot q \dots m \cdot nb$ stand at the same number of steps from $a$ and $b$ in their respective chains of equimultiples.
