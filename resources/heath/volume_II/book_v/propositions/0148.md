Here again the proof breaks $m$ into its units, and then breaks $n$ into its units; and we are practically shown that the multiple of $a$ arrived at, viz. $m.na$, is the multiple denoted by the product of the numbers $m$, $n$, i.e. the $(mn)$th multiple, or in other words that

$$m.na = mn.a.$$

## PROPOSITION 4.

*If a first magnitude have to a second the same ratio as a third to a fourth, any equimultiples whatever of the first and third will also have the same ratio to any equimultiples whatever of the second and fourth respectively, taken in corresponding order.*

For let a first magnitude <span data-ref="0148.g1:A">$A$</span> have to a second <span data-ref="0148.g1:B">$B$</span> the same ratio as a third <span data-ref="0148.g1:C">$C$</span> to a fourth <span data-ref="0148.g1:D">$D$</span>; and let equimultiples <span data-ref="0148.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0148.g1:F">$F$</span> be taken of <span data-ref="0148.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0148.g1:C">$C$</span>, and <span data-ref="0148.g1:G">$G$</span>, <span data-ref="0148.g1:H">$H$</span> other, chance, equimultiples of <span data-ref="0148.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0148.g1:D">$D$</span>;

I say that, as <span data-ref="0148.g1:E">$E$</span> is to <span data-ref="0148.g1:G">$G$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0148.g1:F">$F$</span> to <span data-ref="0148.g1:H">$H$</span>.

![Proposition 4 diagram](0148.g1.svg)

For let equimultiples <span data-ref="0148.g1:K">$K$</span>, <span data-ref="0148.g1:L">$L$</span> be taken of <span data-ref="0148.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0148.g1:F">$F$</span>, and other, chance, equimultiples <span data-ref="0148.g1:M">$M$</span>, <span data-ref="0148.g1:N">$N$</span> of <span data-ref="0148.g1:G">$G$</span>, <span data-ref="0148.g1:H">$H$</span>.

Since <span data-ref="0148.g1:E">$E$</span> is the same multiple of <span data-ref="0148.g1:A">$A$</span> that <span data-ref="0148.g1:F">$F$</span> is of <span data-ref="0148.g1:C">$C$</span>,
and equimultiples <span data-ref="0148.g1:K">$K$</span>, <span data-ref="0148.g1:L">$L$</span> of <span data-ref="0148.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0148.g1:F">$F$</span> have been taken,
therefore <span data-ref="0148.g1:K">$K$</span> is the same multiple of <span data-ref="0148.g1:A">$A$</span> that <span data-ref="0148.g1:L">$L$</span> is of <span data-ref="0148.g1:C">$C$</span>. [V. 3]

For the same reason
<span data-ref="0148.g1:M">$M$</span> is the same multiple of <span data-ref="0148.g1:B">$B$</span> that <span data-ref="0148.g1:N">$N$</span> is of <span data-ref="0148.g1:D">$D$</span>.
