## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **First Application of Eudoxus's Criterion of Proportion (V. Def. 5)**: Proposition 4 is the first proposition in Book V to deal with ratios and proportions (Propositions 1–3 dealt solely with equimultiples). Given $A : B = C : D$, to prove $mA : nB = mC : nD$ ($E : G = F : H$), Euclid takes arbitrary equimultiples $K, L = pE, pF$ and $M, N = qG, qH$, uses Proposition 3 to deduce that $K, L = pmA, pmC$ are equimultiples of $A, C$ and $M, N = qnB, qnD$ are equimultiples of $B, D$, and on the next page applies Def. 5 twice.
- **Translation of $\ddot{\alpha} \text{ } \ἔ\tau\upsilon\chi\epsilon\nu$ ("other, chance, equimultiples")**: Heath translates $\ddot{\alpha}\lambda\lambda\alpha, \ddot{\alpha} \text{ } \ἔ\tau\upsilon\chi\epsilon\nu, \ἰ\sigma\ά\kappa\iota\varsigma \text{ } \pi\omicron\lambda\lambda\alpha\pi\lambda\ά\sigma\iota\alpha$ as "other, chance, equimultiples," a rendering he defends at length against Robert Simson's criticisms on Pages 143–144.
