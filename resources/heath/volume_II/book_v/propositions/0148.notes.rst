2. Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
-----------------------------------------------

- **First Application of Eudoxus's Criterion of Proportion (V. Def. 5)**: Proposition 4 is the foundational theorem establishing that ratios are preserved under equimultiplication of antecedents and consequents: if $A : B = C : D$, then $mA : nB = mC : nD$. Propositions 1–3 dealt solely with equimultiples of magnitudes; Proposition 4 is the first proposition to manipulate proportions directly.
- **Deductive Bridge via Proposition 3**: To prove $E : G = F : H$ where $E = mA, F = mC$ and $G = nB, H = nD$, Euclid takes arbitrary multipliers $p$ and $q$ producing $K = pE, L = pF$ and $M = qG, N = qH$. Proposition 3 guarantees that $K, L$ are equimultiples of $A, C$ ($pm \cdot A, pm \cdot C$) and $M, N$ are equimultiples of $B, D$ ($qn \cdot B, qn \cdot D$).
- **Translation of $\ddot{\alpha} \text{ } \ἔ\tau\upsilon\chi\epsilon\nu$ ("other, chance, equimultiples")**: Heath translates $\ddot{\alpha}\lambda\lambda\alpha, \ddot{\alpha} \text{ } \ἔ\tau\upsilon\chi\epsilon\nu, \ἰ\sigma\ά\kappa\iota\varsigma \text{ } \pi\omicron\lambda\lambda\alpha\pi\lambda\ά\sigma\iota\alpha$ as "other, chance, equimultiples," a rendering he defends against Simson's proposed omissions and additions on pages 143–144.
