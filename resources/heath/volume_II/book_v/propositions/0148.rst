| Here again the proof breaks $m$ into its units, and then breaks $n$ into its
| units; and we are practically shown that the multiple of $a$ arrived at, viz.
| $m.na$, is the multiple denoted by the product of the numbers $m$, $n$, i.e. the
| $(mn)$th multiple, or in other words that

$$m.na = mn.a.$$

----

PROPOSITION 4.
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

.. enunciation::

   If a first magnitude have to a second the same ratio as a third to a fourth, any equimultiples whatever of the first and third will also have the same ratio to any equimultiples whatever of the second and fourth respectively, taken in corresponding order.

| For let a first magnitude :g:`$A$ <0148.g1:A>` have to a second :g:`$B$ <0148.g1:B>` the same
| ratio as a third :g:`$C$ <0148.g1:C>` to a fourth :g:`$D$ <0148.g1:D>`; and let equimultiples :g:`$E$ <0148.g1:E>`, :g:`$F$ <0148.g1:F>`
| be taken of :g:`$A$ <0148.g1:A>`, :g:`$C$ <0148.g1:C>`, and :g:`$G$ <0148.g1:G>`, :g:`$H$ <0148.g1:H>` other, chance, equimultiples of
| :g:`$B$ <0148.g1:B>`, :g:`$D$ <0148.g1:D>`;

I say that, as :g:`$E$ <0148.g1:E>` is to :g:`$G$ <0148.g1:G>`, so is :g:`$F$ <0148.g1:F>` to :g:`$H$ <0148.g1:H>`.

.. picture:: 0148.g1.svg
   :alt: Proposition 4 diagram

| For let equimultiples :g:`$K$ <0148.g1:K>`, :g:`$L$ <0148.g1:L>` be taken of :g:`$E$ <0148.g1:E>`, :g:`$F$ <0148.g1:F>`, and other,
| chance, equimultiples :g:`$M$ <0148.g1:M>`, :g:`$N$ <0148.g1:N>` of :g:`$G$ <0148.g1:G>`, :g:`$H$ <0148.g1:H>`.

| Since :g:`$E$ <0148.g1:E>` is the same multiple of :g:`$A$ <0148.g1:A>` that :g:`$F$ <0148.g1:F>` is of :g:`$C$ <0148.g1:C>`,
| and equimultiples :g:`$K$ <0148.g1:K>`, :g:`$L$ <0148.g1:L>` of :g:`$E$ <0148.g1:E>`, :g:`$F$ <0148.g1:F>` have been taken,

.. assertion:: [V. 3] <V.3>

   therefore :g:`$K$ <0148.g1:K>` is the same multiple of :g:`$A$ <0148.g1:A>` that :g:`$L$ <0148.g1:L>` is of :g:`$C$ <0148.g1:C>`.

For the same reason

.. assertion::

   :g:`$M$ <0148.g1:M>` is the same multiple of :g:`$B$ <0148.g1:B>` that :g:`$N$ <0148.g1:N>` is of :g:`$D$ <0148.g1:D>`.
