This proposition shows that, if $a$, $b$, $c$, $d$ are proportionals, then

$$ma \text{ is to } nb \text{ as } mc \text{ is to } nd;$$

and the proof is as follows:

Take $pma$, $pmc$ any equimultiples of $ma$, $mc$, and $qnb$, $qnd$ any equimultiples of $nb$, $nd$.

Since $a : b = c : d$, it follows [V. Def. 5] that,

$$\text{according as } pma \gtreqqless qnb, \ pmc \gtreqqless qnd.$$

But the $p$- and $q$-equimultiples are *any* equimultiples;  
therefore [V. Def. 5]

$$ma : nb = mc : nd.$$

It will be observed that Euclid's phrase for taking *any* equimultiples of <span data-ref="0148.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0148.g1:C">$C$</span> and *any* other equimultiples of <span data-ref="0148.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0148.g1:D">$D$</span> is "let there be taken equimultiples <span data-ref="0148.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0148.g1:F">$F$</span> of <span data-ref="0148.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0148.g1:C">$C$</span>, and <span data-ref="0148.g1:G">$G$</span>, <span data-ref="0148.g1:H">$H$</span> other, *chance*, equimultiples of <span data-ref="0148.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0148.g1:D">$D$</span>," <span data-ref="0148.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0148.g1:F">$F$</span> being called $\ἰ\sigma\ά\kappa\iota\varsigma \text{ } \pi\omicron\lambda\lambda\alpha\pi\lambda\ά\sigma\iota\alpha$ simply, and <span data-ref="0148.g1:G">$G$</span>, <span data-ref="0148.g1:H">$H$</span> $\ddot{\alpha}\lambda\lambda\alpha, \ddot{\alpha} \text{ } \ἔ\tau\upsilon\chi\epsilon\nu, \ἰ\sigma\ά\kappa\iota\varsigma \text{ } \pi\omicron\lambda\lambda\alpha\pi\lambda\ά\sigma\iota\alpha$. And similarly, when *any* equimultiples (<span data-ref="0148.g1:K">$K$</span>, <span data-ref="0148.g1:L">$L$</span>) of <span data-ref="0148.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0148.g1:F">$F$</span> come to be taken, and *any* other equimultiples (<span data-ref="0148.g1:M">$M$</span>, <span data-ref="0148.g1:N">$N$</span>) of <span data-ref="0148.g1:G">$G$</span>, <span data-ref="0148.g1:H">$H$</span>. But later on Euclid uses the same phrases about the *new* equimultiples with reference to the original magnitudes, reciting that "there have been taken, of <span data-ref="0148.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0148.g1:C">$C$</span>, equimultiples <span data-ref="0148.g1:K">$K$</span>, <span data-ref="0148.g1:L">$L$</span> and of <span data-ref="0148.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0148.g1:D">$D$</span>, other, *chance*, equimultiples <span data-ref="0148.g1:M">$M$</span>, <span data-ref="0148.g1:N">$N$</span>"; whereas <span data-ref="0148.g1:M">$M$</span>, <span data-ref="0148.g1:N">$N$</span> are not *any equimultiples whatever* of <span data-ref="0148.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0148.g1:D">$D$</span>, but are *any* equimultiples of the *particular* multiples (<span data-ref="0148.g1:G">$G$</span>, <span data-ref="0148.g1:H">$H$</span>) which have been taken of <span data-ref="0148.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0148.g1:D">$D$</span> respectively, though these *latter* have been taken at random. Simson would, in the first place, add $\ddot{\alpha} \text{ } \ἔ\tau\upsilon\chi\epsilon\nu$ in the passages where *any* equimultiples <span data-ref="0148.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0148.g1:F">$F$</span> are taken of <span data-ref="0148.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0148.g1:C">$C$</span> and *any* equimultiples <span data-ref="0148.g1:K">$K$</span>, <span data-ref="0148.g1:L">$L$</span> are taken of <span data-ref="0148.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0148.g1:F">$F$</span>, because the words are "wholly necessary" and, in the second place, would leave them out where <span data-ref="0148.g1:M">$M$</span>, <span data-ref="0148.g1:N">$N$</span> are called $\ddot{\alpha}\lambda\lambda\alpha, \ddot{\alpha} \text{ } \ἔ\tau\upsilon\chi\epsilon\nu, \ἰ\sigma\ά\kappa\iota\varsigma \text{ } \pi\omicron\lambda\lambda\alpha\pi\lambda\ά\sigma\iota\alpha$ of <span data-ref="0148.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0148.g1:D">$D$</span>, because it is not true that of <span data-ref="0148.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0148.g1:D">$D$</span> have been taken "any equimultiples whatever ($\ddot{\alpha} \text{ } \ἔ\tau\upsilon\chi\epsilon$), <span data-ref="0148.g1:M">$M$</span>, <span data-ref="0148.g1:N">$N$</span>." Simson adds: "And it is strange that neither Mr Briggs, who did right to leave out these words in one place of Prop. 13 of this book, nor Dr Gregory, who changed them into the word 'some' in three places, and left them out in a fourth of that same Prop. 13, did not also leave them out in this place of Prop. 4 and in the second of the two places where they occur in Prop. 17 of this book, in neither of which they can stand consistent with truth: And in none of all these places, even in those which they corrected in their Latin translation, have they cancelled the words $\ddot{\alpha} \text{ } \ἔ\tau\upsilon\chi\epsilon$ in the Greek text, as they ought to have done.
