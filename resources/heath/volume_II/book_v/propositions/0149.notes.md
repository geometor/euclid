## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Logical Structure of the Def. 5 Invocation**: Notice the crucial logical distinction between the two invocations of `[V. Def. 5]`:
  1. In the first invocation, $a : b = c : d$ holds *for all* multipliers of $a, c$ and $b, d$; therefore it holds in particular for the specific multipliers $pm$ and $qn$ ($K, L = pmA, pmC$ and $M, N = qnB, qnD$).
  2. In the second invocation, since $p$ and $q$ were chosen arbitrarily, the comparison $pE \gtreqqless qG \implies pF \gtreqqless qH$ holds for *any* equimultiples of $E, F$ and $G, H$, which by Def. 5 establishes $E : G = F : H$.
- **Simson, Henry Briggs, and David Gregory on $\ddot{\alpha} \text{ } \ἔ\tau\upsilon\chi\epsilon\nu$**: Simson objected to calling $M, N = qnB, qnD$ "any equimultiples whatever" ($\ddot{\alpha} \text{ } \ἔ\tau\upsilon\chi\epsilon\nu$) of $B, D$, since $M, N$ are restricted to multiples of $nB, nD$. However, as Heath explains on Page 144, for the *direct* application of Def. 5 (deducing $K \gtreqqless M \implies L \gtreqqless N$ from $A : B = C : D$), $K, L$ and $M, N$ only need to be *some* (chance) equimultiples of $A, C$ and $B, D$, whereas for the *converse* application of Def. 5 (deducing $E : G = F : H$), $K, L$ and $M, N$ must be *any* arbitrary equimultiples of $E, F$ and $G, H$.
