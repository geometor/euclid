## 2. Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Heath's Defense of the Manuscript Reading (ἃ ἔτυχε)**: Heath demonstrates that Simson's objection stems from translating ἃ ἔτυχε as "any whatever" (universal quantification) rather than "chance" / "whatever they may happen to be" (an arbitrary or random instance). Because the product of two randomly chosen multipliers $q$ and $n$ is still a chance multiplier $qn$, Euclid's stereotyped phrase ἄλλα, ἃ ἔτυχεν, ἰσάκις πολλαπλάσια is both philologically authentic and logically sound without altering the Greek text.
- **Theon's Porism and Inversion of Ratios (ἀνάπαλιν, Simson's Prop. B)**: Theon appended a porism to V. 4 asserting that proportional magnitudes are proportional inversely ($A : B = C : D \implies B : A = D : C$). As Simson noted, Theon's argument relies on the original proportion $A : B = C : D$, not the conclusion of V. 4; inversion is an immediate consequence of V. Def. 5 itself, which Simson formalized as Prop. B.
- **Simson's Corollary to V. 4 (Case $m=1$ or $n=1$)**: Because Euclidean terminology for multiples ($\pi\omicron\lambda\lambda\alpha\pi\lambda\alpha\sigma\iota\alpha$) historically connoted $m \ge 2$, Simson added explicit corollaries for $ma : b = mc : d$ and $a : nb = c : nd$.
