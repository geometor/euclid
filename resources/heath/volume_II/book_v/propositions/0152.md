**10. let EB be made that multiple of <span data-ref="0151.g1:/ C G /">$CG$</span>**, τοσαυταπλάσιον γεγονέτω καὶ τὸ ΕΒ τοῦ ΓΗ. From this way of stating the construction one might suppose that $CG$ was given and <span data-ref="0151.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span> had to be found equal to a certain multiple of it. But in fact <span data-ref="0151.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span> is what is given and <span data-ref="0151.g1:/ C G /">$CG$</span> has to be found, i.e. <span data-ref="0151.g1:/ C G /">$CG$</span> has to be constructed as a certain *submultiple* of <span data-ref="0151.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span>.

This proposition corresponds to V. 1, with subtraction taking the place of addition. It proves the formula

$$ma - mb = m(a - b).$$

Euclid's construction assumes that, if <span data-ref="0151.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> is any multiple of <span data-ref="0151.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span>, and <span data-ref="0151.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span> is any other magnitude, a fourth straight line can be found such that <span data-ref="0151.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span> is the same multiple of it that <span data-ref="0151.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> is of <span data-ref="0151.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span>, or in other words that, given any magnitude, we can divide it into any number of equal parts. This is however not proved, even of straight lines, much less other magnitudes, until VI. 9. Peletarius had already seen this objection to the construction. The difficulty is not got over by regarding it merely as a *hypothetical* construction ; for hypothetical constructions are not in Euclid's manner. The remedy is to substitute the alternative construction given by Simson, after Peletarius and Campanus' translation from the Arabic, which only requires us to add a magnitude to itself a certain number of times. The demonstration follows Euclid's line exactly.
