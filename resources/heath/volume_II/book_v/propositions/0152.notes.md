## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Submultiple Assumption in Line 10 (`[^146_1]`)**: The Greek text ($\tau\omicron\sigma\alpha\upsilon\tau\alpha\pi\lambda\\sigma\iota\omicron\nu\ \gamma\epsilon\gamma\omicron\nu\epsilontonos\tau\omega\ \kappa\alpha\grave{\iota}\ \tau\grave{\omicron}\ \text{EB}\ \tau\omicron\tilde{\upsilon}\ \Gamma\text{H}$) requires constructing $CG$ as an $m$-th part (submultiple) of the given remainder $EB$. As Jacques Peletier (Peletarius) observed, dividing an arbitrary magnitude into $m$ equal parts is an unproved assumption (even for straight lines, where it is not proved until VI. 9).
- **Peletarius, Campanus, and Simson's Remedy**: Instead of constructing a submultiple $CG$ of $EB$, Simson (following Peletarius and Campanus's Latin translation from the Arabic) constructs $AG$ as a *multiple* of the known remainder $FD$ ($AG = m \cdot FD$), which only requires finite addition of a given magnitude to itself.
