2. Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
-----------------------------------------------

- **Submultiple Assumption in Line 10**: The Greek text (τοσαυταπλάσιον γεγονέτω καὶ τὸ ΕΒ τοῦ ΓΗ) requires constructing $CG$ as an $m$-th part (submultiple) of the given remainder $EB$. As Jacques Peletier (Peletarius) observed, dividing an arbitrary magnitude into $m$ equal parts is an unproved assumption (even for straight lines, where it is not proved until VI. 9).
- **Peletarius, Campanus, and Simson's Remedy**: Instead of constructing a submultiple $CG$ of $EB$, Simson (following Peletarius and Campanus's Latin translation from the Arabic) constructs $AG$ as a *multiple* of the known remainder $FD$ ($AG = m \cdot FD$), which only requires finite addition of a given magnitude to itself.
