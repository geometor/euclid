Euclid's proof amounts to this.

Suppose a magnitude $x$ taken such that

$$ma - mb = mx, \text{ say.}$$

Add $mb$ to each side, whence (by V. 1)

$$ma = m(x + b).$$

Therefore

$$a = x + b, \text{ or } x = a - b,$$

so that

$$ma - mb = m(a - b).$$

Simson's proof, on the other hand, argues thus.

Take $x = m(a - b)$, the same multiple of $(a - b)$ that $mb$ is of $b$.

Then, by addition of $mb$ to both sides, we have <span class="justification">[V. 1]</span>

$$x + mb = ma,$$

or

$$x = ma - mb.$$

That is,

$$ma - mb = m(a - b).$$

### PROPOSITION 6.

<p class="enunciation">If two magnitudes be equimultiples of two magnitudes, and<br />
any magnitudes subtracted from them be equimultiples of the<br />
same, the remainders also are either equal to the same or equi-<br />
multiples of them.</p>

For let two magnitudes <span data-ref="0153.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0153.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> be equimultiples of two<br />
magnitudes <span data-ref="0153.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0153.g1:F">$F$</span>, and let <span data-ref="0153.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0153.g1:/ C H /">$CH$</span><br />
subtracted from them be equi-<br />
multiples of the same two <span data-ref="0153.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0153.g1:F">$F$</span>;<br />
I say that the remainders also, <span data-ref="0153.g1:/ G B /">$GB$</span>,<br />
<span data-ref="0153.g1:/ H D /">$HD$</span>, are either equal to <span data-ref="0153.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0153.g1:F">$F$</span> or<br />
equimultiples of them.

![Proposition 6 Diagram](0153.g1.svg)

For, first, let <span data-ref="0153.g1:/ G B /">$GB$</span> be equal to <span data-ref="0153.g1:E">$E$</span>;<br />
I say that <span data-ref="0153.g1:/ H D /">$HD$</span> is also equal to <span data-ref="0153.g1:F">$F$</span>.

For let <span data-ref="0153.g1:/ C K /">$CK$</span> be made equal to <span data-ref="0153.g1:F">$F$</span>.

Since <span data-ref="0153.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span> is the same multiple of <span data-ref="0153.g1:E">$E$</span> that <span data-ref="0153.g1:/ C H /">$CH$</span> is of <span data-ref="0153.g1:F">$F$</span>,<br />
<p class="sub-clause">while <span data-ref="0153.g1:/ G B /">$GB$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0153.g1:E">$E$</span> and <span data-ref="0153.g1:/ C K /">$KC$</span> to <span data-ref="0153.g1:F">$F$</span>,</p>
therefore <span data-ref="0153.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is the same multiple of <span data-ref="0153.g1:E">$E$</span> that <span data-ref="0153.g1:/ K H /">$KH$</span> is of <span data-ref="0153.g1:F">$F$</span>.<br />
<span class="justification">[V. 2]</span>

But, by hypothesis, <span data-ref="0153.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is the same multiple of <span data-ref="0153.g1:E">$E$</span> that<br />
<span data-ref="0153.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> is of <span data-ref="0153.g1:F">$F$</span>;
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0153.g1:/ K H /">$KH$</span> is the same multiple of <span data-ref="0153.g1:F">$F$</span> that <span data-ref="0153.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> is of <span data-ref="0153.g1:F">$F$</span>.</p>

Since then each of the magnitudes <span data-ref="0153.g1:/ K H /">$KH$</span>, <span data-ref="0153.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> is the same<br />
multiple of <span data-ref="0153.g1:F">$F$</span>,
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0153.g1:/ K H /">$KH$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0153.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>.</p>
