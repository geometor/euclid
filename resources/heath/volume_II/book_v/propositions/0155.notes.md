## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Idiom $\grave{\omicron}\ \epsilontonos\tau\upsilon\chi\epsilon\nu$ ("another, chance, magnitude")**: Heath notes that just as in V. 4, the Greek text refers back to the already-chosen multiple $F$ of $C$ as "another, chance, magnitude" ($\alpha\lambda\lambda\omicron\ ,\ \grave{\omicron}\ \epsilontonos\tau\upsilon\chi\epsilon\nu\ ,\ \mu\epsilontonos\gamma\epsilon\theta\omicron\varsigma$) simply to mean a third magnitude distinct from $D$ and $E$ against which their equality is compared.
- **Critique of the Porism (Inversion)**: While Heiberg and August defended the placement of the Porism after V. 7 (found in Vat. gr. 190, whereas Theon placed it after V. 4), Heath points out that the Porism ("if any magnitudes are proportional, they will also be proportional inversely", i.e. $A : B = C : D \implies B : A = D : C$) cannot logically be inferred from V. 7, which only proves $A = B \implies (A : C = B : C \land C : A = C : B)$ from the hypothesis $A = B$.
- **Aristotle Reference**: *Meteorologica* III. 5, 376a14–16 (in the geometry of the rainbow / Apollonian circle).
