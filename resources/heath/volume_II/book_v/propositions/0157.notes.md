## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Application of Definition 7 (`[V. Def. 7]`)**: Having shown $FH = m \cdot AB > N = n \cdot D$ while $K = m \cdot C \le N = n \cdot D$, Euclid concludes $AB : D > C : D$ directly by Definition 7. For the second part ($D : C > D : AB$), since $N$ is chosen as the *first* multiple of $D$ that is greater than $K$, $N > K$ strictly, while $FH > N$ implies $N$ is not in excess of $FH$, satisfying Definition 7 in reverse order.
- **Structure of Case 2**: In Case 2 ($AE > EB$), the roles of $AE$ and $EB$ are swapped: $GH = m \cdot EB$ is made greater than $D$, and $N$ is chosen as the first multiple of $D$ greater than $FG = m \cdot AE$.
