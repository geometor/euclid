But <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span> is greater than <span data-ref="0157.g1:D">$D$</span>;
therefore the whole <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ F H /">$FH$</span> is in excess of <span data-ref="0157.g1:D">$D$</span>, <span data-ref="0157.g1:M">$M$</span>, that is, of <span data-ref="0157.g1:N">$N$</span>.

Now <span data-ref="0157.g1:K">$K$</span> is not in excess of <span data-ref="0157.g1:N">$N$</span>, inasmuch as <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span> also, which is greater than <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span>, that is, than <span data-ref="0157.g1:K">$K$</span>, is not in excess of <span data-ref="0157.g1:N">$N$</span>.

---

The two separate cases found in the Greek text of the demonstration can practically be compressed into one. Also the expositor of the two cases makes them differ more than they need. It is necessary in each case to select the *smaller* of the two segments <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span>, <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span> of <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> with a view to taking a multiple of it which is greater than <span data-ref="0157.g1:D">$D$</span>; in the first case therefore <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> is taken, in the second <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span>. But, while in the first case successive multiples of <span data-ref="0157.g1:D">$D$</span> are taken in order to find the first multiple that is greater than <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span> (or <span data-ref="0157.g1:K">$K$</span>), in the second case the multiple is taken which is the first that is greater than <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span>. This difference is not necessary; the first multiple of <span data-ref="0157.g1:D">$D$</span> that is greater than <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span> would equally serve in the second case. Lastly, the use of the magnitude <span data-ref="0157.g1:K">$K$</span> might have been dispensed with in both cases; it is of no practical use and only lengthens the proofs. For these reasons Simson considers that Theon, or some other unskilful editor, has vitiated the proposition. This however seems an unsafe assumption; for, while it was not the habit of the great Greek geometers to discuss separately a number of different cases (e.g. in I. 7 and I. 35 Euclid proves one case and leaves the others to the reader), there are many exceptions to prove the rule, e.g. Eucl. III. 25 and 33; and we know that many fundamental propositions, afterwards proved generally, were first discovered in relation to particular cases and then generalised, so that Book V., presenting a comparatively new theory, might fairly be expected to exhibit more instances than the earlier books do of unnecessary subdivision. The use of the <span data-ref="0157.g1:K">$K$</span> is no more conclusive against the genuineness of the proofs.

Nevertheless Simson's version of the proof is certainly shorter, and moreover it takes account of the case in which <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> is *equal* to <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span>, and of the case in which <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span>, <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span> are both greater than <span data-ref="0157.g1:D">$D$</span> (though these cases are scarcely worth separate mention).

"If the magnitude which is not the greater of the two <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span>, <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span> be (1) not less than <span data-ref="0157.g1:D">$D$</span>, take <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span>, <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span> the doubles of <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span>, <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span>.

But if that which is not the greater of the two <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span>, <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span> be (2) less than <span data-ref="0157.g1:D">$D$</span>, this magnitude can be multiplied so as to become greater than <span data-ref="0157.g1:D">$D$</span> whether it be <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> or <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span>.

Let it be multiplied until it becomes greater than <span data-ref="0157.g1:D">$D$</span>, and let the other be multiplied as often; let <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span> be the multiple thus taken of <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> and <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span> the same multiple of <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span>,
therefore <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span> and <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span> are each of them greater than <span data-ref="0157.g1:D">$D$</span>.

And, in every one of the cases, take <span data-ref="0157.g1:L">$L$</span> the double of <span data-ref="0157.g1:D">$D$</span>, <span data-ref="0157.g1:M">$M$</span> its triple and so on, till the multiple of <span data-ref="0157.g1:D">$D$</span> be that which first becomes greater than <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span>.

Let <span data-ref="0157.g1:N">$N$</span> be that multiple of <span data-ref="0157.g1:D">$D$</span> which is first greater than <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span>, and <span data-ref="0157.g1:M">$M$</span> the multiple of <span data-ref="0157.g1:D">$D$</span> which is next less than <span data-ref="0157.g1:N">$N$</span>.
