2. Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
-----------------------------------------------

- **Compression of the Two Cases**: Heath critiques Simson's view that Theon or another editor corrupted the Greek proof by splitting it into two cases ($AE < EB$ and $EB < AE$) and introducing the auxiliary magnitude $K$. Heath notes that Book V represents a comparatively new theory (Eudoxus's theory of proportion) where separate case analysis (as in III. 25 and III. 33) is historically plausible.
- **Simson's Unified Proof**: Simson combines both cases by taking whichever of $AE, EB$ is "not the greater" and multiplying it until it exceeds $D$, while always choosing $N$ as the first multiple of $D$ greater than $GH$ and $M$ as the preceding multiple of $D$.
