Then, because <span data-ref="0157.g1:N">$N$</span> is the multiple of <span data-ref="0157.g1:D">$D$</span> which is the first that becomes greater than <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span>,
the next preceding multiple is not greater than <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span>;
that is, <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span> is not less than <span data-ref="0157.g1:M">$M$</span>.

And, since <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span> is the same multiple of <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> that <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span> is of <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span>,
<span data-ref="0157.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span> is the same multiple of <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span> that <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ F H /">$FH$</span> is of <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>; [V. 1]
wherefore <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ F H /">$FH$</span>, <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span> are equimultiples of <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span>.

And it was shown that <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span> was not less than <span data-ref="0157.g1:M">$M$</span>;
and, by the construction, <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span> is greater than <span data-ref="0157.g1:D">$D$</span>;
therefore the whole <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ F H /">$FH$</span> is greater than <span data-ref="0157.g1:M">$M$</span>, <span data-ref="0157.g1:D">$D$</span> together.

But <span data-ref="0157.g1:M">$M$</span>, <span data-ref="0157.g1:D">$D$</span> together are equal to <span data-ref="0157.g1:N">$N$</span>;
therefore <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ F H /">$FH$</span> is greater than <span data-ref="0157.g1:N">$N$</span>.

But <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span> is not greater than <span data-ref="0157.g1:N">$N$</span>;
and <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ F H /">$FH$</span>, <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span> are equimultiples of <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ E B /">$BE$</span>,
and <span data-ref="0157.g1:N">$N$</span> is a multiple of <span data-ref="0157.g1:D">$D$</span>;
therefore <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> has to <span data-ref="0157.g1:D">$D$</span> a greater ratio than <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ E B /">$BE$</span> (or <span data-ref="0157.g1:C">$C$</span>) has to <span data-ref="0157.g1:D">$D$</span>. [V. Def. 7]

Also <span data-ref="0157.g1:D">$D$</span> has to <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ E B /">$BE$</span> a greater ratio than it has to <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>.

For, having made the same construction, it may be shown, in like manner, that <span data-ref="0157.g1:N">$N$</span> is greater than <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span> but that it is not greater than <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ F H /">$FH$</span>;
and <span data-ref="0157.g1:N">$N$</span> is a multiple of <span data-ref="0157.g1:D">$D$</span>,
and <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span>, <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ F H /">$FH$</span> are equimultiples of <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span>, <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>;
Therefore <span data-ref="0157.g1:D">$D$</span> has to <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span> a greater ratio than it has to <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>." [V. Def. 7]

The proof may perhaps be more readily grasped in the more symbolical form thus.

Take the $m$th equimultiples of <span data-ref="0157.g1:C">$C$</span>, and of the excess of <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> over <span data-ref="0157.g1:C">$C$</span> (that is, of <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span>), such that each is greater than <span data-ref="0157.g1:D">$D$</span>;
and, of the multiples of <span data-ref="0157.g1:D">$D$</span>, let $pD$ be the first that is greater than $mC$, and $nD$ the next less multiple of <span data-ref="0157.g1:D">$D$</span>.

Then, since $mC$ is not less than $nD$,
and, by the construction, $m(AE)$ is greater than <span data-ref="0157.g1:D">$D$</span>,
the sum of $mC$ and $m(AE)$ is greater than the sum of $nD$ and <span data-ref="0157.g1:D">$D$</span>.

<p class="assertion">That is, $m(AB)$ is greater than $pD$.</p>

And, by the construction, $mC$ is less than $pD$.

Therefore [V. Def. 7] <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> has to <span data-ref="0157.g1:D">$D$</span> a greater ratio than <span data-ref="0157.g1:C">$C$</span> has to <span data-ref="0157.g1:D">$D$</span>.

Again, since $pD$ is less than $m(AB)$,
and $pD$ is greater than $mC$,
<span data-ref="0157.g1:D">$D$</span> has to <span data-ref="0157.g1:C">$C$</span> a greater ratio than <span data-ref="0157.g1:D">$D$</span> has to <span data-ref="0157.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>.

### PROPOSITION 9.

<p class="enunciation">Magnitudes which have the same ratio to the same are<br />
equal to one another ; and magnitudes to which the same has<br />
the same ratio are equal.</p>
