Then, because $N$ is the multiple of $D$ which is the first that becomes greater than $GH$,
the next preceding multiple is not greater than $GH$ ;
<p class="assertion">that is, $GH$ is not less than $M$.</p>

And, since $FG$ is the same multiple of $AE$ that $GH$ is of $EB$,
$GH$ is the same multiple of $EB$ that $FH$ is of $AB$ ; <span class="justification">[V. 1]</span>
wherefore $FH, GH$ are equimultiples of $AB, EB$.

And it was shown that $GH$ was not less than $M$ ;
<p class="sub-clause">and, by the construction, $FG$ is greater than $D$ ;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the whole $FH$ is greater than $M, D$ together.</p>

But $M, D$ together are equal to $N$ ;
<p class="assertion">therefore $FH$ is greater than $N$.</p>

But $GH$ is not greater than $N$ ;
and $FH, GH$ are equimultiples of $AB, BE$,
<p class="sub-clause">and $N$ is a multiple of $D$ ;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore $AB$ has to $D$ a greater ratio than $BE$ (or $C$) has to $D$. <span class="justification">[V. Def. 7]</span></p>

Also $D$ has to $BE$ a greater ratio than it has to $AB$.

For, having made the same construction, it may be shown, in like manner,
that $N$ is greater than $GH$ but that it is not greater than $FH$ ;
and $N$ is a multiple of $D$,
and $GH, FH$ are equimultiples of $EB, AB$ ;
<p class="assertion">Therefore $D$ has to $EB$ a greater ratio than it has to $AB$." <span class="justification">[V. Def. 7]</span></p>

The proof may perhaps be more readily grasped in the more symbolical form thus.

Take the $m$th equimultiples of $C$, and of the excess of $AB$ over $C$ (that is, of $AE$), such that each is greater than $D$ ;
and, of the multiples of $D$, let $pD$ be the first that is greater than $mC$, and $nD$ the next less multiple of $D$.

Then, since $mC$ is not less than $nD$,
and, by the construction, $m(AE)$ is greater than $D$,
the sum of $mC$ and $m(AE)$ is greater than the sum of $nD$ and $D$.

<p class="assertion">That is, $m(AB)$ is greater than $pD$.</p>

And, by the construction, $mC$ is less than $pD$.

<p class="assertion">Therefore <span class="justification">[V. Def. 7]</span> $AB$ has to $D$ a greater ratio than $C$ has to $D$.</p>

Again, since $pD$ is less than $m(AB)$,
and $pD$ is greater than $mC$,
<p class="assertion">$D$ has to $C$ a greater ratio than $D$ has to $AB$.</p>

### PROPOSITION 9.

<p class="enunciation">Magnitudes which have the same ratio to the same are<br />
equal to one another ; and magnitudes to which the same has<br />
the same ratio are equal.</p>
