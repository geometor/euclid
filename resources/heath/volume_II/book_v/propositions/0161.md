### PROPOSITION 10.

<p class="enunciation">Of magnitudes which have a ratio to the same, that<br />
which has a greater ratio is greater ; and that to which the<br />
same has a greater ratio is less.</p>

For let $A$ have to $C$ a greater ratio than $B$ has to $C$ ;
I say that $A$ is greater than $B$.

![Folio 0161 Figure 1](0161.g1.svg)

For, if not, $A$ is either equal to $B$ or less.

Now $A$ is not equal to $B$ ;
for in that case each of the magnitudes $A, B$ would have
had the same ratio to $C$ ; <span class="justification">[V. 7]</span>
but they have not ;
<p class="assertion">therefore $A$ is not equal to $B$.</p>

Nor again is $A$ less than $B$ ;
for in that case $A$ would have had to $C$ a less ratio than $B$
has to $C$ ; <span class="justification">[V. 8]</span>
but it has not ;
<p class="assertion">therefore $A$ is not less than $B$.</p>

But it was proved not to be equal either ;
<p class="assertion">therefore $A$ is greater than $B$.</p>

Again, let $C$ have to $B$ a greater ratio than $C$ has to $A$ ;
I say that $B$ is less than $A$.

For, if not, it is either equal or greater.

Now $B$ is not equal to $A$ ;
for in that case $C$ would have had the same ratio to each of
the magnitudes $A, B$ ; <span class="justification">[V. 7]</span>
but it has not ;
<p class="assertion">therefore $A$ is not equal to $B$.</p>
