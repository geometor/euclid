Nor again is <span data-ref="0161.g1:B">$B$</span> greater than <span data-ref="0161.g1:A">$A$</span> ;
for in that case <span data-ref="0161.g1:C">$C$</span> would have had to <span data-ref="0161.g1:B">$B$</span> a less ratio than it
has to <span data-ref="0161.g1:A">$A$</span> ; <span class="justification">[V. 8]</span>
but it has not ;
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0161.g1:B">$B$</span> is not greater than <span data-ref="0161.g1:A">$A$</span>.</p>

But it was proved that it is not equal either ;
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0161.g1:B">$B$</span> is less than <span data-ref="0161.g1:A">$A$</span>.</p>

<p class="qed">Therefore etc.<span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

---

No better example can, I think, be found of the acuteness which Simson brought to bear in his critical examination of the $Elements$, and of his great services to the study of Euclid, than is furnished by the admirable note on this proposition where he points out a serious flaw in the proof as given in the text.

For the first time Euclid is arguing about *greater* and *less* ratios, and it will be found by an examination of the steps of the proof that he assumes more with regard to the meaning of the terms than he is entitled to assume, having regard to the fact that the definition of greater ratio (Def. 7) is all that, as yet, he has to go upon. That we cannot argue, at present, about *greater* and *less* as applied to *ratios* in the same way as about the same terms in relation to *magnitudes* is indeed sufficiently indicated by the fact that Euclid does not assume for ratios what is in Book I. an axiom, viz. that things which are equal to the same thing are equal to one another; on the contrary, he *proves*, in Prop. 11, that ratios which are the same with the same ratio are the same with one another.

Let us now examine the steps of the proof in the text. First we are told that

"<span data-ref="0161.g1:A">$A$</span> is greater than <span data-ref="0161.g1:B">$B$</span>.
For, if not, it is either equal to <span data-ref="0161.g1:B">$B$</span> or less than it.
Now <span data-ref="0161.g1:A">$A$</span> is not equal to <span data-ref="0161.g1:B">$B$</span>;
for in that case each of the two magnitudes <span data-ref="0161.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0161.g1:B">$B$</span> would have had the same ratio to <span data-ref="0161.g1:C">$C$</span>: <span class="justification">[V. 7]</span>
but they have not:
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0161.g1:A">$A$</span> is not equal to <span data-ref="0161.g1:B">$B$</span>."</p>

As Simson remarks, the force of this reasoning is as follows.
If <span data-ref="0161.g1:A">$A$</span> has to <span data-ref="0161.g1:C">$C$</span> the same ratio as <span data-ref="0161.g1:B">$B$</span> has to <span data-ref="0161.g1:C">$C$</span>,
then—supposing any equimultiples of <span data-ref="0161.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0161.g1:B">$B$</span> to be taken and any multiple of <span data-ref="0161.g1:C">$C$</span>—
by Def. 5, if the multiple of <span data-ref="0161.g1:A">$A$</span> be greater than the multiple of <span data-ref="0161.g1:C">$C$</span>, the multiple of <span data-ref="0161.g1:B">$B$</span> is also greater than that of <span data-ref="0161.g1:C">$C$</span>.

But it follows from the hypothesis (that <span data-ref="0161.g1:A">$A$</span> has a greater ratio to <span data-ref="0161.g1:C">$C$</span> than <span data-ref="0161.g1:B">$B$</span> has to <span data-ref="0161.g1:C">$C$</span>) that,
by Def. 7, there must be *some* equimultiples of <span data-ref="0161.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0161.g1:B">$B$</span> and *some* multiple of <span data-ref="0161.g1:C">$C$</span> such that the multiple of <span data-ref="0161.g1:A">$A$</span> is greater than the multiple of <span data-ref="0161.g1:C">$C$</span>, but the multiple of <span data-ref="0161.g1:B">$B$</span> is *not* greater than the same multiple of <span data-ref="0161.g1:C">$C$</span>.

And this directly contradicts the preceding deduction from the supposition that <span data-ref="0161.g1:A">$A$</span> has to <span data-ref="0161.g1:C">$C$</span> the same ratio as <span data-ref="0161.g1:B">$B$</span> has to <span data-ref="0161.g1:C">$C$</span>;
<p class="assertion">therefore that supposition is impossible.</p>
