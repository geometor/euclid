## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Two Existential Statements Do Not Contradict Immediately**: To rule out $A < B$ via V. 8, the Greek proof deduces $(B : C) > (A : C)$, which by Def. 7 asserts the existence of *some* integers $m', n'$ with $m'B > n'C$ and $m'A \le n'C$. Meanwhile, the hypothesis $(A : C) > (B : C)$ asserts the existence of *some* integers $m, n$ with $mA > nC$ and $mB \le nC$. Without first proving that $mA > mB$ (which is equivalent to $A > B$, the very conclusion being proved!), these two existential statements are not formally contradictory on the basis of Def. 7 alone.
- **Simson's Direct Proof**: Instead of using *reductio* and V. 8, Simson unpacks Def. 7 directly: since $D = mA > F = nC$ and $E = mB \le F = nC$, we have $mA > mB$, whence $A > B$ immediately.
