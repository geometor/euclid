## 2. Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Failure of Euclid's Reductio Step**: In Euclid's proof, arguing from $A < B$ to $(A:C) < (B:C)$ (or $(B:C) > (A:C)$) via Prop. 8 requires showing that this contradicts $(A:C) > (B:C)$. But Definition 7 asserts the existence of equimultiples $mA > nC$ with $mB \le nC$; to show $(B:C) \not> (A:C)$, one must prove that there cannot exist *different* equimultiples $pB > qC$ with $pA \le qC$. Heath notes (citing Augustus De Morgan's remark on V. Def. 7) that this independence of existential witnesses requires proof, which Euclid omitted.
- **Simson's Substitute Proof**: Simson resolves the gap without using Prop. 8 by picking the Def. 7 witness directly: $D = mA > nC = F \ge mB = E \implies D > E$. Since $D, E$ are equimultiples of $A, B$, by Simson's 4th Axiom (equimultiples preserve order of magnitudes), $A > B$.
