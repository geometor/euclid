### PROPOSITION 11.

<p class="enunciation">Ratios which are the same with the same ratio are also<br />
the same with one another.</p>

For, as $A$ is to $B$, so let $C$ be to $D$,<br />
and, as $C$ is to $D$, so let $E$ be to $F$;<br />
I say that, as $A$ is to $B$, so is $E$ to $F$.

![Folio 0164 Figure 1](0164.g1.svg)

For of $A$, $C$, $E$ let equimultiples $G$, $H$, $K$ be taken, and<br />
of $B$, $D$, $F$ other, chance, equimultiples $L$, $M$, $N$.

Then since, as $A$ is to $B$, so is $C$ to $D$,<br />
and of $A$, $C$ equimultiples $G$, $H$ have been taken,<br />
and of $B$, $D$ other, chance, equimultiples $L$, $M$,

<p class="assertion">therefore, if $G$ is in excess of $L$, $H$ is also in excess of $M$,<br />
if equal, equal,<br />
and if less, less.</p>

Again, since, as $C$ is to $D$, so is $E$ to $F$,<br />
and of $C$, $E$ equimultiples $H$, $K$ have been taken,<br />
and of $D$, $F$ other, chance, equimultiples $M$, $N$,

<p class="assertion">therefore, if $H$ is in excess of $M$, $K$ is also in excess of $N$,<br />
if equal, equal,<br />
and if less, less.</p>

But we saw that, if $H$ was in excess of $M$, $G$ was also<br />
in excess of $L$; if equal, equal; and if less, less;

<p class="assertion">so that, in addition, if $G$ is in excess of $L$, $K$ is also in excess<br />
of $N$,<br />
if equal, equal,<br />
and if less, less.</p>

And $G$, $K$ are equimultiples of $A$, $E$,<br />
while $L$, $N$ are other, chance, equimultiples of $B$, $F$;

<p class="assertion">therefore, as $A$ is to $B$, so is $E$ to $F$.</p>

<p class="qed">Therefore etc.</p>
