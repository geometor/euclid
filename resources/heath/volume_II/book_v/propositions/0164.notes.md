## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Transitivity of Proportion**: Proves that if $A : B = C : D$ and $C : D = E : F$, then $A : B = E : F$. As Heath noted on Page 156, Euclid does not assume Common Notion 1 ("Things which are equal to the same thing are also equal to one another") for ratios, because equality of ratios is defined via the infinite family of equimultiple comparisons in V. Def. 5.
- **"other, chance, equimultiples"**: Translates $\alpha \lambda \lambda \alpha, \grave{\alpha} \varepsilon \tau \cup \chi \varepsilon \nu, \grave{\iota} \sigma \alpha \kappa \iota \varsigma \pi \circ \lambda \lambda \alpha \pi \lambda \alpha \sigma \iota \alpha$—arbitrary equimultiples $L, M, N$ of $B, D, F$ tested against arbitrary equimultiples $G, H, K$ of $A, C, E$.
