Algebraically, if
$$\begin{aligned}
a : b &= c : d, \\
\text{and}\qquad c : d &= e : f, \\
\text{then}\qquad a : b &= e : f.
\end{aligned}$$

The idiomatic use of the imperfect in quoting a result previously obtained<br />
is noteworthy. Instead of saying "But *it was proved that*, if $H$ is in excess<br />
of $M$, $G$ is also in excess of $L$," the Greek text has "But if $H$ *was* in excess<br />
of $M$, $G$ *was* also in excess of $L$," ἀλλὰ εἰ ὑπερεῖχε τὸ Θ τοῦ Μ, ὑπερεῖχε καὶ<br />
τὸ Η τοῦ Λ.

This proposition is tacitly used in combination with v. 16 and v. 24 in the<br />
geometrical passage in Aristotle, *Meteorologica* III. 5, 376 a 22—26.

### PROPOSITION 12.

<p class="enunciation">If any number of magnitudes be proportional, as one of<br />
the antecedents is to one of the consequents, so will all the<br />
antecedents be to all the consequents.</p>

Let any number of magnitudes $A$, $B$, $C$, $D$, $E$, $F$ be<br />
proportional, so that, as $A$ is to $B$, so is $C$ to $D$ and $E$<br />
to $F$;<br />
I say that, as $A$ is to $B$, so are $A$, $C$, $E$ to $B$, $D$, $F$.

![Proposition 12 diagram](0165.g1.svg)

For of $A$, $C$, $E$ let equimultiples $G$, $H$, $K$ be taken,<br />
and of $B$, $D$, $F$ other, chance, equimultiples $L$, $M$, $N$.

Then since, as $A$ is to $B$, so is $C$ to $D$, and $E$ to $F$,<br />
and of $A$, $C$, $E$ equimultiples $G$, $H$, $K$ have been taken,<br />
and of $B$, $D$, $F$ other, chance, equimultiples $L$, $M$, $N$,

<p class="assertion">therefore, if $G$ is in excess of $L$, $H$ is also in excess of $M$,<br />
and $K$ of $N$,<br />
if equal, equal,<br />
and if less, less;</p>

<p class="assertion">so that, in addition,<br />
if $G$ is in excess of $L$, then $G$, $H$, $K$ are in excess of $L$, $M$, $N$,<br />
if equal, equal,<br />
and if less, less.</p>
