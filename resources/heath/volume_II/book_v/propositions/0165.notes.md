## 2. Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Imperfect of Prior Proof (Philosophical/Grammatical Imperfect)**: Heath notes the Greek idiom ἀλλὰ εἰ ὑπερεῖχε τὸ Θ τοῦ Μ, ὑπερεῖχε καὶ τὸ Η τοῦ Λ ("But if $H$ *was* in excess of $M$, $G$ *was* also in excess of $L$"), where Greek uses the imperfect tense (`ὑπερεῖχε`) to refer back to a relation established earlier in the demonstration (note that Greek `Η, Θ, Κ, Λ, Μ, Ν` correspond to Heath's $G, H, K, L, M, N$).
- **Aristotelian Citation**: Heath cites Aristotle, *Meteorologica* III. 5, 376a22–26 (the locus demonstration for the rainbow) as tacitly combining V. 11, V. 16, and V. 24.
- **Proposition 12 (Summation of Antecedents and Consequents)**: States that if $A : B = C : D = E : F$, then $A : B = (A + C + E) : (B + D + F)$. Euclid takes equimultiples $G, H, K$ of the antecedents $A, C, E$ and $L, M, N$ of the consequents $B, D, F$.
