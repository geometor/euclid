Algebraically, if $a : a' = b : b' = c : c'$ etc., each ratio is equal to the ratio<br />
$(a + b + c + \dots) : (a' + b' + c' + \dots)$.

This theorem is quoted by Aristotle, *Eth. Nic.* V. 7, 1131 b 14, in the<br />
shortened form "the whole is to the whole what each part is to each part<br />
(respectively)."

### PROPOSITION 13.

<p class="enunciation">If a first magnitude have to a second the same ratio as a<br />
third to a fourth, and the third have to the fourth a greater<br />
ratio than a fifth has to a sixth, the first will also have to the<br />
second a greater ratio than the fifth to the sixth.</p>

For let a first magnitude <span data-ref="0166.g1:A">$A$</span> have to a second <span data-ref="0166.g1:B">$B$</span> the same ratio as a third <span data-ref="0166.g1:C">$C$</span> has to a fourth <span data-ref="0166.g1:D">$D$</span>,
and let the third <span data-ref="0166.g1:C">$C$</span> have to the fourth <span data-ref="0166.g1:D">$D$</span> a greater ratio than a fifth <span data-ref="0166.g1:E">$E$</span> has to a sixth <span data-ref="0166.g1:F">$F$</span>;
I say that the first <span data-ref="0166.g1:A">$A$</span> will also have to the second <span data-ref="0166.g1:B">$B$</span> a greater ratio than the fifth <span data-ref="0166.g1:E">$E$</span> to the sixth <span data-ref="0166.g1:F">$F$</span>.
