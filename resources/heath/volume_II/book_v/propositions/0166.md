Algebraically, if $a : a' = b : b' = c : c'$ etc., each ratio is equal to the ratio<br />
$(a + b + c + \dots) : (a' + b' + c' + \dots)$.

This theorem is quoted by Aristotle, *Eth. Nic.* V. 7, 1131 b 14, in the<br />
shortened form "the whole is to the whole what each part is to each part<br />
(respectively)."

### PROPOSITION 13.

<p class="enunciation">If a first magnitude have to a second the same ratio as a<br />
third to a fourth, and the third have to the fourth a greater<br />
ratio than a fifth has to a sixth, the first will also have to the<br />
second a greater ratio than the fifth to the sixth.</p>

For let a first magnitude $A$ have to a second $B$ the<br />
same ratio as a third $C$ has to a fourth $D$,<br />
and let the third $C$ have to the fourth $D$ a greater ratio than<br />
a fifth $E$ has to a sixth $F$;<br />
I say that the first $A$ will also have to the second $B$ a greater<br />
ratio than the fifth $E$ to the sixth $F$.
