and $N$, $L$ other, chance, equimultiples of $B$, $F$;

Algebraically, if
$$\begin{aligned}
a : b &= c : d, \\
\text{and}\qquad c : d &> e : f, \\
\text{then}\qquad a : b &> e : f.
\end{aligned}$$

After the words "for, since" in the first line of the proof, Theon added "$C$ has to $D$ a greater ratio than $E$ has to $F$," so that "there are some equimultiples" began, with him, the principal sentence.

The Greek text has, after "of $D$, $F$ other, chance, equimultiples," "*and* the multiple of $C$ is in excess of the multiple of $D$...." The meaning being "such that," I have substituted this for "and," after Simson.

The following will show the method of Euclid's proof.

Since
$$c : d > e : f,$$
there will be some equimultiples $mc$, $me$ of $c$, $e$, and some equimultiples $nd$, $nf$ of $d$, $f$, such that
$$mc > nd, \text{ while } me \ngtr nf.$$
