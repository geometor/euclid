## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Theonine Interpolation**: Heath notes that Theon inserted "$C$ has to $D$ a greater ratio than $E$ has to $F$" after "For, since" in the first line of the proof, changing the syntactic structure so that "there are some equimultiples" became the main clause rather than the causal clause ("since there are some equimultiples... let them be taken").
- **Parataxis ("and" for "such that")**: Heath points out the Greek paratactic καί ("*and* the multiple of $C$ is in excess...") following "other, chance, equimultiples," which he renders as "such that" following Robert Simson.
- **Notation**: Uses $\ngtr$ ("is not greater than") in `mc > nd, while me \ngtr nf` to represent Euclid's "is not in excess of" (οὐχ ὑπερέχει).
