### PROPOSITION 14.

<p class="enunciation">If a first magnitude have to a second the same ratio as a<br />
third has to a fourth, and the first be greater than the third,<br />
the second will also be greater than the fourth ; if equal, equal ;<br />
and if less, less.</p>

For let a first magnitude $A$ have the same ratio to a<br />
second $B$ as a third $C$ has to a fourth $D$ ; and let $A$ be<br />
greater than $C$ ;<br />
I say that $B$ is also greater than $D$.

![Proposition 14 diagram](0168.g1.svg)

For, since $A$ is greater than $C$,<br />
and $B$ is another, chance, magnitude,<br />
<p class="assertion">therefore $A$ has to $B$ a greater ratio than $C$ has to $B$. <span class="justification">[V. 8]</span></p>

But, as $A$ is to $B$, so is $C$ to $D$ ;<br />
<p class="assertion">therefore $C$ has also to $D$ a greater ratio than $C$ has to $B$. <span class="justification">[V. 13]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But that to which the same has a greater ratio is less ; <span class="justification">[V. 10]</span></p>
<p class="assertion">therefore $D$ is less than $B$ ;<br />
so that $B$ is greater than $D$.</p>

Similarly we can prove that, if $A$ be equal to $C$, $B$ will<br />
also be equal to $D$ ;<br />
and, if $A$ be less than $C$, $B$ will also be less than $D$.

<p class="qed">Therefore etc.</p>
