Algebraically, if $a : b = c : d$,<br />
then, according as $a > = < c$, $b > = < d$.

Simson adds the specific proof of the second and third parts of this<br />
proposition, which Euclid dismisses with "Similarly we can prove...."

"Secondly, if $A$ be equal to $C$, $B$ is equal to $D$ ; for $A$ is to $B$ as $C$, that<br />
is $A$, is to $D$ ;
<p class="assertion">therefore $B$ is equal to $D$. <span class="justification">[V. 9]</span></p>

Thirdly, if $A$ be less than $C$, $B$ shall be less than $D$.<br />
For $C$ is greater than $A$ ;<br />
and, because $C$ is to $D$ as $A$ is to $B$,
<p class="assertion">$D$ is greater than $B$, by the first case.</p>
Wherefore $B$ is less than $D$."

Aristotle, *Meteorol.* III. 5, 376 a 11—14, quotes the equivalent proposition<br />
that, if $a > b$, $c > d$.

### PROPOSITION 15.

<p class="enunciation">Parts have the same ratio as the same multiples of them<br />
taken in corresponding order.</p>

For let $AB$ be the same multiple of $C$ that $DE$ is of $F$ ;<br />
I say that, as $C$ is to $F$, so is $AB$ to $DE$.

![Proposition 15 diagram](0169.g1.svg)

For, since $AB$ is the same multiple of $C$ that $DE$ is of $F$,<br />
as many magnitudes as there are in $AB$ equal to $C$, so many<br />
are there also in $DE$ equal to $F$.

Let $AB$ be divided into the magnitudes $AG$, $GH$, $HB$<br />
equal to $C$,<br />
and $DE$ into the magnitudes $DK$, $KL$, $LE$ equal to $F$ ;<br />
then the multitude of the magnitudes $AG$, $GH$, $HB$ will be<br />
equal to the multitude of the magnitudes $DK$, $KL$, $LE$.

And, since $AG$, $GH$, $HB$ are equal to one another,<br />
and $DK$, $KL$, $LE$ are also equal to one another,<br />
<p class="assertion">therefore, as $AG$ is to $DK$, so is $GH$ to $KL$, and $HB$ to $LE$. <span class="justification">[V. 7]</span></p>

Therefore, as one of the antecedents is to one of the<br />
consequents, so will all the antecedents be to all the<br />
consequents ; <span class="justification">[V. 12]</span><br />
<p class="assertion">therefore, as $AG$ is to $DK$, so is $AB$ to $DE$.</p>
