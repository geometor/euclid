## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Simson's Explicit Cases for V. 14**: Because the cases $A = C$ and $A < C$ use V. 9 (or reduction to the first case) rather than the exact chain V. 8 $\to$ V. 13 $\to$ V. 10, Simson supplied separate proofs for both cases rather than relying on Euclid's "Similarly we can prove...".
- **Proposition 15**: Proves $C : F = mC : mF$ ("Parts have the same ratio as the same multiples of them taken in corresponding order") by partitioning $AB = mC$ and $DE = mF$ into $m$ equal parts ($m = 3$ in the diagram) and applying V. 7 and V. 12.
- **Typographical Detail**: In the sentence "And, since $AG$, $GH$, $HB$ are equal to one another,", the comma after $AG$ is slightly worn in the scan, resembling a period (`AG.`), which is normalized to a comma in the transcription.
