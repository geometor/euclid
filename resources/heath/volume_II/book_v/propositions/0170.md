### PROPOSITION 16.

<p class="enunciation">If four magnitudes be proportional, they will also be<br />
proportional alternately.</p>

Let $A$, $B$, $C$, $D$ be four proportional magnitudes,<br />
so that, as $A$ is to $B$, so is $C$ to $D$ ;<br />
I say that they will also be so alternately, that is, as $A$ is<br />
to $C$, so is $B$ to $D$.

![Proposition 16 diagram](0170.g1.svg)

For of $A$, $B$ let equimultiples $E$, $F$ be taken,<br />
and of $C$, $D$ other, chance, equimultiples $G$, $H$.

Then, since $E$ is the same multiple of $A$ that $F$ is of $B$,<br />
and parts have the same ratio as the same multiples of<br />
them, <span class="justification">[V. 15]</span><br />
<p class="assertion">therefore, as $A$ is to $B$, so is $E$ to $F$.</p>

But as $A$ is to $B$, so is $C$ to $D$ ;<br />
<p class="assertion">therefore also, as $C$ is to $D$, so is $E$ to $F$. <span class="justification">[V. 11]</span></p>

Again, since $G$, $H$ are equimultiples of $C$, $D$,<br />
<p class="assertion">therefore, as $C$ is to $D$, so is $G$ to $H$. <span class="justification">[V. 15]</span></p>

But, as $C$ is to $D$, so is $E$ to $F$ ;<br />
<p class="assertion">therefore also, as $E$ is to $F$, so is $G$ to $H$. <span class="justification">[V. 11]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But, if four magnitudes be proportional, and the first be<br />
greater than the third,<br />
the second will also be greater than the fourth ;<br />
if equal, equal ;<br />
and if less, less. <span class="justification">[V. 14]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore, if $E$ is in excess of $G$, $F$ is also in excess of $H$,<br />
if equal, equal,<br />
and if less, less.</p>
