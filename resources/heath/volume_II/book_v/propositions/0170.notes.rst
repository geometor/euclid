2. Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
-----------------------------------------------

- **Proposition 16** (*Alternando* **/ ἐναλλάξ**): If $A : B = C : D$ (where $A, B, C, D$ are magnitudes of the same kind), then $A : C = B : D$.
- **Architecture of the Proof**: Because V. Def. 5 cannot compare $A, C$ and $B, D$ directly from equimultiples of $A, C$ and $B, D$ using the hypothesis $A : B = C : D$, Euclid takes equimultiples $E, F$ of $A, B$ and $G, H$ of $C, D$, uses V. 15 and V. 11 twice to deduce $E : F = G : H$, and then applies V. 14 to infer that $E > = < G \implies F > = < H$, which is the exact criterion of V. Def. 5 for $A : C = B : D$ since $E, F$ are equimultiples of $A, B$ and $G, H$ of $C, D$.
