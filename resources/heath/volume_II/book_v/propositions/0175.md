Subtract from $m(a + b), (m + n)b$ the common part $mb$, and from<br />
$m(c + d), (m + n)d$ the common part $md$; and we have,<br />
according as $ma > = < nb, \quad mc > = < nd$.

But $ma, mc$ are any equimultiples of $a, c$, and $nb, nd$ any equimultiples of<br />
$b, d$,<br />
therefore, by V. Def. 5,

$$a \text{ is to } b \text{ as } c \text{ is to } d.$$

Smith and Bryant's proof follows, *mutatis mutandis*, their alternative proof<br />
of the next proposition (see pp. 173—4 below).

### PROPOSITION 18.

<p class="enunciation">If magnitudes be proportional separando, they will also be<br />
proportional componendo.</p>

Let <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span>, <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span>, <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span>, <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ F D /">$FD$</span> be magnitudes proportional<br />
*separando*, so that, as <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> is<br />
to <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span> to <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ F D /">$FD$</span>;<br />
I say that they will also be<br />
proportional *componendo*, that<br />
is, as <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ E B /">$BE$</span>, so is<br />
<span data-ref="0175.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> to <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ F D /">$FD$</span>.

![Proposition 18 diagram of line segments](0175.g1.svg)

For, if <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> be not to <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ F D /">$DF$</span> as <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> to <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ E B /">$BE$</span>,<br />
then, as <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ E B /">$BE$</span>, so will <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> be either to some<br />
magnitude less than <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ F D /">$DF$</span> or to a greater.

First, let it be in that ratio to a less magnitude <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ G D /">$DG$</span>.

Then, since, as <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ E B /">$BE$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> to <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ G D /">$DG$</span>,<br />
they are magnitudes proportional *componendo*;
<p class="assertion">so that they will also be proportional *separando*. <span class="justification">[V. 17]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore, as <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> is to <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ C G /">$CG$</span> to <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ G D /">$GD$</span>.</p>

But also, by hypothesis,
<p class="assertion">as <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> is to <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span> to <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ F D /">$FD$</span>.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore also, as <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ C G /">$CG$</span> is to <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ G D /">$GD$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span> to <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ F D /">$FD$</span>. <span class="justification">[V. 11]</span></p>

But the first <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ C G /">$CG$</span> is greater than the third <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span>;
<p class="assertion">therefore the second <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ G D /">$GD$</span> is also greater than the fourth<br />
<span data-ref="0175.g1:/ F D /">$FD$</span>. <span class="justification">[V. 14]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But it is also less: which is impossible.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore, as <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ E B /">$BE$</span>, so is not <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> to a less<br />
magnitude than <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ F D /">$FD$</span>.</p>
