Subtract from $m(a + b), (m + n)b$ the common part $mb$, and from<br />
$m(c + d), (m + n)d$ the common part $md$; and we have,<br />
according as $ma > = < nb, \quad mc > = < nd$.

But $ma, mc$ are any equimultiples of $a, c$, and $nb, nd$ any equimultiples of<br />
$b, d$,<br />
therefore, by V. Def. 5,

$$a \text{ is to } b \text{ as } c \text{ is to } d.$$

Smith and Bryant's proof follows, *mutatis mutandis*, their alternative proof<br />
of the next proposition (see pp. 173—4 below).

### PROPOSITION 18.

<p class="enunciation">If magnitudes be proportional separando, they will also be<br />
proportional componendo.</p>

Let <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span>, <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span>, <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span>, <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ F D /">$FD$</span> be magnitudes proportional *separando*, so that, as <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> is to <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span> to <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ F D /">$FD$</span>;
I say that they will also be proportional *componendo*, that is, as <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ E B /">$BE$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> to <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ F D /">$FD$</span>.

![Proposition 18 diagram of line segments](0175.g1.svg)

For, if <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> be not to <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ F D /">$DF$</span> as <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> to <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ E B /">$BE$</span>,
then, as <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ E B /">$BE$</span>, so will <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> be either to some magnitude less than <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ F D /">$DF$</span> or to a greater.

First, let it be in that ratio to a less magnitude <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ G D /">$DG$</span>.

Then, since, as <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ E B /">$BE$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> to <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ G D /">$DG$</span>,
they are magnitudes proportional *componendo*;
<p class="assertion">so that they will also be proportional *separando*. <span class="justification">[V. 17]</span></p>

Therefore, as <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> is to <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ C G /">$CG$</span> to <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ G D /">$GD$</span>.

But also, by hypothesis,
as <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> is to <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span> to <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ F D /">$FD$</span>.

Therefore also, as <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ C G /">$CG$</span> is to <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ G D /">$GD$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span> to <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ F D /">$FD$</span>. [V. 11]

But the first <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ C G /">$CG$</span> is greater than the third <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span>;
therefore the second <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ G D /">$GD$</span> is also greater than the fourth <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ F D /">$FD$</span>. [V. 14]

<p class="sub-clause">But it is also less: which is impossible.</p>

Therefore, as <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ E B /">$BE$</span>, so is not <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> to a less magnitude than <span data-ref="0175.g1:/ F D /">$FD$</span>.
