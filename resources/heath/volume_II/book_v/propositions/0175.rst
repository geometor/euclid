Subtract from $m(a + b), (m + n)b$ the common part $mb$, and from $m(c + d), (m + n)d$ the common part $md$; and we have, according as $ma > = < nb, \quad mc > = < nd$.

But $ma, mc$ are any equimultiples of $a, c$, and $nb, nd$ any equimultiples of $b, d$, therefore, by V. Def. 5,

$$a \text{ is to } b \text{ as } c \text{ is to } d.$$

Smith and Bryant's proof follows, :term:`*mutatis mutandis* <mutatis-mutandis>`, their alternative proof of the next proposition (see pp. 173—4 below).

----

PROPOSITION 18.
---------------

.. enunciation::

   If magnitudes be proportional separando, they will also be proportional componendo.

Let :g:`$AE$ <0175.g1:/ A E />`, :g:`$EB$ <0175.g1:/ E B />`, :g:`$CF$ <0175.g1:/ C F />`, :g:`$FD$ <0175.g1:/ F D />` be magnitudes proportional :term:`*separando* <separando>`, so that, as :g:`$AE$ <0175.g1:/ A E />` is to :g:`$EB$ <0175.g1:/ E B />`, so is :g:`$CF$ <0175.g1:/ C F />` to :g:`$FD$ <0175.g1:/ F D />`;

I say that they will also be proportional :term:`*componendo* <componendo>`, that is, as :g:`$AB$ <0175.g1:/ A B />` is to :g:`$BE$ <0175.g1:/ E B />`, so is :g:`$CD$ <0175.g1:/ C D />` to :g:`$FD$ <0175.g1:/ F D />`.

.. picture:: 0175.g1.svg
   :alt: Proposition 18 diagram of line segments

For, if :g:`$CD$ <0175.g1:/ C D />` be not to :g:`$DF$ <0175.g1:/ F D />` as :g:`$AB$ <0175.g1:/ A B />` to :g:`$BE$ <0175.g1:/ E B />`, then, as :g:`$AB$ <0175.g1:/ A B />` is to :g:`$BE$ <0175.g1:/ E B />`, so will :g:`$CD$ <0175.g1:/ C D />` be either to some magnitude less than :g:`$DF$ <0175.g1:/ F D />` or to a greater.

First, let it be in that ratio to a less magnitude :g:`$DG$ <0175.g1:/ G D />`.

Then, since, as :g:`$AB$ <0175.g1:/ A B />` is to :g:`$BE$ <0175.g1:/ E B />`, so is :g:`$CD$ <0175.g1:/ C D />` to :g:`$DG$ <0175.g1:/ G D />`, they are magnitudes proportional :term:`*componendo* <componendo>`;

.. assertion:: [V. 17] <V.17>

   so that they will also be proportional :term:`*separando* <separando>`.

.. assertion::

   Therefore, as :g:`$AE$ <0175.g1:/ A E />` is to :g:`$EB$ <0175.g1:/ E B />`, so is :g:`$CG$ <0175.g1:/ C G />` to :g:`$GD$ <0175.g1:/ G D />`.

But also, by hypothesis,

.. assertion::

   as :g:`$AE$ <0175.g1:/ A E />` is to :g:`$EB$ <0175.g1:/ E B />`, so is :g:`$CF$ <0175.g1:/ C F />` to :g:`$FD$ <0175.g1:/ F D />`.

.. assertion:: [V. 11] <V.11>

   Therefore also, as :g:`$CG$ <0175.g1:/ C G />` is to :g:`$GD$ <0175.g1:/ G D />`, so is :g:`$CF$ <0175.g1:/ C F />` to :g:`$FD$ <0175.g1:/ F D />`.

But the first :g:`$CG$ <0175.g1:/ C G />` is greater than the third :g:`$CF$ <0175.g1:/ C F />`;

.. assertion::

   therefore the second :g:`$GD$ <0175.g1:/ G D />` is also greater than the fourth :g:`$FD$ <0175.g1:/ F D />`. :just:`[V. 14] <V.14>`

.. subclause::

   But it is also less: which is impossible.

.. assertion::

   Therefore, as :g:`$AB$ <0175.g1:/ A B />` is to :g:`$BE$ <0175.g1:/ E B />`, so is not :g:`$CD$ <0175.g1:/ C D />` to a less magnitude than :g:`$FD$ <0175.g1:/ F D />`.
