## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **De Morgan's Dedekind-Style Cut Argument**:
  - De Morgan's proof of the existence of $A$ such that $A : B = P : Q$ is a remarkable early topological/order-completeness argument: in (1) he proves that the set of magnitudes $M$ with $M : B > P : Q$ and the set of magnitudes $M$ with $M : B < P : Q$ are both open rays (using an Archimedean subdivision of the excess $Z = 15M - 22B$ into 15ths), and in (2) he invokes order-completeness to find the boundary magnitude $A$, which can belong to neither open ray and must therefore satisfy $A : B = P : Q$.
- **Simson's Conjecture on V. 5 and V. 6**:
  - Simson argued that because V. 5 and V. 6 (distributivity of multiples over subtraction) are never cited in the extant text of Book V, Euclid originally used them to prove V. 18 as the direct converse of V. 17 (which used V. 1 and V. 2).
