Clearly, if
<p class="assertion">$nb$ is greater than $mb$,<br />
$nd$ is greater than $md$;<br />
if equal, equal; and if less, less.</p>

I. Suppose $nb$ not greater than $mb$, so that $nd$ is also not greater than $md$.

<p class="assertion">Now $m(a + b)$ is greater than $mb$:<br />
therefore $m(a + b)$ is greater than $nb$.</p>

<p class="assertion">Similarly $m(c + d)$ is greater than $nd$.</p>

II. Suppose $nb$ greater than $mb$.

Since $m(a + b), mb, m(c + d), md$ are equimultiples of $(a + b), b, (c + d), d$,
<p class="assertion">$ma$ is the same multiple of $a$ that $m(a + b)$ is of $(a + b)$,<br />
and $mc$ is the same multiple of $c$ that $m(c + d)$ is of $(c + d)$,<br />
so that $ma, mc$ are equimultiples of $a, c$. <span class="justification">[V. 5]</span></p>

Again $nb, nd$ are equimultiples of $b, d$,<br />
and so are $mb, md$;<br />
therefore $(n - m)b, (n - m)d$ are equimultiples of $b, d$ and, whether $n - m$<br />
is equal to unity or to any other integer <span class="justification">[V. 6]</span>, it follows, by Def. 5, that,<br />
since $a, b, c, d$ are proportionals,
<p class="assertion">if $ma$ is greater than $(n - m)b$,<br />
then $mc$ is greater than $(n - m)d$;<br />
if equal, equal; and if less, less.</p>

(1) If now $m(a + b)$ is greater than $nb$, subtracting $mb$ from each, we have
<p class="assertion">$ma$ is greater than $(n - m)b$;<br />
therefore $mc$ is greater than $(n - m)d$,</p>
and, if we add $md$ to each,
<p class="assertion">$m(c + d)$ is greater than $nd$.</p>

(2) Similarly it may be proved that,
<p class="assertion">if $m(a + b)$ is equal to $nb$,<br />
then $m(c + d)$ is equal to $nd$,<br />
and (3) that, if $m(a + b)$ is less than $nb$,<br />
then $m(c + d)$ is less than $nd$.</p>

But (under I. above) it was proved that, in the case where $nb$ is not<br />
greater than $mb$,
<p class="assertion">$m(a + b)$ is always greater than $nb$,<br />
and $m(c + d)$ is always greater than $nd$.</p>

Hence, whatever be the values of $m$ and $n$, $m(c + d)$ is always greater than,<br />
equal to, or less than $nd$ according as $m(a + b)$ is greater than, equal to, or<br />
less than $nb$.

Therefore, by Def. 5,
<p class="assertion">$a + b$ is to $b$ as $c + d$ is to $d$.</p>
