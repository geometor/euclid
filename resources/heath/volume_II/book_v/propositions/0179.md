For, because <span data-ref="0179.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> is to <span data-ref="0179.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span> as <span data-ref="0179.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span> is to <span data-ref="0179.g1:/ F D /">$FD$</span>,<br />
therefore, alternately,
<p class="assertion">$AE$ is to $CF$ as <span data-ref="0179.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0179.g1:/ F D /">$FD$</span>. <span class="justification">[V. 16]</span></p>

And, as one of the antecedents is to its consequent, so is the sum of the<br />
antecedents to the sum of the consequents: <span class="justification">[V. 12]</span><br />
therefore, as $EB$ is to $FD$, so are <span data-ref="0179.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span>, <span data-ref="0179.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span> together to <span data-ref="0179.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span>,<br />
<span data-ref="0179.g1:/ F D /">$FD$</span> together;<br />
that is,
<p class="assertion">$AB$ is to $CD$ as $EB$ is to $FD$.</p>

Therefore, alternately,
<p class="assertion">$AB$ is to $BE$ as $CD$ is to $FD$.”</p>

![Simson's proof for V. 18](0179.g1.svg)

The objection to this proof is that it is only valid in the case<br />
where the proposition V. 16 used in it is valid, i.e. where all four<br />
magnitudes are of the same kind.

Smith and Bryant's proof avails where all four magnitudes<br />
are straight lines, where all four magnitudes are rectilineal areas,<br />
or where one antecedent and its consequent are straight lines and the other<br />
antecedent and its consequent rectilineal areas.

Suppose that
<p class="assertion">$A : B = C : D$.</p>

$First$, let all the magnitudes be areas.<br />
Construct a rectangle <span data-ref="0179.g2:< a b c d >">$abcd$</span> equal to <span data-ref="0179.g2:< a b c d >">$A$</span>, and to <span data-ref="0179.g2:/ c b /">$bc$</span> apply the rectangle <span data-ref="0179.g2:< b c e f >">$bcef$</span><br />
equal to <span data-ref="0179.g2:< b c e f >">$B$</span>.<br />
Also to <span data-ref="0179.g2:/ a b /">$ab$</span>, <span data-ref="0179.g2:/ b f /">$bf$</span> apply the rectangles <span data-ref="0179.g2:< a b g h >">$ag$</span>, <span data-ref="0179.g2:< b f k g >">$bk$</span> equal to <span data-ref="0179.g2:< a b g h >">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0179.g2:< b f k g >">$D$</span> respectively.

![Smith and Bryant's proof for V. 18](0179.g2.svg)

Then, since the rectangles <span data-ref="0179.g2:< a b c d >">$ac$</span>, <span data-ref="0179.g2:< b c e f >">$be$</span> have equal<br />
heights <span data-ref="0179.g2:/ c b /">$bc$</span>, they are to one another as their<br />
bases. <span class="justification">[VI. 1]</span>

Hence
<p align="center">ab : bf = rect. ac : rect. be <br />
= A : B <br />
= C : D <br />
= rect. <span data-ref="0179.g2:< a b g h >">$ag$</span> : rect. <span data-ref="0179.g2:< b f k g >">$bk$</span>.</p>

Therefore <span class="justification">[VI. 1, converse]</span> the rectangles $ag, bk$ have the same height, so<br />
that <span data-ref="0179.g2:k">$k$</span> is on the straight line <span data-ref="0179.g2:/ h g /">$hg$</span>.

Hence
<p align="center"><span data-ref="0179.g2:/ a b /">$A$</span> + <span data-ref="0179.g2:/ b f /">$B$</span> : B = rect. ae : rect. be <br />
= af : bf <br />
= rect. ak : rect. bk <br />
= <span data-ref="0179.g2:< a b g h >">$C$</span> + <span data-ref="0179.g2:< b f k g >">$D$</span> : D.</p>

$Secondly$, let the magnitudes $A$, $B$ be straight lines and the magnitudes<br />
$C$, $D$ areas.<br />
Let <span data-ref="0179.g2:/ a b /">$ab$</span>, <span data-ref="0179.g2:/ b f /">$bf$</span> be equal to the straight lines <span data-ref="0179.g2:/ a b /">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0179.g2:/ b f /">$B$</span>, and to <span data-ref="0179.g2:/ a b /">$ab$</span>, <span data-ref="0179.g2:/ b f /">$bf$</span> apply the<br />
rectangles <span data-ref="0179.g2:< a b g h >">$ag$</span>, <span data-ref="0179.g2:< b f k g >">$bk$</span> equal to <span data-ref="0179.g2:< a b g h >">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0179.g2:< b f k g >">$D$</span> respectively.<br />
Then, as before, the rectangles <span data-ref="0179.g2:< a b g h >">$ag$</span>, <span data-ref="0179.g2:< b f k g >">$bk$</span> have the same height.<br />
Now
$$\begin{aligned}
A + B : B &= af : bf \\
&= \text{rect. } ak : \text{rect. } bk \\
&= C + D : D.
\end{aligned}$$

Apply to the straight lines $C$, $D$ rectangles $P$, $Q$ having the same height.
