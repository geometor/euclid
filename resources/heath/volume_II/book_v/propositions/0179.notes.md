# Transcription Notes: Folio 0179 (Page 173)

## Summary & Folio Continuity
- **Preceding Folio (`0178` / Page 172)**: Contains the conclusion of Euclid's proof of Book V, Proposition 18 (*componendo*) and the beginning of Heath's commentary, including the opening lines of Robert Simson's alternative demonstration.
- **Current Folio (`0179` / Page 173)**: Continues Heath's commentary on Proposition 18:
  1. Concludes the quotation of Simson's proof (using V. 16 and V. 12) and notes the limitation that V. 16 requires all four magnitudes to be of the same kind.
  2. Presents Charles Smith and Sophie Bryant's alternative proof (using VI. 1 and its converse via application of areas) covering three cases: (First) all four magnitudes are rectilineal areas; (Secondly) one antecedent-consequent pair consists of straight lines and the other of areas; (Thirdly) all four magnitudes are straight lines.
- **Following Folio (`0180` / Page 174)**: Concludes the third case of Smith and Bryant's proof at the top of the page, followed by Book V, Proposition 19.

## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Limitation of Simson's Proof via V. 16**: Heath points out that Simson's direct proof of V. 18 from V. 17 relies on alternation (V. 16) and addition of antecedents/consequents (V. 12), which restricts the proof to four magnitudes all of the same species, whereas Euclid's generality in Book V requires only that $A, B$ be of one kind and $C, D$ be of another (or the same) kind.
- **Anachronistic Use of VI. 1 in Smith & Bryant's Proof**: Smith and Bryant's proof uses the application of rectangles (`rect. ac`, `rect. be`, `rect. ag`, `rect. bk`) and Book VI, Proposition 1 (rectangles of equal height are to one another as their bases) to bypass the fourth-proportional existence postulate, at the cost of restricting magnitudes to straight lines and rectilineal areas and relying on Book VI results.
