2. Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
-----------------------------------------------

- **Greek Citation**: ``ἀπραγματεύτως (Proclus, p. 303, 6)`` ("without effort or trouble"), describing the nature of a genuine Euclidean porism as an immediate corollary.
- **Critique of the Interpolated Porism**: Heath demonstrates algebraically why the manuscript argument for *convertendo* in V. 19 is logically flawed: starting from $a : b = c : d$, it merely observes that $a : (a - c) = b : b - d$ and $a : c = b : d$ both hold, rather than deducing $(a + b) : a = (c + d) : c$ (or $a : (a - b) = c : (c - d)$) from $a : b = c : d$ without alternation, which would fail when $a, b$ and $c, d$ are of different kinds.
- **Simson's Proposition E (Clavius)**: Proves *convertendo* ($AB : BE = CD : DF \implies BA : AE = DC : CF$) generally for magnitudes of different kinds by combining V. 17 (*separando*), inversion, and V. 18 (*componendo*), avoiding V. 16 (*alternando*) altogether.
