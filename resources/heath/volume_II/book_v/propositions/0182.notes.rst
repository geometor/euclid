2. Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
-----------------------------------------------

- **Implicit Use of Inversion**: In the proof of V. 20, Euclid writes "and, as $C$ is to $B$, inversely, so is $F$ to $E$" without citing an explicit proposition, since inversion ($\text{ἀνάπαλιν}$) is an immediate consequence of V. Def. 5 (explicitly stated in the Porism to V. 7, though bracketed by Heiberg, and supplied by Simson as Prop. B).
- **Dismissal of Remaining Cases**: Euclid proves the case $A > C \implies D > F$ in full using V. 8, V. 13, and V. 10, and dismisses the cases $A = C$ and $A < C$ with "Similarly we can prove that..." ($\text{ὁμοίως δὴ δείξομεν ὅτι...}$), which Simson expands explicitly (quoted on pp. 177–178).
