## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Role of Equimultiples in V. 22 and V. 23**: Heath highlights the difference between the equimultiple transformations in V. 22 and V. 23: in V. 22, $a : b = d : e$ and $b : c = e : f$ can be scaled directly by V. 4 using three multipliers ($m, n, p$) so that the middle terms $nb$ and $ne$ match (`ma : nb = md : ne` and `nb : pc = ne : pf`). In V. 23, because the proportions are perturbed ($a : b = e : f$ and $b : c = d : e$), $a, b, d$ are scaled by $m$ and $c, e, f$ by $n$ via V. 15 and V. 4 (`ma : mb = ne : nf` and `mb : nc = md : ne`) before applying V. 21.
- **Compound Order Notation**: Heath uses the compact tri-relational notation $a > = < c$ and $d > = < f$ to denote the three parallel cases ("greater than, equal to, or less than").
