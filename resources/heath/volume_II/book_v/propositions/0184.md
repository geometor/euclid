### PROPOSITION 21.

<p class="enunciation">If there be three magnitudes, and others equal to them in<br />
multitude, which taken two and two together are in the same<br />
ratio, and the proportion of them be perturbed, then, if ex<br />
aequali the first magnitude is greater than the third, the<br />
fourth will also be greater than the sixth; if equal, equal;<br />
and if less, less.</p>

Let there be three magnitudes $A$, $B$, $C$, and others $D$, $E$, $F$<br />
equal to them in multitude, which taken two and two are in<br />
the same ratio, and let the proportion of them be perturbed,<br />
so that,
<p class="assertion">as $A$ is to $B$, so is $E$ to $F$,</p>
<p class="assertion">and, as $B$ is to $C$, so is $D$ to $E$,</p>
and let $A$ be greater than $C$ *ex aequali*;<br />
I say that $D$ will also be greater than $F$; if $A$ is equal to<br />
$C$, equal; and if less, less.

![Proposition 21](0184.g1.svg)

For, since $A$ is greater than $C$,<br />
and $B$ is some other magnitude,
<p class="assertion">therefore $A$ has to $B$ a greater ratio than $C$ has to $B$. <span class="justification">[V. 8]</span></p>

But, as $A$ is to $B$, so is $E$ to $F$,<br />
and, as $C$ is to $B$, inversely, so is $E$ to $D$.<br />
<p class="assertion">Therefore also $E$ has to $F$ a greater ratio than $E$ has to $D$. <span class="justification">[V. 13]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But that to which the same has a greater ratio is less; <span class="justification">[V. 10]</span></p>
<p class="assertion">therefore $F$ is less than $D$;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore $D$ is greater than $F$.</p>
