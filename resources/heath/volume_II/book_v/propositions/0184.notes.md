## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Asymmetry Between V. 20 and V. 21**: In V. 20 ($A : B = D : E$ and $B : C = E : F$), $A > C$ leads to $D : E > F : E$, where $E$ is the common consequent (first part of V. 10: that which has a greater ratio to the same is greater). In V. 21 ($A : B = E : F$ and $B : C = D : E$), $A > C$ leads to $E : F > E : D$, where $E$ is the common antecedent, requiring the second part of V. 10 ("that to which the same has a greater ratio is less") to conclude $F < D$, i.e., $D > F$.
