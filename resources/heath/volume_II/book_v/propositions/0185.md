Algebraically, if
$$\begin{aligned}
& a : b = e : f, \\
\text{and} \quad & b : c = d : e,
\end{aligned}$$
$$\text{then, according as } a > = < c, \quad d > = < f.$$

Simson's alterations correspond to those which he makes in Prop. 20. After<br />
the first case he proceeds thus.

"Secondly, let $A$ be equal to $C$; then shall $D$ be equal to $F$.<br />
Because $A$ and $C$ are equal,
<p class="assertion">$A$ is to $B$ as $C$ is to $B$. <span class="justification">[V. 7]</span></p>
But $A$ is to $B$ as $E$ is to $F$,<br />
and $C$ is to $B$ as $E$ is to $D$:
<p class="assertion">wherefore $E$ is to $F$ as $E$ to $D$, <span class="justification">[V. 11]</span></p>
<p class="assertion">and therefore $D$ is equal to $F$. <span class="justification">[V. 9]</span></p>

Next, let $A$ be less than $C$; then shall $D$ be less than $F$.<br />
For $C$ is greater than $A$,<br />
and, as was shown,
<p class="assertion">$C$ is to $B$ as $E$ to $D$,</p>
and, in like manner,
<p class="assertion">$B$ is to $A$ as $F$ to $E$;</p>
therefore $F$ is greater than $D$, by the first case,<br />
and therefore $D$ is less than $F$."

The proof may be shown thus.
$$\text{According as } a > = < c, \quad a : b > = < c : b.$$
But $a : b = e : f$, and, by inversion, $c : b = e : d$.<br />
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Therefore, according as } a > = < c, \quad & e : f > = < e : d, \\
\text{and therefore } & d > = < f.
\end{aligned}$$

### PROPOSITION 22.

<p class="enunciation">If there be any number of magnitudes whatever, and others<br />
equal to them in multitude, which taken two and two together<br />
are in the same ratio, they will also be in the same ratio ex<br />
aequali.</p>

Let there be any number of magnitudes $A$, $B$, $C$, and<br />
others $D$, $E$, $F$ equal to them in multitude, which taken two<br />
and two together are in the same ratio, so that,
<p class="assertion">as $A$ is to $B$, so is $D$ to $E$,</p>
<p class="assertion">and, as $B$ is to $C$, so is $E$ to $F$;</p>
I say that they will also be in the same ratio *ex aequali*,
<p class="sub-clause">$\langle\text{that is, as } A \text{ is to } C\text{, so is } D \text{ to } F\rangle$.</p>
