## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Angle Brackets in V. 22 Specification**: The explanatory clause `<that is, as A is to C, so is D to F>` ($\text{τουτέστιν, ὡς τὸ Α πρὸς τὸ Γ, οὕτως τὸ Δ πρὸς τὸ Ζ}$) is enclosed in pointed brackets `< >` by Heath following Heiberg, indicating a gloss or interpolation in the manuscript tradition clarifying the meaning of $\text{δι' ἴσου}$ (*ex aequali*).
- **General Multitude of Magnitudes**: Proposition 22 generalizes from "three magnitudes" ($\text{τρία μεγέθη}$ in V. 20–21) to "any number of magnitudes whatever" ($\text{ὁσαοῦν μεγέθη}$), although Euclid's concrete demonstration uses three magnitudes $A, B, C$ and $D, E, F$ as representatives, since the step from three to any finite number follows by immediate induction.
