For of $A$, $D$ let equimultiples $G$, $H$ be taken,<br />
and of $B$, $E$ other, chance, equimultiples $K$, $L$;<br />
and, further, of $C$, $F$ other, chance, equimultiples $M$, $N$.

![Book V, Proposition 22, Line Segments](0186.g1.svg)

Then, since, as $A$ is to $B$, so is $D$ to $E$,<br />
and of $A$, $D$ equimultiples $G$, $H$ have been taken,<br />
and of $B$, $E$ other, chance, equimultiples $K$, $L$,
<p class="assertion">therefore, as $G$ is to $K$, so is $H$ to $L$. <span class="justification">[V. 4]</span></p>

For the same reason also,
<p class="assertion">as $K$ is to $M$, so is $L$ to $N$.</p>

Since, then, there are three magnitudes $G$, $K$, $M$, and<br />
others $H$, $L$, $N$ equal to them in multitude, which taken two<br />
and two together are in the same ratio,
<p class="assertion">therefore, *ex aequali*, if $G$ is in excess of $M$, $H$ is also in excess<br />
of $N$;<br />
if equal, equal; and if less, less. <span class="justification">[V. 20]</span></p>

And $G$, $H$ are equimultiples of $A$, $D$,<br />
and $M$, $N$ other, chance, equimultiples of $C$, $F$.

<p class="assertion">Therefore, as $A$ is to $C$, so is $D$ to $F$. <span class="justification">[V. Def. 5]</span></p>

<p class="qed">Therefore etc.<span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

---

Euclid enunciates this proposition as true of *any number of magnitudes*<br />
*whatever* forming two sets connected in the manner described, but his proof is<br />
confined to the case where each set consists of three magnitudes only. The<br />
extension to any number of magnitudes is, however, easy, as shown by<br />
Simson.

"Next let there be four magnitudes $A$, $B$, $C$, $D$, and other four $E$, $F$, $G$, $H$,<br />
which two and two have the same ratio, viz.:
<p class="assertion">as $A$ is to $B$, so is $E$ to $F$,</p>
<p class="assertion">and as $B$ is to $C$, so is $F$ to $G$,</p>
<p class="assertion">and as $C$ is to $D$, so is $G$ to $H$;</p>
$A$ shall be to $D$ as $E$ to $H$.

![Simson's Four Magnitudes Table](0186.g2.svg)

Because $A$, $B$, $C$ are three magnitudes, and $E$, $F$, $G$ other three, which<br />
taken two and two have the same ratio,<br />
by the foregoing case,
<p class="assertion">$A$ is to $C$ as $E$ to $G$.</p>
