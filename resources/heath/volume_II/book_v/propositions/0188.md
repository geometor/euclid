But, as $B$ is to $D$, so is $C$ to $E$;<br />
<p class="assertion">therefore also, as $H$ is to $K$, so is $C$ to $E$. <span class="justification">[V. 11]</span></p>

Again, since $L$, $M$ are equimultiples of $C$, $E$,<br />
<p class="assertion">therefore, as $C$ is to $E$, so is $L$ to $M$. <span class="justification">[V. 15]</span></p>

But, as $C$ is to $E$, so is $H$ to $K$;<br />
<p class="assertion">therefore also, as $H$ is to $K$, so is $L$ to $M$, <span class="justification">[V. 11]</span><br />
and, alternately, as $H$ is to $L$, so is $K$ to $M$. <span class="justification">[V. 16]</span></p>

But it was also proved that,<br />
<p class="assertion">as $G$ is to $H$, so is $M$ to $N$.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">Since, then, there are three magnitudes $G$, $H$, $L$, and<br />
others equal to them in multitude $K$, $M$, $N$, which taken two<br />
and two together are in the same ratio,<br />
and the proportion of them is perturbed,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore, <em>ex aequali</em>, if $G$ is in excess of $L$, $K$ is also in excess<br />
of $N$;<br />
if equal, equal; and if less, less. <span class="justification">[V. 21]</span></p>

And $G$, $K$ are equimultiples of $A$, $D$,<br />
and $L$, $N$ of $C$, $F$.

<p class="assertion">Therefore, as $A$ is to $C$, so is $D$ to $F$.</p>

<p class="qed">Therefore etc.<span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

There is an important difference between the version given by Simson of<br />
one part of the proof of this proposition and that found in the Greek text of<br />
Heiberg. Peyrard's ms. has the version given by Heiberg, but Simson's<br />
version has the authority of other mss. The Basel *editio princeps* gives both<br />
versions (Simson's being the first). After it has been proved by means of<br />
v. 15 and v. 11 that,
<p class="assertion">as $G$ is to $H$, so is $M$ to $N$,</p>
or, with the notation used in the note on Prop. 20,
<p class="assertion">$ma : mb = ne : nf$,</p>
it has to be proved further that,
<p class="assertion">as $H$ is to $L$, so is $K$ to $M$,</p>
or
<p class="assertion">$mb : nc = md : ne$,</p>
and it is clear that the latter result may be directly inferred from v. 4. The<br />
reading translated by Simson makes this inference:

"And because, as $B$ is to $C$, so is $D$ to $E$,<br />
and $H$, $K$ are equimultiples of $B$, $D$,<br />
and $L$, $M$ of $C$, $E$,<br />
<p class="assertion">therefore, as $H$ is to $L$, so is $K$ to $M$." <span class="justification">[V. 4]</span></p>
