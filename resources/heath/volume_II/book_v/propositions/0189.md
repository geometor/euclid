### PROPOSITION 24.

<p class="enunciation">If a first magnitude have to a second the same ratio as a<br />
third has to a fourth, and also a fifth have to the second the<br />
same ratio as a sixth to the fourth, the first and fifth added<br />
together will have to the second the same ratio as the third and<br />
sixth have to the fourth.</p>

Let a first magnitude <span data-ref="0189.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span> have to a second <span data-ref="0189.g2:C">$C$</span> the same ratio as a third <span data-ref="0189.g2:/ D E /">$DE$</span> has to a fourth <span data-ref="0189.g2:F">$F$</span>;
and let also a fifth <span data-ref="0189.g2:/ B G /">$BG$</span> have to the second <span data-ref="0189.g2:C">$C$</span> the same ratio as a sixth <span data-ref="0189.g2:/ E H /">$EH$</span> has to the fourth <span data-ref="0189.g2:F">$F$</span>;
I say that the first and fifth added together, <span data-ref="0189.g2:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, will have to the second <span data-ref="0189.g2:C">$C$</span> the same ratio as the third and sixth, <span data-ref="0189.g2:/ D H /">$DH$</span>, has to the fourth <span data-ref="0189.g2:F">$F$</span>.
