Simson rightly observes that in the last step of the proof it should be<br />

He also gives the extension of the proposition to any number of magnitudes,<br />
enunciating it thus:

"If there be any number of magnitudes, and as many others, which, taken<br />
two and two, in a cross order, have the same ratio; the first shall have to the<br />
last of the first magnitudes the same ratio which the first of the others has to<br />
the last";<br />
and adding to the proof as follows:

"Next, let there be four magnitudes $A$, $B$, $C$, $D$, and other four $E$, $F$, $G$, $H$,<br />
which, taken two and two in a cross order, have the same ratio, viz.:
$$\begin{aligned}
A \text{ to } B & \text{ as } G \text{ to } H\text{,} \\
B \text{ to } C & \text{ as } F \text{ to } G\text{,} \\
\text{and } \quad C \text{ to } D & \text{ as } E \text{ to } F\text{;}
\end{aligned}$$
then $A$ is to $D$ as $E$ to $H$.

Because $A$, $B$, $C$ are three magnitudes, and $F$, $G$, $H$ other three which,<br />
taken two and two in a cross order, have the same ratio,<br />
by the first case,<br />
<p class="assertion">$A$ is to $C$ as $F$ to $H$.</p>

But<br />
<p class="assertion">$C$ is to $D$ as $E$ to $F$;</p>

wherefore again, by the first case,<br />
<p class="assertion">$A$ is to $D$ as $E$ to $H$.</p>

And so on, whatever be the number of magnitudes."

### PROPOSITION 24.

<p class="enunciation">If a first magnitude have to a second the same ratio as a<br />
third has to a fourth, and also a fifth have to the second the<br />
same ratio as a sixth to the fourth, the first and fifth added<br />
together will have to the second the same ratio as the third and<br />
sixth have to the fourth.</p>

Let a first magnitude $AB$ have to a second $C$ the same<br />
ratio as a third $DE$ has to a<br />
fourth $F$;<br />
and let also a fifth $BG$ have to<br />
the second $C$ the same ratio as<br />
a sixth $EH$ has to the fourth<br />
$F$;<br />
I say that the first and fifth added together, $AG$, will have<br />
to the second $C$ the same ratio as the third and sixth, $DH$,<br />
has to the fourth $F$.
