### PROPOSITION 25.

<p class="enunciation">If four magnitudes be proportional, the greatest and the<br />
least are greater than the remaining two.</p>

Let the four magnitudes $AB$, $CD$, $E$, $F$ be proportional<br />
so that, as $AB$ is to $CD$, so is $E$ to<br />
$F$, and let $AB$ be the greatest of them<br />
and $F$ the least;<br />
I say that $AB$, $F$ are greater than<br />
$CD$, $E$.

For let $AG$ be made equal to $E$,<br />
and $CH$ equal to $F$.

![Book V, Proposition 25, Line Segments](0191.g1.svg)

Since, as $AB$ is to $CD$, so is $E$<br />
to $F$,<br />
and $E$ is equal to $AG$, and $F$ to $CH$,<br />
<p class="assertion">therefore, as $AB$ is to $CD$, so is $AG$ to $CH$.</p>

And since, as the whole $AB$ is to the whole $CD$, so is<br />
the part $AG$ subtracted to the part $CH$ subtracted,<br />
<p class="assertion">the remainder $GB$ will also be to the remainder $HD$ as<br />
the whole $AB$ is to the whole $CD$. <span class="justification">[V. 19]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But $AB$ is greater than $CD$;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore $GB$ is also greater than $HD$.</p>

And, since $AG$ is equal to $E$, and $CH$ to $F$,<br />
<p class="assertion">therefore $AG$, $F$ are equal to $CH$, $E$.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">And if, $GB$, $HD$ being unequal, and $GB$ greater, $AG$, $F$<br />
be added to $GB$ and $CH$, $E$ be added to $HD$,</p>
<p class="assertion">it follows that $AB$, $F$ are greater than $CD$, $E$.</p>

<p class="qed">Therefore etc.<span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

Algebraically, if
$$a : b = c : d\text{,}$$
and $a$ is the greatest of the four magnitudes and $d$ the least,
$$a + d > b + c.$$

Simson is right in inserting a word in the setting-out, "let $AB$ be the<br />
greatest of them and *&lt;consequently&gt;* $F$ the least." This follows from the<br />
particular case, really included in Def. 5, which Simson makes the subject of<br />
his proposition A, the case namely where the equimultiples taken are *once* the<br />
several magnitudes.

The proof is as follows.<br />
Since
$$a : b = c : d\text{,}$$
$$a - c : b - d = a : b. \qquad [\text{V. } 19]$$
