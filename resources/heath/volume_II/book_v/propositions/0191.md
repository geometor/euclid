### PROPOSITION 25.

<p class="enunciation">If four magnitudes be proportional, the greatest and the<br />
least are greater than the remaining two.</p>

Let the four magnitudes <span data-ref="0191.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0191.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, <span data-ref="0191.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0191.g1:F">$F$</span> be proportional so that, as <span data-ref="0191.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0191.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0191.g1:E">$E$</span> to <span data-ref="0191.g1:F">$F$</span>, and let <span data-ref="0191.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> be the greatest of them and <span data-ref="0191.g1:F">$F$</span> the least;
I say that <span data-ref="0191.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0191.g1:F">$F$</span> are greater than <span data-ref="0191.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, <span data-ref="0191.g1:E">$E$</span>.

![Book V, Proposition 25, Line Segments](0191.g1.svg)

For let <span data-ref="0191.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span> be made equal to <span data-ref="0191.g1:E">$E$</span>, and <span data-ref="0191.g1:/ C H /">$CH$</span> equal to <span data-ref="0191.g1:F">$F$</span>.

Since, as <span data-ref="0191.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0191.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0191.g1:E">$E$</span> to <span data-ref="0191.g1:F">$F$</span>,
and <span data-ref="0191.g1:E">$E$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0191.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, and <span data-ref="0191.g1:F">$F$</span> to <span data-ref="0191.g1:/ C H /">$CH$</span>,
therefore, as <span data-ref="0191.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0191.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0191.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span> to <span data-ref="0191.g1:/ C H /">$CH$</span>.

And since, as the whole <span data-ref="0191.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is to the whole <span data-ref="0191.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, so is the part <span data-ref="0191.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span> subtracted to the part <span data-ref="0191.g1:/ C H /">$CH$</span> subtracted,
the remainder <span data-ref="0191.g1:/ G B /">$GB$</span> will also be to the remainder <span data-ref="0191.g1:/ H D /">$HD$</span> as the whole <span data-ref="0191.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is to the whole <span data-ref="0191.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>. [v. 19]

But <span data-ref="0191.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is greater than <span data-ref="0191.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>;
therefore <span data-ref="0191.g1:/ G B /">$GB$</span> is also greater than <span data-ref="0191.g1:/ H D /">$HD$</span>.

And, since <span data-ref="0191.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0191.g1:E">$E$</span>, and <span data-ref="0191.g1:/ C H /">$CH$</span> to <span data-ref="0191.g1:F">$F$</span>,
therefore <span data-ref="0191.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0191.g1:F">$F$</span> are equal to <span data-ref="0191.g1:/ C H /">$CH$</span>, <span data-ref="0191.g1:E">$E$</span>.

And if, <span data-ref="0191.g1:/ G B /">$GB$</span>, <span data-ref="0191.g1:/ H D /">$HD$</span> being unequal, and <span data-ref="0191.g1:/ G B /">$GB$</span> greater, <span data-ref="0191.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0191.g1:F">$F$</span> be added to <span data-ref="0191.g1:/ G B /">$GB$</span> and <span data-ref="0191.g1:/ C H /">$CH$</span>, <span data-ref="0191.g1:E">$E$</span> be added to <span data-ref="0191.g1:/ H D /">$HD$</span>,
it follows that <span data-ref="0191.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0191.g1:F">$F$</span> are greater than <span data-ref="0191.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, <span data-ref="0191.g1:E">$E$</span>.

Therefore etc.

Q. E. D.

---

Algebraically, if
$$a : b = c : d\text{,}$$
and $a$ is the greatest of the four magnitudes and $d$ the least,
$$a + d > b + c.$$

Simson is right in inserting a word in the setting-out, "let <span data-ref="0191.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> be the greatest of them and *<consequently>* <span data-ref="0191.g1:F">$F$</span> the least." This follows from the particular case, really included in Def. 5, which Simson makes the subject of his proposition A, the case namely where the equimultiples taken are once the several magnitudes.

The proof is as follows.<br />
Since
$$a : b = c : d\text{,}$$
$$a - c : b - d = a : b. \qquad [\text{V. } 19]$$
