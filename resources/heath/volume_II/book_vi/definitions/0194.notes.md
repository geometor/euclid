## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Greek Text of Definition 1**: `Ὅμοια σχήματά ἐστιν εὐθύγραμμα, ὅσα τάς τε γωνίας ἴσας ἔχει κατὰ μίαν καὶ τὰς περὶ τὰς ἴσας γωνίας πλευρὰς ἀνάλογον.`
- **Aristotle Citation**: Aristotle (*Anal. post.* II. 17, 99 a 13) quotes the definition using `ἴσως` ("let us say" or "perhaps"), which Heiberg interprets as evidence that the definition was still fluid or newly standardized in Aristotle's era.
- **Van Swinden & Jacobi Critique**: Van Swinden notes the omission of cyclic ordering: corresponding sides must be opposite equal angles so that their orientation and cyclic order match.
- **Minimal Conditions for Similarity**: Heath outlines the redundancy in Euclid's definition, specifying that similarity of polygons can be established while omitting:
  1. Three consecutive angles,
  2. Two consecutive angles and the common side,
  3. Two consecutive sides and the included angle.
- **Austin's Objection**: Austin's objection regarding the co-existence of angle equality and side proportionality is dismissed by Heath, noting that existence is demonstrated constructively later in VI. 18.
