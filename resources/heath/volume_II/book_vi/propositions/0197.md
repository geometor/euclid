# BOOK VI. PROPOSITIONS.

### PROPOSITION 1.

<p class="enunciation">Triangles and parallelograms which are under the same<br />
height are to one another as their bases.</p>

Let $ABC$, $ACD$ be triangles and $EC$, $CF$ parallelograms  
under the same height;  
<sup>5</sup> I say that, as the base $BC$ is to the base $CD$, so is the  
triangle $ABC$ to the triangle $ACD$, and the parallelogram  
$EC$ to the parallelogram $CF$.

![Book VI Proposition 1](0197.g1.svg)

For let $BD$ be produced in both directions to the points  
$H$, $L$ and let [any number of straight lines] $BG$, $GH$ be  
<sup>10</sup> made equal to the base $BC$, and any number of straight lines  
$DK$, $KL$ equal to the base $CD$;  
let $AG$, $AH$, $AK$, $AL$ be joined.

Then, since $CB$, $BG$, $GH$ are equal to one another,
<p class="assertion">the triangles $ABC$, $AGB$, $AHG$ are also equal to one<br />
<sup>15</sup> another. <span class="justification">[I. 38]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore, whatever multiple the base $HC$ is of the base<br />
$BC$, that multiple also is the triangle $AHC$ of the triangle<br />
$ABC$.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">For the same reason,</p>
<p class="assertion"><sup>20</sup> whatever multiple the base $LC$ is of the base $CD$, that<br />
multiple also is the triangle $ALC$ of the triangle $ACD$;</p>
<p class="assertion">and, if the base $HC$ is equal to the base $CL$, the triangle<br />
$AHC$ is also equal to the triangle $ACL$, <span class="justification">[I. 38]</span></p>
