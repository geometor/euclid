### PROPOSITION 2.

<p class="enunciation">If a straight line be drawn parallel to one of the sides of a<br />
triangle, it will cut the sides of the triangle proportionally;<br />
and, if the sides of the triangle be cut proportionally, the line<br />
joining the points of section will be parallel to the remaining<br />
side of the triangle.</p>

For let $DE$ be drawn parallel to $BC$, one of the sides of  
the triangle $ABC$;  
I say that, as $BD$ is to $DA$, so is $CE$ to  
$EA$.

![Book VI Proposition 2](0200.g1.svg)

For let $BE$, $CD$ be joined.

<p class="assertion">Therefore the triangle $BDE$ is equal to<br />
the triangle $CDE$;</p>
for they are on the same base $DE$ and in  
the same parallels $DE$, $BC$. <span class="justification">[I. 38]</span>

And the triangle $ADE$ is another area.

But equals have the same ratio to the same; <span class="justification">[V. 7]</span>
<p class="assertion">therefore, as the triangle $BDE$ is to the triangle $ADE$, so<br />
is the triangle $CDE$ to the triangle $ADE$.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">But, as the triangle $BDE$ is to $ADE$, so is $BD$ to $DA$;<br />
for, being under the same height, the perpendicular drawn<br />
from $E$ to $AB$, they are to one another as their bases. <span class="justification">[VI. 1]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">For the same reason also,<br />
as the triangle $CDE$ is to $ADE$, so is $CE$ to $EA$.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore also, as $BD$ is to $DA$, so is $CE$ to $EA$. <span class="justification">[V. 11]</span></p>
