<p class="enunciation">remaining sides of the triangle, the straight line joined from<br />
the vertex to the point of section will bisect the angle of the<br />
triangle.</p>

Let *ABC* be a triangle, and let the angle *BAC* be bisected  
by the straight line *AD*;

I say that, as *BD* is to *CD*, so  
is *BA* to *AC*.

![Proposition 3](0202.g1.svg)

For let *CE* be drawn through  
*C* parallel to *DA*, and let *BA*  
be carried through and meet it  
at *E*.

Then, since the straight line  
*AC* falls upon the parallels *AD*,  
*EC*,

<p class="assertion">the angle *ACE* is equal to the angle *CAD*. <span class="justification">[I. 29]</span></p>

But the angle *CAD* is by hypothesis equal to the angle  
*BAD*;

<p class="assertion">therefore the angle *BAD* is also equal to the angle *ACE*.</p>

Again, since the straight line *BAE* falls upon the parallels  
*AD*, *EC*,

<p class="assertion">the exterior angle *BAD* is equal to the interior angle<br />
*AEC*. <span class="justification">[I. 29]</span></p>

But the angle *ACE* was also proved equal to the angle  
*BAD*;

<p class="assertion">therefore the angle *ACE* is also equal to the angle *AEC*,</p>

<p class="assertion">so that the side *AE* is also equal to the side *AC*. <span class="justification">[I. 6]</span></p>

And, since *AD* has been drawn parallel to *EC*, one of  
the sides of the triangle *BCE*,

<p class="assertion">therefore, proportionally, as *BD* is to *DC*, so is *BA* to *AE*.<br />
<span class="justification">[VI. 2]</span></p>

But *AE* is equal to *AC*;

<p class="assertion">therefore, as *BD* is to *DC*, so is *BA* to *AC*.</p>

Again, let *BA* be to *AC* as *BD* to *DC*, and let *AD* be  
joined;

I say that the angle *BAC* has been bisected by the straight  
line *AD*.
