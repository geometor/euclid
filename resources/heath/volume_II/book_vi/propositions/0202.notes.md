## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Proposition 3 (Angle Bisector Theorem)**:
  - Direct part: Triangle $ABC$, angle $BAC$ bisected by $AD$. Construction: $CE \parallel DA$ meeting $BA$ produced at $E$.
  - By alternate angles on parallels $AD, EC$, $\angle ACE = \angle CAD$ [I. 29].
  - By corresponding angles, exterior $\angle BAD = \angle AEC$ [I. 29].
  - Since $\angle CAD = \angle BAD$, $\angle ACE = \angle AEC$, whence $AE = AC$ [I. 6].
  - By VI. 2 in $\triangle BCE$, $BD : DC = BA : AE = BA : AC$.
  - Converse setup: Given $BA : AC = BD : DC$, prove $\angle BAD = \angle CAD$. Folio leaves off at bottom: "since, as *BD* is to *DC*, so is *BA* to *AC*,".
