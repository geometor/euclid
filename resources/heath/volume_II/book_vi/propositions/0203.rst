The demonstration assumes that :g:`CE <0202.g1:/ C E />` will meet :g:`BA <0202.g1:/ B A />` produced in some point :g:`E <0202.g1:E>`. This is proved in the same way as it is proved in **VI. 4** that :g:`BA <0207.g1:/ B A />`, :g:`ED <0207.g1:/ E D />` will meet if produced. The angles :g:`ABD <0202.g1:< A B D )>`, :g:`BDA <0202.g1:< B D A )>` in the figure of **VI. 3** are together less than two right angles, and the angle :g:`BDA <0202.g1:< B D A )>` is :eq:`equal <0202.g1:< B D A ) = 0202.g1:< B C E )>` to the angle :g:`BCE <0202.g1:< B C E )>`, since :g:`DA <0202.g1:/ D A />`, :g:`CE <0202.g1:/ C E />` are parallel. Therefore the angles :g:`ABC <0202.g1:< A B C )>`, :g:`BCE <0202.g1:< B C E )>` are together less than two right angles; and :g:`BA <0202.g1:/ B A />`, :g:`CE <0202.g1:/ C E />` must meet, by **I. Post. 5**.

The corresponding proposition about the segments into which :g:`BC <0203.g1:/ B C />` is divided *externally* by the bisector of the *external* angle at :g:`A <0203.g1:A>` when that bisector meets :g:`BC <0203.g1:/ B C />` produced (i.e. when the sides :g:`AB <0203.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`AC <0203.g1:/ A C />` are not :eq:`equal <0203.g1:/ A B / = 0203.g1:/ A C />`) is important. :person:`Simson <simson>` gives it as a separate proposition, A, noting the fact that :person:`Pappus <pappus>` assumes the result without proof (:person:`Pappus <pappus>`, **VII.** p. 730, 24).

The best plan is however, as :person:`De Morgan <de-morgan>` says, to combine Props. 3 and A in one proposition, which may be enunciated thus: *If an angle of a triangle be bisected internally or externally by a straight line which cuts the opposite side or the opposite side produced, the segments of that side will have the same ratio as the other sides of the triangle; and, if a side of a triangle be divided internally or externally so that its segments have the same ratio as the other sides of the triangle, the straight line drawn from the point of section to the angular point which is opposite to the first mentioned side will bisect the interior or exterior angle at that angular point.*
