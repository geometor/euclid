| :g:`$BA$ <0204.g1:/ B A />` to :g:`$AC$ <0204.g1:/ A C />`.

.. assertion::

   Therefore :g:`$BE$ <0204.g1:/ B E />` is to :g:`$EC$ <0204.g1:/ E C />` as :g:`$BD$ <0204.g1:/ B D />` to :g:`$DC$ <0204.g1:/ D C />`,

| that is, :g:`$BE$ <0204.g1:/ B E />` is to :g:`$EC$ <0204.g1:/ E C />` as the difference between :g:`$BE$ <0204.g1:/ B E />` and :g:`$ED$ <0204.g1:/ E D />` is to the
| difference between :g:`$ED$ <0204.g1:/ E D />` and :g:`$EC$ <0204.g1:/ E C />`,
| whence :g:`$BE$ <0204.g1:/ B E />`, :g:`$ED$ <0204.g1:/ E D />`, :g:`$EC$ <0204.g1:/ E C />` are in *harmonic progression*, or :g:`$DE$ <0204.g1:/ D E />` is a *harmonic mean*
| between :g:`$BE$ <0204.g1:/ B E />` and :g:`$EC$ <0204.g1:/ E C />`, or again :g:`$B$ <0204.g1:B>`, :g:`$D$ <0204.g1:D>`, :g:`$C$ <0204.g1:C>`, :g:`$E$ <0204.g1:E>` is a *harmonic range*.

| Since the angle :g:`$DAC$ <0204.g1:< D A C )>` is half of the angle :g:`$BAC$ <0204.g1:< B A C )>`,
| and the angle :g:`$CAE$ <0204.g1:< C A E )>` half of the angle :g:`$CAF$ <0204.g1:< C A F )>`,

   the angle :g:`$DAE$ <0204.g1:< D A E )>` is a right angle.

Hence the circle described on :g:`$DE$ <0204.g1:/ D E />` as diameter passes through :g:`$A$ <0204.g1:A>`.

| Now, if the ratio of :g:`$BA$ <0204.g1:/ B A />` to :g:`$AC$ <0204.g1:/ A C />` is given, and if :g:`$BC$ <0204.g1:/ B C />` is given, the points
| :g:`$D$ <0204.g1:D>`, :g:`$E$ <0204.g1:E>` on :g:`$BC$ <0204.g1:/ B C />` and :g:`$BC$ <0204.g1:/ B C />` produced are given, and therefore so is the circle on
| :g:`$D$ <0204.g1:D>`, :g:`$E$ <0204.g1:E>` as diameter. Hence *the locus of a point such that its distances from two
| given points are in a given ratio (not being a ratio of equality) is a circle*.

| This locus was discussed by :person:`Apollonius <apollonius>` in his :cite:`*Plane Loci* <apollonius:plane-loci>`, Book **II.**, as we
| know from :person:`Pappus <pappus>` (**VII.** p. 666), who says that the book contained the
| theorem that, if from two given points straight lines inflected to another
| point are in a given ratio, the point in which they meet will lie on either a
| straight line or a circumference of a circle. The straight line is of course the
| locus when the ratio is one of equality. The other case is quoted in the
| following form by :person:`Eutocius <eutocius>` (:person:`Apollonius <apollonius>`, ed. :person:`Heiberg <heiberg>`, **II.** pp. 180—4).

| *Given two points in a plane and a proportion between unequal straight lines,
| it is possible to describe a circle in the plane so that the straight lines inflected
| from the given points to the circumference of the circle shall have a ratio the
| same as the given one.*
