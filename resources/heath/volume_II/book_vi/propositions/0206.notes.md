# Transcription Notes: Folio 0206 (Page 200)

## Summary & Folio Continuity
- **Folio Range**: Concludes the commentary on Proposition 3 (synthesis and proof of the Apollonius circle by Apollonius/Eutocius and Aristotle) and introduces Proposition 4 with its enunciation.
- **Preceding Context**: Folio 0205 (Page 199) sets up the algebraic relations $(\alpha), (\beta), (\delta)$ and the geometric analysis.
- **Succeeding Context**: Folio 0207 (Page 201) gives the proof of Proposition 4.

## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Apollonius vs. Aristotle on the Locus**:
  - Both establish $r^2 = BO \cdot CO$ and similarity of $\triangle BOP \sim \triangle POC$.
  - Apollonius draws $CL \parallel BP$ to create similar triangles $\triangle BPC \sim \triangle PCL$, showing $BP^2 : PC^2 = BP : CL = BO : OC = BO^2 : OP^2$, whence $BP : PC = BO : OP = h : k$.
  - Aristotle deduces $BP : PC = BO : OP = h : k$ directly from $\triangle POB \sim \triangle COP$.
  - Uniqueness: Apollonius concludes with a *reductio ad absurdum* showing that no point outside or inside the circle satisfies the ratio.
- **Proposition 4 Enunciation**: Heath translates the foundational theorem of similar triangles: equiangular triangles have proportional sides, with corresponding sides subtending equal angles.
