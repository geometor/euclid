Let *ABC*, *DCE* be equiangular triangles having the<br />
angle *ABC* equal to the angle<br />
*DCE*, the angle *BAC* to the<br />
angle *CDE*, and further the angle<br />
*ACB* to the angle *CED*;

I say that in the triangles *ABC*,<br />
*DCE* the sides about the equal<br />
angles are proportional, and those<br />
are corresponding sides which<br />
subtend the equal angles.

![Proposition 4](0207.g1.svg)

For let *BC* be placed in a<br />
straight line with *CE*.

Then, since the angles *ABC*, *ACB* are less than two right<br />
angles, <span class="justification">[I. 17]</span><br />
and the angle *ACB* is equal to the angle *DEC*,
<p class="assertion">therefore the angles *ABC*, *DEC* are less than two right<br />
angles;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore *BA*, *ED*, when produced, will meet. <span class="justification">[I. Post. 5]</span></p>

Let them be produced and meet at *F*.

Now, since the angle *DCE* is equal to the angle *ABC*,
<p class="assertion">*BF* is parallel to *CD*. <span class="justification">[I. 28]</span></p>

Again, since the angle *ACB* is equal to the angle *DEC*,
<p class="assertion">*AC* is parallel to *FE*. <span class="justification">[I. 28]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore *FACD* is a parallelogram;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore *FA* is equal to *DC*, and *AC* to *FD*. <span class="justification">[I. 34]</span></p>

And, since *AC* has been drawn parallel to *FE*, one side<br />
of the triangle *FBE*,
<p class="assertion">therefore, as *BA* is to *AF*, so is *BC* to *CE*. <span class="justification">[VI. 2]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But *AF* is equal to *CD*;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore, as *BA* is to *CD*, so is *BC* to *CE*,<br />
and alternately, as *AB* is to *BC*, so is *DC* to *CE*. <span class="justification">[V. 16]</span></p>

Again, since *CD* is parallel to *BF*,
<p class="assertion">therefore, as *BC* is to *CE*, so is *FD* to *DE*. <span class="justification">[VI. 2]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But *FD* is equal to *AC*;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore, as *BC* is to *CE*, so is *AC* to *DE*,<br />
and alternately, as *BC* is to *CA*, so is *CE* to *ED*. <span class="justification">[V. 16]</span></p>
