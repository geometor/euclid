Todhunter remarks that "the manner in which the two triangles are to be<br />
placed is very imperfectly described; their bases are to be in the same straight<br />
line and contiguous, their vertices are to be on the same side of the base, and<br />
each of the two angles which have a common vertex is to be equal to the<br />
remote angle of the other triangle." But surely Euclid's description is<br />
sufficient, except for not saying that <span data-ref="0207.g1:B">$B$</span> and <span data-ref="0207.g1:D">$D$</span> must be on the same side<br />
of <span data-ref="0207.g1:/ B C E /">$BCE$</span>.

VI. 4 can be immediately deduced from VI. 2 if we superpose one triangle<br />
on the other three times in succession, so that each angle successively<br />
coincides with its equal, the triangles being similarly situated, e.g. if (<span data-ref="0207.g1:< A )">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0207.g1:< B )">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0207.g1:< C )">$C$</span><br />
and <span data-ref="0209.g2:< D )">$D$</span>, <span data-ref="0209.g2:< E )">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0209.g2:< F )">$F$</span> being the equal angles respectively) we apply the angle <span data-ref="0209.g2:< D E F )">$DEF$</span> to<br />

says, "the peculiar mode of construction by which Euclid proves two cases at<br />
once; make an angle coincide with its equal, and suppose this process repeated<br />
three times, one for each angle."

### PROPOSITION 5.

<p class="enunciation">If two triangles have their sides proportional, the triangles<br />
will be equiangular and will have those angles equal which the<br />
corresponding sides subtend.</p>

Let <span data-ref="0209.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span>, <span data-ref="0209.g2:< D E F >">$DEF$</span> be two triangles having their sides<br />
proportional, so that,
<p class="assertion">as <span data-ref="0209.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0209.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0209.g2:/ D E /">$DE$</span> to <span data-ref="0209.g2:/ E F /">$EF$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">as <span data-ref="0209.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is to <span data-ref="0209.g1:/ C A /">$CA$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0209.g2:/ E F /">$EF$</span> to <span data-ref="0209.g2:/ F D /">$FD$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">and further, as <span data-ref="0209.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span> is to <span data-ref="0209.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0209.g2:/ E D /">$ED$</span> to <span data-ref="0209.g2:/ D F /">$DF$</span>;</p>
I say that the triangle <span data-ref="0209.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> is equiangular with the triangle<br />
<span data-ref="0209.g2:< D E F >">$DEF$</span>, and they will have those angles equal which the corre-<br />
sponding sides subtend, namely the angle <span data-ref="0209.g1:< A B C )">$ABC$</span> to the angle<br />
<span data-ref="0209.g2:< D E F )">$DEF$</span>, the angle <span data-ref="0209.g1:< B C A )">$BCA$</span> to the angle <span data-ref="0209.g2:< E F D )">$EFD$</span>, and further the<br />
angle <span data-ref="0209.g1:< B A C )">$BAC$</span> to the angle <span data-ref="0209.g2:< E D F )">$EDF$</span>.
