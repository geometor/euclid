   | and, as :g:`$BC$ <0207.g1:/ B C />` is to :g:`$CA$ <0207.g1:/ C A />`, so is :g:`$CE$ <0207.g1:/ C E />` to :g:`$ED$ <0207.g1:/ E D />`;

.. assertion:: [V. 22] <V.22>

   therefore, :term:`*ex aequali* <ex-aequali>`, as :g:`$BA$ <0207.g1:/ B A />` is to :g:`$AC$ <0207.g1:/ A C />`, so is :g:`$CD$ <0207.g1:/ C D />` to :g:`$DE$ <0207.g1:/ D E />`.

| :person:`Todhunter <todhunter>` remarks that "the manner in which the two triangles are to be
| placed is very imperfectly described; their bases are to be in the same straight
| line and contiguous, their vertices are to be on the same side of the base, and
| each of the two angles which have a common vertex is to be equal to the
| remote angle of the other triangle." But surely Euclid's description is
| sufficient, except for not saying that :g:`$B$ <0207.g1:B>` and :g:`$D$ <0207.g1:D>` must be on the same side
| of :g:`$BCE$ <0207.g1:/ B C E />`.

| VI. 4 can be immediately deduced from VI. 2 if we superpose one triangle
| on the other three times in succession, so that each angle successively
| coincides with its equal, the triangles being similarly situated, e.g. if (:g:`$A$ <0207.g1:< A )>`, :g:`$B$ <0207.g1:< B )>`, :g:`$C$ <0207.g1:< C )>`
| and :g:`$D$ <0209.g2:< D )>`, :g:`$E$ <0209.g2:< E )>`, :g:`$F$ <0209.g2:< F )>` being the equal angles respectively) we apply the angle :g:`$DEF$ <0209.g2:< D E F )>` to
| the angle :g:`$ABC$ <0207.g1:< A B C )>` so that :g:`$D$ <0209.g2:D>` lies on :g:`$AB$ <0207.g1:/ A B />` (produced if necessary) and :g:`$F$ <0209.g2:F>` on :g:`$BC$ <0207.g1:/ B C />`
| (produced if necessary). :person:`De Morgan <de-morgan>` prefers this method. "Abandon," he
| says, "the peculiar mode of construction by which Euclid proves two cases at
| once; make an angle coincide with its equal, and suppose this process repeated
| three times, one for each angle."

PROPOSITION 5.
--------------

.. enunciation::

   If two triangles have their sides proportional, the triangles will be equiangular and will have those angles equal which the corresponding sides subtend.

| Let :g:`$ABC$ <0209.g1:< A B C >>`, :g:`$DEF$ <0209.g2:< D E F >>` be two triangles having their sides
| proportional, so that,

.. assertion::

   as :g:`$AB$ <0209.g1:/ A B />` is to :g:`$BC$ <0209.g1:/ B C />`, so is :g:`$DE$ <0209.g2:/ D E />` to :g:`$EF$ <0209.g2:/ E F />`,

.. assertion::

   as :g:`$BC$ <0209.g1:/ B C />` is to :g:`$CA$ <0209.g1:/ C A />`, so is :g:`$EF$ <0209.g2:/ E F />` to :g:`$FD$ <0209.g2:/ F D />`,

.. assertion::

   and further, as :g:`$BA$ <0209.g1:/ B A />` is to :g:`$AC$ <0209.g1:/ A C />`, so is :g:`$ED$ <0209.g2:/ E D />` to :g:`$DF$ <0209.g2:/ D F />`;

| I say that the triangle :g:`$ABC$ <0209.g1:< A B C >>` is equiangular with the triangle
| :g:`$DEF$ <0209.g2:< D E F >>`, and they will have those angles equal which the corre-
| sponding sides subtend, namely the angle :g:`$ABC$ <0209.g1:< A B C )>` to the angle
| :g:`$DEF$ <0209.g2:< D E F )>`, the angle :g:`$BCA$ <0209.g1:< B C A )>` to the angle :g:`$EFD$ <0209.g2:< E F D )>`, and further the
| angle :g:`$BAC$ <0209.g1:< B A C )>` to the angle :g:`$EDF$ <0209.g2:< E D F )>`.

| For on the straight line :g:`$EF$ <0209.g2:/ E F />`, and at the points :g:`$E$ <0209.g2:E>`, :g:`$F$ <0209.g2:F>` on
| it, let there be constructed the angle :g:`$FEG$ <0209.g2:< F E G )>` equal to the angle
| :g:`$ABC$ <0209.g1:< A B C )>`, and the angle :g:`$EFG$ <0209.g2:< E F G )>` equal to the angle :g:`$ACB$ <0209.g1:< A C B )>`; :just:`[I. 23] <I.23>`
