<p class="assertion">therefore the remaining angle at $A$ is equal to the remaining<br />
angle at $G$. <span class="justification">[I. 32]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore the triangle $ABC$ is equiangular with the<br />
triangle $GEF$.</p>

![Proposition 5 diagram](0209.g1.svg)

Therefore in the triangles $ABC, GEF$ the sides about<br />
the equal angles are proportional, and those are corresponding<br />
sides which subtend the equal angles; <span class="justification">[VI. 4]</span>
<p class="assertion">therefore, as $AB$ is to $BC$, so is $GE$ to $EF$.</p>

But, as $AB$ is to $BC$, so by hypothesis is $DE$ to $EF$;
<p class="assertion">therefore, as $DE$ is to $EF$, so is $GE$ to $EF$. <span class="justification">[V. 11]</span></p>

Therefore each of the straight lines $DE, GE$ has the<br />
same ratio to $EF$;
<p class="assertion">therefore $DE$ is equal to $GE$. <span class="justification">[V. 9]</span></p>

For the same reason
<p class="assertion">$DF$ is also equal to $GF$.</p>

Since then $DE$ is equal to $EG$,<br />
and $EF$ is common,
<p class="sub-clause">the two sides $DE, EF$ are equal to the two sides $GE, EF$;</p>
<p class="sub-clause">and the base $DF$ is equal to the base $FG$;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the angle $DEF$ is equal to the angle $GEF$, <span class="justification">[I. 8]</span></p>
<p class="sub-clause">and the triangle $DEF$ is equal to the triangle $GEF$,<br />
and the remaining angles are equal to the remaining angles,<br />
namely those which the equal sides subtend. <span class="justification">[I. 4]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore the angle $DFE$ is also equal to the angle $GFE$,<br />
and the angle $EDF$ to the angle $EGF$.</p>

And, since the angle $FED$ is equal to the angle $GEF$,
<p class="sub-clause">while the angle $GEF$ is equal to the angle $ABC$,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the angle $ABC$ is also equal to the angle $DEF$.</p>
