This proposition is the complete converse, VI. 6 a partial converse, of VI. 4.

Todhunter, after Walker, remarks that the enunciation should make it
clear that the sides of the triangles *taken in order* are proportional. It is quite
possible that there should be two triangles <span data-ref="0209.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span>, <span data-ref="0209.g2:< D E F >">$DEF$</span> such that
<p class="assertion"><span data-ref="0209.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0209.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> as <span data-ref="0209.g2:/ D E /">$DE$</span> to <span data-ref="0209.g2:/ E F /">$EF$</span>,<br />
and <span data-ref="0209.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is to <span data-ref="0209.g1:/ C A /">$CA$</span> as <span data-ref="0209.g2:/ D F /">$DF$</span> to <span data-ref="0209.g2:/ E D /">$ED$</span> (instead of <span data-ref="0209.g2:/ E F /">$EF$</span> to <span data-ref="0209.g2:/ F D /">$FD$</span>),<br />
so that <span data-ref="0209.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0209.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> as <span data-ref="0209.g2:/ D F /">$DF$</span> to <span data-ref="0209.g2:/ E F /">$EF$</span><br />
(*ex aequali in perturbed proportion*);</p>
in this case the sides of the triangles are proportional, but not in the same
order, and the triangles are not necessarily equiangular to one another. For a
numerical illustration we may suppose the sides of one triangle to be 3, 4 and
5 feet respectively, and those of another to be 12, 15 and 20 feet respectively.

In VI. 5 there is the same *apparent* avoidance of indirect demonstration
which has been noticed on I. 48.

### PROPOSITION 6.

<p class="enunciation">If two triangles have one angle equal to one angle and the<br />
sides about the equal angles proportional, the triangles will be<br />
equiangular and will have those angles equal which the corre-<br />
sponding sides subtend.</p>

Let <span data-ref="0211.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span>, <span data-ref="0211.g1:< D E F >">$DEF$</span> be two triangles having one angle <span data-ref="0211.g1:< B A C )">$BAC$</span>
equal to one angle <span data-ref="0211.g1:< E D F )">$EDF$</span> and the sides about the equal angles
proportional, so that,
<p class="assertion">as <span data-ref="0211.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span> is to <span data-ref="0211.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0211.g1:/ E D /">$ED$</span> to <span data-ref="0211.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span>;</p>
I say that the triangle <span data-ref="0211.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> is equiangular with the triangle
<span data-ref="0211.g1:< D E F >">$DEF$</span>, and will have the angle <span data-ref="0211.g1:< A B C )">$ABC$</span> equal to the angle <span data-ref="0211.g1:< D E F )">$DEF$</span>,
and the angle <span data-ref="0211.g1:< A C B )">$ACB$</span> to the angle <span data-ref="0211.g1:< D F E )">$DFE$</span>.

For on the straight line <span data-ref="0211.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span>, and at the points <span data-ref="0211.g1:D">$D$</span>, <span data-ref="0211.g1:F">$F$</span> on it,
let there be constructed the angle <span data-ref="0211.g1:< F D G )">$FDG$</span> equal to either of the
angles <span data-ref="0211.g1:< B A C )">$BAC$</span>, <span data-ref="0211.g1:< E D F )">$EDF$</span>, and the angle <span data-ref="0211.g1:< D F G )">$DFG$</span> equal to the angle
<span data-ref="0211.g1:< A C B )">$ACB$</span>; <span class="justification">[I. 23]</span>
<p class="assertion">therefore the remaining angle at <span data-ref="0211.g1:< B )">$B$</span> is equal to the remaining<br />
angle at <span data-ref="0211.g1:< G )">$G$</span>. <span class="justification">[I. 32]</span></p>
