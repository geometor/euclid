For the same reason

.. assertion::

   | the angle :g:`$ACB$ <0209.g1:< A C B )>` is also equal to the angle :g:`$DFE$ <0209.g2:< D F E )>`,
   | and further, the angle at :g:`$A$ <0209.g1:< A )>` to the angle at :g:`$D$ <0209.g2:< D )>`;

.. assertion::

   | therefore the triangle :g:`$ABC$ <0209.g1:< A B C >>` is equiangular with the triangle
   | :g:`$DEF$ <0209.g2:< D E F >>`.

.. qed::

   Therefore etc. :just:`Q. E. D.`

----

This proposition is the complete converse, VI. 6 a partial converse, of VI. 4.

:person:`Todhunter <todhunter>`, after Walker, remarks that the enunciation should make it
clear that the sides of the triangles *taken in order* are proportional. It is quite
possible that there should be two triangles :g:`$ABC$ <0209.g1:< A B C >>`, :g:`$DEF$ <0209.g2:< D E F >>` such that

.. assertion::

   | :g:`$AB$ <0209.g1:/ A B />` is to :g:`$BC$ <0209.g1:/ B C />` as :g:`$DE$ <0209.g2:/ D E />` to :g:`$EF$ <0209.g2:/ E F />`,
   | and :g:`$BC$ <0209.g1:/ B C />` is to :g:`$CA$ <0209.g1:/ C A />` as :g:`$DF$ <0209.g2:/ D F />` to :g:`$ED$ <0209.g2:/ E D />` (instead of :g:`$EF$ <0209.g2:/ E F />` to :g:`$FD$ <0209.g2:/ F D />`),
   | so that :g:`$AB$ <0209.g1:/ A B />` is to :g:`$AC$ <0209.g1:/ A C />` as :g:`$DF$ <0209.g2:/ D F />` to :g:`$EF$ <0209.g2:/ E F />`
   | (*ex aequali in perturbed proportion*);

in this case the sides of the triangles are proportional, but not in the same
order, and the triangles are not necessarily equiangular to one another. For a
numerical illustration we may suppose the sides of one triangle to be 3, 4 and
5 feet respectively, and those of another to be 12, 15 and 20 feet respectively.

In VI. 5 there is the same *apparent* avoidance of indirect demonstration
which has been noticed on I. 48.

PROPOSITION 6.
--------------

.. enunciation::

   If two triangles have one angle equal to one angle and the sides about the equal angles proportional, the triangles will be equiangular and will have those angles equal which the corre- sponding sides subtend.

Let :g:`$ABC$ <0211.g1:< A B C >>`, :g:`$DEF$ <0211.g1:< D E F >>` be two triangles having one angle :g:`$BAC$ <0211.g1:< B A C )>`
equal to one angle :g:`$EDF$ <0211.g1:< E D F )>` and the sides about the equal angles
proportional, so that,

.. assertion::

   as :g:`$BA$ <0211.g1:/ B A />` is to :g:`$AC$ <0211.g1:/ A C />`, so is :g:`$ED$ <0211.g1:/ E D />` to :g:`$DF$ <0211.g1:/ D F />`;

I say that the triangle :g:`$ABC$ <0211.g1:< A B C >>` is equiangular with the triangle
:g:`$DEF$ <0211.g1:< D E F >>`, and will have the angle :g:`$ABC$ <0211.g1:< A B C )>` equal to the angle :g:`$DEF$ <0211.g1:< D E F )>`,
and the angle :g:`$ACB$ <0211.g1:< A C B )>` to the angle :g:`$DFE$ <0211.g1:< D F E )>`.

For on the straight line :g:`$DF$ <0211.g1:/ D F />`, and at the points :g:`$D$ <0211.g1:D>`, :g:`$F$ <0211.g1:F>` on it,
let there be constructed the angle :g:`$FDG$ <0211.g1:< F D G )>` equal to either of the
angles :g:`$BAC$ <0211.g1:< B A C )>`, :g:`$EDF$ <0211.g1:< E D F )>`, and the angle :g:`$DFG$ <0211.g1:< D F G )>` equal to the angle
:g:`$ACB$ <0211.g1:< A C B )>`; :just:`[I. 23] <I.23>`

.. assertion::

   | therefore the remaining angle at :g:`$B$ <0211.g1:< B )>` is equal to the remaining
   | angle at :g:`$G$ <0211.g1:< G )>`. :just:`[I. 32] <I.32>`
