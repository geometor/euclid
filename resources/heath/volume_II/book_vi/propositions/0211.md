<p class="assertion">Therefore the triangle <span data-ref="0211.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> is equiangular with the<br />
triangle <span data-ref="0211.g1:< D G F >">$DGF$</span>.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore, proportionally, as <span data-ref="0211.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span> is to <span data-ref="0211.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0211.g1:/ G D /">$GD$</span> to<br />
<span data-ref="0211.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span>. <span class="justification">[VI. 4]</span></p>

But, by hypothesis, as <span data-ref="0211.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span> is to <span data-ref="0211.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, so also is <span data-ref="0211.g1:/ E D /">$ED$</span> to <span data-ref="0211.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span>;
<p class="assertion">therefore also, as <span data-ref="0211.g1:/ E D /">$ED$</span> is to <span data-ref="0211.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0211.g1:/ G D /">$GD$</span> to <span data-ref="0211.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span>. <span class="justification">[V. 11]</span></p>

![Proposition 6 diagram](0211.g1.svg)

<p class="assertion">Therefore <span data-ref="0211.g1:/ E D /">$ED$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0211.g1:/ D G /">$DG$</span>; <span class="justification">[V. 9]</span></p>
and <span data-ref="0211.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span> is common;
<p class="sub-clause">therefore the two sides <span data-ref="0211.g1:/ E D /">$ED$</span>, <span data-ref="0211.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span> are equal to the two sides<br />
<span data-ref="0211.g1:/ G D /">$GD$</span>, <span data-ref="0211.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span>; and the angle <span data-ref="0211.g1:< E D F )">$EDF$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0211.g1:< G D F )">$GDF$</span>;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the base <span data-ref="0211.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> is equal to the base <span data-ref="0211.g1:/ G F /">$GF$</span>,</p>
<p class="sub-clause">and the triangle <span data-ref="0211.g1:< D E F >">$DEF$</span> is equal to the triangle <span data-ref="0211.g1:< D G F >">$DGF$</span>,<br />
and the remaining angles will be equal to the remaining angles,<br />
namely those which the equal sides subtend. <span class="justification">[I. 4]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore the angle <span data-ref="0211.g1:< D F G )">$DFG$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0211.g1:< D F E )">$DFE$</span>,<br />
and the angle <span data-ref="0211.g1:< D G F )">$DGF$</span> to the angle <span data-ref="0211.g1:< D E F )">$DEF$</span>.</p>

But the angle <span data-ref="0211.g1:< D F G )">$DFG$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0211.g1:< A C B )">$ACB$</span>;
<p class="assertion">therefore the angle <span data-ref="0211.g1:< A C B )">$ACB$</span> is also equal to the angle <span data-ref="0211.g1:< D F E )">$DFE$</span>.</p>

And, by hypothesis, the angle <span data-ref="0211.g1:< B A C )">$BAC$</span> is also equal to the<br />
angle <span data-ref="0211.g1:< E D F )">$EDF$</span>;
<p class="assertion">therefore the remaining angle at <span data-ref="0211.g1:< B )">$B$</span> is also equal to the<br />
remaining angle at <span data-ref="0211.g1:< E )">$E$</span>; <span class="justification">[I. 32]</span></p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the triangle <span data-ref="0211.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> is equiangular with the triangle<br />
<span data-ref="0211.g1:< D E F >">$DEF$</span>.</p>

<p class="qed">Therefore etc.<span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>
