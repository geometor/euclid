Therefore the triangle <span data-ref="0211.g1:&lt; A B C &gt;">$ABC$</span> is equiangular with the triangle <span data-ref="0211.g1:&lt; D G F &gt;">$DGF$</span>.

Therefore, proportionally, as <span data-ref="0211.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span> is to <span data-ref="0211.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0211.g1:/ G D /">$GD$</span> to <span data-ref="0211.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span>. [VI. 4]

But, by hypothesis, as <span data-ref="0211.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span> is to <span data-ref="0211.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, so also is <span data-ref="0211.g1:/ E D /">$ED$</span> to <span data-ref="0211.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span>;
therefore also, as <span data-ref="0211.g1:/ E D /">$ED$</span> is to <span data-ref="0211.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0211.g1:/ G D /">$GD$</span> to <span data-ref="0211.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span>. [V. 11]

![Proposition 6 diagram](0211.g1.svg)

Therefore <span data-ref="0211.g1:/ E D /">$ED$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0211.g1:/ D G /">$DG$</span>; [V. 9]
and <span data-ref="0211.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span> is common;
therefore the two sides <span data-ref="0211.g1:/ E D /">$ED$</span>, <span data-ref="0211.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span> are equal to the two sides <span data-ref="0211.g1:/ G D /">$GD$</span>, <span data-ref="0211.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span>; and the angle <span data-ref="0211.g1:&lt; E D F )">$EDF$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0211.g1:&lt; G D F )">$GDF$</span>;
therefore the base <span data-ref="0211.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> is equal to the base <span data-ref="0211.g1:/ G F /">$GF$</span>,
and the triangle <span data-ref="0211.g1:&lt; D E F &gt;">$DEF$</span> is equal to the triangle <span data-ref="0211.g1:&lt; D G F &gt;">$DGF$</span>,
and the remaining angles will be equal to the remaining angles, namely those which the equal sides subtend. [I. 4]
Therefore the angle <span data-ref="0211.g1:&lt; D F G )">$DFG$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0211.g1:&lt; D F E )">$DFE$</span>,
and the angle <span data-ref="0211.g1:&lt; D G F )">$DGF$</span> to the angle <span data-ref="0211.g1:&lt; D E F )">$DEF$</span>.
But the angle <span data-ref="0211.g1:&lt; D F G )">$DFG$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0211.g1:&lt; A C B )">$ACB$</span>;
therefore the angle <span data-ref="0211.g1:&lt; A C B )">$ACB$</span> is also equal to the angle <span data-ref="0211.g1:&lt; D F E )">$DFE$</span>.
And, by hypothesis, the angle <span data-ref="0211.g1:&lt; B A C )">$BAC$</span> is also equal to the angle <span data-ref="0211.g1:&lt; E D F )">$EDF$</span>;
therefore the remaining angle at <span data-ref="0211.g1:&lt; B )">$B$</span> is also equal to the remaining angle at <span data-ref="0211.g1:&lt; E )">$E$</span>; [I. 32]
therefore the triangle <span data-ref="0211.g1:&lt; A B C &gt;">$ABC$</span> is equiangular with the triangle <span data-ref="0211.g1:&lt; D E F &gt;">$DEF$</span>.

Therefore etc.

$$\text{Q. E. D.}$$
