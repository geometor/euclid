<p class="assertion">Therefore the triangle $ABC$ is equiangular with the<br />
triangle $DGF$.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore, proportionally, as $BA$ is to $AC$, so is $GD$ to<br />
$DF$. <span class="justification">[VI. 4]</span></p>

But, by hypothesis, as $BA$ is to $AC$, so also is $ED$ to $DF$;
<p class="assertion">therefore also, as $ED$ is to $DF$, so is $GD$ to $DF$. <span class="justification">[V. 11]</span></p>

![Proposition 6 diagram](0211.g1.svg)

<p class="assertion">Therefore $ED$ is equal to $DG$; <span class="justification">[V. 9]</span></p>
and $DF$ is common;
<p class="sub-clause">therefore the two sides $ED, DF$ are equal to the two sides<br />
$GD, DF$; and the angle $EDF$ is equal to the angle $GDF$;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the base $EF$ is equal to the base $GF$,</p>
<p class="sub-clause">and the triangle $DEF$ is equal to the triangle $DGF$,<br />
and the remaining angles will be equal to the remaining angles,<br />
namely those which the equal sides subtend. <span class="justification">[I. 4]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore the angle $DFG$ is equal to the angle $DFE$,<br />
and the angle $DGF$ to the angle $DEF$.</p>

But the angle $DFG$ is equal to the angle $ACB$;
<p class="assertion">therefore the angle $ACB$ is also equal to the angle $DFE$.</p>

And, by hypothesis, the angle $BAC$ is also equal to the<br />
angle $EDF$;
<p class="assertion">therefore the remaining angle at $B$ is also equal to the<br />
remaining angle at $E$; <span class="justification">[I. 32]</span></p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the triangle $ABC$ is equiangular with the triangle<br />
$DEF$.</p>

<p class="qed">Therefore etc.<span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>
