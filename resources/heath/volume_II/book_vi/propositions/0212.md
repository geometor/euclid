### PROPOSITION 7.

*If two triangles have one angle equal to one angle, the sides about other angles proportional, and the remaining angles either both less or both not less than a right angle, the triangles will be equiangular and will have those angles equal, the sides about which are proportional.*

Let <span data-ref="0212.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span>, <span data-ref="0212.g1:< D E F >">$DEF$</span> be two triangles having one angle equal to one angle, the angle <span data-ref="0212.g1:< B A C )">$BAC$</span> to the angle <span data-ref="0212.g1:< E D F )">$EDF$</span>, the sides about other angles <span data-ref="0212.g1:< A B C )">$ABC$</span>, <span data-ref="0212.g1:< D E F )">$DEF$</span> proportional, so that, as <span data-ref="0212.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0212.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0212.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> to <span data-ref="0212.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, and, first, each of the remaining angles at <span data-ref="0212.g1:< C )">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0212.g1:< F )">$F$</span> less than a right angle;
I say that the triangle <span data-ref="0212.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> is equiangular with the triangle <span data-ref="0212.g1:< D E F >">$DEF$</span>, the angle <span data-ref="0212.g1:< A B C )">$ABC$</span> will be equal to the angle <span data-ref="0212.g1:< D E F )">$DEF$</span>, and the remaining angle, namely the angle at <span data-ref="0212.g1:< C )">$C$</span>, equal to the remaining angle, the angle at <span data-ref="0212.g1:< F )">$F$</span>.

![Proposition 7 diagram, Case 1](0212.g1.svg)

For, if the angle <span data-ref="0212.g1:< A B C )">$ABC$</span> is unequal to the angle <span data-ref="0212.g1:< D E F )">$DEF$</span>, one of them is greater.

Let the angle <span data-ref="0212.g1:< A B C )">$ABC$</span> be greater;
and on the straight line <span data-ref="0212.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, and at the point <span data-ref="0212.g1:B">$B$</span> on it, let the angle <span data-ref="0212.g1:< A B G )">$ABG$</span> be constructed equal to the angle <span data-ref="0212.g1:< D E F )">$DEF$</span>. [I. 23]

Then, since the angle <span data-ref="0212.g1:< A )">$A$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0212.g1:< D )">$D$</span>,
and the angle <span data-ref="0212.g1:< A B G )">$ABG$</span> to the angle <span data-ref="0212.g1:< D E F )">$DEF$</span>,
therefore the remaining angle <span data-ref="0212.g1:< A G B )">$AGB$</span> is equal to the remaining angle <span data-ref="0212.g1:< D F E )">$DFE$</span>. [I. 32]

Therefore the triangle <span data-ref="0212.g1:< A B G >">$ABG$</span> is equiangular with the triangle <span data-ref="0212.g1:< D E F >">$DEF$</span>.

Therefore, as <span data-ref="0212.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0212.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0212.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> to <span data-ref="0212.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> [VI. 4]

But, as <span data-ref="0212.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> is to <span data-ref="0212.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, so by hypothesis is <span data-ref="0212.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> to <span data-ref="0212.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>;
therefore <span data-ref="0212.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> has the same ratio to each of the straight lines <span data-ref="0212.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, <span data-ref="0212.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span>; [V. 11]
therefore <span data-ref="0212.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0212.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span>, [V. 9]
so that the angle at <span data-ref="0212.g1:< C )">$C$</span> is also equal to the angle <span data-ref="0212.g1:< B G C )">$BGC$</span>. [I. 5]
