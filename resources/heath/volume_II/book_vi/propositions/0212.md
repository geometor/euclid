### PROPOSITION 7.

<p class="enunciation">If two triangles have one angle equal to one angle, the<br />
sides about other angles proportional, and the remaining angles<br />
either both less or both not less than a right angle, the triangles<br />
will be equiangular and will have those angles equal, the sides<br />
about which are proportional.</p>

Let $ABC, DEF$ be two triangles having one angle equal
to one angle, the angle $BAC$ to
the angle $EDF$, the sides about
other angles $ABC, DEF$ propor-
tional, so that, as $AB$ is to $BC$,
so is $DE$ to $EF$, and, first, each
of the remaining angles at $C, F$
less than a right angle;
I say that the triangle $ABC$ is
equiangular with the triangle
$DEF$, the angle $ABC$ will be
equal to the angle $DEF$, and the remaining angle, namely
the angle at $C$, equal to the remaining angle, the angle
at $F$.

![Proposition 7 diagram, Case 1](0212.g1.svg)

For, if the angle $ABC$ is unequal to the angle $DEF$, one
of them is greater.

Let the angle $ABC$ be greater;
and on the straight line $AB$, and at the point $B$ on it, let the
angle $ABG$ be constructed equal to the angle $DEF$. <span class="justification">[I. 23]</span>

Then, since the angle $A$ is equal to $D$,
and the angle $ABG$ to the angle $DEF$,
<p class="assertion">therefore the remaining angle $AGB$ is equal to the remaining<br />
angle $DFE$. <span class="justification">[I. 32]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore the triangle $ABG$ is equiangular with the<br />
triangle $DEF$.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore, as $AB$ is to $BG$, so is $DE$ to $EF$. <span class="justification">[VI. 4]</span></p>

But, as $DE$ is to $EF$, so by hypothesis is $AB$ to $BC$;
<p class="assertion">therefore $AB$ has the same ratio to each of the straight<br />
lines $BC, BG$; <span class="justification">[V. 11]</span></p>
<p class="assertion">therefore $BC$ is equal to $BG$, <span class="justification">[V. 9]</span></p>
<p class="assertion">so that the angle at $C$ is also equal to the angle $BGC$. <span class="justification">[I. 5]</span></p>
