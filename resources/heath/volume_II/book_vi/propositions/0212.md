### PROPOSITION 7.

<p class="enunciation">If two triangles have one angle equal to one angle, the<br />
sides about other angles proportional, and the remaining angles<br />
either both less or both not less than a right angle, the triangles<br />
will be equiangular and will have those angles equal, the sides<br />
about which are proportional.</p>

Let <span data-ref="0212.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span>, <span data-ref="0212.g1:< D E F >">$DEF$</span> be two triangles having one angle equal
to one angle, the angle <span data-ref="0212.g1:< B A C )">$BAC$</span> to
the angle <span data-ref="0212.g1:< E D F )">$EDF$</span>, the sides about
other angles <span data-ref="0212.g1:< A B C )">$ABC$</span>, <span data-ref="0212.g1:< D E F )">$DEF$</span> propor-
tional, so that, as <span data-ref="0212.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0212.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>,
so is <span data-ref="0212.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> to <span data-ref="0212.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, and, first, each
of the remaining angles at <span data-ref="0212.g1:< C )">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0212.g1:< F )">$F$</span>
less than a right angle;
I say that the triangle <span data-ref="0212.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> is
equiangular with the triangle
<span data-ref="0212.g1:< D E F >">$DEF$</span>, the angle <span data-ref="0212.g1:< A B C )">$ABC$</span> will be
equal to the angle <span data-ref="0212.g1:< D E F )">$DEF$</span>, and the remaining angle, namely
the angle at <span data-ref="0212.g1:< C )">$C$</span>, equal to the remaining angle, the angle
at <span data-ref="0212.g1:< F )">$F$</span>.

![Proposition 7 diagram, Case 1](0212.g1.svg)

For, if the angle <span data-ref="0212.g1:< A B C )">$ABC$</span> is unequal to the angle <span data-ref="0212.g1:< D E F )">$DEF$</span>, one
of them is greater.

Let the angle <span data-ref="0212.g1:< A B C )">$ABC$</span> be greater;
and on the straight line <span data-ref="0212.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, and at the point <span data-ref="0212.g1:B">$B$</span> on it, let the
angle <span data-ref="0212.g1:< A B G )">$ABG$</span> be constructed equal to the angle <span data-ref="0212.g1:< D E F )">$DEF$</span>. <span class="justification">[I. 23]</span>

Then, since the angle <span data-ref="0212.g1:< A )">$A$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0212.g1:< D )">$D$</span>,
and the angle <span data-ref="0212.g1:< A B G )">$ABG$</span> to the angle <span data-ref="0212.g1:< D E F )">$DEF$</span>,
<p class="assertion">therefore the remaining angle <span data-ref="0212.g1:< A G B )">$AGB$</span> is equal to the remaining<br />
angle <span data-ref="0212.g1:< D F E )">$DFE$</span>. <span class="justification">[I. 32]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore the triangle <span data-ref="0212.g1:< A B G >">$ABG$</span> is equiangular with the<br />
triangle <span data-ref="0212.g1:< D E F >">$DEF$</span>.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore, as <span data-ref="0212.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0212.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0212.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> to <span data-ref="0212.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>. <span class="justification">[VI. 4]</span></p>

But, as <span data-ref="0212.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> is to <span data-ref="0212.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, so by hypothesis is <span data-ref="0212.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> to <span data-ref="0212.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>;
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0212.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> has the same ratio to each of the straight<br />
lines <span data-ref="0212.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, <span data-ref="0212.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span>; <span class="justification">[V. 11]</span></p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0212.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0212.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span>, <span class="justification">[V. 9]</span></p>
<p class="assertion">so that the angle at <span data-ref="0212.g1:< C )">$C$</span> is also equal to the angle <span data-ref="0212.g1:< B G C )">$BGC$</span>. <span class="justification">[I. 5]</span></p>
