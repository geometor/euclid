But, by hypothesis, the angle at <span data-ref="0212.g1:< B C A )">$C$</span> is less than a right angle;
therefore the angle <span data-ref="0212.g1:< B G C )">$BGC$</span> is also less than a right angle;
so that the angle <span data-ref="0212.g1:< A G B )">$AGB$</span> adjacent to it is greater than a right angle. [I. 13]

And it was proved equal to the angle at <span data-ref="0212.g1:< D F E )">$F$</span>;
therefore the angle at <span data-ref="0212.g1:< D F E )">$F$</span> is also greater than a right angle.

But it is by hypothesis less than a right angle: which is absurd.

Therefore the angle <span data-ref="0212.g1:< A B C )">$ABC$</span> is not unequal to the angle <span data-ref="0212.g1:< D E F )">$DEF$</span>;
therefore it is equal to it.

But the angle at <span data-ref="0212.g1:< B A C )">$A$</span> is also equal to the angle at <span data-ref="0212.g1:< E D F )">$D$</span>;
therefore the remaining angle at <span data-ref="0212.g1:< B C A )">$C$</span> is equal to the remaining angle at <span data-ref="0212.g1:< D F E )">$F$</span>. [I. 32]

Therefore the triangle <span data-ref="0212.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> is equiangular with the triangle <span data-ref="0212.g1:< D E F >">$DEF$</span>.

But, again, let each of the angles at <span data-ref="0213.g1:< B C A )">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0213.g1:< D F E )">$F$</span> be supposed not less than a right angle;
I say again that, in this case too, the triangle <span data-ref="0213.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> is equiangular with the triangle <span data-ref="0213.g1:< D E F >">$DEF$</span>.

For, with the same construction,
we can prove similarly that
<span data-ref="0213.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0213.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span>;
so that the angle at <span data-ref="0213.g1:< B C A )">$C$</span> is also equal to the angle <span data-ref="0213.g1:< B G C )">$BGC$</span>. [I. 5]

But the angle at <span data-ref="0213.g1:< B C A )">$C$</span> is not less than a right angle;
therefore neither is the angle <span data-ref="0213.g1:< B G C )">$BGC$</span> less than a right angle.

Thus in the triangle <span data-ref="0213.g1:< B G C >">$BGC$</span> the two angles are not less than two right angles: which is impossible. [I. 17]

Therefore, once more, the angle <span data-ref="0213.g1:< A B C )">$ABC$</span> is not unequal to the angle <span data-ref="0213.g1:< D E F )">$DEF$</span>;
therefore it is equal to it.

But the angle at <span data-ref="0213.g1:< B A C )">$A$</span> is also equal to the angle at <span data-ref="0213.g1:< E D F )">$D$</span>;
therefore the remaining angle at <span data-ref="0213.g1:< B C A )">$C$</span> is equal to the remaining angle at <span data-ref="0213.g1:< D F E )">$F$</span>. [I. 32]
