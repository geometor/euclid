But, by hypothesis, the angle at $C$ is less than a right<br />
angle;
<p class="assertion">therefore the angle $BGC$ is also less than a right angle;</p>
<p class="assertion">so that the angle $AGB$ adjacent to it is greater than a right<br />
angle. <span class="justification">[I. 13]</span></p>

And it was proved equal to the angle at $F$;
<p class="assertion">therefore the angle at $F$ is also greater than a right angle.</p>

But it is by hypothesis less than a right angle: which is<br />
absurd.

<p class="assertion">Therefore the angle $ABC$ is not unequal to the angle<br />
$DEF$;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore it is equal to it.</p>

But the angle at $A$ is also equal to the angle at $D$;
<p class="assertion">therefore the remaining angle at $C$ is equal to the remaining<br />
angle at $F$. <span class="justification">[I. 32]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore the triangle $ABC$ is equiangular with the triangle<br />
$DEF$.</p>

But, again, let each of the angles at $C, F$ be supposed not<br />
less than a right angle;
I say again that, in this case too, the<br />
triangle $ABC$ is equiangular with the<br />
triangle $DEF$.

![Proposition 7 diagram, Case 2](0213.g1.svg)

For, with the same construction,<br />
we can prove similarly that
<p class="assertion">$BC$ is equal to $BG$;</p>
<p class="assertion">so that the angle at $C$ is also equal to<br />
the angle $BGC$. <span class="justification">[I. 5]</span></p>

But the angle at $C$ is not less than a right angle;
<p class="assertion">therefore neither is the angle $BGC$ less than a right angle.</p>

<p class="assertion">Thus in the triangle $BGC$ the two angles are not less<br />
than two right angles: which is impossible. <span class="justification">[I. 17]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore, once more, the angle $ABC$ is not unequal to<br />
the angle $DEF$;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore it is equal to it.</p>

But the angle at $A$ is also equal to the angle at $D$;
<p class="assertion">therefore the remaining angle at $C$ is equal to the remaining<br />
angle at $F$. <span class="justification">[I. 32]</span></p>
