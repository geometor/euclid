But, by hypothesis, the angle at <span data-ref="0212.g1:< B C A )">$C$</span> is less than a right<br />
angle;
<p class="assertion">therefore the angle <span data-ref="0212.g1:< B G C )">$BGC$</span> is also less than a right angle;</p>
<p class="assertion">so that the angle <span data-ref="0212.g1:< A G B )">$AGB$</span> adjacent to it is greater than a right<br />
angle. <span class="justification">[I. 13]</span></p>

And it was proved equal to the angle at <span data-ref="0212.g1:< D F E )">$F$</span>;
<p class="assertion">therefore the angle at <span data-ref="0212.g1:< D F E )">$F$</span> is also greater than a right angle.</p>

But it is by hypothesis less than a right angle: which is<br />
absurd.

<p class="assertion">Therefore the angle <span data-ref="0212.g1:< A B C )">$ABC$</span> is not unequal to the angle<br />
<span data-ref="0212.g1:< D E F )">$DEF$</span>;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore it is equal to it.</p>

But the angle at <span data-ref="0212.g1:< B A C )">$A$</span> is also equal to the angle at <span data-ref="0212.g1:< E D F )">$D$</span>;
<p class="assertion">therefore the remaining angle at <span data-ref="0212.g1:< B C A )">$C$</span> is equal to the remaining<br />
angle at <span data-ref="0212.g1:< D F E )">$F$</span>. <span class="justification">[I. 32]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore the triangle <span data-ref="0212.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> is equiangular with the triangle<br />
<span data-ref="0212.g1:< D E F >">$DEF$</span>.</p>

But, again, let each of the angles at <span data-ref="0213.g1:< B C A )">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0213.g1:< D F E )">$F$</span> be supposed not<br />
less than a right angle;
I say again that, in this case too, the<br />
triangle <span data-ref="0213.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> is equiangular with the<br />
triangle <span data-ref="0213.g1:< D E F >">$DEF$</span>.

![Proposition 7 diagram, Case 2](0213.g1.svg)

For, with the same construction,<br />
we can prove similarly that
<p class="assertion"><span data-ref="0213.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0213.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span>;</p>
<p class="assertion">so that the angle at <span data-ref="0213.g1:< B C A )">$C$</span> is also equal to<br />
the angle <span data-ref="0213.g1:< B G C )">$BGC$</span>. <span class="justification">[I. 5]</span></p>

But the angle at <span data-ref="0213.g1:< B C A )">$C$</span> is not less than a right angle;
<p class="assertion">therefore neither is the angle <span data-ref="0213.g1:< B G C )">$BGC$</span> less than a right angle.</p>

<p class="assertion">Thus in the triangle <span data-ref="0213.g1:< B G C >">$BGC$</span> the two angles are not less<br />
than two right angles: which is impossible. <span class="justification">[I. 17]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore, once more, the angle <span data-ref="0213.g1:< A B C )">$ABC$</span> is not unequal to<br />
the angle <span data-ref="0213.g1:< D E F )">$DEF$</span>;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore it is equal to it.</p>

But the angle at <span data-ref="0213.g1:< B A C )">$A$</span> is also equal to the angle at <span data-ref="0213.g1:< E D F )">$D$</span>;
<p class="assertion">therefore the remaining angle at <span data-ref="0213.g1:< B C A )">$C$</span> is equal to the remaining<br />
angle at <span data-ref="0213.g1:< D F E )">$F$</span>. <span class="justification">[I. 32]</span></p>
