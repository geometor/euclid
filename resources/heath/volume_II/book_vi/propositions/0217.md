Simson remarks on this proposition: "It seems plain that some editor<br />
has changed the demonstration that Euclid gave of this proposition: For,<br />
after he has demonstrated that the triangles are equiangular to one another,<br />
he particularly shows that their sides about the equal angles are proportionals,<br />
as if this had not been done in the demonstration of prop. 4 of this book:<br />
this superfluous part is not found in the translation from the Arabic, and is<br />
now left out."

This seems a little hypercritical, for the "particular showing" that the<br />
sides about the equal angles are proportionals is really nothing more than<br />
a somewhat full citation of VI. 4. Moreover to shorten his proof still<br />
more, Simson says, after proving that each of the triangles $ABD$, $ADC$ is<br />
similar to the whole triangle $ABC$, "And the triangles $ABD$, $ADC$ being<br />
both equiangular and similar to $ABC$ are equiangular and similar to one<br />
another," thus assuming a particular case of VI. 21, which might well be<br />
proved here, as Euclid proves it, with somewhat more detail.

We observe that, here as generally, Euclid seems to disdain to give the<br />
reader such small help as might be afforded by arranging the letters used to<br />
denote the triangles so as to show the corresponding angular points in the<br />
same order for each pair of triangles; $A$ is the first letter throughout, and the<br />
other two for each triangle are in the order of the figure from left to right. It<br />
may be in compensation for this that he states at such length which side<br />
corresponds to which when he comes to the proportions.

In the Greek texts there is an addition to the Porism inserted after<br />
"(Being) what it was required to prove," viz. "and further that between the<br />
base and any one of the segments the side adjacent to the segment is a mean<br />
proportional." Heiberg concludes that these words are an interpolation<br />
(1) because they come after the words ὅπερ ἔδει δεῖξαι which as a rule follow the<br />
Porism, (2) they are absent from the best Theonine MSS., though $P$ and<br />
Campanus have them without the ὅπερ ἔδει δεῖξαι. Heiberg's view seems to<br />
be confirmed by the fact noted by Austin, that, whereas the first part of the<br />
Porism is quoted later in VI. 13, in the lemma before X. 33 and in the lemma<br />
after XIII. 13, the second part is *proved* in the former lemma, and elsewhere,<br />
as also in Pappus (III. p. 72, 9—23).
