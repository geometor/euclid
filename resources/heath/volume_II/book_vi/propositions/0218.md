**6. any angle.** The expression here and in the two following propositions is τυχοῦσα<br />
γωνία, corresponding exactly to τυχὸν σημεῖον which I have translated as "a point (taken)<br />
at random"; but "an angle (taken) at random" would not be so appropriate where it is a<br />
question, not of *taking* any angle at all, but of drawing a straight line casually so as to make<br />
any angle with another straight line.

Simson observes that "this is demonstrated in a particular case, viz. that<br />
in which the third part of a straight line is required to be cut off; which is<br />
not at all like Euclid's manner. Besides, the author of that demonstration,<br />
from four magnitudes being proportionals, concludes that the third of them is<br />
the same multiple of the fourth which the first is of the second; now this is<br />
nowhere demonstrated in the 5th book, as we now have it; but the editor<br />
assumes it from the confused notion which the vulgar have of proportionals."

The truth of the assumption referred to is proved by Simson in his<br />
proposition D given above (p. 128); hence he is<br />
able to supply a general and legitimate proof<br />
of the present proposition.
