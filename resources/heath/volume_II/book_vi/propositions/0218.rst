:title: Proposition 9

Let the third part be that prescribed.

Let a straight line :g:`$AC$ <0218.g1:/ A C />` be drawn through from :g:`$A$ <0218.g1:A>` containing with :g:`$AB$ <0218.g1:/ A B />` any angle;\ [#f1]_ let a point :g:`$D$ <0218.g1:D>` be taken at random on :g:`$AC$ <0218.g1:/ A C />`, and let :g:`$DE$ <0218.g1:/ D E />`, :g:`$EC$ <0218.g1:/ E C />` be made :eq:`equal <0218.g1:/ D E / = 0218.g1:/ E C / = 0218.g1:/ A D />` to :g:`$AD$ <0218.g1:/ A D />`. :just:`[I. 3] <I.3>`

.. picture:: 0218.g1.svg
   :alt: Proposition 9

Let :g:`$BC$ <0218.g1:/ B C />` be joined, and through :g:`$D$ <0218.g1:D>` let :g:`$DF$ <0218.g1:/ D F />` be drawn parallel to it. :just:`[I. 31] <I.31>`

Then, since :g:`$FD$ <0218.g1:/ F D />` has been drawn parallel to :g:`$BC$ <0218.g1:/ B C />`, one of the sides of the triangle :g:`$ABC$ <0218.g1:< A B C >>`,

.. assertion::

   therefore, proportionally, as :g:`$CD$ <0218.g1:/ C D />` is to :g:`$DA$ <0218.g1:/ D A />`, so is :g:`$BF$ <0218.g1:/ B F />` to :g:`$FA$ <0218.g1:/ F A />`. :just:`[VI. 2] <VI.2>`

.. assertion::

   But :g:`$CD$ <0218.g1:/ C D />` is double of :g:`$DA$ <0218.g1:/ D A />`; therefore :g:`$BF$ <0218.g1:/ B F />` is also double of :g:`$FA$ <0218.g1:/ F A />`; therefore :g:`$BA$ <0218.g1:/ B A />` is triple of :g:`$AF$ <0218.g1:/ A F />`.

.. qed::

   Therefore from the given straight line :g:`$AB$ <0218.g1:/ A B />` the prescribed third part :g:`$AF$ <0218.g1:/ A F />` has been cut off. :just:`Q. E. F.`

----

.. [#f1] **any angle.** The expression here and in the two following propositions is τυχοῦσα γωνία, corresponding exactly to τυχὸν σημεῖον which I have translated as "a point (taken) at random"; but "an angle (taken) at random" would not be so appropriate where it is a question, not of *taking* any angle at all, but of drawing a straight line casually so as to make any angle with another straight line.

:person:`Simson <simson>` observes that "this is demonstrated in a particular case, viz. that in which the third part of a straight line is required to be cut off; which is not at all like Euclid's manner. Besides, the author of that demonstration, from four magnitudes being proportionals, concludes that the third of them is the same multiple of the fourth which the first is of the second; now this is nowhere demonstrated in the 5th book, as we now have it; but the editor assumes it from the confused notion which the vulgar have of proportionals."

The truth of the assumption referred to is proved by :person:`Simson <simson>` in his proposition D given above (p. 128); hence he is able to supply a general and legitimate proof of the present proposition.

"Let :g:`$AB$ <0218.g2:/ A B />` be the given straight line; it is required to cut off any part from it.

From the point :g:`$A$ <0218.g2:A>` draw a straight line :g:`$AC$ <0218.g2:/ A C />` making any angle with :g:`$AB$ <0218.g2:/ A B />`; in :g:`$AC$ <0218.g2:/ A C />` take any point :g:`$D$ <0218.g2:D>`, and take :g:`$AC$ <0218.g2:/ A C />` the same multiple of :g:`$AD$ <0218.g2:/ A D />` that :g:`$AB$ <0218.g2:/ A B />` is of the part which is to be cut off from it;

   :alt: Proposition 9, Simson's Alternative

join :g:`$BC$ <0218.g2:/ B C />`, and draw :g:`$DE$ <0218.g2:/ D E />` parallel to it: then :g:`$AE$ <0218.g2:/ A E />` is the part required to be cut off.
