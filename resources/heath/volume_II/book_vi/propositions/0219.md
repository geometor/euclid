Because <span data-ref="0218.g2:/ E D /">$ED$</span> is parallel to one of the sides of the triangle <span data-ref="0218.g2:< A B C >">$ABC$</span>, viz. to <span data-ref="0218.g2:/ B C /">$BC$</span>,
<p class="assertion">as <span data-ref="0218.g2:/ C D /">$CD$</span> is to <span data-ref="0218.g2:/ D A /">$DA$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0218.g2:/ B E /">$BE$</span> to <span data-ref="0218.g2:/ E A /">$EA$</span>, <span class="justification">[VI. 2]</span></p>
and, *componendo*,
<p class="assertion"><span data-ref="0218.g2:/ C A /">$CA$</span> is to <span data-ref="0218.g2:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, as <span data-ref="0218.g2:/ B A /">$BA$</span> to <span data-ref="0218.g2:/ A E /">$AE$</span>. <span class="justification">[V. 18]</span></p>

But <span data-ref="0218.g2:/ C A /">$CA$</span> is a multiple of <span data-ref="0218.g2:/ A D /">$AD$</span>;
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0218.g2:/ B A /">$BA$</span> is the same multiple of <span data-ref="0218.g2:/ A E /">$AE$</span>. <span class="justification">[Prop. D]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">Whatever part therefore <span data-ref="0218.g2:/ A D /">$AD$</span> is of <span data-ref="0218.g2:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0218.g2:/ A E /">$AE$</span> is the same part of <span data-ref="0218.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span>;<br />
wherefore from the straight line <span data-ref="0218.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span> the part required is cut off."</p>

The use of Simson's Prop. D can be avoided, as noted by Camerer after<br />
Baermann, in the following way. We first prove, as above, that
<p class="assertion"><span data-ref="0218.g2:/ C A /">$CA$</span> is to <span data-ref="0218.g2:/ A D /">$AD$</span> as <span data-ref="0218.g2:/ B A /">$BA$</span> is to <span data-ref="0218.g2:/ A E /">$AE$</span>.</p>

Then we infer that, alternately,
<p class="assertion"><span data-ref="0218.g2:/ C A /">$CA$</span> is to <span data-ref="0218.g2:/ B A /">$BA$</span> as <span data-ref="0218.g2:/ A D /">$AD$</span> to <span data-ref="0218.g2:/ A E /">$AE$</span>. <span class="justification">[V. 16]</span></p>

But <span data-ref="0218.g2:/ A D /">$AD$</span> is to <span data-ref="0218.g2:/ A E /">$AE$</span> as n · <span data-ref="0218.g2:/ A D /">$AD$</span> to n · <span data-ref="0218.g2:/ A E /">$AE$</span><br />
(where $n$ is the number of times that <span data-ref="0218.g2:/ A D /">$AD$</span> is contained in <span data-ref="0218.g2:/ A C /">$AC$</span>); <span class="justification">[V. 15]</span>
<p class="assertion">whence <span data-ref="0218.g2:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is to <span data-ref="0218.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span> as n · <span data-ref="0218.g2:/ A D /">$AD$</span> is to n · <span data-ref="0218.g2:/ A E /">$AE$</span>. <span class="justification">[V. 11]</span></p>

In this proportion the first term is equal to the third; therefore <span class="justification">[V. 14]</span><br />
the second is equal to the fourth,
<p class="assertion">so that <span data-ref="0218.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is equal to $n$ times <span data-ref="0218.g2:/ A E /">$AE$</span>.</p>

Prop. 9 is of course only a particular case of Prop. 10.
