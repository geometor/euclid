Because $ED$ is parallel to one of the sides of the triangle $ABC$, viz. to $BC$,
<p class="assertion">as $CD$ is to $DA$, so is $BE$ to $EA$, <span class="justification">[VI. 2]</span></p>
and, *componendo*,
<p class="assertion">$CA$ is to $AD$, as $BA$ to $AE$. <span class="justification">[V. 18]</span></p>

But $CA$ is a multiple of $AD$;
<p class="assertion">therefore $BA$ is the same multiple of $AE$. <span class="justification">[Prop. D]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">Whatever part therefore $AD$ is of $AC$, $AE$ is the same part of $AB$;<br />
wherefore from the straight line $AB$ the part required is cut off."</p>

The use of Simson's Prop. D can be avoided, as noted by Camerer after<br />
Baermann, in the following way. We first prove, as above, that
<p class="assertion">$CA$ is to $AD$ as $BA$ is to $AE$.</p>

Then we infer that, alternately,
<p class="assertion">$CA$ is to $BA$ as $AD$ to $AE$. <span class="justification">[V. 16]</span></p>

But $AD$ is to $AE$ as $n \cdot AD$ to $n \cdot AE$<br />
(where $n$ is the number of times that $AD$ is contained in $AC$); <span class="justification">[V. 15]</span>
<p class="assertion">whence $AC$ is to $AB$ as $n \cdot AD$ is to $n \cdot AE$. <span class="justification">[V. 11]</span></p>

In this proportion the first term is equal to the third; therefore <span class="justification">[V. 14]</span><br />
the second is equal to the fourth,
<p class="assertion">so that $AB$ is equal to $n$ times $AE$.</p>

Prop. 9 is of course only a particular case of Prop. 10.

### PROPOSITION 10.

<p class="enunciation">To cut a given uncut straight line similarly to a given cut<br />
straight line.</p>

Let $AB$ be the given uncut straight line, and $AC$ the<br />
straight line cut at the points $D$,<br />
$E$; and let them be so placed as<br />
to contain any angle;<br />
let $CB$ be joined, and through $D$,<br />
$E$ let $DF$, $EG$ be drawn parallel<br />
to $BC$, and through $D$ let $DHK$<br />
be drawn parallel to $AB$. <span class="justification">[I. 31]</span>

<p class="sub-clause">Therefore each of the figures<br />
$FH$, $HB$ is a parallelogram;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore $DH$ is equal to $FG$ and $HK$ to $GB$. <span class="justification">[I. 34]</span></p>

Now, since the straight line $HE$ has been drawn parallel<br />
to $KC$, one of the sides of the triangle $DKC$,
<p class="assertion">therefore, proportionally, as $CE$ is to $ED$, so is $KH$ to $HD$.<br />
