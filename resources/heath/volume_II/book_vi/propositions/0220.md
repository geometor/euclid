### PROPOSITION 11.

<p class="enunciation">To two given straight lines to find a third proportional.</p>

Let $BA, AC$ be the two given straight lines, and let<br />
them be placed so as to contain any<br />
angle;<br />
thus it is required to find a third pro-<br />
portional to $BA, AC$.

For let them be produced to the<br />
points $D, E$, and let $BD$ be made equal<br />
to $AC$; <span class="justification">[I. 3]</span><br />
let $BC$ be joined, and through $D$ let $DE$<br />
be drawn parallel to it. <span class="justification">[I. 31]</span>

![Proposition 11](0220.g1.svg)

Since, then, $BC$ has been drawn<br />
parallel to $DE$, one of the sides of the triangle $ADE$,
<p class="assertion">proportionally, as $AB$ is to $BD$, so is $AC$ to $CE$. <span class="justification">[VI. 2]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">But $BD$ is equal to $AC$;<br />
therefore, as $AB$ is to $AC$, so is $AC$ to $CE$.</p>

<p class="qed">Therefore to two given straight lines $AB$, $AC$ a third<br />
proportional to them, $CE$, has been found.<span class="justification">Q. E. F.</span></p>

**1. to find.** The Greek word, here and in the next two propositions, is προσευρεῖν,<br />
literally "to find *in addition*."

This proposition is again a particular case of the succeeding Prop. 12.

Given a ratio between straight lines, VI. 11 enables us to find the ratio<br />
which is its duplicate.
