PROPOSITION 12.
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

.. enunciation::

   To three given straight lines to find a fourth proportional.

| Let :g:`$A$ <0221.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0221.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0221.g1:C>` be the three given straight lines;
| thus it is required to find a fourth proportional to :g:`$A$ <0221.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0221.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0221.g1:C>`.

.. picture:: 0221.g1.svg
   :alt: Proposition 12

| Let two straight lines :g:`$DE$ <0221.g1:/ D E />`, :g:`$DF$ <0221.g1:/ D F />` be set out containing any
| angle :g:`$EDF$ <0221.g1:< E D F )>`;
| let :g:`$DG$ <0221.g1:/ D G />` be made equal to :g:`$A$ <0221.g1:A>`, :g:`$GE$ <0221.g1:/ G E />` equal to :g:`$B$ <0221.g1:B>`, and further :g:`$DH$ <0221.g1:/ D H />`
| equal to :g:`$C$ <0221.g1:C>`;
| let :g:`$GH$ <0221.g1:/ G H />` be joined, and let :g:`$EF$ <0221.g1:/ E F />` be drawn through :g:`$E$ <0221.g1:E>` parallel
| to it. :just:`[I. 31] <I.31>`

.. subclause::

   | Since, then, :g:`$GH$ <0221.g1:/ G H />` has been drawn parallel to :g:`$EF$ <0221.g1:/ E F />`, one of
   | the sides of the triangle :g:`$DEF$ <0221.g1:< D E F >>`,

.. assertion:: [VI. 2] <VI.2>

   therefore, as :g:`$DG$ <0221.g1:/ D G />` is to :g:`$GE$ <0221.g1:/ G E />`, so is :g:`$DH$ <0221.g1:/ D H />` to :g:`$HF$ <0221.g1:/ H F />`.

.. subclause::

   But :g:`$DG$ <0221.g1:/ D G />` is equal to :g:`$A$ <0221.g1:A>`, :g:`$GE$ <0221.g1:/ G E />` to :g:`$B$ <0221.g1:B>`, and :g:`$DH$ <0221.g1:/ D H />` to :g:`$C$ <0221.g1:C>`;

.. assertion::

   therefore, as :g:`$A$ <0221.g1:A>` is to :g:`$B$ <0221.g1:B>`, so is :g:`$C$ <0221.g1:C>` to :g:`$HF$ <0221.g1:/ H F />`.

.. qed::

   | Therefore to the three given straight lines :g:`$A$ <0221.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0221.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0221.g1:C>` a fourth
   | proportional :g:`$HF$ <0221.g1:/ H F />` has been found. :just:`Q. E. F.`

----

We have here the geometrical equivalent of the "rule of three."

| It is of course immaterial whether, as in Euclid's proof, the first and
| second straight lines are measured on one of the lines forming the angle and
| the third on the other, or the first and third are measured on one and the
| second on the other.

| If it should be desired that the first and the required fourth be measured
| on one of the lines, and the second and third on
| the other, we can use the following construction.
| Measure :g:`$DE$ <0221.g2:/ D E />` on one straight line equal to :g:`$A$ <0221.g1:A>`, and
| on any other straight line making an angle with
| the first at the point :g:`$D$ <0221.g2:D>` measure :g:`$DF$ <0221.g2:/ D F />` equal to :g:`$B$ <0221.g1:B>`,
| and :g:`$DG$ <0221.g2:/ D G />` equal to :g:`$C$ <0221.g1:C>`. Join :g:`$EF$ <0221.g2:/ E F />`, and through :g:`$G$ <0221.g2:G>`
| draw :g:`$GH$ <0221.g2:/ G H />` anti-parallel to :g:`$EF$ <0221.g2:/ E F />`, i.e. make the angle
| :g:`$DGH$ <0221.g2:< D G H )>` equal to the angle :g:`$DEF$ <0221.g2:< D E F )>`; let :g:`$GH$ <0221.g2:/ G H />` meet
| :g:`$DE$ <0221.g2:/ D E />` (produced if necessary) in :g:`$H$ <0221.g2:H>`.
