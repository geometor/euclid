Let <span data-ref="0223.g1:< A B >">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0223.g1:< B C >">$BC$</span> be equal and equiangular parallelograms<br />
having the angles at <span data-ref="0223.g1:< D B F )">$B$</span> equal, and<br />
let <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span>, <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span> be placed in a straight<br />
line;
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ F B /">$FB$</span>, <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span> are also in<br />
a straight line. <span class="justification">[I. 14]</span></p>

I say that, in <span data-ref="0223.g1:< A B >">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0223.g1:< B C >">$BC$</span>, the<br />
sides about the equal angles are<br />
reciprocally proportional, that is to<br />
say, that, as <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span>, so is<br />
<span data-ref="0223.g1:/ G B /">$GB$</span> to <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ B F /">$BF$</span>.

![Book VI Proposition 14](0223.g1.svg)

For let the parallelogram <span data-ref="0223.g1:< F E >">$FE$</span> be completed.

<p class="sub-clause">Since, then, the parallelogram <span data-ref="0223.g1:< A B >">$AB$</span> is equal to the parallelo-<br />
gram <span data-ref="0223.g1:< B C >">$BC$</span>,</p>

<p class="sub-clause">and <span data-ref="0223.g1:< F E >">$FE$</span> is another area,</p>

<p class="assertion">therefore, as <span data-ref="0223.g1:< A B >">$AB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0223.g1:< F E >">$FE$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0223.g1:< B C >">$BC$</span> to <span data-ref="0223.g1:< F E >">$FE$</span>. <span class="justification">[V. 7]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But, as <span data-ref="0223.g1:< A B >">$AB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0223.g1:< F E >">$FE$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> to <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span>, <span class="justification">[VI. 1]</span><br />
and, as <span data-ref="0223.g1:< B C >">$BC$</span> is to <span data-ref="0223.g1:< F E >">$FE$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ G B /">$GB$</span> to <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ B F /">$BF$</span>. <span class="justification">[*id.*]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">therefore also, as <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ G B /">$GB$</span> to <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ B F /">$BF$</span>. <span class="justification">[V. 11]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore in the parallelograms <span data-ref="0223.g1:< A B >">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0223.g1:< B C >">$BC$</span> the sides about<br />
the equal angles are reciprocally proportional.</p>

Next, let <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ G B /">$GB$</span> be to <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ B F /">$BF$</span> as <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> to <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span>;<br />
I say that the parallelogram <span data-ref="0223.g1:< A B >">$AB$</span> is equal to the parallelogram<br />
<span data-ref="0223.g1:< B C >">$BC$</span>.

<p class="sub-clause">For since, as <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ G B /">$GB$</span> to <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ B F /">$BF$</span>,<br />
while, as <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span>, so is the parallelogram <span data-ref="0223.g1:< A B >">$AB$</span> to the<br />
parallelogram <span data-ref="0223.g1:< F E >">$FE$</span>, <span class="justification">[VI. 1]</span><br />
and, as <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ G B /">$GB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ B F /">$BF$</span>, so is the parallelogram <span data-ref="0223.g1:< B C >">$BC$</span> to the<br />
parallelogram <span data-ref="0223.g1:< F E >">$FE$</span>, <span class="justification">[VI. 1]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">therefore also, as <span data-ref="0223.g1:< A B >">$AB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0223.g1:< F E >">$FE$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0223.g1:< B C >">$BC$</span> to <span data-ref="0223.g1:< F E >">$FE$</span>; <span class="justification">[V. 11]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">therefore the parallelogram <span data-ref="0223.g1:< A B >">$AB$</span> is equal to the parallelogram<br />
<span data-ref="0223.g1:< B C >">$BC$</span>. <span class="justification">[V. 9]</span></p>

<p class="qed">Therefore etc.<span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

---

De Morgan says upon this proposition: “Owing to the disjointed manner<br />
in which Euclid treats compound ratio, this proposition is strangely out of<br />
place. It is a particular case of VI. 23, being that in which the ratio of the<br />
sides, compounded, gives a ratio of equality. The proper definition of four<br />
magnitudes being reciprocally proportional is that the ratio compounded of<br />
their ratios is that of equality.”
