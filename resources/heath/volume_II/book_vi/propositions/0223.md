Let *AB*, *BC* be equal and equiangular parallelograms<br />
having the angles at *B* equal, and<br />
let *DB*, *BE* be placed in a straight<br />
line;
<p class="assertion">therefore *FB*, *BG* are also in<br />
a straight line. <span class="justification">[I. 14]</span></p>

I say that, in *AB*, *BC*, the<br />
sides about the equal angles are<br />
reciprocally proportional, that is to<br />
say, that, as *DB* is to *BE*, so is<br />
*GB* to *BF*.

![Book VI Proposition 14](0223.g1.svg)

For let the parallelogram *FE* be completed.

<p class="sub-clause">Since, then, the parallelogram *AB* is equal to the parallelo-<br />
gram *BC*,</p>

<p class="sub-clause">and *FE* is another area,</p>

<p class="assertion">therefore, as *AB* is to *FE*, so is *BC* to *FE*. <span class="justification">[V. 7]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But, as *AB* is to *FE*, so is *DB* to *BE*, <span class="justification">[VI. 1]</span><br />
and, as *BC* is to *FE*, so is *GB* to *BF*. <span class="justification">[*id.*]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">therefore also, as *DB* is to *BE*, so is *GB* to *BF*. <span class="justification">[V. 11]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore in the parallelograms *AB*, *BC* the sides about<br />
the equal angles are reciprocally proportional.</p>

Next, let *GB* be to *BF* as *DB* to *BE*;<br />
I say that the parallelogram *AB* is equal to the parallelogram<br />
*BC*.

<p class="sub-clause">For since, as *DB* is to *BE*, so is *GB* to *BF*,<br />
while, as *DB* is to *BE*, so is the parallelogram *AB* to the<br />
parallelogram *FE*, <span class="justification">[VI. 1]</span><br />
and, as *GB* is to *BF*, so is the parallelogram *BC* to the<br />
parallelogram *FE*, <span class="justification">[VI. 1]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">therefore also, as *AB* is to *FE*, so is *BC* to *FE*; <span class="justification">[V. 11]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">therefore the parallelogram *AB* is equal to the parallelogram<br />
*BC*. <span class="justification">[V. 9]</span></p>

<p class="qed">Therefore etc.<span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

---

De Morgan says upon this proposition: “Owing to the disjointed manner<br />
in which Euclid treats compound ratio, this proposition is strangely out of<br />
place. It is a particular case of VI. 23, being that in which the ratio of the<br />
sides, compounded, gives a ratio of equality. The proper definition of four<br />
magnitudes being reciprocally proportional is that the ratio compounded of<br />
their ratios is that of equality.”
