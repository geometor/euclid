Let <span data-ref="0223.g1:< A B >">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0223.g1:< B C >">$BC$</span> be equal and equiangular parallelograms having the angles at <span data-ref="0223.g1:< D B F )">$B$</span> equal, and let <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span>, <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span> be placed in a straight line;
therefore <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ F B /">$FB$</span>, <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span> are also in a straight line. [I. 14]

![Book VI Proposition 14](0223.g1.svg)

I say that, in <span data-ref="0223.g1:< A B >">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0223.g1:< B C >">$BC$</span>, the sides about the equal angles are reciprocally proportional, that is to say, that, as <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ G B /">$GB$</span> to <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ B F /">$BF$</span>.

For let the parallelogram <span data-ref="0223.g1:< F E >">$FE$</span> be completed.

Since, then, the parallelogram <span data-ref="0223.g1:< A B >">$AB$</span> is equal to the parallelogram <span data-ref="0223.g1:< B C >">$BC$</span>,
and <span data-ref="0223.g1:< F E >">$FE$</span> is another area,
therefore, as <span data-ref="0223.g1:< A B >">$AB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0223.g1:< F E >">$FE$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0223.g1:< B C >">$BC$</span> to <span data-ref="0223.g1:< F E >">$FE$</span>. [V. 7]

But, as <span data-ref="0223.g1:< A B >">$AB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0223.g1:< F E >">$FE$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> to <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span>, [VI. 1]
and, as <span data-ref="0223.g1:< B C >">$BC$</span> is to <span data-ref="0223.g1:< F E >">$FE$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ G B /">$GB$</span> to <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ B F /">$BF$</span>. [*id.*]
therefore also, as <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ G B /">$GB$</span> to <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ B F /">$BF$</span>. [V. 11]

Therefore in the parallelograms <span data-ref="0223.g1:< A B >">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0223.g1:< B C >">$BC$</span> the sides about the equal angles are reciprocally proportional.

Next, let <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ G B /">$GB$</span> be to <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ B F /">$BF$</span> as <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> to <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span>;
I say that the parallelogram <span data-ref="0223.g1:< A B >">$AB$</span> is equal to the parallelogram <span data-ref="0223.g1:< B C >">$BC$</span>.

For since, as <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ G B /">$GB$</span> to <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ B F /">$BF$</span>,
while, as <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span>, so is the parallelogram <span data-ref="0223.g1:< A B >">$AB$</span> to the parallelogram <span data-ref="0223.g1:< F E >">$FE$</span>, [VI. 1]
and, as <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ G B /">$GB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0223.g1:/ B F /">$BF$</span>, so is the parallelogram <span data-ref="0223.g1:< B C >">$BC$</span> to the parallelogram <span data-ref="0223.g1:< F E >">$FE$</span>, [VI. 1]
therefore also, as <span data-ref="0223.g1:< A B >">$AB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0223.g1:< F E >">$FE$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0223.g1:< B C >">$BC$</span> to <span data-ref="0223.g1:< F E >">$FE$</span>; [V. 11]
therefore the parallelogram <span data-ref="0223.g1:< A B >">$AB$</span> is equal to the parallelogram <span data-ref="0223.g1:< B C >">$BC$</span>. [V. 9]

Therefore etc.

$$\text{Q. E. D.}$$

---

De Morgan says upon this proposition: “Owing to the disjointed manner in which Euclid treats compound ratio, this proposition is strangely out of place. It is a particular case of VI. 23, being that in which the ratio of the sides, compounded, gives a ratio of equality. The proper definition of four magnitudes being reciprocally proportional is that the ratio compounded of their ratios is that of equality.”
