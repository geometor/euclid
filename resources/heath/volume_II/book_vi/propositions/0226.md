For let them be placed so that *CA* is in a straight<br />
line with *AD*;
<p class="assertion">therefore *EA* is also in a straight line with<br />
*AB*. <span class="justification">[I. 14]</span></p>

Let *BD* be joined.

![Book VI Proposition 15](0226.g1.svg)

Since then the triangle *ABC* is equal to<br />
the triangle *ADE*, and *BAD* is another<br />
area,
<p class="assertion">therefore, as the triangle *CAB* is to the<br />
triangle *BAD*, so is the triangle *EAD* to<br />
the triangle *BAD*. <span class="justification">[V. 7]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">But, as *CAB* is to *BAD*, so is *CA* to *AD*, <span class="justification">[VI. 1]</span><br />
and, as *EAD* is to *BAD*, so is *EA* to *AB*. <span class="justification">[*id.*]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore also, as *CA* is to *AD*, so is *EA* to *AB*. <span class="justification">[V. 11]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore in the triangles *ABC*, *ADE* the sides about<br />
the equal angles are reciprocally proportional.</p>

Next, let the sides of the triangles *ABC*, *ADE* be reci-<br />
procally proportional, that is to say, let *EA* be to *AB* as *CA*<br />
to *AD*;
I say that the triangle *ABC* is equal to the triangle *ADE*.

For, if *BD* be again joined,<br />
since, as *CA* is to *AD*, so is *EA* to *AB*,<br />
while, as *CA* is to *AD*, so is the triangle *ABC* to the triangle<br />
*BAD*,<br />
and, as *EA* is to *AB*, so is the triangle *EAD* to the triangle<br />
*BAD*, <span class="justification">[VI. 1]</span>
<p class="assertion">therefore, as the triangle *ABC* is to the triangle *BAD*, so is<br />
the triangle *EAD* to the triangle *BAD*. <span class="justification">[V. 11]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">Therefore each of the triangles *ABC*, *EAD* has the same<br />
ratio to *BAD*.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore the triangle *ABC* is equal to the triangle *EAD*.<br />
<span class="justification">[V. 9]</span></p>

<p class="qed">Therefore etc.<span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

---

As indicated in the partial converse given in the last note, this proposition<br />
is equally true if the angle included by the two sides in one triangle is<br />
supplementary, instead of being equal, to the angle included by the two sides<br />
in the other.
