Let *ABC*, *ADE* be two triangles such that the angles *BAC*, *DAE* are<br />
supplementary, and also
<p class="assertion">*CA* : *AD* = *EA* : *AB*.</p>

![Proposition 15, supplementary case](0227.g1.svg)

In this case we can place the triangles so that<br />
*CA* is in a straight line with *AD*, and *AB* lies<br />
along *AE* (since the angle *EAC*, being supple-<br />
mentary to the angle *EAD*, is equal to the angle<br />
*BAC*).<br />
If we join *BD*, the proof given by Euclid<br />
applies to this case also.

It is true that VI. 15 can be immediately inferred from VI. 14, since a<br />
triangle is half of a parallelogram with the same base and height. But,<br />
Euclid's object being to give the student a grasp of *methods* rather than<br />
results, there seems to be no advantage in deducing one proposition from the<br />
other instead of using the same method on each.

### PROPOSITION 16.

<p class="enunciation">If four straight lines be proportional, the rectangle con-<br />
tained by the extremes is equal to the rectangle contained by<br />
the means; and, if the rectangle contained by the extremes be<br />
equal to the rectangle contained by the means, the four straight<br />
lines will be proportional.</p>

Let the four straight lines *AB*, *CD*, *E*, *F* be proportional,<br />
so that,
<p class="assertion">as *AB* is to *CD*, so is *E* to *F*;</p>
I say that the rectangle contained by *AB*, *F* is equal to the<br />
rectangle contained by *CD*, *E*.

![Book VI Proposition 16](0227.g2.svg)
![Book VI Proposition 16 (cont.)](0227.g3.svg)

Let *AG*, *CH* be drawn from the points *A*, *C* at right<br />
angles to the straight lines *AB*, *CD*, and let *AG* be made<br />
equal to *F*, and *CH* equal to *E*.

Let the parallelograms *BG*, *DH* be completed.

Then since, as *AB* is to *CD*, so is *E* to *F*,<br />
<p class="sub-clause">while *E* is equal to *CH*, and *F* to *AG*,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore, as *AB* is to *CD*, so is *CH* to *AG*.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore in the parallelograms *BG*, *DH* the sides about<br />
the equal angles are reciprocally proportional.</p>
