:title: Proposition 16

Let :g:`$ABC$ <0227.g1:< A B C >>`, :g:`$ADE$ <0227.g1:< A D E >>` be two triangles such that the angles :g:`$BAC$ <0227.g1:< B A C )>`, :g:`$DAE$ <0227.g1:< D A E )>` are supplementary, and also

.. assertion::

   :g:`$CA$ <0227.g1:/ C A />` : :g:`$AD$ <0227.g1:/ A D />` = :g:`$EA$ <0227.g1:/ E A />` : :g:`$AB$ <0227.g1:/ A B />`.

.. picture:: 0227.g1.svg
   :alt: Proposition 15, supplementary case

In this case we can place the triangles so that :g:`$CA$ <0227.g1:/ C A />` is in a straight line with :g:`$AD$ <0227.g1:/ A D />`, and :g:`$AB$ <0227.g1:/ A B />` lies along :g:`$AE$ <0227.g1:/ A E />` (since the angle :g:`$EAC$ <0227.g1:< E A C )>`, being supplementary to the angle :g:`$EAD$ <0227.g1:< E A D )>`, is equal to the angle :g:`$BAC$ <0227.g1:< B A C )>`). If we join :g:`$BD$ <0227.g1:/ B D />`, the proof given by Euclid applies to this case also.

It is true that VI. 15 can be immediately inferred from VI. 14, since a triangle is half of a parallelogram with the same base and height. But, Euclid's object being to give the student a grasp of *methods* rather than results, there seems to be no advantage in deducing one proposition from the other instead of using the same method on each.

----

PROPOSITION 16.
---------------

.. enunciation::

   If four straight lines be proportional, the rectangle contained by the extremes is equal to the rectangle contained by the means; and, if the rectangle contained by the extremes be equal to the rectangle contained by the means, the four straight lines will be proportional.

Let the four straight lines :g:`$AB$ <0227.g2:/ A B />`, :g:`$CD$ <0227.g3:/ C D />`, :g:`$E$ <0227.g2:/ E />`, :g:`$F$ <0227.g3:/ F />` be proportional, so that,

.. assertion::

   as :g:`$AB$ <0227.g2:/ A B />` is to :g:`$CD$ <0227.g3:/ C D />`, so is :g:`$E$ <0227.g2:/ E />` to :g:`$F$ <0227.g3:/ F />`;

I say that the rectangle contained by :g:`$AB$ <0227.g2:/ A B />`, :g:`$F$ <0227.g3:/ F />` is equal to the rectangle contained by :g:`$CD$ <0227.g3:/ C D />`, :g:`$E$ <0227.g2:/ E />`.

.. picture:: 0227.g2.svg
   :alt: Book VI Proposition 16 (BG and E)

.. picture:: 0227.g3.svg
   :alt: Book VI Proposition 16 (DH and F)

Let :g:`$AG$ <0227.g2:/ A G />`, :g:`$CH$ <0227.g3:/ C H />` be drawn from the points :g:`$A$ <0227.g2:A>`, :g:`$C$ <0227.g3:C>` at right angles to the straight lines :g:`$AB$ <0227.g2:/ A B />`, :g:`$CD$ <0227.g3:/ C D />`, and let :g:`$AG$ <0227.g2:/ A G />` be made equal to :g:`$F$ <0227.g3:/ F />`, and :g:`$CH$ <0227.g3:/ C H />` equal to :g:`$E$ <0227.g2:/ E />`.

Let the parallelograms :g:`$BG$ <0227.g2:< B G >>`, :g:`$DH$ <0227.g3:< D H >>` be completed.

Then since, as :g:`$AB$ <0227.g2:/ A B />` is to :g:`$CD$ <0227.g3:/ C D />`, so is :g:`$E$ <0227.g2:/ E />` to :g:`$F$ <0227.g3:/ F />`, 

.. subclause::

   while :g:`$E$ <0227.g2:/ E />` is equal to :g:`$CH$ <0227.g3:/ C H />`, and :g:`$F$ <0227.g3:/ F />` to :g:`$AG$ <0227.g2:/ A G />`,

.. assertion::

   therefore, as :g:`$AB$ <0227.g2:/ A B />` is to :g:`$CD$ <0227.g3:/ C D />`, so is :g:`$CH$ <0227.g3:/ C H />` to :g:`$AG$ <0227.g2:/ A G />`.

.. assertion::

   Therefore in the parallelograms :g:`$BG$ <0227.g2:< B G >>`, :g:`$DH$ <0227.g3:< D H >>` the sides about the equal angles are reciprocally proportional.
