<p class="enunciation"><span data-ref="0229.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> meeting <span data-ref="0229.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> (*produced if necessary*) in <span data-ref="0229.g1:D">$D$</span> and <span data-ref="0229.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> meeting the circle again<br />
in <span data-ref="0229.g1:E">$E$</span>, such that the angles <span data-ref="0229.g1:< D A B )">$DAB$</span>, <span data-ref="0229.g1:< E A C )">$EAC$</span> are equal, then the rectangle <span data-ref="0229.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0229.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> is<br />
equal to the rectangle <span data-ref="0229.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span>, <span data-ref="0229.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>.</p>

![Proposition B general theorem (internal)](0229.g1.svg)
![Proposition B general theorem (external)](0229.g2.svg)

Join <span data-ref="0229.g1:/ C E /">$CE$</span>.

The angles <span data-ref="0229.g1:< B A D )">$BAD$</span>, <span data-ref="0229.g1:< E A C )">$EAC$</span> are equal, by hypothesis;<br />
and the angles <span data-ref="0229.g1:< A B D )">$ABD$</span>, <span data-ref="0229.g1:< A E C )">$AEC$</span> are equal. <span class="justification">[III. 21, 22]</span>

<p class="assertion">Therefore the triangles <span data-ref="0229.g1:< A B D >">$ABD$</span>, <span data-ref="0229.g1:< A E C >">$AEC$</span> are equiangular.</p>

Hence
<p class="assertion"><span data-ref="0229.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span> is to <span data-ref="0229.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> as <span data-ref="0229.g1:/ E A /">$EA$</span> is to <span data-ref="0229.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">and therefore the rectangle <span data-ref="0229.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span>, <span data-ref="0229.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is equal to the rectangle <span data-ref="0229.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0229.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span>.<br />
<span class="justification">[VI. 16]</span></p>

There are now two particular cases to be considered.

(*a*) Suppose that <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ A E /">$AE$</span> coincide;<br />
<span data-ref="0229.g3:/ A D E /">$ADE$</span> will then bisect the angle <span data-ref="0229.g3:< B A C )">$BAC$</span>.

(*b*) Suppose that <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ A E /">$AE$</span> are in one straight line but that <span data-ref="0229.g3:D">$D$</span>, <span data-ref="0229.g3:E">$E$</span> are on<br />
opposite sides of <span data-ref="0229.g3:A">$A$</span>;<br />
<span data-ref="0229.g3:/ A D /">$AD$</span> will then bisect the external angle at <span data-ref="0229.g3:A">$A$</span>.

![Proposition B particular cases (a) and (b)](0229.g3.svg)

In the first case (*a*) we have
<p class="assertion">the rectangle <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ B A /">$BA$</span>, <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ A C /">$AC$</span> equal to the rectangle <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ E A /">$EA$</span>, <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ A D /">$AD$</span>;</p>
<p class="sub-clause">and the rectangle <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ E A /">$EA$</span>, <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ A D /">$AD$</span> is equal to the rectangle <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ E D /">$ED$</span>, <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ D A /">$DA$</span> together with<br />
the square on <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span class="justification">[II. 3]</span><br />
i.e. to the rectangle <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ B D /">$BD$</span>, <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ D C /">$DC$</span> together with the square on <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ A D /">$AD$</span>. <span class="justification">[III. 35]</span></p>
<p class="assertion">Therefore the rectangle <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ B A /">$BA$</span>, <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is equal to the rectangle <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ B D /">$BD$</span>, <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ D C /">$DC$</span><br />
together with the square on <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ A D /">$AD$</span>. <span class="justification">[This is Simson's Prop. B]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">In case (*b*) the rectangle <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ E A /">$EA$</span>, <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ A D /">$AD$</span> is equal to the excess of the rectangle<br />
<span data-ref="0229.g3:/ E D /">$ED$</span>, <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ D A /">$DA$</span> over the square on <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ A D /">$AD$</span>;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the rectangle <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ B A /">$BA$</span>, <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is equal to the excess of the rectangle <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ B D /">$BD$</span>,<br />
<span data-ref="0229.g3:/ D C /">$DC$</span> over the square on <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ A D /">$AD$</span>.</p>
