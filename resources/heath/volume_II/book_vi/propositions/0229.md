*<span data-ref="0229.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> meeting <span data-ref="0229.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> (produced if necessary) in <span data-ref="0229.g1:D">$D$</span> and <span data-ref="0229.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> meeting the circle again in <span data-ref="0229.g1:E">$E$</span>, such that the angles <span data-ref="0229.g1:< D A B )">$DAB$</span>, <span data-ref="0229.g1:< E A C )">$EAC$</span> are equal, then the rectangle <span data-ref="0229.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0229.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> is equal to the rectangle <span data-ref="0229.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span>, <span data-ref="0229.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>.*

![Proposition B general theorem (internal)](0229.g1.svg)

![Proposition B general theorem (external)](0229.g2.svg)

Join <span data-ref="0229.g1:/ C E /">$CE$</span>.

The angles <span data-ref="0229.g1:< B A D )">$BAD$</span>, <span data-ref="0229.g1:< E A C )">$EAC$</span> are equal, by hypothesis;
and the angles <span data-ref="0229.g1:< A B D )">$ABD$</span>, <span data-ref="0229.g1:< A E C )">$AEC$</span> are equal. [III. 21, 22]

Therefore the triangles <span data-ref="0229.g1:< A B D >">$ABD$</span>, <span data-ref="0229.g1:< A E C >">$AEC$</span> are equiangular.
Hence

$$<span data-ref="0229.g1:/ B A /">BA</span> \text{ is to } <span data-ref="0229.g1:/ A D /">AD</span> \text{ as } <span data-ref="0229.g1:/ E A /">EA</span> \text{ is to } <span data-ref="0229.g1:/ A C /">AC</span>,$$

and therefore the rectangle <span data-ref="0229.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span>, <span data-ref="0229.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is equal to the rectangle <span data-ref="0229.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0229.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span>. [VI. 16]

There are now two particular cases to be considered.

(a) Suppose that <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ A E /">$AE$</span> coincide;
<span data-ref="0229.g3:/ A D E /">$ADE$</span> will then bisect the angle <span data-ref="0229.g3:< B A C )">$BAC$</span>.

(b) Suppose that <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ A E /">$AE$</span> are in one straight line but that <span data-ref="0229.g3:D">$D$</span>, <span data-ref="0229.g3:E">$E$</span> are on opposite sides of <span data-ref="0229.g3:A">$A$</span>;
<span data-ref="0229.g3:/ A D /">$AD$</span> will then bisect the external angle at <span data-ref="0229.g3:A">$A$</span>.

![Proposition B particular cases (a) and (b)](0229.g3.svg)

In the first case (a) we have the rectangle <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ B A /">$BA$</span>, <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ A C /">$AC$</span> equal to the rectangle <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ E A /">$EA$</span>, <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ A D /">$AD$</span>;
and the rectangle <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ E A /">$EA$</span>, <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ A D /">$AD$</span> is equal to the rectangle <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ E D /">$ED$</span>, <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ D A /">$DA$</span> together with the square on <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, [II. 3]
i.e. to the rectangle <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ B D /">$BD$</span>, <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ D C /">$DC$</span> together with the square on <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ A D /">$AD$</span>. [III. 35]
Therefore the rectangle <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ B A /">$BA$</span>, <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is equal to the rectangle <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ B D /">$BD$</span>, <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ D C /">$DC$</span> together with the square on <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ A D /">$AD$</span>. [This is Simson's Prop. B]

In case (b) the rectangle <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ E A /">$EA$</span>, <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ A D /">$AD$</span> is equal to the excess of the rectangle <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ E D /">$ED$</span>, <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ D A /">$DA$</span> over the square on <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ A D /">$AD$</span>;
therefore the rectangle <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ B A /">$BA$</span>, <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is equal to the excess of the rectangle <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ B D /">$BD$</span>, <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ D C /">$DC$</span> over the square on <span data-ref="0229.g3:/ A D /">$AD$</span>.
