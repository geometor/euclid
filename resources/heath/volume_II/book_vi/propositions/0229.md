<p class="enunciation">*AD* meeting *BC* (*produced if necessary*) in *D* and *AE* meeting the circle again<br />
in *E*, such that the angles *DAB*, *EAC* are equal, then the rectangle *AD*, *AE* is<br />
equal to the rectangle *BA*, *AC*.</p>

![Proposition B general theorem, internal](0229.g1.svg)
![Proposition B general theorem, external](0229.g2.svg)

Join *CE*.

The angles *BAD*, *EAC* are equal, by hypothesis;<br />
and the angles *ABD*, *AEC* are equal. <span class="justification">[III. 21, 22]</span>

<p class="assertion">Therefore the triangles *ABD*, *AEC* are equiangular.</p>

Hence
<p class="assertion">*BA* is to *AD* as *EA* is to *AC*,</p>
<p class="assertion">and therefore the rectangle *BA*, *AC* is equal to the rectangle *AD*, *AE*.<br />
<span class="justification">[VI. 16]</span></p>

There are now two particular cases to be considered.

(*a*) Suppose that *AD*, *AE* coincide;<br />
*ADE* will then bisect the angle *BAC*.

(*b*) Suppose that *AD*, *AE* are in one straight line but that *D*, *E* are on<br />
opposite sides of *A*;<br />
*AD* will then bisect the external angle at *A*.

![Proposition B particular cases (a) and (b)](0229.g3.svg)

In the first case (*a*) we have
<p class="assertion">the rectangle *BA*, *AC* equal to the rectangle *EA*, *AD*;</p>
<p class="sub-clause">and the rectangle *EA*, *AD* is equal to the rectangle *ED*, *DA* together with<br />
the square on *AD*, <span class="justification">[II. 3]</span><br />
i.e. to the rectangle *BD*, *DC* together with the square on *AD*. <span class="justification">[III. 35]</span></p>
<p class="assertion">Therefore the rectangle *BA*, *AC* is equal to the rectangle *BD*, *DC*<br />
together with the square on *AD*. <span class="justification">[This is Simson's Prop. B]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">In case (*b*) the rectangle *EA*, *AD* is equal to the excess of the rectangle<br />
*ED*, *DA* over the square on *AD*;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the rectangle *BA*, *AC* is equal to the excess of the rectangle *BD*,<br />
*DC* over the square on *AD*.</p>
