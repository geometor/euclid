The following converse of Simson's Prop. B may be given: *If a straight<br />
line AD be drawn from the vertex A of a triangle to meet the base, so that the<br />
square on AD together with the rectangle BD, DC is equal to the rectangle BA,<br />
AC, the line AD will bisect the angle BAC except when the sides AB, AC are<br />
equal, in which case every line drawn to the base will have the property men-<br />
tioned.*

Let the circumscribed circle be drawn, and let *AD* produced meet it in<br />
*E*; join *CE*.

<p class="assertion">The rectangle *BD*, *DC* is equal to the rectangle *ED*, *DA*. <span class="justification">[III. 35]</span></p>

Add to each the square on *AD*;<br />
<p class="assertion">therefore the rectangle *BA*, *AC* is equal to the rectangle *EA*, *AD*.<br />
<span class="justification">[hyp. and II. 3]</span></p>

Hence
<p class="assertion">*AB* is to *AD* as *AE* to *AC*. <span class="justification">[VI. 16]</span></p>
<p class="sub-clause">But the angle *ABD* is equal to the angle *AEC*. <span class="justification">[III. 21]</span></p>
<p class="assertion">Therefore the angles *BDA*, *ECA* are either equal or supplementary.<br />
<span class="justification">[VI. 7 and note]</span></p>

(*a*) If they are equal, the angles *BAD*, *EAC*<br />
are also equal, and *AD* bisects the angle *BAC*.

(*b*) If they are supplementary, the angle *ADC*<br />
must be equal to the angle *ACE*.

Therefore the angles *BAD*, *ABD* are together<br />
equal to the angles *ACB*, *BCE*, i.e. to the angles<br />
*ACD*, *BAD*.

Take away the common angle *BAD*, and
<p class="assertion">the angles *ABD*, *ACD* are equal, or<br />
*AB* is equal to *AC*.</p>

![Converse of Simson's Prop. B](0230.g1.svg)

Euclid himself assumes, in Prop. 67 of the *Data*, the result of so much of<br />
this proposition as relates to the case where *BA* = *AC*. He assumes namely,<br />
without proof, that, if *BA* = *AC*, and if *D* be any point on *BC*, the rectangle<br />
*BD*, *DC* together with the square on *AD* is equal to the square on *AB*.

### PROPOSITION C.

<p class="enunciation">If from any angle of a triangle a straight line be drawn perpendicular to the<br />
opposite side, the rectangle contained by the other two sides of the triangle is equal<br />
to the rectangle contained by the perpendicular and the diameter of the circle<br />
circumscribed about the triangle.</p>

Let *ABC* be a triangle and *AD* the perpendicular on *BC*. Draw the<br />
diameter *AE* of the circle circumscribed about the triangle *ABC*.

![Proposition C, obtuse triangle](0230.g2.svg)

<p class="assertion">Then shall the rectangle *BA*, *AC* be equal to the rectangle *EA*, *AD*.</p>
