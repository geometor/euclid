Since the right angle <span data-ref="0230.g3:< B D A )">$BDA$</span> is equal to the right angle <span data-ref="0230.g3:< E C A )">$ECA$</span> in a semi-circle, [III. 31]
and the angles <span data-ref="0230.g3:< A B D )">$ABD$</span>, <span data-ref="0230.g3:< A E C )">$AEC$</span> in the same segment are equal, [III. 21]
$$\text{the triangles <span data-ref="0230.g3:< A B D >">$ABD$</span>, <span data-ref="0230.g3:< A E C >">$AEC$</span> are equiangular.}$$
Therefore,
$$\text{as <span data-ref="0230.g3:/ B A /">$BA$</span> is to <span data-ref="0230.g3:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0230.g3:/ E A /">$EA$</span> to <span data-ref="0230.g3:/ A C /">$AC$</span>,}\quad\text{[VI. 4]}$$
whence the rectangle <span data-ref="0230.g3:/ B A /">$BA$</span>, <span data-ref="0230.g3:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is equal to the rectangle <span data-ref="0230.g3:/ E A /">$EA$</span>, <span data-ref="0230.g3:/ A D /">$AD$</span>. [VI. 16]

This result corresponds to the trigonometrical formula for $R$, the radius of the circumscribed circle,
$$R = \frac{abc}{4\Delta}.$$

### PROPOSITION D.

This is the highly important lemma given by Ptolemy (ed. Heiberg, Vol. I, pp. 36—7) which is the basis of his calculation of the table of chords in the section of Book I. of the μεγάλη σύνταξις entitled "concerning the size of the straight lines [i.e. chords] in the circle" (περὶ τῆς πηλικότητος τῶν ἐν τῷ κύκλῳ εὐθειῶν).

The theorem may be enunciated thus.

*The rectangle contained by the diagonals of any quadrilateral inscribed in a circle is equal to the sum of the rectangles contained by the pairs of opposite sides.*

I shall give the proof in Ptolemy's words, with the addition only, in brackets, of two words applying to a second figure not given by Ptolemy.

"Let there be a circle with any quadrilateral <span data-ref="0231.g2:< A B C D >">$ABCD$</span> inscribed in it, and let <span data-ref="0231.g2:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0231.g2:/ B D /">$BD$</span> be joined.

It is to be proved that the rectangle contained by <span data-ref="0231.g2:/ A C /">$AC$</span> and <span data-ref="0231.g2:/ B D /">$BD$</span> is equal to the sum of the rectangles <span data-ref="0231.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0231.g2:/ D C /">$DC$</span> and <span data-ref="0231.g2:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0231.g2:/ B C /">$BC$</span>.

For let the angle <span data-ref="0231.g2:< A B E )">$ABE$</span> be made equal to the angle contained by <span data-ref="0231.g2:/ D B /">$DB$</span>, <span data-ref="0231.g2:/ B C /">$BC$</span>.

![Proposition D (Ptolemy's figure)](0231.g2.svg)

![Proposition D (supplementary figure)](0231.g1.svg)

If then we add [or subtract] the angle <span data-ref="0231.g2:< E B D )">$EBD$</span>,
the angle <span data-ref="0231.g2:< A B D )">$ABD$</span> will also be equal to the angle <span data-ref="0231.g2:< E B C )">$EBC$</span>.

But the angle <span data-ref="0231.g2:< B D A )">$BDA$</span> is also equal to the angle <span data-ref="0231.g2:< B C E )">$BCE$</span>, [III. 21]
for they subtend the same segment;
therefore the triangle <span data-ref="0231.g2:< A B D >">$ABD$</span> is equiangular with the triangle <span data-ref="0231.g2:< E B C >">$EBC$</span>.

Hence, proportionally,
$$\text{as <span data-ref="0231.g2:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is to <span data-ref="0231.g2:/ C E /">$CE$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0231.g2:/ B D /">$BD$</span> to <span data-ref="0231.g2:/ D A /">$DA$</span>.}\quad\text{[VI. 4]}$$

Therefore the rectangle <span data-ref="0231.g2:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, <span data-ref="0231.g2:/ A D /">$AD$</span> is equal to the rectangle <span data-ref="0231.g2:/ B D /">$BD$</span>, <span data-ref="0231.g2:/ C E /">$CE$</span>. [VI. 16]

Again, since the angle <span data-ref="0231.g2:< A B E )">$ABE$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0231.g2:< D B C )">$DBC$</span>,
and the angle <span data-ref="0231.g2:< B A E )">$BAE$</span> is also equal to the angle <span data-ref="0231.g2:< B D C )">$BDC$</span>, [III. 21]
$$\text{the triangle <span data-ref="0231.g2:< A B E >">$ABE$</span> is equiangular with the triangle <span data-ref="0231.g2:< D B C >">$DBC$</span>.}$$
