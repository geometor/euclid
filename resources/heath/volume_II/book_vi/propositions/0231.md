Since the right angle <span data-ref="0230.g3:< B D A )">$BDA$</span> is equal to the right angle <span data-ref="0230.g3:< E C A )">$ECA$</span> in a semi-<br />
circle, <span class="justification">[III. 31]</span><br />
and the angles <span data-ref="0230.g3:< A B D )">$ABD$</span>, <span data-ref="0230.g3:< A E C )">$AEC$</span> in the same segment are equal, <span class="justification">[III. 21]</span>
<p class="assertion">the triangles <span data-ref="0230.g3:< A B D >">$ABD$</span>, <span data-ref="0230.g3:< A E C >">$AEC$</span> are equiangular.</p>

Therefore,
<p class="assertion">as <span data-ref="0230.g3:/ B A /">$BA$</span> is to <span data-ref="0230.g3:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0230.g3:/ E A /">$EA$</span> to <span data-ref="0230.g3:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span class="justification">[VI. 4]</span></p>
<p class="assertion">whence the rectangle <span data-ref="0230.g3:/ B A /">$BA$</span>, <span data-ref="0230.g3:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is equal to the rectangle <span data-ref="0230.g3:/ E A /">$EA$</span>, <span data-ref="0230.g3:/ A D /">$AD$</span>. <span class="justification">[VI. 16]</span></p>

This result corresponds to the trigonometrical formula for $R$, the radius of<br />
the circumscribed circle,

### PROPOSITION D.

This is the highly important lemma given by Ptolemy (ed. Heiberg, Vol. I,<br />
pp. 36—7) which is the basis of his calculation of the table of chords in the<br />
section of Book I. of the μεγάλη σύνταξις entitled "concerning the size of the<br />
straight lines [i.e. chords] in the circle" (περὶ τῆς πηλικότητος τῶν ἐν τῷ κύκλῳ<br />
εὐθειῶν).

The theorem may be enunciated thus.

<p class="enunciation">The rectangle contained by the diagonals of any quadrilateral inscribed in a<br />
circle is equal to the sum of the rectangles contained by the pairs of opposite sides.</p>

I shall give the proof in Ptolemy's words, with the addition only, in<br />
brackets, of two words applying to a second figure not given by Ptolemy.

"Let there be a circle with any quadrilateral <span data-ref="0231.g2:< A B C D >">$ABCD$</span> inscribed in it, and<br />
let <span data-ref="0231.g2:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0231.g2:/ B D /">$BD$</span> be joined.

It is to be proved that the rectangle contained by <span data-ref="0231.g2:/ A C /">$AC$</span> and <span data-ref="0231.g2:/ B D /">$BD$</span> is equal<br />
to the sum of the rectangles <span data-ref="0231.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0231.g2:/ D C /">$DC$</span> and <span data-ref="0231.g2:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0231.g2:/ B C /">$BC$</span>.

For let the angle <span data-ref="0231.g2:< A B E )">$ABE$</span> be made equal to the angle contained by <span data-ref="0231.g2:/ D B /">$DB$</span>, <span data-ref="0231.g2:/ B C /">$BC$</span>.

![Proposition D (Ptolemy's figure)](0231.g2.svg)
![Proposition D (supplementary figure)](0231.g1.svg)

If then we add [or subtract] the angle <span data-ref="0231.g2:< E B D )">$EBD$</span>,<br />
<p class="assertion">the angle <span data-ref="0231.g2:< A B D )">$ABD$</span> will also be equal to the angle <span data-ref="0231.g2:< E B C )">$EBC$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">But the angle <span data-ref="0231.g2:< B D A )">$BDA$</span> is also equal to the angle <span data-ref="0231.g2:< B C E )">$BCE$</span>, <span class="justification">[III. 21]</span><br />
for they subtend the same segment;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the triangle <span data-ref="0231.g2:< A B D >">$ABD$</span> is equiangular with the triangle <span data-ref="0231.g2:< E B C >">$EBC$</span>.</p>

Hence, proportionally,
<p class="assertion">as <span data-ref="0231.g2:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is to <span data-ref="0231.g2:/ C E /">$CE$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0231.g2:/ B D /">$BD$</span> to <span data-ref="0231.g2:/ D A /">$DA$</span>. <span class="justification">[VI. 4]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore the rectangle <span data-ref="0231.g2:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, <span data-ref="0231.g2:/ A D /">$AD$</span> is equal to the rectangle <span data-ref="0231.g2:/ B D /">$BD$</span>, <span data-ref="0231.g2:/ C E /">$CE$</span>.<br />
<span class="justification">[VI. 16]</span></p>

Again, since the angle <span data-ref="0231.g2:< A B E )">$ABE$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0231.g2:< D B C )">$DBC$</span>,<br />
and the angle <span data-ref="0231.g2:< B A E )">$BAE$</span> is also equal to the angle <span data-ref="0231.g2:< B D C )">$BDC$</span>, <span class="justification">[III. 21]</span>
<p class="assertion">the triangle <span data-ref="0231.g2:< A B E >">$ABE$</span> is equiangular with the triangle <span data-ref="0231.g2:< D B C >">$DBC$</span>.</p>
