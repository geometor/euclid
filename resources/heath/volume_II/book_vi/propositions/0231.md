Since the right angle *BDA* is equal to the right angle *ECA* in a semi-<br />
circle, <span class="justification">[III. 31]</span><br />
and the angles *ABD*, *AEC* in the same segment are equal, <span class="justification">[III. 21]</span>
<p class="assertion">the triangles *ABD*, *AEC* are equiangular.</p>

Therefore,
<p class="assertion">as *BA* is to *AD*, so is *EA* to *AC*, <span class="justification">[VI. 4]</span></p>
<p class="assertion">whence the rectangle *BA*, *AC* is equal to the rectangle *EA*, *AD*. <span class="justification">[VI. 16]</span></p>

This result corresponds to the trigonometrical formula for *R*, the radius of<br />
the circumscribed circle,
$$R = \frac{abc}{4\Delta}.$$

### PROPOSITION D.

This is the highly important lemma given by Ptolemy (ed. Heiberg, Vol. I,<br />
pp. 36—7) which is the basis of his calculation of the table of chords in the<br />
section of Book I. of the μεγάλη σύνταξις entitled "concerning the size of the<br />
straight lines [i.e. chords] in the circle" (περὶ τῆς πηλικότητος τῶν ἐν τῷ κύκλῳ<br />
εὐθειῶν).

The theorem may be enunciated thus.

<p class="enunciation">The rectangle contained by the diagonals of any quadrilateral inscribed in a<br />
circle is equal to the sum of the rectangles contained by the pairs of opposite sides.</p>

I shall give the proof in Ptolemy's words, with the addition only, in<br />
brackets, of two words applying to a second figure not given by Ptolemy.

"Let there be a circle with any quadrilateral *ABCD* inscribed in it, and<br />
let *AC*, *BD* be joined.

It is to be proved that the rectangle contained by *AC* and *BD* is equal<br />
to the sum of the rectangles *AB*, *DC* and *AD*, *BC*.

For let the angle *ABE* be made equal to the angle contained by *DB*, *BC*.

![Proposition D, first figure](0231.g2.svg)
![Proposition D, second figure](0231.g1.svg)

If then we add [or subtract] the angle *EBD*,<br />
<p class="assertion">the angle *ABD* will also be equal to the angle *EBC*.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">But the angle *BDA* is also equal to the angle *BCE*, <span class="justification">[III. 21]</span><br />
for they subtend the same segment;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the triangle *ABD* is equiangular with the triangle *EBC*.</p>

Hence, proportionally,
<p class="assertion">as *BC* is to *CE*, so is *BD* to *DA*. <span class="justification">[VI. 4]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore the rectangle *BC*, *AD* is equal to the rectangle *BD*, *CE*.<br />
<span class="justification">[VI. 16]</span></p>

Again, since the angle *ABE* is equal to the angle *DBC*,<br />
and the angle *BAE* is also equal to the angle *BDC*, <span class="justification">[III. 21]</span>
<p class="assertion">the triangle *ABE* is equiangular with the triangle *DBC*.</p>
