| Since the right angle :g:`$BDA$ <0230.g3:< B D A )>` is equal to the right angle :g:`$ECA$ <0230.g3:< E C A )>` in a semi-
| circle, :just:`[III. 31] <III.31>`
| and the angles :g:`$ABD$ <0230.g3:< A B D )>`, :g:`$AEC$ <0230.g3:< A E C )>` in the same segment are equal, :just:`[III. 21] <III.21>`

.. assertion::

   the triangles :g:`$ABD$ <0230.g3:< A B D >>`, :g:`$AEC$ <0230.g3:< A E C >>` are equiangular.

Therefore,

.. assertion:: [VI. 4] <VI.4>

   as :g:`$BA$ <0230.g3:/ B A />` is to :g:`$AD$ <0230.g3:/ A D />`, so is :g:`$EA$ <0230.g3:/ E A />` to :g:`$AC$ <0230.g3:/ A C />`,

.. assertion:: [VI. 16] <VI.16>

   whence the rectangle :g:`$BA$ <0230.g3:/ B A />`, :g:`$AC$ <0230.g3:/ A C />` is equal to the rectangle :g:`$EA$ <0230.g3:/ E A />`, :g:`$AD$ <0230.g3:/ A D />`.

| This result corresponds to the trigonometrical formula for $R$, the radius of
| the circumscribed circle,

$$R = \frac{abc}{4\Delta}.$$

PROPOSITION D.
--------------

| This is the highly important lemma given by :person:`Ptolemy <ptolemy>` (ed. :person:`Heiberg <heiberg>`, Vol. I,
| pp. 36—7) which is the basis of his calculation of the table of chords in the
| section of Book I. of the μεγάλη σύνταξις entitled "concerning the size of the
| straight lines [i.e. chords] in the circle" (περὶ τῆς πηλικότητος τῶν ἐν τῷ κύκλῳ
| εὐθειῶν).

The theorem may be enunciated thus.

.. enunciation::

   The rectangle contained by the diagonals of any quadrilateral inscribed in a circle is equal to the sum of the rectangles contained by the pairs of opposite sides.

| I shall give the proof in :person:`Ptolemy <ptolemy>`'s words, with the addition only, in
| brackets, of two words applying to a second figure not given by :person:`Ptolemy <ptolemy>`.

| "Let there be a circle with any quadrilateral :g:`$ABCD$ <0231.g2:< A B C D >>` inscribed in it, and
| let :g:`$AC$ <0231.g2:/ A C />`, :g:`$BD$ <0231.g2:/ B D />` be joined.

| It is to be proved that the rectangle contained by :g:`$AC$ <0231.g2:/ A C />` and :g:`$BD$ <0231.g2:/ B D />` is equal
| to the sum of the rectangles :g:`$AB$ <0231.g2:/ A B />`, :g:`$DC$ <0231.g2:/ D C />` and :g:`$AD$ <0231.g2:/ A D />`, :g:`$BC$ <0231.g2:/ B C />`.

For let the angle :g:`$ABE$ <0231.g2:< A B E )>` be made equal to the angle contained by :g:`$DB$ <0231.g2:/ D B />`, :g:`$BC$ <0231.g2:/ B C />`.

.. picture:: 0231.g2.svg
   :alt: Proposition D (Ptolemy's figure)

.. picture:: 0231.g1.svg
   :alt: Proposition D (supplementary figure)

If then we add [or subtract] the angle :g:`$EBD$ <0231.g2:< E B D )>`, 

.. assertion::

   the angle :g:`$ABD$ <0231.g2:< A B D )>` will also be equal to the angle :g:`$EBC$ <0231.g2:< E B C )>`.

.. subclause::

   | But the angle :g:`$BDA$ <0231.g2:< B D A )>` is also equal to the angle :g:`$BCE$ <0231.g2:< B C E )>`, :just:`[III. 21] <III.21>`
   | for they subtend the same segment;

.. assertion::

   therefore the triangle :g:`$ABD$ <0231.g2:< A B D >>` is equiangular with the triangle :g:`$EBC$ <0231.g2:< E B C >>`.

Hence, proportionally,

.. assertion:: [VI. 4] <VI.4>

   as :g:`$BC$ <0231.g2:/ B C />` is to :g:`$CE$ <0231.g2:/ C E />`, so is :g:`$BD$ <0231.g2:/ B D />` to :g:`$DA$ <0231.g2:/ D A />`.

.. assertion::

   | Therefore the rectangle :g:`$BC$ <0231.g2:/ B C />`, :g:`$AD$ <0231.g2:/ A D />` is equal to the rectangle :g:`$BD$ <0231.g2:/ B D />`, :g:`$CE$ <0231.g2:/ C E />`.
   | :just:`[VI. 16] <VI.16>`

| Again, since the angle :g:`$ABE$ <0231.g2:< A B E )>` is equal to the angle :g:`$DBC$ <0231.g2:< D B C )>`,
| and the angle :g:`$BAE$ <0231.g2:< B A E )>` is also equal to the angle :g:`$BDC$ <0231.g2:< B D C )>`, :just:`[III. 21] <III.21>`

.. assertion::

   the triangle :g:`$ABE$ <0231.g2:< A B E >>` is equiangular with the triangle :g:`$DBC$ <0231.g2:< D B C >>`.
