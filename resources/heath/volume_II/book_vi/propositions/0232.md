Another proof of this proposition, and of its converse, is indicated by<br />
Dr Lachlan (*Elements of Euclid*, pp. 273—4). It depends on two preliminary<br />
propositions.

(1) *If two circles be divided, by a chord in each, into segments which are<br />
similar respectively, the chords are proportional to the corresponding diameters.*

The proof is instantaneous if we join the ends of each chord to the centre<br />
of the circle which it divides, when we obtain two similar triangles.

(2) *If D be any point on the circle circumscribed about a triangle ABC, and<br />
DX, DY, DZ be perpendicular to the sides BC, CA, AB of the triangle<br />
respectively, then X, Y, Z lie in one straight line; and, conversely, if the feet of<br />
the perpendiculars from any point D on the sides of a triangle lie in one straight<br />
line, D lies on the circle circumscribed about the triangle.*

The proof depending on III. 21, 22 is well known.

Now suppose that *D* is any point in the plane of a triangle *ABC*, and<br />
that *DX*, *DY*, *DZ* are perpendicular to the sides<br />
*BC*, *CA*, *AB* respectively.

Join *YZ*, *DA*.

Then, since the angles at *Y*, *Z* are right,<br />
*A*, *Y*, *D*, *Z* lie on a circle of which *DA* is the<br />
diameter.

And *YZ* divides this circle into segments which<br />
are similar respectively to the segments into which<br />
*BC* divides the circle circumscribing *ABC*, since<br />
the angles *ZAY*, *BAC* coincide, and their supple-<br />
ments are equal.

Therefore, if *d* be the diameter of the circle<br />
circumscribing *ABC*,
<p class="assertion">*BC* is to *d* as *YZ* is to *DA*;</p>
<p class="assertion">and therefore the rectangle *AD*, *BC* is equal to the rectangle *d*, *YZ*.</p>

<p class="assertion">Similarly the rectangle *BD*, *CA* is equal to the rectangle *d*, *ZX*, and the<br />
rectangle *CD*, *AB* is equal to the rectangle *d*, *XY*.</p>

Hence, in a quadrilateral in general, the rectangle<br />
contained by the diagonals is less than the sum of the<br />
rectangles contained by the pairs of opposite sides.
