| Another proof of this proposition, and of its converse, is indicated by
| Dr Lachlan (*Elements of Euclid*, pp. 273—4). It depends on two preliminary
| propositions.

| (1) *If two circles be divided, by a chord in each, into segments which are
| similar respectively, the chords are proportional to the corresponding diameters.*

| The proof is instantaneous if we join the ends of each chord to the centre
| of the circle which it divides, when we obtain two similar triangles.

| (2) *If :g:`$D$ <0232.g2:D>` be any point on the circle circumscribed about a triangle :g:`$ABC$ <0232.g2:< A B C >>`, and
| :g:`$DX$ <0232.g2:/ D X />`, :g:`$DY$ <0232.g2:/ D Y />`, :g:`$DZ$ <0232.g2:/ D Z />` be perpendicular to the sides :g:`$BC$ <0232.g2:/ B C />`, :g:`$CA$ <0232.g2:/ C A />`, :g:`$AB$ <0232.g2:/ A B />` of the triangle
| respectively, then :g:`$X$ <0232.g2:X>`, :g:`$Y$ <0232.g2:Y>`, :g:`$Z$ <0232.g2:Z>` lie in one straight line; and, conversely, if the feet of
| the perpendiculars from any point :g:`$D$ <0232.g2:D>` on the sides of a triangle lie in one straight
| line, :g:`$D$ <0232.g2:D>` lies on the circle circumscribed about the triangle.*

The proof depending on III. 21, 22 is well known.

| Now suppose that :g:`$D$ <0232.g1:D>` is any point in the plane of a triangle :g:`$ABC$ <0232.g1:< A B C >>`, and
| that :g:`$DX$ <0232.g1:/ D X />`, :g:`$DY$ <0232.g1:/ D Y />`, :g:`$DZ$ <0232.g1:/ D Z />` are perpendicular to the sides
| :g:`$BC$ <0232.g1:/ B C />`, :g:`$CA$ <0232.g1:/ C A />`, :g:`$AB$ <0232.g1:/ A B />` respectively.

Join :g:`$YZ$ <0232.g1:/ Y Z />`, :g:`$DA$ <0232.g1:/ D A />`.

| Then, since the angles at :g:`$Y$ <0232.g1:Y>`, :g:`$Z$ <0232.g1:Z>` are right,
| :g:`$A$ <0232.g1:A>`, :g:`$Y$ <0232.g1:Y>`, :g:`$D$ <0232.g1:D>`, :g:`$Z$ <0232.g1:Z>` lie on a circle of which :g:`$DA$ <0232.g1:/ D A />` is the
| diameter.

| And :g:`$YZ$ <0232.g1:/ Y Z />` divides this circle into segments which
| are similar respectively to the segments into which
| :g:`$BC$ <0232.g1:/ B C />` divides the circle circumscribing :g:`$ABC$ <0232.g1:< A B C >>`, since
| the angles :g:`$ZAY$ <0232.g1:< Z A Y )>`, :g:`$BAC$ <0232.g1:< B A C )>` coincide, and their supple-
| ments are equal.

| Therefore, if *d* be the diameter of the circle
| circumscribing :g:`$ABC$ <0232.g1:< A B C >>`,

.. assertion::

   :g:`$BC$ <0232.g1:/ B C />` is to *d* as :g:`$YZ$ <0232.g1:/ Y Z />` is to :g:`$DA$ <0232.g1:/ D A />`;

.. assertion::

   and therefore the rectangle :g:`$AD$ <0232.g1:/ A D />`, :g:`$BC$ <0232.g1:/ B C />` is equal to the rectangle *d*, :g:`$YZ$ <0232.g1:/ Y Z />`.

.. assertion::

   | Similarly the rectangle :g:`$BD$ <0232.g1:/ B D />`, :g:`$CA$ <0232.g1:/ C A />` is equal to the rectangle *d*, :g:`$ZX$ <0232.g1:/ Z X />`, and the
   | rectangle :g:`$CD$ <0232.g1:/ C D />`, :g:`$AB$ <0232.g1:/ A B />` is equal to the rectangle *d*, :g:`$XY$ <0232.g1:/ X Y />`.

| Hence, in a quadrilateral in general, the rectangle
| contained by the diagonals is less than the sum of the
| rectangles contained by the pairs of opposite sides.
