### PROPOSITION E.

Lastly, the following proposition may be given which Playfair added as<br />
VI. E. It appears in the *Data* of Euclid, Prop. 93, and may be thus<br />
enunciated.

<p class="enunciation">If the angle *BAC* of a triangle *ABC* be bisected by the straight line *AD*<br />
meeting the circle circumscribed about the triangle in *D*, and if *BD* be joined,<br />
then<br />
the sum of *BA*, *AC* is to *AD* as *BC* is to *BD*.</p>

Join *CD*. Then, since *AD* bisects the angle *BAC*, the subtended arcs<br />
*BD*, *DC*, and therefore the chords *BD*, *DC*, are<br />
equal.

![Proposition E](0233.g1.svg)

(1) The result can now be easily deduced from<br />
Ptolemy's theorem.

For the rectangle *AD*, *BC* is equal to the sum of<br />
the rectangles *AB*, *DC* and *AC*, *BD*, *i.e.* (since<br />
*BD*, *CD* are equal) to the rectangle contained by<br />
*BA* + *AC* and *BD*.

<p class="assertion">Therefore the sum of *BA*, *AC* is to *AD* as *BC*<br />
is to *BD*. <span class="justification">[VI. 16]</span></p>

(2) Euclid proves it differently in *Data*, Prop. 93.

Let *AD* meet *BC* in *E*.

Then, since *AE* bisects the angle *BAC*,
<p class="assertion">*BA* is to *AC* as *BE* to *EC*, <span class="justification">[VI. 3]</span></p>
or, alternately,
<p class="assertion">*AB* is to *BE* as *AC* to *CE*. <span class="justification">[V. 16]</span></p>

Therefore also
<p class="assertion">*BA* + *AC* is to *BC* as *AC* to *CE*. <span class="justification">[V. 12]</span></p>

Again, since the angles *BAD*, *EAC* are equal, and the angles *ADB*, *ACE*<br />
are also equal, <span class="justification">[III. 21]</span>
<p class="assertion">the triangles *ABD*, *AEC* are equiangular.</p>

Therefore
<p class="assertion">*AC* is to *CE* as *AD* to *BD*. <span class="justification">[VI. 4]</span></p>
