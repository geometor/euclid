| $Conversely$, if the latter statement is true, while we are supposed to know
| nothing about the position of :g:`$D$ <0232.g2:D>`, it follows that

.. assertion::

   :g:`$XZ$ <0232.g2:/ X Z />` must be equal to the sum of :g:`$XY$ <0232.g2:/ X Y />`, :g:`$YZ$ <0232.g2:/ Y Z />`,

so that :g:`$X$ <0232.g2:X>`, :g:`$Y$ <0232.g2:Y>`, :g:`$Z$ <0232.g2:Z>` must be in a straight line.

| Hence, from the theorem (2) above, it follows that :g:`$D$ <0232.g2:D>` must lie on the
| circle circumscribed about :g:`$ABC$ <0232.g2:< A B C >>`, *i.e.* that :g:`$ABCD$ <0232.g2:< A B C D >>` is a quadrilateral about
| which a circle can be described.

| All the above propositions can be proved on the basis of Book III. and
| without using Book VI., since it is possible by the aid of III. 21 and 35 alone
| to prove that *in equiangular triangles the rectangles contained by the non-
| corresponding sides about equal angles are equal to one another* (a result arrived
| at by combining VI. 4 and VI. 16). This is the method adopted by Casey,
| H. M. Taylor, and Lachlan; but I fail to see any particular advantage in it.

PROPOSITION E.
--------------

| Lastly, the following proposition may be given which :person:`Playfair <playfair>` added as
| VI. E. It appears in the $Data$ of Euclid, Prop. 93, and may be thus
| enunciated.

.. enunciation::

   If the angle :g:`$BAC$ <0233.g1:< B A C )>` of a triangle :g:`$ABC$ <0233.g1:< A B C >>` be bisected by the straight line :g:`$AD$ <0233.g1:/ A D />` meeting the circle circumscribed about the triangle in :g:`$D$ <0233.g1:D>`, and if :g:`$BD$ <0233.g1:/ B D />` be joined, then the sum of :g:`$BA$ <0233.g1:/ B A />`, :g:`$AC$ <0233.g1:/ A C />` is to :g:`$AD$ <0233.g1:/ A D />` as :g:`$BC$ <0233.g1:/ B C />` is to :g:`$BD$ <0233.g1:/ B D />`.

| Join :g:`$CD$ <0233.g1:/ C D />`. Then, since :g:`$AD$ <0233.g1:/ A D />` bisects the angle :g:`$BAC$ <0233.g1:< B A C )>`, the subtended arcs
| :g:`$BD$ <0233.g1:( O B )< B O D >>`, :g:`$DC$ <0233.g1:( O D )< D O C >>`, and therefore the chords :g:`$BD$ <0233.g1:/ B D />`, :g:`$DC$ <0233.g1:/ D C />`, are
| equal.

.. picture:: 0233.g1.svg
   :alt: Proposition E

| (1) The result can now be easily deduced from
| :person:`Ptolemy <ptolemy>`'s theorem.

| For the rectangle :g:`$AD$ <0233.g1:/ A D />`, :g:`$BC$ <0233.g1:/ B C />` is equal to the sum of
| the rectangles :g:`$AB$ <0233.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$DC$ <0233.g1:/ D C />` and :g:`$AC$ <0233.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$BD$ <0233.g1:/ B D />`, *i.e.* (since
| :g:`$BD$ <0233.g1:/ B D />`, :g:`$CD$ <0233.g1:/ C D />` are equal) to the rectangle contained by
| :g:`$BA$ <0233.g1:/ B A />` + :g:`$AC$ <0233.g1:/ A C />` and :g:`$BD$ <0233.g1:/ B D />`.

.. assertion::

   | Therefore the sum of :g:`$BA$ <0233.g1:/ B A />`, :g:`$AC$ <0233.g1:/ A C />` is to :g:`$AD$ <0233.g1:/ A D />` as :g:`$BC$ <0233.g1:/ B C />`
   | is to :g:`$BD$ <0233.g1:/ B D />`. :just:`[VI. 16] <VI.16>`

(2) Euclid proves it differently in $Data$, Prop. 93.

Let :g:`$AD$ <0233.g1:/ A D />` meet :g:`$BC$ <0233.g1:/ B C />` in :g:`$E$ <0233.g1:E>`.

Then, since :g:`$AE$ <0233.g1:/ A E />` bisects the angle :g:`$BAC$ <0233.g1:< B A C )>`,

.. assertion:: [VI. 3] <VI.3>

   :g:`$BA$ <0233.g1:/ B A />` is to :g:`$AC$ <0233.g1:/ A C />` as :g:`$BE$ <0233.g1:/ B E />` to :g:`$EC$ <0233.g1:/ E C />`,

or, alternately,

.. assertion:: [V. 16] <V.16>

   :g:`$AB$ <0233.g1:/ A B />` is to :g:`$BE$ <0233.g1:/ B E />` as :g:`$AC$ <0233.g1:/ A C />` to :g:`$CE$ <0233.g1:/ C E />`.

Therefore also

.. assertion:: [V. 12] <V.12>

   :g:`$BA$ <0233.g1:/ B A />` + :g:`$AC$ <0233.g1:/ A C />` is to :g:`$BC$ <0233.g1:/ B C />` as :g:`$AC$ <0233.g1:/ A C />` to :g:`$CE$ <0233.g1:/ C E />`.

| Again, since the angles :g:`$BAD$ <0233.g1:< B A D )>`, :g:`$EAC$ <0233.g1:< E A C )>` are equal, and the angles :g:`$ADB$ <0233.g1:< A D B )>`, :g:`$ACE$ <0233.g1:< A C E )>`
| are also equal, :just:`[III. 21] <III.21>`

.. assertion::

   the triangles :g:`$ABD$ <0233.g1:< A B D >>`, :g:`$AEC$ <0233.g1:< A E C >>` are equiangular.

Therefore

.. assertion:: [VI. 4] <VI.4>

   :g:`$AC$ <0233.g1:/ A C />` is to :g:`$CE$ <0233.g1:/ C E />` as :g:`$AD$ <0233.g1:/ A D />` to :g:`$BD$ <0233.g1:/ B D />`.
