### PROPOSITION 17.

<p class="enunciation">If three straight lines be proportional, the rectangle con-<br />
tained by the extremes is equal to the square on the mean;<br />
and, if the rectangle contained by the extremes be equal to the<br />
square on the mean, the three straight lines will be proportional.</p>

Let the three straight lines *A*, *B*, *C* be proportional, so<br />
that, as *A* is to *B*, so is *B* to *C*;<br />
I say that the rectangle contained by *A*, *C* is equal to the<br />
square on *B*.

![Proposition 17](0234.g1.svg)

Let *D* be made equal to *B*.

Then, since, as *A* is to *B*, so is *B* to *C*,<br />
<p class="sub-clause">and *B* is equal to *D*,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore, as *A* is to *B*, so is *D* to *C*.</p>

But, if four straight lines be proportional, the rectangle<br />
contained by the extremes is equal to the rectangle contained<br />
by the means. <span class="justification">[VI. 16]</span>

<p class="assertion">Therefore the rectangle *A*, *C* is equal to the rectangle<br />
*B*, *D*.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">But the rectangle *B*, *D* is the square on *B*, for *B* is<br />
equal to *D*;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the rectangle contained by *A*, *C* is equal to the<br />
square on *B*.</p>

Next, let the rectangle *A*, *C* be equal to the square on *B*;<br />
I say that, as *A* is to *B*, so is *B* to *C*.
