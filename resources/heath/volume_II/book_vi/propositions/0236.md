Again, on the straight line <span data-ref="0235.g2:/ B G /">$BG$</span>, and at the points <span data-ref="0235.g2:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0235.g2:G">$G$</span> on it, let the angle <span data-ref="0235.g2:< B G H )">$BGH$</span> be constructed equal to the angle <span data-ref="0235.g1:< D F E )">$DFE$</span>, and the angle <span data-ref="0235.g2:< G B H )">$GBH$</span> equal to the angle <span data-ref="0235.g1:< F D E )">$FDE$</span>. [I. 23]

Therefore the remaining angle at <span data-ref="0235.g1:< F E D )">$E$</span> is equal to the remaining angle at <span data-ref="0235.g2:< G H B )">$H$</span>; [I. 32]
therefore the triangle <span data-ref="0235.g1:< F D E >">$FDE$</span> is equiangular with the triangle <span data-ref="0235.g2:< G B H >">$GBH$</span>;
therefore, proportionally, as <span data-ref="0235.g1:/ F D /">$FD$</span> is to <span data-ref="0235.g2:/ G B /">$GB$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0235.g1:/ F E /">$FE$</span> to <span data-ref="0235.g2:/ G H /">$GH$</span>, and <span data-ref="0235.g1:/ E D /">$ED$</span> to <span data-ref="0235.g2:/ H B /">$HB$</span>. [VI. 4]

But it was also proved that, as <span data-ref="0235.g1:/ F D /">$FD$</span> is to <span data-ref="0235.g2:/ G B /">$GB$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0235.g1:/ F C /">$FC$</span> to <span data-ref="0235.g2:/ G A /">$GA$</span>, and <span data-ref="0235.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> to <span data-ref="0235.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span>;
therefore also, as <span data-ref="0235.g1:/ F C /">$FC$</span> is to <span data-ref="0235.g2:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0235.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> to <span data-ref="0235.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, and <span data-ref="0235.g1:/ F E /">$FE$</span> to <span data-ref="0235.g2:/ G H /">$GH$</span>, and further <span data-ref="0235.g1:/ E D /">$ED$</span> to <span data-ref="0235.g2:/ H B /">$HB$</span>.

And, since the angle <span data-ref="0235.g1:< C F D )">$CFD$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0235.g2:< A G B )">$AGB$</span>,
and the angle <span data-ref="0235.g1:< D F E )">$DFE$</span> to the angle <span data-ref="0235.g2:< B G H )">$BGH$</span>,
therefore the whole angle <span data-ref="0235.g1:< C F E )">$CFE$</span> is equal to the whole angle <span data-ref="0235.g2:< A G H )">$AGH$</span>.

For the same reason
<p align="center">the angle <span data-ref="0235.g1:< C D E )">$CDE$</span> is also equal to the angle <span data-ref="0235.g2:< A B H )">$ABH$</span>.</p>
And the angle at <span data-ref="0235.g1:< F C D )">$C$</span> is also equal to the angle at <span data-ref="0235.g2:< G A B )">$A$</span>,
and the angle at <span data-ref="0235.g1:< F E D )">$E$</span> to the angle at <span data-ref="0235.g2:< G H B )">$H$</span>.

Therefore <span data-ref="0235.g2:< A H >">$AH$</span> is equiangular with <span data-ref="0235.g1:< C E >">$CE$</span>;
and they have the sides about their equal angles proportional;
therefore the rectilinear figure <span data-ref="0235.g2:< A H >">$AH$</span> is similar to the rectilinear figure <span data-ref="0235.g1:< C E >">$CE$</span>. [VI. Def. 1]

Therefore on the given straight line <span data-ref="0235.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span> the rectilinear figure <span data-ref="0235.g2:< A H >">$AH$</span> has been described similar and similarly situated to the given rectilinear figure <span data-ref="0235.g1:< C E >">$CE$</span>.
<p align="right">Q. E. F.</p>

Simson thinks the proof of this proposition has been vitiated, his grounds for this view being (1) that it is demonstrated only with reference to quadrilaterals, and does not show how it may be extended to figures of five or more sides, (2) that Euclid infers, from the fact of two triangles being equiangular, that a side of the one is to the corresponding side of the other as another side of the first is to the side corresponding to it in the other, *i.e.* he permutes, without mentioning the fact that he does so, the proportions obtained in VI. 4, whereas the proof of the very next proposition gives, in a similar case, the intermediate step of permutation.
