Nor is the first objection of any importance. We have only to take as the

![Extension to polygon](0237.g1.svg)

given polygon a polygon of five sides at least, as *CDEFG*, join one extremity<br />
of *CD*, say *D*, to each of the angular points other than *C* and *E*, and then<br />
use the same mode of construction as Euclid's for any number of successive<br />
triangles as *ABL*, *LBK*, etc., that may have to be made. Euclid's con-<br />
struction and proof for a quadrilateral are quite sufficient to show how to deal<br />
with the case of a figure of five or any greater number of sides.

Clavius has a construction which, given the power of moving a figure

![Clavius's construction](0237.g2.svg)

bodily from one position to any other, is easier. *CDEFG* being the given<br />
polygon, join *CE*, *CF*. Place *AB* on *CD* so that *A* falls on *C*, and let *B*<br />
fall on $D'$, which may either lie on *CD* or on *CD* produced.

Now draw $D'E'$ parallel to *DE*, meeting *CE*, produced if necessary, in $E'$,<br />
$E'F'$ parallel to *EF*, meeting *CF*, produced if necessary, in $F'$, and so on.

Let the parallel to the last side but one, *FG*, meet *CG*, produced if<br />
necessary, in $G'$.

Then $CD'E'F'G'$ is similar and similarly situated to *CDEFG*, and it is<br />
constructed on $CD'$, a straight line equal to *AB*.

The proof of this is obvious.

A more general construction is indicated in the subjoined figure. If<br />
*CDEFG* be the given polygon, suppose its angular points all joined to any<br />
point $O$ and the connecting straight lines produced both ways. Then, if $C'D'$,<br />
a straight line equal to *AB*, be placed so that it is parallel to *CD*, and $C'$, $D'$<br />
lie respectively on *OC*, *OD* (this can of course be done by finding fourth<br />
proportionals), we have only to draw $D'E'$, $E'F'$, etc., parallel to the<br />
corresponding sides of the original polygon in the manner shown.
