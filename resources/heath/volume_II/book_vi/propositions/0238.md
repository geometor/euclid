De Morgan would rearrange Props. 18 and 20 in the following manner. He would combine Prop. 18 and the first part of Prop. 20 into one, with the enunciation :

*Pairs of similar triangles, similarly put together, give similar figures ; and every pair of similar figures is composed of pairs of similar triangles similarly put together.*

He would then make the *problem* of VI. 18 an application of the first part. In form this would certainly appear to be an improvement; but, provided that the relation of the propositions is understood, the matter of form is perhaps not of great importance.

### PROPOSITION 19.

*Similar triangles are to one another in the duplicate ratio of the corresponding sides.*

Let <span data-ref="0238.g2:&lt; A B C &gt;">$ABC$</span>, <span data-ref="0238.g2:&lt; D E F &gt;">$DEF$</span> be similar triangles having the angle at <span data-ref="0238.g2:&lt; A B C )">$B$</span> equal to the angle at <span data-ref="0238.g2:&lt; D E F )">$E$</span>, and such that, as <span data-ref="0238.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0238.g2:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, so <sup>5</sup>is <span data-ref="0238.g2:/ D E /">$DE$</span> to <span data-ref="0238.g2:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, so that <span data-ref="0238.g2:/ B C /">$BC$</span> corresponds to <span data-ref="0238.g2:/ E F /">$EF$</span>; [V. Def. 11]

I say that the triangle <span data-ref="0238.g2:&lt; A B C &gt;">$ABC$</span> has to the triangle <span data-ref="0238.g2:&lt; D E F &gt;">$DEF$</span> a ratio duplicate of that which <span data-ref="0238.g2:/ B C /">$BC$</span> has to <span data-ref="0238.g2:/ E F /">$EF$</span>.
