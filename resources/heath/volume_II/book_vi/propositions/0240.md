of the *necessary operation*, as well as the theoretical proof, in this proposition<br />
seems to me to have been distinctly worth while, and, as it is somewhat<br />

accordance with the plan of enabling the difficulties of Book VI. to be more<br />
easily and gradually surmounted to give the simpler case first.

That Euclid wished to emphasise the importance of the *method* adopted,<br />
as well as of the result obtained, in VI. 19 seems to me clearly indicated by<br />
the Porism which follows the proposition. It is as if he should say: "I have<br />
shown you that similar triangles are to one another in the duplicate ratio of<br />
corresponding sides; but I have also shown you incidentally how it is possible<br />
to work conveniently with duplicate ratios, viz. by transforming them into<br />
simple ratios between straight lines. I shall have occasion to illustrate the<br />
use of this method in the proof of VI. 22."

The Porism to VI. 19 presents one difficulty. It will be observed that it<br />
speaks of the *figure* (εἶδος) described on the first straight line and of that which<br />
is similar and similarly described on the second. If "*figure*" could be<br />
regarded as loosely used for the figure of the proposition, *i.e.* for a *triangle*,<br />
there would be no difficulty. If on the other hand "the figure" means any<br />
rectilineal figure, *i.e.* any polygon, the Porism is not really established until<br />
the next proposition, VI. 20, has been proved, and therefore it is out of place<br />
here. Yet the correction τρίγωνον, *triangle*, for εἶδος, *figure*, is due to Theon<br />
alone; P and Campanus have "figure," and the reading of Philoponus and<br />
Psellus, τετράγωνον, *square*, partly supports εἶδος, since it can be reconciled with<br />
εἶδος but not with τρίγωνον. Again the second Porism to VI. 20, in which this<br />
Porism is reasserted for any rectilineal figure, and which is omitted by<br />
Campanus and only given by P in the margin, was probably interpolated by<br />
Theon. Heiberg concludes that Euclid wrote "*figure*" (εἶδος), and Theon,<br />
seeing the difficulty, changed the word into "*triangle*" here and added Por. 2<br />
to VI. 20 in order to make the matter clear. If one may hazard a guess as to<br />
how Euclid made the slip, may it be that he first put it after VI. 20 and then,<br />
observing that the expression of the duplicate ratio by a single ratio between<br />
two straight lines does not come in VI. 20 but in VI. 19, moved the Porism to<br />
the end of VI. 19 in order to make the connexion clearer, without noticing<br />
that, if this were done, εἶδος would need correction?
