### PROPOSITION 20.

<p class="enunciation">Similar polygons are divided into similar triangles, and<br />
into triangles equal in multitude and in the same ratio as<br />
the wholes, and the polygon has to the polygon a ratio duplicate<br />
of that which the corresponding side has to the corresponding<br />
<sup>5</sup>side.</p>

Let *ABCDE*, *FGHKL* be similar polygons, and let *AB*<br />
correspond to *FG*;<br />
I say that the polygons *ABCDE*, *FGHKL* are divided into<br />
similar triangles, and into triangles equal in multitude and in<br />
<sup>10</sup>the same ratio as the wholes, and the polygon *ABCDE* has<br />
to the polygon *FGHKL* a ratio duplicate of that which *AB*<br />
has to *FG*.

Let *BE*, *EC*, *GL*, *LH* be joined.

![Book VI Proposition 20 Diagram](0241.g1.svg)

<p class="sub-clause">Now, since the polygon *ABCDE* is similar to the polygon<br />
<sup>15</sup>*FGHKL*,</p>
<p class="assertion">the angle *BAE* is equal to the angle *GFL*;</p>
<p class="assertion">and, as *BA* is to *AE*, so is *GF* to *FL*. <span class="justification">[VI. Def. 1]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">Since then *ABE*, *FGL* are two triangles having one<br />
angle equal to one angle and the sides about the equal angles<br />
<sup>20</sup>proportional,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the triangle *ABE* is equiangular with the triangle<br />
*FGL*; <span class="justification">[VI. 6]</span></p>
<p class="assertion">so that it is also similar; <span class="justification">[VI. 4 and Def. 1]</span></p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the angle *ABE* is equal to the angle *FGL*.</p>
