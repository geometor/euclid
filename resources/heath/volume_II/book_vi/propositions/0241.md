### PROPOSITION 20.

<p class="enunciation">Similar polygons are divided into similar triangles, and<br />
into triangles equal in multitude and in the same ratio as<br />
the wholes, and the polygon has to the polygon a ratio duplicate<br />
of that which the corresponding side has to the corresponding<br />
<sup>5</sup>side.</p>

Let <span data-ref="0241.g1:< A B C D E >">$ABCDE$</span>, <span data-ref="0241.g1:< F G H K L >">$FGHKL$</span> be similar polygons, and let <span data-ref="0241.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span><br />
correspond to <span data-ref="0241.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span>;<br />
I say that the polygons <span data-ref="0241.g1:< A B C D E >">$ABCDE$</span>, <span data-ref="0241.g1:< F G H K L >">$FGHKL$</span> are divided into<br />
similar triangles, and into triangles equal in multitude and in<br />
<sup>10</sup>the same ratio as the wholes, and the polygon <span data-ref="0241.g1:< A B C D E >">$ABCDE$</span> has<br />
to the polygon <span data-ref="0241.g1:< F G H K L >">$FGHKL$</span> a ratio duplicate of that which <span data-ref="0241.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span><br />
has to <span data-ref="0241.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span>.

Let <span data-ref="0241.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span>, <span data-ref="0241.g1:/ E C /">$EC$</span>, <span data-ref="0241.g1:/ G L /">$GL$</span>, <span data-ref="0241.g1:/ L H /">$LH$</span> be joined.

![Book VI Proposition 20 Diagram](0241.g1.svg)

<p class="sub-clause">Now, since the polygon <span data-ref="0241.g1:< A B C D E >">$ABCDE$</span> is similar to the polygon<br />
<sup>15</sup><span data-ref="0241.g1:< F G H K L >">$FGHKL$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">the angle <span data-ref="0241.g1:< B A E )">$BAE$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0241.g1:< G F L )">$GFL$</span>;</p>
<p class="assertion">and, as <span data-ref="0241.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span> is to <span data-ref="0241.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0241.g1:/ G F /">$GF$</span> to <span data-ref="0241.g1:/ F L /">$FL$</span>. <span class="justification">[VI. Def. 1]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">Since then <span data-ref="0241.g1:< A B E >">$ABE$</span>, <span data-ref="0241.g1:< F G L >">$FGL$</span> are two triangles having one<br />
angle equal to one angle and the sides about the equal angles<br />
<sup>20</sup>proportional,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the triangle <span data-ref="0241.g1:< A B E >">$ABE$</span> is equiangular with the triangle<br />
<span data-ref="0241.g1:< F G L >">$FGL$</span>; <span class="justification">[VI. 6]</span></p>
<p class="assertion">so that it is also similar; <span class="justification">[VI. 4 and Def. 1]</span></p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the angle <span data-ref="0241.g1:< A B E )">$ABE$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0241.g1:< F G L )">$FGL$</span>.</p>
