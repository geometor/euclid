**2.** **in the same ratio as the wholes.** The same word ὁμόλογος is used which I have generally translated by "corresponding." But here it is followed by a dative, ὁμόλογα τοῖς ὅλοις " _homologous with the wholes_," instead of being used absolutely. The meaning can therefore here be nothing else but "in the same ratio with" or "proportional to the wholes"; and Euclid seems to recognise that he is making a special use of the word, because he explains it lower down (l. 46): "the triangles are homologous to the wholes, that is, in such manner that the triangles are proportional, and <span data-ref="0241.g1:< A B E >">$ABE$</span>, <span data-ref="0241.g1:< E B C >">$EBC$</span>, <span data-ref="0241.g1:< E C D >">$ECD$</span> are antecedents, while <span data-ref="0241.g1:< F G L >">$FGL$</span>, <span data-ref="0241.g1:< L G H >">$LGH$</span>, <span data-ref="0241.g1:< L H K >">$LHK$</span> are their consequents."

**49.** **ἑπόμενα αὐτῶν, "their consequents,"** is a little awkward, but may be supposed to indicate which triangles correspond to which as consequent to antecedent.

An alternative proof of the second part of this proposition given after the Porisms is relegated by August and Heiberg to an Appendix as an interpolation. It is shorter than the proof in the text, and is the only one given by many editors, including Clavius, Billingsley, Barrow and Simson. It runs as follows:

"We will now also prove that the triangles are homologous in another and an easier manner.

Again, let the polygons <span data-ref="0244.g1:< A B C D E >">$ABCDE$</span>, <span data-ref="0244.g1:< F G H K L >">$FGHKL$</span> be set out, and let <span data-ref="0244.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span>, <span data-ref="0244.g1:/ E C /">$EC$</span>, <span data-ref="0244.g1:/ G L /">$GL$</span>, <span data-ref="0244.g1:/ L H /">$LH$</span> be joined.

I say that, as the triangle <span data-ref="0244.g1:< A B E >">$ABE$</span> is to <span data-ref="0244.g1:< F G L >">$FGL$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0244.g1:< E B C >">$EBC$</span> to <span data-ref="0244.g1:< L G H >">$LGH$</span> and <span data-ref="0244.g1:< C D E >">$CDE$</span> to <span data-ref="0244.g1:< H K L >">$HKL$</span>.

For, since the triangle <span data-ref="0244.g1:< A B E >">$ABE$</span> is similar to the triangle <span data-ref="0244.g1:< F G L >">$FGL$</span>, the triangle <span data-ref="0244.g1:< A B E >">$ABE$</span> has to the triangle <span data-ref="0244.g1:< F G L >">$FGL$</span> a ratio duplicate of that which <span data-ref="0244.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span> has to <span data-ref="0244.g1:/ G L /">$GL$</span>.
