**2.** **in the same ratio as the wholes.** The same word ὁμόλογος is used which I have<br />
generally translated by "corresponding." But here it is followed by a dative, ὁμόλογα τοῖς<br />
ὅλοις " *homologous with the wholes*," instead of being used absolutely. The meaning can<br />
therefore here be nothing else but "in the same ratio with" or "proportional to the<br />
wholes"; and Euclid seems to recognise that he is making a special use of the word,<br />
because he explains it lower down (l. 46): "the triangles are homologous to the wholes, that<br />
is, in such manner that the triangles are proportional, and <span data-ref="0241.g1:< A B E >">$ABE$</span>, <span data-ref="0241.g1:< E B C >">$EBC$</span>, <span data-ref="0241.g1:< E C D >">$ECD$</span> are ante-<br />
cedents, while <span data-ref="0241.g1:< F G L >">$FGL$</span>, <span data-ref="0241.g1:< L G H >">$LGH$</span>, <span data-ref="0241.g1:< L H K >">$LHK$</span> are their consequents."

**49.** **ἑπόμενα αὐτῶν, "their consequents,"** is a little awkward, but may be supposed to<br />
indicate which triangles correspond to which as consequent to antecedent.

An alternative proof of the second part of this proposition given after the<br />
Porisms is relegated by August and Heiberg to an Appendix as an interpolation.<br />
It is shorter than the proof in the text, and is the only one given by many<br />
editors, including Clavius, Billingsley, Barrow and Simson. It runs as follows:

"We will now also prove that the triangles are homologous in another and<br />
an easier manner.
