Now Euclid cannot fail to have noticed that the second part of his<br />
proposition could be proved in this way. It seems therefore that, in giving<br />
the other and longer method, he deliberately wished to avoid using the result<br />
of VI. 19, preferring to prove the first two parts of the theorem, as they can be<br />
proved, independently of any relation between the areas of similar triangles.

The first part of the Porism, stating that the theorem is true of *quadrilaterals*,<br />
would be superfluous but for the fact that technically, according to Book I.<br />
Def. 19, the term "polygon" (or figure of many sides, πολύπλευρον) used in the<br />
enunciation of the proposition is confined to rectilineal figures of *more than<br />
four sides*, so that a quadrilateral might seem to be excluded. The mention<br />
of the triangle in addition fills up the tale of "similar rectilineal figures."

The second Porism, Theon's interpolation, given in the text by the editors,<br />
but bracketed by Heiberg, is as follows:

"And, if we take $O$ a third proportional to <span data-ref="0241.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0241.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span>, then <span data-ref="0241.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span> has to $O$ a<br />
ratio duplicate of that which <span data-ref="0241.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> has to <span data-ref="0241.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span>.<br />
But the polygon has also to the polygon, or the quadrilateral to the<br />
quadrilateral, a ratio duplicate of that which the corresponding side has to<br />
the corresponding side, that is <span data-ref="0241.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> to <span data-ref="0241.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span>;<br />
and this was proved in the case of triangles also;<br />
so that it is also manifest generally that, if three straight lines be proportional,<br />
as the first is to the third, so will the figure described on the first be to the<br />
similar and similarly described figure on the second."

### PROPOSITION 21.

<p class="enunciation">Figures which are similar to the same rectilineal figure<br />
are also similar to one another.</p>

For let each of the rectilineal figures <span data-ref="0246.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0246.g1:B">$B$</span> be similar to <span data-ref="0246.g1:C">$C$</span>;<br />
I say that <span data-ref="0246.g1:A">$A$</span> is also similar to <span data-ref="0246.g1:B">$B$</span>.
