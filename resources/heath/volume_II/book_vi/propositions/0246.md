For, since <span data-ref="0246.g1:A">$A$</span> is similar to <span data-ref="0246.g1:C">$C$</span>,

Again, since <span data-ref="0246.g1:B">$B$</span> is similar to <span data-ref="0246.g1:C">$C$</span>,
it is equiangular with it and has the sides about the equal
angles proportional.
Therefore each of the figures <span data-ref="0246.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0246.g1:B">$B$</span> is equiangular with <span data-ref="0246.g1:C">$C$</span>

therefore <span data-ref="0246.g1:A">$A$</span> is similar to <span data-ref="0246.g1:B">$B$</span>.

It will be observed that the text above omits a step which the editions generally have before the final inference "Therefore <span data-ref="0246.g1:A">$A$</span> is similar to <span data-ref="0246.g1:B">$B$</span>." The words omitted are "so that <span data-ref="0246.g1:A">$A$</span> is also equiangular with <span data-ref="0246.g1:B">$B$</span> and [with <span data-ref="0246.g1:B">$B$</span>] has the sides about the equal angles proportional." Heiberg follows P in leaving them out, conjecturing that they may be an addition of Theon's.

### PROPOSITION 22.

*If four straight lines be proportional, the rectilineal figures similar and similarly described upon them will also be proportional; and, if the rectilineal figures similar and similarly described upon them be proportional, the straight lines will themselves also be proportional.*

Let the four straight lines <span data-ref="0247.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0247.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, <span data-ref="0247.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, <span data-ref="0247.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span> be proportional,
so that, as <span data-ref="0247.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0247.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0247.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> to <span data-ref="0247.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span>,
and let there be described on <span data-ref="0247.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0247.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> the similar and similarly situated rectilineal figures <span data-ref="0247.g1:< K A B >">$KAB$</span>, <span data-ref="0247.g1:< L C D >">$LCD$</span>,
and on <span data-ref="0247.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, <span data-ref="0247.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span> the similar and similarly situated rectilineal figures <span data-ref="0247.g1:< M F >">$MF$</span>, <span data-ref="0247.g1:< N H >">$NH$</span>;
I say that, as <span data-ref="0247.g1:< K A B >">$KAB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0247.g1:< L C D >">$LCD$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0247.g1:< M F >">$MF$</span> to <span data-ref="0247.g1:< N H >">$NH$</span>.

For let there be taken a third proportional <span data-ref="0247.g1:O">$O$</span> to <span data-ref="0247.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0247.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>,
and a third proportional <span data-ref="0247.g1:P">$P$</span> to <span data-ref="0247.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, <span data-ref="0247.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span>. [VI. 11]
