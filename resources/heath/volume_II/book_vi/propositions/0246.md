<p class="sub-clause">For, since *A* is similar to *C*,</p>
<p class="assertion">it is equiangular with it and has the sides about the equal<br />
angles proportional. <span class="justification">[VI. Def. 1]</span></p>

![Book VI, Proposition 21](0246.g1.svg)

<p class="sub-clause">Again, since *B* is similar to *C*,</p>
<p class="assertion">it is equiangular with it and has the sides about the equal<br />
angles proportional.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore each of the figures *A*, *B* is equiangular with *C*<br />
and with *C* has the sides about the equal angles proportional;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore *A* is similar to *B*.</p>

<p class="qed"><span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

---

It will be observed that the text above omits a step which the editions<br />
generally have before the final inference "Therefore *A* is similar to *B*." The<br />
words omitted are "so that *A* is also equiangular with *B* and [with *B*] has the<br />
sides about the equal angles proportional." Heiberg follows *P* in leaving<br />
them out, conjecturing that they may be an addition of Theon's.

### PROPOSITION 22.

<p class="enunciation">If four straight lines be proportional, the rectilineal figures<br />
similar and similarly described upon them will also be pro-<br />
portional; and, if the rectilineal figures similar and similarly<br />
described upon them be proportional, the straight lines will<br />
themselves also be proportional.</p>

Let the four straight lines *AB*, *CD*, *EF*, *GH* be pro-<br />
portional,<br />
so that, as *AB* is to *CD*, so is *EF* to *GH*,<br />
and let there be described on *AB*, *CD* the similar and similarly<br />
situated rectilineal figures *KAB*, *LCD*,<br />
and on *EF*, *GH* the similar and similarly situated rectilineal<br />
figures *MF*, *NH*;<br />
I say that, as *KAB* is to *LCD*, so is *MF* to *NH*.

For let there be taken a third proportional *O* to *AB*, *CD*,<br />
and a third proportional *P* to *EF*, *GH*. <span class="justification">[VI. 11]</span>
