<p class="sub-clause">Again, since <span data-ref="0246.g1:B">$B$</span> is similar to <span data-ref="0246.g1:C">$C$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">it is equiangular with it and has the sides about the equal<br />

<p class="assertion">Therefore each of the figures <span data-ref="0246.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0246.g1:B">$B$</span> is equiangular with <span data-ref="0246.g1:C">$C$</span><br />

<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0246.g1:A">$A$</span> is similar to <span data-ref="0246.g1:B">$B$</span>.</p>

It will be observed that the text above omits a step which the editions<br />
generally have before the final inference "Therefore <span data-ref="0246.g1:A">$A$</span> is similar to <span data-ref="0246.g1:B">$B$</span>." The<br />
words omitted are "so that <span data-ref="0246.g1:A">$A$</span> is also equiangular with <span data-ref="0246.g1:B">$B$</span> and [with <span data-ref="0246.g1:B">$B$</span>] has the<br />
sides about the equal angles proportional." Heiberg follows $P$ in leaving<br />
them out, conjecturing that they may be an addition of Theon's.

### PROPOSITION 22.

<p class="enunciation">If four straight lines be proportional, the rectilineal figures<br />
similar and similarly described upon them will also be pro-<br />
portional; and, if the rectilineal figures similar and similarly<br />
described upon them be proportional, the straight lines will<br />
themselves also be proportional.</p>

Let the four straight lines <span data-ref="0247.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0247.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, <span data-ref="0247.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, <span data-ref="0247.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span> be pro-<br />
portional,<br />
so that, as <span data-ref="0247.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0247.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0247.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> to <span data-ref="0247.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span>,<br />
and let there be described on <span data-ref="0247.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0247.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> the similar and similarly<br />
situated rectilineal figures <span data-ref="0247.g1:< K A B >">$KAB$</span>, <span data-ref="0247.g1:< L C D >">$LCD$</span>,<br />
and on <span data-ref="0247.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, <span data-ref="0247.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span> the similar and similarly situated rectilineal<br />
figures <span data-ref="0247.g1:< M F >">$MF$</span>, <span data-ref="0247.g1:< N H >">$NH$</span>;<br />
I say that, as <span data-ref="0247.g1:< K A B >">$KAB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0247.g1:< L C D >">$LCD$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0247.g1:< M F >">$MF$</span> to <span data-ref="0247.g1:< N H >">$NH$</span>.

For let there be taken a third proportional <span data-ref="0247.g1:O">$O$</span> to <span data-ref="0247.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0247.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>,<br />
and a third proportional <span data-ref="0247.g1:P">$P$</span> to <span data-ref="0247.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, <span data-ref="0247.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span>. <span class="justification">[VI. 11]</span>
