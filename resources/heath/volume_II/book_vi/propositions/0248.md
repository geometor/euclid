The second assumption in the first step of the first part of the proof, viz.<br />
that, as <span data-ref="0247.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> is to <span data-ref="0247.g1:O">$O$</span>, so <span data-ref="0247.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span> to <span data-ref="0247.g1:P">$P$</span>, should perhaps be explained. It is a<br />
deduction [by V. 11] from the facts that
<p align="center"><span data-ref="0247.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0247.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> as <span data-ref="0247.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> to <span data-ref="0247.g1:O">$O$</span>, <br />
<span data-ref="0247.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> is to <span data-ref="0247.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span> as <span data-ref="0247.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span> to <span data-ref="0247.g1:P">$P$</span>, <br />
and <span data-ref="0247.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0247.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> as <span data-ref="0247.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> to <span data-ref="0247.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span>.</p>

The defect in the proof of this proposition is well known, namely the<br />
assumption, without proof, that, because the figures <span data-ref="0247.g1:< N H >">$NH$</span>, <span data-ref="0247.g1:< S R >">$SR$</span> are equal,<br />
besides being similar and similarly situated, their corresponding sides <span data-ref="0247.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span>, <span data-ref="0247.g1:/ Q R /">$QR$</span><br />
are equal. Hence the minimum addition necessary to make the proof<br />
complete is a proof of a lemma to the effect that, *if two similar figures are also<br />
equal, any pair of corresponding sides are equal*.

To supply this lemma is one alternative; another is to prove, as a<br />
preliminary proposition, a much more general theorem, viz. that, *if the<br />
duplicate ratios of two ratios are equal, the two ratios are themselves equal*.<br />
When this is proved, the second part of VI. 22 is an immediate inference from<br />
it, and the effect is, of course, to substitute a new proof instead of<br />
supplementing Euclid's.

**I.** It is to be noticed that the lemma required as a minimum is very like<br />
what is needed to supplement VI. 28 and 29, in the proofs of which Euclid<br />
assumes that, *if two similar parallelograms are unequal, any side in the greater<br />
is greater than the corresponding side in the smaller*. Therefore, on the whole, it<br />
seems preferable to adopt the alternative of proving the simpler lemma which<br />
will serve to supplement all three proofs, viz. that, *if of two similar rectilineal<br />
figures the first is greater than, equal to, or less than, the second, any side of the<br />
first is greater than, equal to, or less than, the corresponding side of the second<br />
respectively*.

The case of equality of the figures is the case required for VI. 22; and the<br />
proof of it is given in the Greek text after the proposition. Since to give such<br />
a "lemma" after the proposition in which it is required is contrary to Euclid's<br />
manner, Heiberg concludes that it is an interpolation, though it is earlier than<br />
Theon. The lemma runs thus:

"But that, if rectilineal figures be equal and similar, their corresponding<br />
sides are equal to one another we will prove thus.

Let <span data-ref="0247.g1:< N H >">$NH$</span>, <span data-ref="0247.g1:< S R >">$SR$</span> be equal and similar rectilineal figures, and suppose that,
<p align="center">as <span data-ref="0247.g1:/ H G /">$HG$</span> is to <span data-ref="0247.g1:/ G N /">$GN$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0247.g1:/ R Q /">$RQ$</span> to <span data-ref="0247.g1:/ Q S /">$QS$</span>;</p>
I say that
<p align="center"><span data-ref="0247.g1:/ R Q /">$RQ$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0247.g1:/ H G /">$HG$</span>.</p>
For, if they are unequal, one of them is greater;<br />
let <span data-ref="0247.g1:/ R Q /">$RQ$</span> be greater than <span data-ref="0247.g1:/ H G /">$HG$</span>.
