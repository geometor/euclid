The lemma might also be arrived at by proving that, *if a ratio is greater than<br />
a ratio of equality, the ratio which is its duplicate is also greater than a ratio of<br />
equality; and if the ratio which is duplicate of another ratio is greater than a ratio<br />
of equality, the ratio of which it is the duplicate is also greater than a ratio<br />
of equality*. It is not difficult to prove this from the particular case of V. 25 in<br />
which the second magnitude is equal to the third, i.e. from the fact that in<br />
this case the sum of the extreme terms is greater than double the middle term.

**II.** We now come to the alternative which substitutes a new proof for the<br />
second part of the proposition, making the whole proposition an immediate<br />
inference from one to which it is practically equivalent, viz. that

(1) *If two ratios be equal, their duplicate ratios are equal, and* (2) *con-<br />
versely, if the duplicate ratios of two ratios be equal, the ratios are equal*.

The proof of part (1) is after the manner of Euclid's own proof of the first<br />
part of VI. 22.

Let $A$ be to $B$ as $C$ to $D$,<br />
and let $X$ be a third proportional to $A$, $B$, and $Y$ a third proportional to $C$, $D$,<br />
so that
$$\begin{align*}
&A\text{ is to }B\text{ as }B\text{ to }X\text{,} \\
\text{and }&C\text{ is to }D\text{ as }D\text{ to }Y\text{;}
\end{align*}$$
whence $A$ is to $X$ in the duplicate ratio of $A$ to $B$,<br />
and $C$ is to $Y$ in the duplicate ratio of $C$ to $D$.<br />
Since
$$A\text{ is to }B\text{ as }C\text{ to }D\text{,}$$
and
$$\begin{align*}
B\text{ is to }X&\text{ as }A\text{ to }B\text{,} \\
&\text{i.e. as }C\text{ to }D\text{,} \\
&\text{i.e. as }D\text{ to }Y\text{,} &\text{[V. 11]}
\end{align*}$$
therefore, *ex aequali*,
$$A\text{ is to }X\text{ as }C\text{ to }Y\text{.}$$
