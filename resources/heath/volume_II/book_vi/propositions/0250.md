Two proofs which are given of the proposition depend upon the following<br />
lemma.

*If A, B, C be three magnitudes of one kind, and D, E, F three magnitudes<br />
of one kind, then, if*
$$\begin{align*}
&\textit{the ratio of A to B is greater than that of D to E,} \\
\textit{and }&\textit{the ratio of B to C greater than that of E to F,} \\
\textit{ex aequali, }&\textit{the ratio of A to C is greater than that of D to F.}
\end{align*}$$

One proof of this does not depend upon the assumption referred to, and<br />
therefore, if this proof is used, the theorem can be added to Book V. The<br />
proof is that of Hauber (Camerer's Euclid, p. 358 of Vol. II.) and is reproduced<br />
by Mr H. M. Taylor. For brevity we will use symbols.

Take equimultiples *mA*, *mD* of *A*, *D* and *nB*, *nE* of *B*, *E* such that
$$mA > nB\text{, but } mD \ngtr nE\text{.}$$
Also let *pB*, *pE* be equimultiples of *B*, *E* and *qC*, *qF* equimultiples of<br />
*C*, *F* such that
$$pB > qC\text{, but } pE \ngtr qF\text{.}$$
Therefore, multiplying the first line by *p* and the second by *n*, we have
$$\begin{align*}
&pmA > pnB\text{, } pmD \ngtr pnE\text{,} \\
\text{and }&npB > nqC\text{, } npE \ngtr nqF\text{,} \\
\text{whence }&pmA > nqC\text{, } pmD \ngtr nqF\text{.}
\end{align*}$$
Now *pmA*, *pmD* are equimultiples of *mA*, *mD*,<br />
and *nqC*, *nqF* equimultiples of *qC*, *qF*.<br />
Therefore [V. 3] they are respectively equimultiples of *A*, *D* and of *C*, *F*.<br />
Hence [V. Def. 7]
$$A : C > D : F\text{.}$$
