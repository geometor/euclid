$$\begin{align*}
\text{But}\quad\quad\quad\quad A : B &> D : E\text{.} \\
\text{Therefore, \textit{a fortiori},}\quad\quad A : G &> D : E\text{.}
\end{align*}$$
Suppose $H$ taken such that
$$H : G = D : E\text{.}$$
$$\begin{align*}
\text{Therefore}\quad\quad\quad\quad A &> H\text{.} &\text{[V. 13, 10]} \\
\text{Hence}\quad\quad\quad\quad A : C &> H : C\text{.} &\text{[V. 8]} \\
\text{But}\quad\quad\quad\quad H : G &= D : E\text{,} \\
G : C &= E : F\text{.} \\
\text{Therefore, \textit{ex aequali},}\quad\quad H : C &= D : F\text{.} &\text{[V. 22]} \\
\text{Hence}\quad\quad\quad\quad A : C &> D : F\text{.} &\text{[V. 13]}
\end{align*}$$

Now we can prove that<br />
*Ratios of which equal ratios are duplicate are equal.*
$$\begin{align*}
\text{Suppose that}\quad\quad\quad\quad A : B &= B : C\text{,} \\
\text{and}\quad\quad\quad\quad D : E &= E : F\text{,} \\
\text{and further that}\quad\quad\quad\quad A : C &= D : F\text{.}
\end{align*}$$
it is required to prove that
$$A : B = D : E\text{.}$$
For, if not, one of the ratios must be greater than the other.<br />
Let $A : B$ be the greater.<br />
Then, since
$$\begin{align*}
A : B &= B : C\text{,} \\
\text{and}\quad\quad\quad\quad D : E &= E : F\text{,} \\
\text{while}\quad\quad\quad\quad A : B &> D : E\text{,} \\
\text{it follows that}\quad\quad\quad\quad B : C &> E : F\text{.} &\text{[V. 13]}
\end{align*}$$
Hence, by the lemma, *ex aequali*,
$$A : C > D : F\text{,}$$
which contradicts the hypothesis.<br />
Thus the ratios $A : B$ and $D : E$ cannot be unequal; that is, they are equal.

Another proof, given by Dr Lachlan, also assumes the existence of a<br />
fourth proportional, *but depends upon a simpler lemma to the effect that*<br />
*It is impossible that two different ratios can have the same duplicate ratio.*<br />
For, if possible, let the ratio $A : B$ be duplicate both of $A : X$ and $A : Y$,<br />
so that
$$\begin{align*}
A : X &= X : B\text{,} \\
\text{and}\quad\quad\quad\quad A : Y &= Y : B\text{.}
\end{align*}$$
Let $X$ be greater than $Y$.
$$\begin{align*}
\text{Then}\quad\quad\quad\quad A : X &< A : Y\text{;} &\text{[V. 8]} \\
\text{that is,}\quad\quad\quad\quad X : B &< Y : B\text{,} &\text{[V. 11, 13]} \\
\text{or}\quad\quad\quad\quad X &< Y\text{.} &\text{[V. 10]}
\end{align*}$$
But $X$ is greater than $Y$ : which is absurd, etc.
$$\text{Hence}\quad\quad\quad\quad X = Y\text{.}$$
