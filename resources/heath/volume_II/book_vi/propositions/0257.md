Since each parallelogram in the figure of the proposition can be divided<br />
into pairs of equal triangles, and all the triangles which are the halves of either<br />
parallelogram have two sides respectively equal and the angles included by<br />
them equal or supplementary, it can be at once deduced from VI. 23 (or it<br />
can be independently proved by the same method) that *triangles which have<br />
one angle of the one equal or supplementary to one angle of the other are in the<br />
ratio compounded of the ratios of the sides about the equal or supplementary<br />
angles*. Cf. Pappus VII. pp. 894—6.

VI. 23 also shows that *rectangles, and therefore parallelograms or triangles,<br />
are to one another in the ratio compounded of the ratios of their bases and<br />
heights*.

The converse of VI. 23 is also true, as is easily proved by *reductio ad<br />
absurdum*. More generally, *if two parallelograms or triangles are in the ratio<br />
compounded of the ratios of two adjacent sides, the angles included by those sides<br />
are either equal or supplementary*.

### PROPOSITION 24.

<p class="enunciation">In any parallelogram the parallelograms about the diameter<br />
are similar both to the whole and to one another.</p>

Let <span data-ref="0257.g1:< A B C D >">$ABCD$</span> be a parallelogram, and <span data-ref="0257.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> its diameter,<br />
and let <span data-ref="0257.g1:< E G >">$EG$</span>, <span data-ref="0257.g1:< H K >">$HK$</span> be parallelograms<br />
about <span data-ref="0257.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> ;<br />
I say that each of the parallelograms<br />
<span data-ref="0257.g1:< E G >">$EG$</span>, <span data-ref="0257.g1:< H K >">$HK$</span> is similar both to the whole<br />
<span data-ref="0257.g1:< A B C D >">$ABCD$</span> and to the other.
