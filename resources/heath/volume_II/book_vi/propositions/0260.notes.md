## Mathematical & Historical Commentary
- Proposition VI. 25 solves the classical problem of constructing a figure with the shape (*form*) of one figure and the area (*size*) of another: $X \sim ABC$ and $\text{Area}(X) = \text{Area}(D)$.
- Method: Euclid applies a parallelogram equal to $ABC$ on $BC$, extends $BC$ to $F$ with a parallelogram equal to $D$, finds the mean proportional $GH$ between $BC$ and $CF$ via VI. 13, and constructs on $GH$ a figure similar to $ABC$ by VI. 18. By VI. 19 Porism, similar figures are in the duplicate ratio of corresponding sides, which is $BC : CF = \text{Area}(ABC) : \text{Area}(D)$, yielding $\text{Area}(KGH) = D$.
