The other observation is more important. After Euclid has proved that
$$\text{(fig. } ABC\text{) : (fig. } KGH\text{)} = (BE) : (EF),$$
he might have inferred *directly* from V. 14 that, since *ABC* is equal to *BE*,<br />
*KGH* is equal to *EF*. For V. 14 includes the proof of the fact that, if *A* is<br />
to *B* as *C* is to *D*, and *A* is equal to *C*, then *B* is equal to *D*, or that of four<br />
proportional magnitudes, if the first is equal to the third, the second is equal<br />
to the fourth. Instead of proceeding in this way, Euclid first permutes the<br />
proportion by V. 16 into
$$\text{(fig. } ABC\text{) : }(BE) = \text{(fig. } KGH\text{) : }(EF),$$
and then infers, as if the inference were easier in this form, that, since the<br />
*first* is equal to the *second*, the *third* is equal to the *fourth*. Yet there is no<br />
proposition to this effect in Euclid. The same unnecessary step of permutation<br />
is also found in the Greek text of XI. 23 and XII. 2, 5, 11, 12 and 18. In<br />
reproducing the proofs we may simply leave out the steps and refer to V. 14.

### PROPOSITION 26.

<p class="enunciation">If from a parallelogram there be taken away a parallelo-<br />
gram similar and similarly situated to the whole and having<br />
a common angle with it, it is about the same diameter with the<br />
whole.</p>

For from the parallelogram *ABCD* let there be taken<br />
away the parallelogram *AF* similar and<br />
similarly situated to *ABCD*, and having<br />
the angle *DAB* common with it ;

I say that *ABCD* is about the same<br />
diameter with *AF*.

![Proposition 26](0261.g1.svg)

<p class="sub-clause">For suppose it is not, but, if possible,<br />
let *AHC* be the diameter &lt;*of ABCD*&gt;,<br />
let *GF* be produced and carried through<br />
to *H*, and let *HK* be drawn through *H*<br />
parallel to either of the straight lines *AD*, *BC*. <span class="justification">[I. 31]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">Since, then, *ABCD* is about the same diameter with *KG*,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore, as *DA* is to *AB*, so is *GA* to *AK*. <span class="justification">[VI. 24]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But also, because of the similarity of *ABCD*, *EG*,</p>
<p class="assertion">as *DA* is to *AB*, so is *GA* to *AE* ;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore also, as *GA* is to *AK*, so is *GA* to *AE*. <span class="justification">[V. 11]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore *GA* has the same ratio to each of the straight<br />
lines *AK*, *AE*.</p>
