<p align="center">(fig. <span data-ref="0259.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span>) : (fig. <span data-ref="0259.g1:< K G H >">$KGH$</span>) = (<span data-ref="0259.g1:< B L E C >">$BE$</span>) : (<span data-ref="0259.g1:< C E M F >">$EF$</span>),</p>

<p align="center">(fig. <span data-ref="0259.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span>) : (<span data-ref="0259.g1:< B L E C >">$BE$</span>) = (fig. <span data-ref="0259.g1:< K G H >">$KGH$</span>) : (<span data-ref="0259.g1:< C E M F >">$EF$</span>),</p>

proposition to this effect in Euclid. The same unnecessary step of permutation<br />
is also found in the Greek text of XI. 23 and XII. 2, 5, 11, 12 and 18. In<br />
reproducing the proofs we may simply leave out the steps and refer to V. 14.

### PROPOSITION 26.

<p class="enunciation">If from a parallelogram there be taken away a parallelo-<br />
gram similar and similarly situated to the whole and having<br />
a common angle with it, it is about the same diameter with the<br />
whole.</p>

For from the parallelogram <span data-ref="0261.g1:< A B C D >">$ABCD$</span> let there be taken<br />
away the parallelogram <span data-ref="0261.g1:< A E F G >">$AF$</span> similar and<br />
similarly situated to <span data-ref="0261.g1:< A B C D >">$ABCD$</span>, and having<br />
the angle <span data-ref="0261.g1:< D A B )">$DAB$</span> common with it ;

I say that <span data-ref="0261.g1:< A B C D >">$ABCD$</span> is about the same<br />
diameter with <span data-ref="0261.g1:< A E F G >">$AF$</span>.

![Proposition 26](0261.g1.svg)

<p class="sub-clause">For suppose it is not, but, if possible,<br />
let <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ A H C /">$AHC$</span> be the diameter &lt;*of <span data-ref="0261.g1:< A B C D >">$ABCD$</span>*&gt;,<br />
let <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ G F /">$GF$</span> be produced and carried through<br />
to <span data-ref="0261.g1:H">$H$</span>, and let <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ K H /">$HK$</span> be drawn through <span data-ref="0261.g1:H">$H$</span><br />
parallel to either of the straight lines <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>. <span class="justification">[I. 31]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">Since, then, <span data-ref="0261.g1:< A B C D >">$ABCD$</span> is about the same diameter with <span data-ref="0261.g1:< A K H G >">$KG$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore, as <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ A D /">$DA$</span> is to <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ A G /">$GA$</span> to <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ A K /">$AK$</span>. <span class="justification">[VI. 24]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But also, because of the similarity of <span data-ref="0261.g1:< A B C D >">$ABCD$</span>, <span data-ref="0261.g1:< A E F G >">$EG$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">as <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ A D /">$DA$</span> is to <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ A G /">$GA$</span> to <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> ;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore also, as <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ A G /">$GA$</span> is to <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ A K /">$AK$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ A G /">$GA$</span> to <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span>. <span class="justification">[V. 11]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ A G /">$GA$</span> has the same ratio to each of the straight<br />
lines <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ A K /">$AK$</span>, <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span>.</p>
