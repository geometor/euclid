## Textual & Lexical Observations
- **Textual Insertion**: Heath inserts `<of ABCD>` in brackets in the reductio premise: "let *AHC* be the diameter <*of ABCD*>". This clarifies the ambiguous Greek phrase `ἔστω αὐτῶν διάμετρος ἡ ΑΘΓ` discussed at length in the commentary on folio 0262.
- **Cross-References in Commentary**: Heath notes the repetitive, redundant use of permutation (V. 16) in Euclid's proofs where direct inference by V. 14 would suffice; cites parallel instances in Greek text at XI. 23 and XII. 2, 5, 11, 12, 18.

## Mathematical & Historical Commentary
- Proposition VI. 26 is the converse of VI. 24: If a parallelogram similar and similarly situated to the whole is subtracted sharing a corner angle, their diagonals coincide (they are "about the same diameter").
- Proof method is *reductio ad absurdum*: assuming the diameter of $ABCD$ is a different straight line $AHC$, meeting produced $GF$ at $H$, drawing $HK \parallel AD$, and deducing via VI. 24 and V. 11 that $GA : AK = GA : AE$, which forces $AE = AK$, contradicting $AK > AE$.
