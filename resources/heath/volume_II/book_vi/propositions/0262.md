<p class="assertion">Therefore <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ A K /">$AK$</span> <span class="justification">[V. 9]</span>, the less to the<br />
greater : which is impossible.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore <span data-ref="0261.g1:< A B C D >">$ABCD$</span> cannot but be about the same diameter<br />
with <span data-ref="0261.g1:< A E F G >">$AF$</span> ;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the parallelogram <span data-ref="0261.g1:< A B C D >">$ABCD$</span> is about the same diameter<br />
with the parallelogram <span data-ref="0261.g1:< A E F G >">$AF$</span>.</p>

<p class="qed">Therefore etc.<span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

---

**"For suppose it is not, but, if possible, let <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ A H C /">$AHC$</span> be the diameter."** What<br />
is meant is "For, if <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ A C /">$AFC$</span> is not the diameter of the parallelogram <span data-ref="0261.g1:< A B C D >">$AC$</span>, let<br />
<span data-ref="0261.g1:/ A H C /">$AHC$</span> be its diameter." The Greek text has ἔστω αὐτῶν διάμετρος ἡ ΑΘΓ ;<br />
but clearly αὐτῶν is wrong, as we cannot assume that one straight line is the<br />
diameter of both parallelograms, which is just what we have to prove. F and<br />
V omit the αὐτῶν, and Heiberg prefers this correction to substituting αὐτοῦ<br />
after Peyrard. I have inserted "&lt;of <span data-ref="0261.g1:< A B C D >">$ABCD$</span>&gt;" to make the meaning clear.

If the straight line <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ A H C /">$AHC$</span> does not pass through <span data-ref="0261.g1:F">$F$</span>, it must meet either<br />
<span data-ref="0261.g1:/ G F /">$GF$</span> or <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ G H /">$GF$</span> produced in some point <span data-ref="0261.g1:H">$H$</span>. The reading in the text "and let<br />
<span data-ref="0261.g1:/ G F /">$GF$</span> be *produced and carried through to <span data-ref="0261.g1:H">$H$</span>*" (καὶ ἐκβληθεῖσα ἡ ΗΖ διήχθω ἐπὶ<br />
τὸ Θ) corresponds to the supposition that <span data-ref="0261.g1:H">$H$</span> is on <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ G F /">$GF$</span> produced. The words<br />
were left out by Theon, evidently because in the figure of the MSS. the letters<br />
E, Z and K, Θ were interchanged. Heiberg therefore, following August, has<br />
preferred to retain the words and to correct the figure, as well as the passage in<br />
the text where <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span>, <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ A K /">$AK$</span> were interchanged to be in accord with the MS. figure.

It is of course possible to prove the proposition directly, as is done by<br />
Dr Lachlan. Let <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span>, <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> be the diagonals, and let us make no assumption<br />
as to how they fall.

Then, since <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> is parallel to <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span> and therefore to <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>,<br />
the angles <span data-ref="0261.g1:< A E F )">$AEF$</span>, <span data-ref="0261.g1:< A B C )">$ABC$</span> are equal.

And, since the parallelograms are similar,
<p align="center"><span data-ref="0261.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> : <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> = <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> : <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>. [VI. Def. 1]</p>

Hence the triangles <span data-ref="0261.g1:< A E F >">$AEF$</span>, <span data-ref="0261.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> are similar, <span class="justification">[VI. 6]</span><br />
and therefore the angle <span data-ref="0261.g1:< F A E )">$FAE$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0261.g1:< F A E )">$CAB$</span>.

Therefore <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span> falls on <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>.

The proposition is equally true if the parallelogram which is similar and<br />
similarly situated to the given parallelogram is not "taken<br />
away" from it, but is so placed that it is entirely outside the<br />
other, while two sides form an angle vertically opposite to<br />
an angle of the other. In this case the diameters are not<br />
"the same," in the words of the enunciation, but are in<br />
a straight line with one another. This extension of the<br />
proposition is, as will be seen, necessary for obtaining,<br />
according to the method adopted by Euclid in his solu-<br />
tion of the problem in VI. 28, the second solution of that<br />
problem.
