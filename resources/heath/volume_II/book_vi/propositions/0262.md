<p class="assertion">Therefore *AE* is equal to *AK* <span class="justification">[V. 9]</span>, the less to the<br />
greater : which is impossible.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore *ABCD* cannot but be about the same diameter<br />
with *AF* ;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the parallelogram *ABCD* is about the same diameter<br />
with the parallelogram *AF*.</p>

<p class="qed">Therefore etc.<span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

---

**"For suppose it is not, but, if possible, let *AHC* be the diameter."** What<br />
is meant is "For, if *AFC* is not the diameter of the parallelogram *AC*, let<br />
*AHC* be its diameter." The Greek text has ἔστω αὐτῶν διάμετρος ἡ ΑΘΓ ;<br />
but clearly αὐτῶν is wrong, as we cannot assume that one straight line is the<br />
diameter of both parallelograms, which is just what we have to prove. F and<br />
V omit the αὐτῶν, and Heiberg prefers this correction to substituting αὐτοῦ<br />
after Peyrard. I have inserted "&lt;of *ABCD*&gt;" to make the meaning clear.

If the straight line *AHC* does not pass through *F*, it must meet either<br />
*GF* or *GF* produced in some point *H*. The reading in the text "and let<br />
*GF* be *produced and carried through to H*" (καὶ ἐκβληθεῖσα ἡ ΗΖ διήχθω ἐπὶ<br />
τὸ Θ) corresponds to the supposition that *H* is on *GF* produced. The words<br />
were left out by Theon, evidently because in the figure of the MSS. the letters<br />
E, Z and K, Θ were interchanged. Heiberg therefore, following August, has<br />
preferred to retain the words and to correct the figure, as well as the passage in<br />
the text where *AE*, *AK* were interchanged to be in accord with the MS. figure.

It is of course possible to prove the proposition directly, as is done by<br />
Dr Lachlan. Let *AF*, *AC* be the diagonals, and let us make no assumption<br />
as to how they fall.

Then, since *EF* is parallel to *AG* and therefore to *BC*,<br />
the angles *AEF*, *ABC* are equal.

And, since the parallelograms are similar,
$$AE : EF = AB : BC.\qquad\text{[VI. Def. 1]}$$

Hence the triangles *AEF*, *ABC* are similar, <span class="justification">[VI. 6]</span><br />
and therefore the angle *FAE* is equal to the angle *CAB*.

Therefore *AF* falls on *AC*.

The proposition is equally true if the parallelogram which is similar and<br />
similarly situated to the given parallelogram is not "taken<br />
away" from it, but is so placed that it is entirely outside the<br />
other, while two sides form an angle vertically opposite to<br />
an angle of the other. In this case the diameters are not<br />
"the same," in the words of the enunciation, but are in<br />
a straight line with one another. This extension of the<br />
proposition is, as will be seen, necessary for obtaining,<br />
according to the method adopted by Euclid in his solu-<br />
tion of the problem in VI. 28, the second solution of that<br />
problem.
