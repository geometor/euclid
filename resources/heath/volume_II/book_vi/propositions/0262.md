Therefore <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ A K /">$AK$</span> [V. 9], the less to the greater: which is impossible.

Therefore <span data-ref="0261.g1:&lt; A B C D &gt;">$ABCD$</span> cannot but be about the same diameter with <span data-ref="0261.g1:&lt; A E F G &gt;">$AF$</span>; therefore the parallelogram <span data-ref="0261.g1:&lt; A B C D &gt;">$ABCD$</span> is about the same diameter with the parallelogram <span data-ref="0261.g1:&lt; A E F G &gt;">$AF$</span>.

Therefore etc.

<span style="float: right;">Q. E. D.</span>

---

**"For suppose it is not, but, if possible, let <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ A H C /">$AHC$</span> be the diameter."** What is meant is "For, if <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ A C /">$AFC$</span> is not the diameter of the parallelogram <span data-ref="0261.g1:&lt; A B C D &gt;">$AC$</span>, let <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ A H C /">$AHC$</span> be its diameter." The Greek text has ἔστω αὐτῶν διάμετρος ἡ ΑΘΓ; but clearly αὐτῶν is wrong, as we cannot assume that one straight line is the diameter of both parallelograms, which is just what we have to prove. F and V omit the αὐτῶν, and Heiberg prefers this correction to substituting αὐτοῦ after Peyrard. I have inserted "<of <span data-ref="0261.g1:&lt; A B C D &gt;">$ABCD$</span>>" to make the meaning clear.

If the straight line <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ A H C /">$AHC$</span> does not pass through <span data-ref="0261.g1:F">$F$</span>, it must meet either <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ G F /">$GF$</span> or <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ G H /">$GF$</span> produced in some point <span data-ref="0261.g1:H">$H$</span>. The reading in the text "and let <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ G F /">$GF$</span> be *produced and carried through to* <span data-ref="0261.g1:H">$H$</span>" (καὶ ἐκβληθεῖσα ἡ ΗΖ διήχθω ἐπὶ τὸ Θ) corresponds to the supposition that <span data-ref="0261.g1:H">$H$</span> is on <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ G F /">$GF$</span> produced. The words were left out by Theon, evidently because in the figure of the MSS. the letters E, Z and K, Θ were interchanged. Heiberg therefore, following August, has preferred to retain the words and to correct the figure, as well as the passage in the text where <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span>, <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ A K /">$AK$</span> were interchanged to be in accord with the MS. figure.

It is of course possible to prove the proposition directly, as is done by Dr Lachlan. Let <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span>, <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> be the diagonals, and let us make no assumption as to how they fall.

Then, since <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> is parallel to <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span> and therefore to <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, the angles <span data-ref="0261.g1:&lt; A E F )">$AEF$</span>, <span data-ref="0261.g1:&lt; A B C )">$ABC$</span> are equal.

And, since the parallelograms are similar,
$$<span data-ref="0261.g1:/ A E /">AE</span> : <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ E F /">EF</span> = <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ A B /">AB</span> : <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ B C /">BC</span>.\qquad\text{[VI. Def. 1]}$$

Hence the triangles <span data-ref="0261.g1:&lt; A E F &gt;">$AEF$</span>, <span data-ref="0261.g1:&lt; A B C &gt;">$ABC$</span> are similar,
<span style="float: right;">[VI. 6]</span>
and therefore the angle <span data-ref="0261.g1:&lt; F A E )">$FAE$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0261.g1:&lt; F A E )">$CAB$</span>.

Therefore <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span> falls on <span data-ref="0261.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>.

The proposition is equally true if the parallelogram which is similar and similarly situated to the given parallelogram is not "taken away" from it, but is so placed that it is entirely outside the other, while two sides form an angle vertically opposite to an angle of the other. In this case the diameters are not "the same," in the words of the enunciation, but are in a straight line with one another. This extension of the proposition is, as will be seen, necessary for obtaining, according to the method adopted by Euclid in his solution of the problem in VI. 28, the second solution of that problem.
