### PROPOSITION 27.

*Of all the parallelograms applied to the same straight line and deficient by parallelogrammic figures similar and similarly situated to that described on the half of the straight line, that parallelogram is greatest which is applied to the half of the straight line and is similar to the defect.*

Let <span data-ref="0263.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> be a straight line and let it be bisected at <span data-ref="0263.g1:C">$C$</span>; let there be applied to the straight line <span data-ref="0263.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> the parallelogram <span data-ref="0263.g1:&lt; A D &gt;">$AD$</span> deficient by the parallelogrammic figure <span data-ref="0263.g1:&lt; D B &gt;">$DB$</span> described on the half of <span data-ref="0263.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, that is, <span data-ref="0263.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span>;

I say that, of all the parallelograms applied to <span data-ref="0263.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> and deficient by parallelogrammic figures similar and similarly situated to <span data-ref="0263.g1:&lt; D B &gt;">$DB$</span>, <span data-ref="0263.g1:&lt; A D &gt;">$AD$</span> is greatest.

![Proposition 27](0263.g1.svg)

For let there be applied to the straight line <span data-ref="0263.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> the parallelogram <span data-ref="0263.g1:&lt; A F &gt;">$AF$</span> deficient by the parallelogrammic figure <span data-ref="0263.g1:&lt; F B &gt;">$FB$</span> similar and similarly situated to <span data-ref="0263.g1:&lt; D B &gt;">$DB$</span>;

I say that <span data-ref="0263.g1:&lt; A D &gt;">$AD$</span> is greater than <span data-ref="0263.g1:&lt; A F &gt;">$AF$</span>.

For, since the parallelogram <span data-ref="0263.g1:&lt; D B &gt;">$DB$</span> is similar to the parallelogram <span data-ref="0263.g1:&lt; F B &gt;">$FB$</span>, they are about the same diameter.
<span style="float: right;">[VI. 26]</span>

Let their diameter <span data-ref="0263.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> be drawn, and let the figure be described.

Then, since <span data-ref="0263.g1:&lt; C F &gt;">$CF$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0263.g1:&lt; F E &gt;">$FE$</span>,
<span style="float: right;">[I. 43]</span>
and <span data-ref="0263.g1:&lt; F B &gt;">$FB$</span> is common, therefore the whole <span data-ref="0263.g1:&lt; C H &gt;">$CH$</span> is equal to the whole <span data-ref="0263.g1:&lt; K E &gt;">$KE$</span>.

But <span data-ref="0263.g1:&lt; C H &gt;">$CH$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0263.g1:&lt; C G &gt;">$CG$</span>, since <span data-ref="0263.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is also equal to <span data-ref="0263.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span>.
<span style="float: right;">[I. 36]</span>

Therefore <span data-ref="0263.g1:&lt; C G &gt;">$GC$</span> is also equal to <span data-ref="0263.g1:&lt; K E &gt;">$EK$</span>.

Let <span data-ref="0263.g1:&lt; C F &gt;">$CF$</span> be added to each; therefore the whole <span data-ref="0263.g1:&lt; A F &gt;">$AF$</span> is equal to the gnomon <span data-ref="0263.g1:&lt; L M N &gt;">$LMN$</span>; so that the parallelogram <span data-ref="0263.g1:&lt; D B &gt;">$DB$</span>, that is, <span data-ref="0263.g1:&lt; A D &gt;">$AD$</span>, is greater than the parallelogram <span data-ref="0263.g1:&lt; A F &gt;">$AF$</span>.

Therefore etc.

<span style="float: right;">Q. E. D.</span>
