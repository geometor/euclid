## Textual & Lexical Observations
- **Technical Vocabulary**:
  - "applied to the same straight line" (παραβαλλόμενα παρὰ τὴν αὐτὴν εὐθεῖαν).
  - "deficient by parallelogrammic figures" (ἐλλείποντα εἴδεσι παραλληλογράμμοις).
  - "gnomon *LMN*": The area remaining when sub-parallelogram $FB$ is removed from $DB$ is identified as gnomon $LMN$.
- **Proof Structure**:
  - Bisect $AB$ at $C$, apply parallelogram $AD$ on $AC$ deficient by $DB$ on $CB$.
  - Any other applied parallelogram $AF$ on $AK$ has defect $FB$ on $KB$.
  - By VI. 26, $DB$ and $FB$ share diameter $DB$.
  - Using complements $CF = FE$ (I. 43) and equal bases $AC = CB$ (I. 36), the area of $AF$ equals gnomon $LMN < DB = AD$.

## Mathematical & Historical Commentary
- Proposition VI. 27 is the first extremum / maximum theorem in Greek geometry for the application of areas with defect.
- Algebraically, it establishes that the function $f(x) = ax - \frac{b}{c}x^2$ attains its absolute maximum when $x = \frac{a}{2}$.
- This provides the necessary and sufficient condition (διορισμός) for the solvability of quadratic equations solved geometrically in VI. 28.
