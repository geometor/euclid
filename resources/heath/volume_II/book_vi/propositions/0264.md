We have already (note on I. 44) seen the significance, in Greek geometry,<br />
of the theory of "the application of areas, their exceeding and their falling-<br />
short." In I. 44 it was a question of "applying to a given straight line<br />
(exactly, without 'excess' or 'defect') a parallelogram equal to a given<br />
rectilineal figure, in a given angle." Here, in VI. 27—29, it is a question<br />
of parallelograms applied to a straight line but "*deficient (or exceeding) by*<br />
*parallelograms similar and similarly*<br />
*situated to a given parallelogram.*"

Apart from size, it is easy to construct<br />
any number of parallelograms "de-<br />
ficient" or "exceeding" in the manner<br />
described. Given the straight line<br />
<span data-ref="0264.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> to which the parallelogram has to<br />
be applied, we describe on the base<br />
<span data-ref="0264.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span>, where <span data-ref="0264.g1:C">$C$</span> is on <span data-ref="0264.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, or on <span data-ref="0264.g1:[ B A ]">$BA$</span><br />
produced beyond <span data-ref="0264.g1:A">$A$</span>, any parallelogram "similarly situated" and either equal<br />
or similar to the given parallelogram (Euclid takes the similar and similarly<br />
situated parallelogram on half the line), draw the diagonal <span data-ref="0264.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>, take on it<br />
(produced if necessary) any points as <span data-ref="0264.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0264.g1:K">$K$</span>, draw <span data-ref="0264.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, or <span data-ref="0264.g1:/ K L /">$KL$</span>, parallel to <span data-ref="0264.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span><br />
to meet <span data-ref="0264.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> or <span data-ref="0264.g1:[ A B ]">$AB$</span> produced and complete the parallelograms, as <span data-ref="0264.g1:< A H >">$AH$</span>, <span data-ref="0264.g1:< M L >">$ML$</span>.

If the point <span data-ref="0264.g1:E">$E$</span> is taken on <span data-ref="0264.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> or <span data-ref="0264.g1:[ B D ]">$BD$</span> produced beyond <span data-ref="0264.g1:D">$D$</span>, it must be so<br />
taken that <span data-ref="0264.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> meets <span data-ref="0264.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> between <span data-ref="0264.g1:A">$A$</span> and <span data-ref="0264.g1:B">$B$</span>. Otherwise the parallelogram<br />
<span data-ref="0264.g1:< A E >">$AE$</span> would not be applied to <span data-ref="0264.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> itself, as it is required to be.

The parallelograms <span data-ref="0264.g1:< B D >">$BD$</span>, <span data-ref="0264.g1:< B E >">$BE$</span>, being about the same diameter, are similar<br />
<span class="justification">[VI. 24]</span>, and <span data-ref="0264.g1:< B E >">$BE$</span> is the defect of the parallelogram <span data-ref="0264.g1:< A E >">$AE$</span> relatively to <span data-ref="0264.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>.<br />
<span data-ref="0264.g1:< A E >">$AE$</span> is then a parallelogram applied to <span data-ref="0264.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> but deficient by a parallelogram<br />
similar and similarly situated to <span data-ref="0264.g1:< B D >">$BD$</span>.

If <span data-ref="0264.g1:K">$K$</span> is on <span data-ref="0264.g1:[ D B ]">$DB$</span> produced, the parallelogram <span data-ref="0264.g1:< B K >">$BK$</span> is similar to <span data-ref="0264.g1:< B D >">$BD$</span>, but it<br />
is the *excess* of the parallelogram <span data-ref="0264.g1:< A K >">$AK$</span> relatively to the base <span data-ref="0264.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>. <span data-ref="0264.g1:< A K >">$AK$</span> is a<br />
parallelogram applied to <span data-ref="0264.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> but exceeding by a parallelogram similar and<br />
similarly situated to <span data-ref="0264.g1:< B D >">$BD$</span>.

Thus it is seen that <span data-ref="0264.g1:[ B D ]">$BD$</span> produced both ways is the *locus* of points, such<br />
as <span data-ref="0264.g1:E">$E$</span> or <span data-ref="0264.g1:K">$K$</span>, which determine, with the direction of <span data-ref="0264.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, the position of <span data-ref="0264.g1:A">$A$</span>, and<br />
the direction of <span data-ref="0264.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, parallelograms applied to <span data-ref="0264.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> and *deficient* or *exceeding*<br />
by parallelograms similar and similarly situated to the given parallelogram.

The importance of VI. 27—29 from a historical point of view cannot be<br />
overrated. They give the geometrical equivalent of the algebraical solution<br />
of the most general form of quadratic equation when that equation has a real<br />
and positive root. It will also enable us to find a real *negative* root of a<br />
quadratic equation ; for such an equation can, by altering the sign of *x*, be<br />
turned into another with a real *positive* root, when the geometrical method<br />
again becomes applicable. It will also, as we shall see, enable us to represent<br />
*both* roots when both are real and positive, and therefore to represent both<br />
roots when both are real but either positive or negative.

The method of these propositions was constantly used by the Greek<br />
geometers in the solution of problems, and they constitute the foundation of<br />
Book X. of the $Elements$ and of Apollonius' treatment of the conic sections.<br />
