## Textual & Lexical Observations
- **Historical & Philosophical Terminology**:
  - Application of areas (παραβολὴ τῶν χωρίων): application without excess or defect (I. 44).
  - Application with defect (ἐλλείπον, ellipse).
  - Application with excess (ὑπερβάλλον, hyperbola).
- **Authors Cited**:
  - Apollonius of Perga (conic sections named from these operations).
  - Robert Simson defending the indispensability of VI. 27–29 against Tacquet and Dechales.

## Mathematical & Historical Commentary
- **Geometric Locus along the Diagonal**:
  - Heath demonstrates that the diagonal $BD$ produced in both directions serves as the geometric *locus* of vertices that generate all possible applied parallelograms:
    - Points $E$ on $BD$ define applied parallelograms with *defect* ($AE$ deficient by $BE$).
    - Points $K$ on $DB$ produced define applied parallelograms with *excess* ($AK$ exceeding by $BK$).
- **Algebraic Connection to Quadratic Equations**:
  - Heath explains how VI. 27–29 represent the geometric solution of general quadratic equations, capable of generating both positive roots and representing negative roots via sign transformation.
