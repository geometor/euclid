VI. 27 contains the διορισμός, the condition for a real solution, of the<br />
problem contained in the proposition following it. The maximum of all the<br />
parallelograms having the given property which can be applied to a given<br />
straight line is that which is described upon half the line (τὸ ἀπὸ τῆς ἡμισείας<br />
ἀναγραφόμενον). This corresponds to the condition that an equation of the<br />
form
$$ax - px^2 = A$$
may have a real root. The correctness of the result may be seen by taking<br />
the case in which the parallelograms are<br />
rectangles, which enables us to leave out<br />
of account the sine of the angle of the<br />
parallelograms without any real loss of<br />
generality. Suppose the sides of the rect-<br />
angle to which the defect is to be similar<br />
to be as *b* to *c*, *b* corresponding to the<br />
side of the defect which lies along *AB*.<br />
Suppose that *AKFG* is any parallelogram<br />
applied to *AB* having the given property, that *AB* = *a*, and that *FK* = *x*.<br />
Then
$$KB = \frac{b}{c}x\text{, and therefore } AK = a - \frac{b}{c}x.$$

![Proposition 27 algebraic interpretation](0265.g1.svg)

Hence $(a - \frac{b}{c}x)x = S$, where $S$ is the area of the rectangle *AKFG*.

Thus, given the equation
$$ax - \frac{b}{c}x^2 = S,$$
where *S* is undetermined, VI. 27 tells us that, if *x* is to have a real value, *S*<br />
cannot be greater than the rectangle *CE*.

Now $CB = \frac{a}{2}$, and therefore $CD = \frac{c}{b}\cdot\frac{a}{2}$ ;<br />
whence
$$S \ngtr \frac{c}{b}\cdot\frac{a^2}{4},$$
which is just the same result as we obtain by the algebraical method.

In the particular case where *the defect of the parallelogram is to be a*<br />
*square*, the condition becomes the statement of the fact that, *if a straight line*<br />
*be divided into two parts, the rectangle contained by the parts cannot exceed the*<br />
*square on half the line*.
