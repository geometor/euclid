This other "case" of the proposition is found in all the mss., but Heiberg relegates it to the Appendix as being very obviously interpolated. The reasons for this course are that it is not in Euclid's manner to give a separate demonstration of such a "case"; it is rather his habit to give one case only and to leave the student to satisfy himself about any others (cf. I. 7). Internal evidence is also against the genuineness of the separate proof. It is put after the *conclusion* of the proposition instead of before it, and, if Euclid had intended to discuss two cases, he would have distinguished them at the beginning of the proposition, as it was his invariable practice to do. Moreover the second "case" is the less worth giving because it can be so easily reduced to the first. For suppose <span data-ref="0266.g1:F'">$F'$</span> to be taken on <span data-ref="0266.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> so that <span data-ref="0266.g1:/ F D /">$FD$</span> = <span data-ref="0266.g1:/ F' D /">$F'D$</span>. Produce <span data-ref="0266.g1:[ B F ]">$BF$</span> to meet <span data-ref="0266.g1:[ A G ]">$AG$</span> produced in <span data-ref="0266.g1:P">$P$</span>. Complete the parallelogram <span data-ref="0266.g1:< B A P Q >">$BAPQ$</span>, and draw through <span data-ref="0266.g1:F'">$F'$</span> straight lines parallel to and meeting its opposite sides.

Then the complement <span data-ref="0266.g1:< F' Q >">$F'Q$</span> is equal to the complement <span data-ref="0266.g1:< A F' >">$AF'$</span>.

And it is at once seen that <span data-ref="0266.g1:< A F >">$AF$</span>, <span data-ref="0266.g1:< F' Q >">$F'Q$</span> are equal and similar. Hence the solution of the problem represented by <span data-ref="0266.g1:< A F >">$AF$</span> or <span data-ref="0266.g1:< F' Q >">$F'Q$</span> gives a parallelogram of the same size as <span data-ref="0266.g1:< A F' >">$AF'$</span> arrived at as in the first "case."

It is worth noting that the actual difference between the parallelogram <span data-ref="0266.g1:< A F >">$AF$</span> and the maximum area <span data-ref="0266.g1:< A D >">$AD$</span> that it can possibly have is represented in the figure.
