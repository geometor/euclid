2. Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
-----------------------------------------------

- **Textual Criticism of Theon's Diorismos**:

  - Heath demonstrates that Theon's version of the diorismos needlessly amplifies Euclid's concise phrasing:

    - ``διορισμός`` (*diorismos*): specification / condition of possibility.
    - ``ᾧ δεῖ ἴσον παραβαλεῖν`` (*hōi dei ison parabalein*): "to which namely the applied parallelogram must be equal" (superfluous gloss on the given rectilineal figure).
    - ``παραβαλλομένου`` (*paraballomenou*, applied) substituted by Theon for Euclid's ``ἀναγραφομένου`` (*anagraphomenou*, described).
    - ``ὁμοίων ὄντων τῶν ἐλλειμμάτων τοῦ τε ἀπὸ τῆς ἡμισείας καὶ ᾧ δεῖ ὅμοιον ἐλλείπειν`` (*homoiōn ontōn tōn elleimmatōn tou te apo tēs hēmiseias kai hōi dei homoion elleipein*): confusing formulation speaking of two defects.

  - Manuscript *P* (Vaticanus 190) retains the pristine Euclidean text, which Heath adopts.

- **Tacit Axiomatic Assumption**:

  - Heath identifies the tacit assumption that for two similar parallelograms, if one is greater than the other in area, each side of the greater is greater than the corresponding side of the less.

- **Cross References**:

  - ``[I. 36]``: Parallelograms on equal bases and in the same parallels are equal.
