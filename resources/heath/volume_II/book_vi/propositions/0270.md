Similarly
<p align="center">$\frac{a}{2} =$ <span data-ref="0270.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span> $=$ <span data-ref="0270.g1:/ G E /">$GE$</span> $\cdot \frac{b}{c}$,</p>
so that
<p align="center"><span data-ref="0270.g1:/ G E /">$GE$</span> $= \frac{c}{b} \cdot \frac{a}{2}$.</p>

Therefore the parallelogram <span data-ref="0270.g1:< G Q >">$GQ$</span> $=$ <span data-ref="0270.g1:/ G O /">$GO^2$</span> $\cdot \frac{b}{c}$,
and the parallelogram <span data-ref="0270.g1:< G B >">$GB$</span> $= \frac{c}{b} \cdot \frac{a^2}{4}$.

Thus, in taking the parallelogram <span data-ref="0270.g1:< G Q >">$GQ$</span> equal to $(GB - S)$, Euclid really finds <span data-ref="0270.g1:/ G O /">$GO$</span> from the equation
<p align="center"><span data-ref="0270.g1:/ G O /">$GO^2$</span> $\cdot \frac{b}{c} = \frac{c}{b} \cdot \frac{a^2}{4} - S$.</p>

The value which he finds is
<p align="center"><span data-ref="0270.g1:/ G O /">$GO$</span> $= \sqrt{\frac{c}{b} \left(\frac{c}{b} \cdot \frac{a^2}{4} - S\right)}$,</p>
and he finds <span data-ref="0270.g1:/ Q S /">$QS$</span> (or $x$) by subtracting <span data-ref="0270.g1:/ G O /">$GO$</span> from <span data-ref="0270.g1:/ G E /">$GE$</span>; whence
$$x = \frac{c}{b} \cdot \frac{a}{2} - \sqrt{\frac{c}{b} \left(\frac{c}{b} \cdot \frac{a^2}{4} - S\right)}.$$

It will be observed that Euclid only gives one solution, that corresponding to the *negative* sign before the radical. But the reason must be the same as that for which he only gives one "case" in VI. 27. He cannot have failed to see how to *add* <span data-ref="0270.g1:/ G O /">$GO$</span> to <span data-ref="0270.g1:/ G E /">$GE$</span> would give another solution. As shown under the last proposition, the other solution can be arrived at

(1) by placing the parallelogram <span data-ref="0270.g1:< G O Q P >">$GOQP$</span> in the angle vertically opposite to <span data-ref="0270.g1:< F G E )">$FGE$</span> so that <span data-ref="0270.g1:< G Q' >">$GQ'$</span> lies along <span data-ref="0270.g1:[ B G ]">$BG$</span> produced. The parallelogram <span data-ref="0270.g1:< A Q' >">$AQ'$</span> then gives the second solution. The side of this parallelogram lying along <span data-ref="0270.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0270.g1:/ S B /">$SB$</span>. The other side is what we have called $x$, and in this case
<p align="center">$x =$ <span data-ref="0270.g1:/ G E /">$EG$</span> $+$ <span data-ref="0270.g1:/ G O /">$GO$</span> $= \frac{c}{b} \cdot \frac{a}{2} + \sqrt{\frac{c}{b} \left(\frac{c}{b} \cdot \frac{a^2}{4} - S\right)}$.</p>
