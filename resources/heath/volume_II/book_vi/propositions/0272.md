Let <span data-ref="0271.g1:[ F L ]">$FL$</span>, <span data-ref="0271.g1:[ F E ]">$FE$</span> be produced,
let <span data-ref="0271.g1:/ F L M /">$FLM$</span> be equal to <span data-ref="0271.g1:/ K H /">$KH$</span>, and <span data-ref="0271.g1:/ F E N /">$FEN$</span> to <span data-ref="0271.g1:/ K G /">$KG$</span>,
and let <span data-ref="0271.g1:< M N >">$MN$</span> be completed;
therefore <span data-ref="0271.g1:< M N >">$MN$</span> is both equal and similar to <span data-ref="0271.g1:< G H >">$GH$</span>.
But <span data-ref="0271.g1:< G H >">$GH$</span> is similar to <span data-ref="0271.g1:< E L >">$EL$</span>;
therefore <span data-ref="0271.g1:< M N >">$MN$</span> is also similar to <span data-ref="0271.g1:< E L >">$EL$</span>; [VI. 21]
therefore <span data-ref="0271.g1:< E L >">$EL$</span> is about the same diameter with <span data-ref="0271.g1:< M N >">$MN$</span>. [VI. 26]
Let their diameter <span data-ref="0271.g1:/ F O /">$FO$</span> be drawn, and let the figure be described.
Since <span data-ref="0271.g1:< G H >">$GH$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0271.g1:< E L >">$EL$</span>, <span data-ref="0271.g1:< C >">$C$</span>,
while <span data-ref="0271.g1:< G H >">$GH$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0271.g1:< M N >">$MN$</span>,
therefore <span data-ref="0271.g1:< M N >">$MN$</span> is also equal to <span data-ref="0271.g1:< E L >">$EL$</span>, <span data-ref="0271.g1:< C >">$C$</span>.
Let <span data-ref="0271.g1:< E L >">$EL$</span> be subtracted from each;
therefore the remainder, the gnomon <span data-ref="0271.g1:< X W V >">$XWV$</span>, is equal to <span data-ref="0271.g1:< C >">$C$</span>.
Now, since <span data-ref="0271.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0271.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span>,
<span data-ref="0271.g1:< A N >">$AN$</span> is also equal to <span data-ref="0271.g1:< N B >">$NB$</span> [I. 36], that is, to <span data-ref="0271.g1:< L P >">$LP$</span> [I. 43].
Let <span data-ref="0271.g1:< E O >">$EO$</span> be added to each;
therefore the whole <span data-ref="0271.g1:< A O >">$AO$</span> is equal to the gnomon <span data-ref="0271.g1:< V W X >">$VWX$</span>.
But the gnomon <span data-ref="0271.g1:< V W X >">$VWX$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0271.g1:< C >">$C$</span>;
therefore <span data-ref="0271.g1:< A O >">$AO$</span> is also equal to <span data-ref="0271.g1:< C >">$C$</span>.
Therefore to the given straight line <span data-ref="0271.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> there has been applied the parallelogram <span data-ref="0271.g1:< A O >">$AO$</span> equal to the given rectilineal figure <span data-ref="0271.g1:< C >">$C$</span> and exceeding by a parallelogrammic figure <span data-ref="0271.g1:< Q P >">$QP$</span> which is similar to <span data-ref="0271.g1:< D >">$D$</span>, since <span data-ref="0271.g1:< P Q >">$PQ$</span> is also similar to <span data-ref="0271.g1:< E L >">$EL$</span> [VI. 24].

<p align="right">Q. E. F.</p>

The corresponding proposition in the *Data* is (Prop. 59), *If a given (area) be applied (i.e. in the form of a parallelogram) to a given straight line exceeding by a figure given in species, the breadths of the excess are given.*

The problem of VI. 29 corresponds of course to the solution of the quadratic equation
$$ax + \frac{b}{c} x^2 = S.$$

The algebraical solution of this equation gives
$$x = - \frac{c}{b} \cdot \frac{a}{2} \pm \sqrt{\frac{c}{b} \left(\frac{c}{b} \cdot \frac{a^2}{4} + S\right)}.$$

The exact correspondence of Euclid's method to the algebraical solution may be seen, as in the case of VI. 28, by supposing the parallelograms to be *rectangles*. In this case Euclid's construction on <span data-ref="0271.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span> of the parallelogram <span data-ref="0271.g1:< E L >">$EL$</span> similar to <span data-ref="0271.g1:< D >">$D$</span> is equivalent to finding that
<p align="center"><span data-ref="0271.g1:/ F E /">$FE$</span> $= \frac{c}{b} \cdot \frac{a}{2}, \quad$ and <span data-ref="0271.g1:< E L >">$EL$</span> $= \frac{c}{b} \cdot \frac{a^2}{4}$.</p>
