His determination of the similar parallelogram *MN* equal to the sum of *EL*<br />
and *S* corresponds to proving that
$$FN^2 \cdot \frac{b}{c} = \frac{c}{b} \cdot \frac{a^2}{4} + S,$$
or
$$FN = \sqrt{\frac{c}{b} \left(\frac{c}{b} \cdot \frac{a^2}{4} + S\right)},$$
whence $x$ is found as
$$x = FN - FE = \sqrt{\frac{c}{b} \left(\frac{c}{b} \cdot \frac{a^2}{4} + S\right)} - \frac{c}{b} \cdot \frac{a}{2}.$$

Euclid takes, in this case, the solution corresponding to the *positive* sign<br />
before the radical because, from his point of view, that would be the *only*<br />
solution.

No διορισμός is necessary because a real geometrical solution is always<br />
possible whatever be the size of $S$.

Again the *Data* has a proposition indicating the particular case in which<br />
the excess is a rhombus with a given angle. Prop. 84 proves that, *If two*<br />
*straight lines contain a given area in a given angle, and one of the straight lines*<br />
*is greater than the other by a given straight line, each of the two straight lines is*<br />
*given also.* The proof reduces the proposition to a particular case of *Data*,<br />
Prop. 59, quoted above as corresponding to VI. 29.

Again there is an important particular case which can be solved by means<br />
of Book II. only, as shown under II. 6 above (Vol. I. pp. 386—8), the case namely<br />
in which the excess is a *square*, corresponding to the solution of the equation
$$ax + x^2 = b^2.$$
*This is the problem of applying to a given straight line a rectangle equal to a*<br />
*given area and exceeding by a square.*

### PROPOSITION 30.

<p class="enunciation">To cut a given finite straight line in extreme and mean<br />
ratio.</p>

Let *AB* be the given finite straight line ;<br />
thus it is required to cut *AB* in extreme and mean ratio.

![Book VI Proposition 30](0273.g1.svg)

<p class="sub-clause">On *AB* let the square *BC* be described ;<br />
and let there be applied to *AC* the parallelo-<br />
gram *CD* equal to *BC* and exceeding by<br />
the figure *AD* similar to *BC*. <span class="justification">[VI. 29]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">Now *BC* is a square ;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore *AD* is also a square.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">And, since *BC* is equal to *CD*,<br />
let *CE* be subtracted from each ;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the remainder *BF* is equal to<br />
the remainder *AD*.</p>
