It will be observed that the construction in the text is a direct application<br />
of the preceding Prop. 29 in the particular case where the *excess* of the<br />
parallelogram which is applied is a *square*. This fact coupled with the<br />
position of VI. 30 is a sufficient indication that the construction is Euclid's.

In one place Theon appears to have amplified the argument. The text<br />
above says "But <span data-ref="0273.g1:/ F E /">$FE$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0273.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>," while the MSS. B, F, V and p have<br />
"But <span data-ref="0273.g1:/ F E /">$FE$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0273.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, that is, to <span data-ref="0273.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>."

The MSS. give after ὅπερ ἔδει ποιῆσαι an alternative construction which<br />
Heiberg relegates to the Appendix. The text-books give this construction<br />
alone and leave out the other. It will be remembered that the alternative<br />
proof does no more than refer to the equivalent construction in II. 11.

"Let <span data-ref="0273.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> be cut at <span data-ref="0273.g1:C">$C$</span> so that the rectangle <span data-ref="0273.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0273.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is equal to the<br />
square on <span data-ref="0273.g1:/ C A /">$CA$</span>. <span class="justification">[II. 11]</span><br />
Since then the rectangle <span data-ref="0273.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0273.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is equal to the square on <span data-ref="0273.g1:/ C A /">$CA$</span>,
<p class="assertion">therefore, as <span data-ref="0273.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span> is to <span data-ref="0273.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0273.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> to <span data-ref="0273.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span>. <span class="justification">[VI. 17]</span></p>
Therefore <span data-ref="0273.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> has been cut in extreme and mean ratio at <span data-ref="0273.g1:C">$C$</span>."

It is intrinsically improbable that this alternative construction was added<br />
to the other by Euclid himself. It is however just the kind of interpolation<br />
that might be expected from an editor. If Euclid had preferred the alternative<br />
construction, he would have been more likely to give it alone.
