It will be observed that the construction in the text is a direct application<br />
of the preceding Prop. 29 in the particular case where the *excess* of the<br />
parallelogram which is applied is a *square*. This fact coupled with the<br />
position of VI. 30 is a sufficient indication that the construction is Euclid's.

In one place Theon appears to have amplified the argument. The text<br />
above says "But *FE* is equal to *AB*," while the MSS. B, F, V and p have<br />
"But *FE* is equal to *AC*, that is, to *AB*."

The MSS. give after ὅπερ ἔδει ποιῆσαι an alternative construction which<br />
Heiberg relegates to the Appendix. The text-books give this construction<br />
alone and leave out the other. It will be remembered that the alternative<br />
proof does no more than refer to the equivalent construction in II. 11.

"Let *AB* be cut at *C* so that the rectangle *AB*, *BC* is equal to the<br />
square on *CA*. <span class="justification">[II. 11]</span><br />
Since then the rectangle *AB*, *BC* is equal to the square on *CA*,
<p class="assertion">therefore, as *BA* is to *AC*, so is *AC* to *CB*. <span class="justification">[VI. 17]</span></p>
Therefore *AB* has been cut in extreme and mean ratio at *C*."

It is intrinsically improbable that this alternative construction was added<br />
to the other by Euclid himself. It is however just the kind of interpolation<br />
that might be expected from an editor. If Euclid had preferred the alternative<br />
construction, he would have been more likely to give it alone.
