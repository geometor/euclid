It will be observed that the construction in the text is a direct application of the preceding Prop. 29 in the particular case where the *excess* of the parallelogram which is applied is a *square*. This fact coupled with the position of VI. 30 is a sufficient indication that the construction is Euclid's.

In one place Theon appears to have amplified the argument. The text above says "But :g:`$FE$ <0273.g1:/ F E />` is :eq:`equal <0273.g1:/ F E / = 0273.g1:/ A B />` to :g:`$AB$ <0273.g1:/ A B />`," while the MSS. B, F, V and p have "But :g:`$FE$ <0273.g1:/ F E />` is :eq:`equal <0273.g1:/ F E / = 0273.g1:/ A C / = 0273.g1:/ A B />` to :g:`$AC$ <0273.g1:/ A C />`, that is, to :g:`$AB$ <0273.g1:/ A B />`."

The MSS. give after :term:`ὅπερ ἔδει ποιῆσαι <hoper-edei-poiesai>` an alternative construction which :person:`Heiberg <heiberg>` relegates to the Appendix. The text-books give this construction alone and leave out the other. It will be remembered that the alternative proof does no more than refer to the equivalent construction in II. 11.

"Let :g:`$AB$ <0273.g1:/ A B />` be cut at :g:`$C$ <0273.g1:C>` so that the rectangle :g:`$AB$ <0273.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$BC$ <0273.g1:/ B C />` is equal to the square on :g:`$CA$ <0273.g1:/ C A />`. :just:`[II. 11] <II.11>`

Since then the rectangle :g:`$AB$ <0273.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$BC$ <0273.g1:/ B C />` is equal to the square on :g:`$CA$ <0273.g1:/ C A />`,

.. assertion:: [VI. 17] <VI.17>

   therefore, as :g:`$BA$ <0273.g1:/ B A />` is to :g:`$AC$ <0273.g1:/ A C />`, so is :g:`$AC$ <0273.g1:/ A C />` to :g:`$CB$ <0273.g1:/ C B />`.

Therefore :g:`$AB$ <0273.g1:/ A B />` has been cut in extreme and mean ratio at :g:`$C$ <0273.g1:C>`."

It is intrinsically improbable that this alternative construction was added to the other by Euclid himself. It is however just the kind of interpolation that might be expected from an editor. If Euclid had preferred the alternative construction, he would have been more likely to give it alone.

.. enunciation::

   In right-angled triangles the figure on the side subtending the right angle is equal to the similar and similarly described figures on the sides containing the right angle.
