## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Euclidean Proof**: Proposition 31 generalizes the Pythagorean theorem (I. 47) from squares to any similar and similarly described rectilinear figures on the sides of a right-angled triangle.
- **Application of Prior Results**: Relies on VI. 8 (similarity of triangles formed by perpendicular from right angle), VI. Def. 1 (definition of similar figures), and VI. 19, Porism (areas of similar figures are in duplicate ratio of corresponding sides, or as first straight line to third in continued proportion).
- **Heath's Critique of Inference**: Heath notes that Euclid directly infers $BC : (BD + CD) = (\text{fig. on } BC) : (\text{sum of figs. on } BA \text{ and } AC)$ from $CB : BD$ and $BC : CD$. Strictly, V. 24 requires antecedent addition when consequents are common ($a:b = c:d$ and $e:b = f:d \implies (a+e):b = (c+f):d$), which requires inversion of the ratios via Simson's Proposition B.
