1. Summary & Folio Continuity
-----------------------------

- **Page Header**: ``VI. 31] PROPOSITIONS 30, 31 269``
- **Previous Folio**: Folio 0274 (Page 268) concluded Proposition 30 (Q.E.F.), provided commentary on its authenticity vs interpolated alternative proofs, and opened Proposition 31.
- **Euclidean Demonstration**: Completes Proposition 31 (the generalization of the Pythagorean theorem, I. 47, to any similar and similarly described rectilineal figures):

  - Right-angled triangle $ABC$ with altitude $AD$ drawn to hypotenuse $BC$.
  - Triangles $ABD, ADC \sim ABC$ via VI. 8.
  - From similarity, $CB : BA = AB : BD$ (VI. Def. 1).
  - Via VI. 19, Porism, triplicate lines yield duplicate ratio of sides: $CB : BD = (\text{fig. on } CB) : (\text{fig. on } BA)$ and $BC : CD = (\text{fig. on } BC) : (\text{fig. on } CA)$.
  - Concludes $BC : (BD + DC) = (\text{fig. on } BC) : (\text{figs. on } BA + AC)$.
  - Since $BC = BD + DC$, the figure on $BC$ equals the sum of the similar figures on $BA$ and $AC$ (Q. E. D.).

- **Commentary on Proposition 31**:

  - Proclus credits this theorem to Euclid personally.
  - Heath critiques the proportion step inferring $BC : (BD + CD)$ directly from $CB : BD$ and $BC : CD$, noting that V. 24 requires common consequents and thus inversion via Simson's Proposition B.

- **Next Folio**: Folio 0276 (Page 270) continues Heath's commentary on the formal justification via Simson's Prop. B and alternative proofs.

2. Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
-----------------------------------------------

- **Proclus Attribution**: In his commentary on I. 47, Proclus specifically attributes this general theorem on similar figures on the sides of a right-angled triangle to Euclid himself.
- **Inference Examination**: Heath analyzes the validity of inferring $(a + e) : b = (c + f) : d$ from $a : b = c : d$ and $e : b = f : d$ via Book V, Prop. 24, requiring antecedent and consequent inversion.
- **Internal Authority Citations**:

  - ``[VI. 8]``: If in a right-angled triangle a perpendicular be drawn from the right angle to the base, the triangles adjoining the perpendicular are similar to the whole and to one another.
  - ``[VI. Def. 1]``: Definition of similar rectilineal figures.
  - ``[VI. 19, Por.]``: Porism stating that similar figures are in the duplicate ratio of corresponding sides (or as first to third in three continued proportionals).
  - ``[I. 47]``: The classical Pythagorean theorem for squares.
  - ``[V. 24]``: If a first magnitude be to a second the same ratio as a third to a fourth, and a fifth to the second as a sixth to the fourth, then the sum of first and fifth to the second is as the sum of third and sixth to the fourth.
