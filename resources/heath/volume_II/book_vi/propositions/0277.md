<p class="sub-clause">For, since $AB$ is parallel to $DC$,<br />
and the straight line $AC$ has fallen upon them,</p>
<p class="assertion">the alternate angles $BAC, ACD$<br />
are equal to one another. <span class="justification">[I. 29]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">For the same reason</p>
<p class="assertion">the angle $CDE$ is also<br />
equal to the angle $ACD$;</p>
<p class="assertion">so that the angle $BAC$ is equal<br />
to the angle $CDE$.</p>

![Proposition 32](0277.g1.svg)

<p class="sub-clause">And, since $ABC, DCE$ are<br />
two triangles having one angle, the angle at $A$, equal to one<br />
angle, the angle at $D$,<br />
and the sides about the equal angles proportional,<br />
so that, as $BA$ is to $AC$, so is $CD$ to $DE$,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the triangle $ABC$ is equiangular with the<br />
triangle $DCE$; <span class="justification">[VI. 6]</span></p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the angle $ABC$ is equal to the angle $DCE$.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">But the angle $ACD$ was also proved equal to the angle<br />
$BAC$;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the whole angle $ACE$ is equal to the two angles<br />
$ABC, BAC$.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">Let the angle $ACB$ be added to each;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the angles $ACE, ACB$ are equal to the angles $BAC$,<br />
$ACB, CBA$.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">But the angles $BAC, ABC, ACB$ are equal to two right<br />
angles; <span class="justification">[I. 32]</span></p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the angles $ACE, ACB$ are also equal to two<br />
right angles.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">Therefore with a straight line $AC$, and at the point $C$ on<br />
it, the two straight lines $BC, CE$ not lying on the same side<br />
make the adjacent angles $ACE, ACB$ equal to two right<br />
angles;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore $BC$ is in a straight line with $CE$. <span class="justification">[I. 14]</span></p>

<p class="qed">Therefore etc.<span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>
