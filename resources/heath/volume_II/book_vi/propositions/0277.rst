.. subclause::

   | For, since :g:`$AB$ <0277.g1:/ A B />` is parallel to :g:`$DC$ <0277.g1:/ D C />`,
   | and the straight line :g:`$AC$ <0277.g1:/ A C />` has fallen upon them,

.. assertion::

   | the alternate angles :g:`$BAC$ <0277.g1:< B A C )>`, :g:`$ACD$ <0277.g1:< A C D )>`
   | are equal to one another. :just:`[I. 29] <I.29>`

.. subclause::

   For the same reason

.. assertion::

   | the angle :g:`$CDE$ <0277.g1:< C D E )>` is also
   | equal to the angle :g:`$ACD$ <0277.g1:< A C D )>`;

.. assertion::

   | so that the angle :g:`$BAC$ <0277.g1:< B A C )>` is equal
   | to the angle :g:`$CDE$ <0277.g1:< C D E )>`.

.. picture:: 0277.g1.svg
   :alt: Proposition 32

.. subclause::

   | And, since :g:`$ABC$ <0277.g1:< A B C >>`, :g:`$DCE$ <0277.g1:< D C E >>` are
   | two triangles having one angle, the angle at :g:`$A$ <0277.g1:A>`, equal to one
   | angle, the angle at :g:`$D$ <0277.g1:D>`,
   | and the sides about the equal angles proportional,
   | so that, as :g:`$BA$ <0277.g1:/ B A />` is to :g:`$AC$ <0277.g1:/ A C />`, so is :g:`$CD$ <0277.g1:/ C D />` to :g:`$DE$ <0277.g1:/ D E />`,

.. assertion::

   | therefore the triangle :g:`$ABC$ <0277.g1:< A B C >>` is equiangular with the
   | triangle :g:`$DCE$ <0277.g1:< D C E >>`; :just:`[VI. 6] <VI.6>`

.. assertion::

   therefore the angle :g:`$ABC$ <0277.g1:< A B C )>` is equal to the angle :g:`$DCE$ <0277.g1:< D C E )>`.

.. subclause::

   | But the angle :g:`$ACD$ <0277.g1:< A C D )>` was also proved equal to the angle
   | :g:`$BAC$ <0277.g1:< B A C )>`;

.. assertion::

   | therefore the whole angle :g:`$ACE$ <0277.g1:< A C E )>` is equal to the two angles
   | :g:`$ABC$ <0277.g1:< A B C )>`, :g:`$BAC$ <0277.g1:< B A C )>`.

.. subclause::

   Let the angle :g:`$ACB$ <0277.g1:< A C B )>` be added to each;

.. assertion::

   | therefore the angles :g:`$ACE$ <0277.g1:< A C E )>`, :g:`$ACB$ <0277.g1:< A C B )>` are equal to the angles :g:`$BAC$ <0277.g1:< B A C )>`,
   | :g:`$ACB$ <0277.g1:< A C B )>`, :g:`$CBA$ <0277.g1:< C B A )>`.

.. subclause::

   | But the angles :g:`$BAC$ <0277.g1:< B A C )>`, :g:`$ABC$ <0277.g1:< A B C )>`, :g:`$ACB$ <0277.g1:< A C B )>` are equal to two right
   | angles; :just:`[I. 32] <I.32>`

.. assertion::

   | therefore the angles :g:`$ACE$ <0277.g1:< A C E )>`, :g:`$ACB$ <0277.g1:< A C B )>` are also equal to two
   | right angles.

.. subclause::

   | Therefore with a straight line :g:`$AC$ <0277.g1:/ A C />`, and at the point :g:`$C$ <0277.g1:C>` on
   | it, the two straight lines :g:`$BC$ <0277.g1:/ B C />`, :g:`$CE$ <0277.g1:/ C E />` not lying on the same side
   | make the adjacent angles :g:`$ACE$ <0277.g1:< A C E )>`, :g:`$ACB$ <0277.g1:< A C B )>` equal to two right
   | angles;

.. assertion:: [I. 14] <I.14>

   therefore :g:`$BC$ <0277.g1:/ B C />` is in a straight line with :g:`$CE$ <0277.g1:/ C E />`.

.. qed::

   Therefore etc. :just:`Q. E. D.`
