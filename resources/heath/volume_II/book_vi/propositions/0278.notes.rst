2. Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
-----------------------------------------------

- **Critical Ambiguity in VI. 32**: Heath illustrates that Euclid's enunciation does not explicitly forbid the included angles from being supplementary rather than equal. If supplementary, $CE$ cannot be collinear with $CB$ (unless both are right angles). Hence the included angles must be equal, and the triangles similar.
- **Defence of Utility**: Refutes Todhunter's claim of uselessness by pointing out Euclid's explicit deployment of VI. 32 in Book XIII, Proposition 17 (construction of the regular dodecahedron).
- **Substitutes Analyzed**:

  1. Augustus De Morgan: Similar triangles placed with parallel bases.
  2. Dr. R. Lachlan: Similar triangles with two pairs of parallel sides implying parallelism of third sides.
  3. H. M. Taylor: Homothetic triangles with sides parallel in pairs, lines joining corresponding vertices meeting in a point or parallel.

- **Simson's Hypothesis**: Simson suggested VI. 32 was intended to provide a direct proof of VI. 26 for vertically opposite similar parallelograms.
