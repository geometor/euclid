### PROPOSITION 33.

*In equal circles angles have the same ratio as the circumferences on which they stand, whether they stand at the centres or at the circumferences.*

Let <span data-ref="0279.g2:( A B C )">$ABC$</span>, <span data-ref="0279.g2:( D E F )">$DEF$</span> be equal circles, and let the angles <span data-ref="0279.g2:< B G C )">$BGC$</span>, <span data-ref="0279.g2:< E H F )">$EHF$</span> be angles at their centres <span data-ref="0279.g2:G">$G$</span>, <span data-ref="0279.g2:H">$H$</span>, and the angles <span data-ref="0279.g2:< B A C )">$BAC$</span>, <span data-ref="0279.g2:< E D F )">$EDF$</span> angles at the circumferences;

I say that, as the circumference <span data-ref="0279.g2:( B C )">$BC$</span> is to the circumference <span data-ref="0279.g2:( E F )">$EF$</span>, so is the angle <span data-ref="0279.g2:< B G C )">$BGC$</span> to the angle <span data-ref="0279.g2:< E H F )">$EHF$</span>, and the angle <span data-ref="0279.g2:< B A C )">$BAC$</span> to the angle <span data-ref="0279.g2:< E D F )">$EDF$</span>.
