### PROPOSITION 33.

<p class="enunciation">In equal circles angles have the same ratio as the circum-<br />
ferences on which they stand, whether they stand at the centres<br />
or at the circumferences.</p>

Let $ABC, DEF$ be equal circles, and let the angles $BGC$,<br />
$EHF$ be angles at their centres $G, H$, and the angles $BAC$,<br />
$EDF$ angles at the circumferences;

I say that, as the circumference $BC$ is to the circumference<br />
$EF$, so is the angle $BGC$ to the angle $EHF$, and the angle<br />
$BAC$ to the angle $EDF$.

![Proposition 33](0279.g2.svg)

For let any number of consecutive circumferences $CK$,<br />
$KL$ be made equal to the circumference $BC$,<br />
and any number of consecutive circumferences $FM, MN$ equal<br />
to the circumference $EF$;<br />
and let $GK, GL, HM, HN$ be joined.

<p class="sub-clause">Then, since the circumferences $BC, CK, KL$ are equal<br />
to one another,</p>
<p class="assertion">the angles $BGC, CGK, KGL$ are also equal to one another; <span class="justification">[III. 27]</span></p>
