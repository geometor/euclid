PROPOSITION 33.
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

.. enunciation::

   In equal circles angles have the same ratio as the circum- ferences on which they stand, whether they stand at the centres or at the circumferences.

| Let :g:`$ABC$ <0279.g2:( A B C )>`, :g:`$DEF$ <0279.g2:( D E F )>` be equal circles, and let the angles :g:`$BGC$ <0279.g2:< B G C )>`,
| :g:`$EHF$ <0279.g2:< E H F )>` be angles at their centres :g:`$G$ <0279.g2:G>`, :g:`$H$ <0279.g2:H>`, and the angles :g:`$BAC$ <0279.g2:< B A C )>`,
| :g:`$EDF$ <0279.g2:< E D F )>` angles at the circumferences;

| I say that, as the circumference :g:`$BC$ <0279.g2:( B C )>` is to the circumference
| :g:`$EF$ <0279.g2:( E F )>`, so is the angle :g:`$BGC$ <0279.g2:< B G C )>` to the angle :g:`$EHF$ <0279.g2:< E H F )>`, and the angle
| :g:`$BAC$ <0279.g2:< B A C )>` to the angle :g:`$EDF$ <0279.g2:< E D F )>`.

.. picture:: 0279.g2.svg
   :alt: Proposition 33

| For let any number of consecutive circumferences :g:`$CK$ <0279.g2:( C K )>`,
| :g:`$KL$ <0279.g2:( K L )>` be made equal to the circumference :g:`$BC$ <0279.g2:( B C )>`,
| and any number of consecutive circumferences :g:`$FM$ <0279.g2:( F M )>`, :g:`$MN$ <0279.g2:( M N )>` equal
| to the circumference :g:`$EF$ <0279.g2:( E F )>`;
| and let :g:`$GK$ <0279.g2:/ G K />`, :g:`$GL$ <0279.g2:/ G L />`, :g:`$HM$ <0279.g2:/ H M />`, :g:`$HN$ <0279.g2:/ H N />` be joined.

.. subclause::

   | Then, since the circumferences :g:`$BC$ <0279.g2:( B C )>`, :g:`$CK$ <0279.g2:( C K )>`, :g:`$KL$ <0279.g2:( K L )>` are equal
   | to one another,
