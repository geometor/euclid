## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Greek Text of Def. 1**: `Μονάς ἐστιν, καθ᾽ ἣν ἕκαστον τῶν ὄντων ἓν λέγεται.` ("An unit is that by virtue of which each of the things that exist is called one.")
- **Historical Analysis**:
  - Iamblichus's commentary on Nicomachus notes that Euclid's definition comes from "more recent" writers (οἱ νεώτεροι) and omitted the qualification "even though it be collective" (κἂν συστηματικὸν ᾖ).
  - Heath surveys six ancient definitions of the monad: Pythagorean boundary between number and parts (ἀριθμοῦ καὶ μορίων μεθόριον); Thymaridas's limiting quantity (περαίνουσα ποσότης); the "form of forms" (εἰδῶν εἶδος, which Heath compares to modern set theory: "Gattung" of "Mengen" / class of classes); Aristotle's indivisible in quantity without position (τὸ κατὰ τὸ ποσὸν ἀδιαίρετον, contrasting unit with point / στιγμὴ ἄθετος); and Chrysippus's Stoic definition as "multitude one" (πλῆθος ἕν).
  - Etymology of μονάς: derived either from remaining unaltered or from isolation (μεμονῶσθαι, μονωτάτη).
