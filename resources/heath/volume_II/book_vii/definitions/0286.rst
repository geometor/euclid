DEFINITION 2.
-------------

᾿Αριθμὸς δὲ τὸ ἐκ :term:`μονάδων <unit>` συγκείμενον πλῆθος.

The definition of a *number* is again only one out of many that are on record. :person:`Nicomachus <nicomachus>` (:cite:`I. 7, 1 <nicomachus:intro-arith:i-7-1>`) combines several into one, saying that it is “a defined multitude (:term:`πλῆθος ὡρισμένον <plethos-horismenon>`), or a collection of units (:term:`μονάδων <unit>` σύστημα), or a flow of quantity made up of units” (ποσότητος χύμα ἐκ :term:`μονάδων <unit>` συγκείμενον). Theon, in words almost identical with those attributed by :person:`Stobaeus <stobaeus>` (:cite:`*Eclogae* I. 1, 8 <stobaeus:eclogae:i-1-8>`) to :person:`Moderatus <moderatus-of-gades>`, a Pythagorean, says (p. 18, 3—5): “A number is a collection of units, or a progression (προποδισμός) of multitude beginning from an unit and a retrogression (ἀναποδισμός) ceasing at an unit.” According to :person:`Iamblichus <iamblichus>` (p. 10) the description “collection of units” (:term:`μονάδων <unit>` σύστημα) was applied to the *how many*, i.e. to number, by :person:`Thales <thales>`, following the Egyptian view (κατὰ τὸ Αἰγυπτιακὸν ἀρέσκον), while it was :person:`Eudoxus <eudoxus>` the Pythagorean who said that a number was “a defined multitude” (:term:`πλῆθος ὡρισμένον <plethos-horismenon>`). :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>` has a number of definitions which come to the same thing: “limited multitude” (πλῆθος τὸ πεπερασμένον, :cite:`*Metaph.* 1020 a 13 <aristotle:met:1020-a-13>`), “multitude” (or “combination”) “of units” or “multitude of indivisibles” (*ibid.* 1053 a 30, 1039 a 12, 1085 b 22), “several *ones*” (ἕνα πλείω, :cite:`*Phys.* III. 7, 207 b 7 <aristotle:phys:iii-7-207-b-7>`), “multitude measurable by one” (:cite:`*Metaph.* 1057 a 3 <aristotle:met:1057-a-3>`) and “multitude measured and multitude of measures,” the “measure” being unity, τὸ ἕν (*ibid.* 1088 a 5).

DEFINITION 3.
-------------

Μέρος ἐστὶν ἀριθμὸς :term:`ἀριθμοῦ <number>` ὁ ἐλάσσων τοῦ μείζονος, ὅταν καταμετρῇ τὸν μείζονα.

By a *part* Euclid means a submultiple, as he does in V. Def. 1, with which definition this one is identical except for the substitution of *number* (:term:`ἀριθμός <number>`) for *magnitude* (:term:`μέγεθος <megethos>`); cf. note on V. Def. 1. :person:`Nicomachus <nicomachus>` uses the word “submultiple” (ὑποπολλαπλάσιος) also. He defines it in a way corresponding to his definition of multiple (see note on Def. 5 below) as follows (I. 18, 2): “The submultiple, which is by nature first in the division of inequality (called) less, is the number which, when compared with a greater, can measure it more times than once so as to fill it exactly (πληρούντως).” Similarly *sub-double* (ὑποδιπλάσιος) is found in :person:`Nicomachus <nicomachus>` meaning *half*, and so on.

DEFINITION 4.
-------------

Μέρη δέ, ὅταν μὴ καταμετρῇ.

By the expression *parts* (:term:`μέρη <parts-non-aliquot>`, the plural of :term:`μέρος <part-aliquot>`) Euclid denotes what we should call a *proper fraction*. That is, a *part* being a submultiple, the rather inconvenient term *parts* means any number of such submultiples making up a fraction less than unity. I have not found the word used in this special sense elsewhere, e.g. in :person:`Nicomachus <nicomachus>`, :person:`Theon of Smyrna <theon-of-smyrna>` or :person:`Iamblichus <iamblichus>`, except in one place of Theon (p. 79, 26) where it is used of a proper fraction, of which $\frac{2}{3}$ is an illustration.
