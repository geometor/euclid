IX. 34 says that "If a number neither be one of those which are continually doubled from 2, nor have its half odd, it is both even-times even and even-times odd." The term :term:`*odd-times even* <odd-times-even-number>` (:term:`περισσάκις ἄρτιος <odd-times-even-number>`) not occurring in these propositions, nor anywhere else after the definition, that definition becomes superfluous. :person:`Iamblichus <iamblichus>` however (p. 24, 7—14) quotes these enunciations differently. In the first he has instead of "even-times odd only" the words "*both even-times odd and odd-times even*"; and, in the second, for "both even-times even and even-times odd" he has "is both even-times even and at the same time even-times odd and even-times even." In both cases therefore "odd-times even" is added to the enunciation as :person:`Iamblichus <iamblichus>` had it; the words cannot have been added by :person:`Iamblichus <iamblichus>` himself because he himself does not use the term :term:`*odd-times even* <odd-times-even-number>`, but the one word :term:`*odd-even* <odd-times-even-number>` (:term:`περισσάρτιος <odd-times-even-number>`). In order to get over the difficulties involved by Def. 10 and these differences of reading we have practically to choose between (1) accepting :person:`Iamblichus <iamblichus>`' reading in all three places and (2) adhering to the reading of our MSS. in IX. 33, 34 and rejecting Def. 10 altogether as an interpolation. Now the readings of our text of IX. 33, 34 are those of the Vatican MS. and the Theonine MSS. as well; hence they must go back to a time before Theon, and must therefore be almost as old as those of :person:`Iamblichus <iamblichus>`. :person:`Heiberg <heiberg>` considers it improbable that Euclid would wish to maintain a pointless distinction between :term:`*even-times odd* <even-times-odd-number>` and :term:`*odd-times even* <odd-times-even-number>`, and on the whole concludes that Def. 10 was first interpolated by some ignorant person who did not notice the difference between the Euclidean and Pythagorean classification, but merely noticed the absence of a definition of :term:`*odd-times even* <odd-times-even-number>` and fabricated one as a companion to the other. When this was done, it would be easy to see that the statement in IX. 33 that the number referred to is "*even-times odd only*" was not strictly true, and that the addition of the words "and odd-times even" was necessary in IX. 33 and IX. 34 as well.

DEFINITION 10.
--------------

Περισσάκις δὲ περισσὸς :term:`ἀριθμός <number>` ἐστιν ὁ ὑπὸ περισσοῦ :term:`ἀριθμοῦ <number>` μετρούμενος κατὰ περισσὸν :term:`ἀριθμόν <number>`.

The :term:`*odd-times odd* <odd-times-odd-number>` number is not defined as such by :person:`Nicomachus <nicomachus>` and :person:`Iamblichus <iamblichus>`; for them these numbers would apparently belong to the *composite* subdivision of *odd* numbers. :person:`Theon of Smyrna <theon-of-smyrna>` on the other hand says (p. 23, 21) that :term:`*odd-times odd* <odd-times-odd-number>` was one of the names applied to *prime* numbers (excluding 2), for these have two odd factors, namely 1 and the number itself. This is certainly a curious use of the term.

DEFINITION 11.
--------------

Πρῶτος :term:`ἀριθμός <number>` ἐστιν ὁ μονάδι μόνῃ μετρούμενος.

A *prime number* (:term:`πρῶτος ἀριθμός <prime-number>`) is called by :person:`Nicomachus <nicomachus>`, Theon, and :person:`Iamblichus <iamblichus>` a "prime and incomposite (ἀσύνθετος) number." Theon (p. 23, 9) defines it practically as Euclid does, viz. as a number "measured by no number, but by an unit only." :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>` too says that a prime number is not measured by any number (:cite:`*Anal. post.* II. 13, 96 a 36 <aristotle:anal-post:ii-13-96-a-36>`), an unit not being a number (:cite:`*Metaph.* 1088 a 6 <aristotle:met:1088-a-6>`), but only the beginning of number (:person:`Theon of Smyrna <theon-of-smyrna>` says the same thing, p. 24, 23).
