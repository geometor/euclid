Summary & Continuity
--------------------

- **Folio**: 0295 (Page 289)
- **Book / Section**: Book VII, Definition 16 (Note on Def. 16)
- **Preceding Context**: Continues from Folio 0294 (Page 288), discussing the distinction between linear and plane numbers represented by pebbles.
- **Succeeding Context**: Connects to Folio 0296 (Page 290), continuing the analysis of successive summation generating gnomons, heteromecic numbers, and square numbers, before reaching Definitions 17 and 18.
- **Core Content**:

  - Polygonal numbers in Pythagorean arithmetic (triangular numbers, square numbers, pentagonal numbers) and gnomons (series of differences: odd numbers for squares, natural numbers for triangles, arithmetic progressions with common difference $k-2$ for $k$-gons).
  - Distinction between *heteromecic* numbers ($n(n+1)$) and *promece* numbers ($n(n+m), m \ge 2$) in Nicomachus, Theon of Smyrna, and Iamblichus.
  - Defense of Euclid against Iamblichus' criticisms regarding the classification of oblong numbers versus squares.

Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
--------------------------------------------

- **Greek Terms & Orthography**:

  - προμήκης (*oblong / promece*)
  - ἑτερομήκης (*heteromecic / oblong with sides differing by 1*)
  - ἑτερομήκεις (*heteromecic numbers*)

- **Mathematical Notes**:

  - Gnomonic generation of figurate numbers:

    - Triangular: $1, 2, 3, 4, \dots \implies T_n = \frac{n(n+1)}{2}$
    - Square: $1, 3, 5, 7, \dots \implies S_n = n^2$
    - Pentagonal: $1, 4, 7, 10, \dots \implies P_n = \frac{n(3n-1)}{2}$

  - Analysis of Iamblichus' claim that 36 and 16 are both square and oblong, showing it rests on a confusion between composite factorization and geometric shape definition.
