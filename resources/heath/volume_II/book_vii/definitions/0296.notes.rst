Transcription Notes: Folio 0296 (Page 290)
==========================================

Summary & Folio Continuity
--------------------------

- **Page Context**: Page 290 continues the commentary on Book VII Definition 16 from Folio 0295, presents Euclid's Greek text for Definition 17 (Solid numbers and their sides), and provides Heath's detailed commentary on ancient classifications of solid numbers.
- **Antecedent Continuity**: The top line concludes Heath's discussion on Definition 16 (gnomonic generation of ἑτερομήκεις numbers by summing even numbers: $1 \cdot 2 = 2$, $2 \cdot 3 = 2+4$, $3 \cdot 4 = 2+4+6$).
- **Subsequent Continuity**: The bottom line introduces two classes of three-factor solid numbers with two equal dimensions; the definitions of these classes (plinth and beam) continue directly onto Folio 0297 (Page 291).

Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
--------------------------------------------

- **Definition 17**:

  - Greek: ``Ὅταν δὲ τρεῖς ἀριθμοὶ πολλαπλασιάσαντες ἀλλήλους ποιῶσί τινα, ὁ γενόμενος στερεός ἐστιν, πλευραὶ δὲ αὐτοῦ οἱ πολλαπλασιάσαντες ἀλλήλους ἀριθμοί.``
  - English equivalent (given collectively by Heath at the start of Book VII): "When three numbers having multiplied one another make some number, the number so produced is solid, and its sides are the numbers which have multiplied one another."

- **Ancient Authorities Cited**:

  - **Aristotle**: Conceptualizes a solid number as "so many times so many times so many" (ποσάκις ποσάκις ποσόν).
  - **Plato**: *Theaetetus* (148 A–B) passage on lines representing square sides and oblong numbers (*roots*, δυνάμεις), extended to solids (καὶ περὶ τὰ στερεὰ ἄλλο τοιοῦτον).
  - **Nicomachus** (*Introductio Arithmetica* II. 13–16), **Theon of Smyrna** (pp. 41–42, ed. Hiller), and **Iamblichus** (*In Nicomachi arithmeticam introductionem*, p. 95, 15 sqq., ed. Pistelli).

- **Representations & Classifications**:

  - *Point representation*: Pyramidal numbers (tetrahedral / polygonal base layers). Smallest is 4 (equilateral triangle base of 3 points + 1 apex point).
  - *Three-factor classifications*:

    1. Equal by equal by equal (ἰσάκις ἰσάκις ἴσος = cube).
    2. Unequal by unequal by unequal (ἀνισάκις ἀνισάκις ἄνισος = scalene, or σφηνίσκοι [wedge-shaped], σφηκίσκοι [from σφήξ, wasp-shaped], βωμίσκοι [altar-shaped]).
    3. Intermediate parallelepipedal numbers whose base planes are ἑτερομήκεις ($n(n+1)$).
