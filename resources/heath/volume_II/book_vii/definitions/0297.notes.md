## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations

- **Definition 17 (continued)**:
  - Item 4: *Plinth* (πλινθίς), equal by equal by less (ἰσάκις ἴσος ἐλαττονάκις), e.g. $8 \cdot 8 \cdot 3$.
  - Item 5: *Beam* (δοκίς), equal by equal by greater (ἰσάκις ἴσος μειζονάκις), e.g. $3 \cdot 3 \cdot 7$; also called στηλίς (diminutive of στήλη, little pillar).
  - Pyramidal frusta: *truncated* (κόλουροι), *twice-truncated* (δικόλουροι), *thrice-truncated* (τρικόλουροι); Greek phrase ἐν συγγράμμασι μάλιστα τοῖς θεωρηματικοῖς; Theon of Smyrna's term ἡ τὴν κορυφὴν ἀποτετμημένη (vertex cut off, compared to a plane trapezium).
- **Definition 18**:
  - Greek: `Τετράγωνος ἀριθμός ἐστιν ὁ ἰσάκις ἴσος ἢ [ὁ] ὑπὸ δύο ἴσων ἀριθμῶν περιεχόμενος.`
  - Heath's commentary discusses *cyclic* numbers (κυκλικοί): square numbers whose final digit in decimal notation matches their root (e.g., $1^2 = 1$, $5^2 = 25$, $6^2 = 36$).
  - *Typographical Erratum*: In line 2 of the note on Definition 18, the original print shows `(in the decimal notatior)` due to a broken/miscast type 'n' resembling an 'r'.
- **Definition 19**:
  - Greek: `Κύβος δὲ ὁ ἰσάκις ἴσος ἰσάκις ἢ [ὁ] ὑπὸ τριῶν ἴσων ἀριθμῶν περιεχόμενος.`
  - Heath's commentary discusses *spherical* (σφαιρικοί) or *recurrent* (ἀποκαταστατικοί) numbers: cubes of numbers ending in 5 or 6 (e.g. $5^3 = 125$, $6^3 = 216$), requiring both the square and cube to end in the same digit. The cube $4^3 = 64$ was excluded because $4^2 = 16$.
  - Nesselmann's observation on the shift in underlying conceptual principles between figured numbers and recurrent digits.
