## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations

- **Definition 20**:
  - Greek: `Ἀριθμοὶ ἀνάλογόν εἰσιν, ὅταν ὁ πρῶτος τοῦ δευτέρου καὶ ὁ τρίτος τοῦ τετάρτου ἰσάκις ᾖ πολλαπλάσιος ἢ τὸ αὐτὸ μέρος ἢ τὰ αὐτὰ μέρη ὦσιν.`
  - English equivalent: "Numbers are proportional when the first is the same multiple, or the same part, or the same parts, of the second that the third is of the fourth."
- **Analysis of Ratio & Proportion**:
  - Heath highlights that Euclid omits a definition of ratio in Book VII, taking it as understood from Book V Def. 3 (with magnitudes replaced by numbers and πηλικότητα omitted).
  - Classification of arithmetical ratios (Theon of Smyrna, quoting Adrastus):
    - πολλαπλάσιος (multiple)
    - ἐπιμόριος (superparticular, $(n+1)/n$)
    - ἐπιμερής (superpartient, $(n+m)/n$)
    - πολλαπλασιεπιμόριος, πολλαπλασιεπιμερής
    - sub-ratios formed by prefixing ὑπό- / *sub-*.
  - Conceptual confusion in antiquity between *proportion* (ἀναλογία) and *mean* (μεσότης):
    - Thrasyllus grouped arithmetic, geometric, and harmonic means as "three proportions".
    - Nicomachus attempted to broaden the term proportion (κυρίως, σύλληψις σχέσεων).
    - Iamblichus and Adrastus preserved the distinction, noting geometric proportion as proportion *par excellence*.
