# Transcription Notes: Folio 0299 (Page 293)

## Summary & Folio Continuity

- **Page Context**: Page 293 concludes the commentary on Definition 20, presents Definition 21 (Similar plane and solid numbers) and Definition 22 (Perfect number), each accompanied by Heath's critical commentary.

## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations

- **Definition 20 (conclusion)**:
  - Distinction between *continuous* (συνεχής / Nicomachus: συνημμένη / Euclid: ἑξῆς ἀνάλογον, $a : b = b : c$) and *separated* (διῃρημένη / Nicomachus: διεζευγμένη, $a : b = c : d$). Aristotle's *Nicomachean Ethics* (1131 a 32) cited.
- **Definition 21**:
  - Greek: `Ὅμοιοι ἐπίπεδοι καὶ στερεοὶ ἀριθμοί εἰσιν οἱ ἀνάλογον ἔχοντες τὰς πλευράς.`
  - English equivalent: "Similar plane and solid numbers are those which have their sides proportional."
  - Heath notes Theon of Smyrna's use of ἑτερομήκης as broadly synonymous with προμήκης (any oblong number), because strictly $n(n+1)$ numbers cannot be similar unless they are equal.
  - Ancient contrast between square numbers (ἰδιομήκης / ταὐτομήκης; "the same and similar", ταὐτούς τε καὶ ὁμοίους) and oblong numbers ("dissimilar and other", ἀνομοίους καὶ θατέρους).
  - *Typographical Erratum*: Line 5 of the note on Definition 21 contains an erroneous period in `but. as synonymous with προμήκης,` in the printed text, faithfully preserved in transcription.
- **Definition 22**:
  - Greek: `Τέλειος ἀριθμός ἐστιν ὁ τοῖς ἑαυτοῦ μέρεσιν ἴσος ὤν.`
  - English equivalent: "A perfect number is that which is equal to its own parts."
  - Referenced later in Euclid's Book IX, Proposition 36 (Euclidean formula for even perfect numbers: $2^{n-1}(2^n - 1)$ where $2^n - 1$ is prime).
  - Nicomachus and Theon contrasted perfect numbers with *over-perfect* (ὑπερτελής / ὑπερτέλειος) and *defective* (ἐλλιπής) numbers.
