## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations

- **Definition 22 (conclusion)**:
  - Over-perfect numbers: $\sigma(n) - n > n$ (e.g. $n = 12 \implies 1+2+3+4+6 = 16 > 12$; $n = 24 \implies 1+2+3+4+6+8+12 = 36 > 24$).
  - Defective numbers (ἐλλιπής): $\sigma(n) - n < n$ (e.g. $n = 8 \implies 1+2+4 = 7 < 8$; $n = 14 \implies 1+2+7 = 10 < 14$).
  - Pythagorean mystical perfection of 10 (the *Tetractys*, $1+2+3+4=10$) and 3 (beginning, middle, end; geometric progression from point to line to plane triangle to solid potentiality).
- **Unexpressed Axioms in Book VII**:
  1. Transitivity of divisibility: $A \mid B \land B \mid C \implies A \mid C$.
  2. Divisibility of difference: $A \mid B \land A \mid C \implies A \mid (B - C)$ for $B > C$.
  3. Divisibility of sum: $A \mid B \land A \mid C \implies A \mid (B + C)$.
- **Pythagorean Theory & Plato's *Timaeus***:
  - Greek verb: `πυθαγορίζει` ("Plato pythagorizes").
  - Plato *Timaeus* 32 A–B: one mean between plane squares, two means between solid cubes (corresponding to Eucl. VIII. 11, 12).
  - Terms: δυνάμεις and ὄγκοι corresponding to ἐπίπεδα and στερεά.
  - Heath refutes Th. Häbler (*Bibliotheca Mathematica*, $\text{VIII}_3$, 1908, pp. 173–174), who connected the passage to the Delian problem (duplication of the cube), citing Nicomachus (II. 24, 6) and Democritus the Platonist (via Proclus).
