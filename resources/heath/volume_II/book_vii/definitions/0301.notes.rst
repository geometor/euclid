Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
--------------------------------------------

- **Pre-Euclidean Arithmetic Sources**:

  - *Sectio Canonis* (κατατομὴ κανόνος): Prop. 2 cites the principle of continued proportion ("we learned", ἐμάθομεν), effectively quoting *Elem.* VIII. 7.
  - Prop. 3 of *Sectio Canonis*: No mean proportional exists between two numbers in superparticular ratio (ἐπιμόριος, $(n+1)/n$).

- **Archytas' Proof**:

  - Preserved by Boethius (*De institutione musica*, III. 11, ed. Friedlein) and edited by Paul Tannery (*Bibliotheca Mathematica*, $\text{VI}_3$, 1905/6, p. 227).
  - Terminology: superparticularis proportio = ἐπιμόριον διάστημα; excess $D$ in $C, DE$ ($DE = D + E$ with $E = C$).
  - Logical structure: Archytas proves $D = 1$ by showing that if $D > 1$, $D \mid DE \implies D \mid E \implies D \mid C$, contradicting the premise that $C, DE$ are prime to one another in lowest terms (presupposing Eucl. VII. 20, 22, 33).
  - Historical conclusion: Definitive evidence that a formal Euclidean-style *Elements of Arithmetic* existed by the time of Archytas (c. 430–365 B.C.) and Eudoxus.

- **Footnote 1 Analysis (concluded)**:

  - Proclus citing Democritus the Platonist (*In Platonis Timaeum commentaria*, 149 c): Plato's passage concerned similar plane and solid numbers with *rational* sides, not the Delian problem of duplicating the cube.
  - Mathematical refutation: Between cubes ($a^3, b^3$), two mean proportionals always exist in rational or integer numbers ($a^2 b, ab^2$). In the Delian duplication problem, the proportionals are irrational straight lines ($1, \sqrt[3]{2}, \sqrt[3]{4}, 2$), which would make Plato's restriction to two means meaningless for general $k$ unless $k$ were a cube.
