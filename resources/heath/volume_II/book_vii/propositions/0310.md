### PROPOSITION 5.

<p class="enunciation">If a number be a part of a number, and another be the<br />
same part of another, the sum will also be the same part of the<br />
sum that the one is of the one.</p>

For let the number <span data-ref="0310.g1:A">$A$</span> be a part of <span data-ref="0310.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>,<br />
and another, <span data-ref="0310.g1:D">$D$</span>, the same part of another <span data-ref="0310.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> that <span data-ref="0310.g1:A">$A$</span> is of <span data-ref="0310.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>;

I say that the sum of <span data-ref="0310.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0310.g1:D">$D$</span> is also the same<br />
part of the sum of <span data-ref="0310.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, <span data-ref="0310.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> that <span data-ref="0310.g1:A">$A$</span> is of <span data-ref="0310.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>.

![Book VII, Proposition 5](0310.g1.svg)

<p class="sub-clause">For since, whatever part <span data-ref="0310.g1:A">$A$</span> is of <span data-ref="0310.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, <span data-ref="0310.g1:D">$D$</span><br />
is also the same part of <span data-ref="0310.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore, as many numbers as there are in<br />
<span data-ref="0310.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> equal to <span data-ref="0310.g1:A">$A$</span>, so many numbers are there<br />
also in <span data-ref="0310.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> equal to <span data-ref="0310.g1:D">$D$</span>.</p>

Let <span data-ref="0310.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> be divided into the numbers equal to <span data-ref="0310.g1:A">$A$</span>, namely<br />
<span data-ref="0310.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span>, <span data-ref="0310.g1:/ G C /">$GC$</span>,<br />
and <span data-ref="0310.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> into the numbers equal to <span data-ref="0310.g1:D">$D$</span>, namely <span data-ref="0310.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span>, <span data-ref="0310.g1:/ H F /">$HF$</span>;
<p class="assertion">then the multitude of <span data-ref="0310.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span>, <span data-ref="0310.g1:/ G C /">$GC$</span> will be equal to the multitude<br />
of <span data-ref="0310.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span>, <span data-ref="0310.g1:/ H F /">$HF$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">And, since <span data-ref="0310.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0310.g1:A">$A$</span>, and <span data-ref="0310.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span> to <span data-ref="0310.g1:D">$D$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0310.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span>, <span data-ref="0310.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span> are also equal to <span data-ref="0310.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0310.g1:D">$D$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">For the same reason</p>
<p class="assertion"><span data-ref="0310.g1:/ G C /">$GC$</span>, <span data-ref="0310.g1:/ H F /">$HF$</span> are also equal to <span data-ref="0310.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0310.g1:D">$D$</span>.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore, as many numbers as there are in <span data-ref="0310.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> equal to<br />
<span data-ref="0310.g1:A">$A$</span>, so many are there also in <span data-ref="0310.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, <span data-ref="0310.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> equal to <span data-ref="0310.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0310.g1:D">$D$</span>.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore, whatever multiple <span data-ref="0310.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is of <span data-ref="0310.g1:A">$A$</span>, the same multiple<br />
also is the sum of <span data-ref="0310.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, <span data-ref="0310.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> of the sum of <span data-ref="0310.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0310.g1:D">$D$</span>.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore, whatever part <span data-ref="0310.g1:A">$A$</span> is of <span data-ref="0310.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, the same part also<br />
is the sum of <span data-ref="0310.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0310.g1:D">$D$</span> of the sum of <span data-ref="0310.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, <span data-ref="0310.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>.</p>

<p class="qed"><span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

If
$$a = \frac{1}{n}b, \text{ and } c = \frac{1}{n}d, \text{ then}$$
$$a + c = \frac{1}{n}(b + d).$$

The proposition is of course true for any quantity of pairs of numbers<br />
similarly related, as is the next proposition also; and both propositions are<br />
used in the extended form in VII. 9, 10.
