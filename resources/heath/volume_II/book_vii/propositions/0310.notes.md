## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Part Definition**: Relies on Book VII Definition 3 ($\mu\acute{\epsilon}\rho o\varsigma$, unit fraction $\frac{1}{n}$). The proposition proves the distributive property of multiplication/division over addition: if $a = \frac{1}{n}b$ and $c = \frac{1}{n}d$, then $a + c = \frac{1}{n}(b + d)$.
- **Parallel with Book V**: Heath notes that this proposition is the arithmetic analogue of Book V, Proposition 1 (which deals with magnitudes and equimultiples).
- **Terminology**: Euclid uses the phrase "multitude of $BG, GC$" ($\tau\grave{o}\ \pi\lambda\tilde{\eta}\theta o\varsigma$) to designate the count of unit parts.
- **Extensions**: Heath notes that the result immediately generalizes to any finite number of pairs of numbers, a fact invoked in VII. 9 and VII. 10.
