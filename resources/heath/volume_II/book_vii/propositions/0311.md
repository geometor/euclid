### PROPOSITION 6.

<p class="enunciation">If a number be parts of a number, and another be the same<br />
parts of another, the sum will also be the same parts of the sum<br />
that the one is of the one.</p>

For let the number <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> be parts of the number <span data-ref="0311.g1:C">$C$</span>,<br />
and another, <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>, the same parts of another,<br />
<span data-ref="0311.g1:F">$F$</span>, that <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is of <span data-ref="0311.g1:C">$C$</span>;

I say that the sum of <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> is also the<br />
same parts of the sum of <span data-ref="0311.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0311.g1:F">$F$</span> that <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is<br />
of <span data-ref="0311.g1:C">$C$</span>.

![Book VII, Proposition 6](0311.g1.svg)

<p class="sub-clause">For since, whatever parts <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is of <span data-ref="0311.g1:C">$C$</span>,<br />
<span data-ref="0311.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> is also the same parts of <span data-ref="0311.g1:F">$F$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore, as many parts of <span data-ref="0311.g1:C">$C$</span> as there are<br />
in <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, so many parts of <span data-ref="0311.g1:F">$F$</span> are there also in <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>.</p>

Let <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> be divided into the parts of <span data-ref="0311.g1:C">$C$</span>, namely <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ G B /">$GB$</span>,<br />
and <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> into the parts of <span data-ref="0311.g1:F">$F$</span>, namely <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ D H /">$DH$</span>, <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ H E /">$HE$</span>;
<p class="assertion">thus the multitude of <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ G B /">$GB$</span> will be equal to the multitude<br />
of <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ D H /">$DH$</span>, <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ H E /">$HE$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">And since, whatever part <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span> is of <span data-ref="0311.g1:C">$C$</span>, the same part is<br />
<span data-ref="0311.g1:/ D H /">$DH$</span> of <span data-ref="0311.g1:F">$F$</span> also,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore, whatever part <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span> is of <span data-ref="0311.g1:C">$C$</span>, the same part also is the<br />
sum of <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ D H /">$DH$</span> of the sum of <span data-ref="0311.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0311.g1:F">$F$</span>. <span class="justification">[VII. 5]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">For the same reason,</p>
<p class="assertion">whatever part <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ G B /">$GB$</span> is of <span data-ref="0311.g1:C">$C$</span>, the same part also is the sum of<br />
<span data-ref="0311.g1:/ G B /">$GB$</span>, <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ H E /">$HE$</span> of the sum of <span data-ref="0311.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0311.g1:F">$F$</span>.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore, whatever parts <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is of <span data-ref="0311.g1:C">$C$</span>, the same parts also<br />
is the sum of <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> of the sum of <span data-ref="0311.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0311.g1:F">$F$</span>.</p>

<p class="qed"><span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

If
$$a = \frac{m}{n}b, \text{ and } c = \frac{m}{n}d,$$
then
$$a + c = \frac{m}{n}(b + d).$$

More generally, if
$$a = \frac{m}{n}b,\ c = \frac{m}{n}d,\ e = \frac{m}{n}f, \dots$$
then
$$(a + c + e + g + \dots) = \frac{m}{n}(b + d + f + h + \dots).$$
