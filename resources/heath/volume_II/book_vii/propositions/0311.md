### PROPOSITION 6.

*If a number be parts of a number, and another be the same
parts of another, the sum will also be the same parts of the sum
that the one is of the one.*

For let the number <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> be parts of the number <span data-ref="0311.g1:C">$C$</span>,
and another, <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>, the same parts of another,
<span data-ref="0311.g1:F">$F$</span>, that <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is of <span data-ref="0311.g1:C">$C$</span>;
I say that the sum of <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> is also the
same parts of the sum of <span data-ref="0311.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0311.g1:F">$F$</span> that <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is
of <span data-ref="0311.g1:C">$C$</span>.

![Book VII, Proposition 6](0311.g1.svg)

For since, whatever parts <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is of <span data-ref="0311.g1:C">$C$</span>,
<span data-ref="0311.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> is also the same parts of <span data-ref="0311.g1:F">$F$</span>,
therefore, as many parts of <span data-ref="0311.g1:C">$C$</span> as there are
in <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, so many parts of <span data-ref="0311.g1:F">$F$</span> are there also in <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>.

Let <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> be divided into the parts of <span data-ref="0311.g1:C">$C$</span>, namely <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ G B /">$GB$</span>,
and <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> into the parts of <span data-ref="0311.g1:F">$F$</span>, namely <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ D H /">$DH$</span>, <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ H E /">$HE$</span>;
thus the multitude of <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ G B /">$GB$</span> will be equal to the multitude
of <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ D H /">$DH$</span>, <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ H E /">$HE$</span>.

And since, whatever part <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span> is of <span data-ref="0311.g1:C">$C$</span>, the same part is
<span data-ref="0311.g1:/ D H /">$DH$</span> of <span data-ref="0311.g1:F">$F$</span> also,
therefore, whatever part <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span> is of <span data-ref="0311.g1:C">$C$</span>, the same part also is the
sum of <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ D H /">$DH$</span> of the sum of <span data-ref="0311.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0311.g1:F">$F$</span>. [VII. 5]

For the same reason,
whatever part <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ G B /">$GB$</span> is of <span data-ref="0311.g1:C">$C$</span>, the same part also is the sum of
<span data-ref="0311.g1:/ G B /">$GB$</span>, <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ H E /">$HE$</span> of the sum of <span data-ref="0311.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0311.g1:F">$F$</span>.

Therefore, whatever parts <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is of <span data-ref="0311.g1:C">$C$</span>, the same parts also
is the sum of <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0311.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> of the sum of <span data-ref="0311.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0311.g1:F">$F$</span>.

<p align="right">Q. E. D.</p>

If
$$a = \frac{m}{n}b, \text{ and } c = \frac{m}{n}d,$$
then
$$a + c = \frac{m}{n}(b + d).$$

More generally, if
$$a = \frac{m}{n}b,\ c = \frac{m}{n}d,\ e = \frac{m}{n}f, \dots$$
then
$$(a + c + e + g + \dots) = \frac{m}{n}(b + d + f + h + \dots).$$
