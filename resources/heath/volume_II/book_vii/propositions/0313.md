If $a = \frac{1}{n}b$ and $c = \frac{1}{n}d$, we are to prove that
$$a - c = \frac{1}{n}(b - d),$$
a result differing from that of VII. 5 in that *minus* is substituted for *plus*.
Euclid's method is as follows.

Suppose that $e$ is taken such that
$$a - c = \frac{1}{n}e. \tag{1}$$
Now
$$c = \frac{1}{n}d.$$
Therefore
$$a = \frac{1}{n}(d + e), \tag{VII. 5}$$
whence, from the hypothesis, $d + e = b$,<br />
so that $e = b - d$,<br />
and, substituting this value of $e$ in (1), we have
$$a - c = \frac{1}{n}(b - d).$$

### PROPOSITION 8.

<p class="enunciation">If a number be the same parts of a number that a number<br />
subtracted is of a number subtracted, the remainder will also<br />
be the same parts of the remainder that the whole is of the<br />
whole.</p>

For let the number <span data-ref="0313.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> be the same parts of the number<br />
<span data-ref="0313.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> that <span data-ref="0313.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> subtracted is of <span data-ref="0313.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span><br />
subtracted;

I say that the remainder <span data-ref="0313.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span> is<br />
also the same parts of the re-<br />
mainder <span data-ref="0313.g1:/ F D /">$FD$</span> that the whole <span data-ref="0313.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span><br />
is of the whole <span data-ref="0313.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>.

<p class="assertion">Therefore, whatever parts <span data-ref="0313.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span> is of <span data-ref="0313.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, the same parts<br />
