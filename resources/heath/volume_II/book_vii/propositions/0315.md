Repeat this for all the parts equal to $e$ and $f$ that there are in $a$, $b$ respectively, and we have, by addition ($a$, $b$ containing $m$ of such parts respectively),
$$m(e - f) = \frac{m}{n}(b - d).$$

But
$$m(e - f) = a - c.$$

Therefore
$$a - c = \frac{m}{n}(b - d).$$

The propositions VII. 7, 8 are complementary to V. 5 which gives the corresponding result with *multiple* in the place of "part" or "parts."

### PROPOSITION 9.

*If a number be a part of a number, and another be the
same part of another, alternately also, whatever part or parts
the first is of the third, the same part, or the same parts, will
the second also be of the fourth.*

For let the number <span data-ref="0315.g1:A">$A$</span> be a part of the number <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>,
and another, <span data-ref="0315.g1:D">$D$</span>, the same part of another, <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>,
that <span data-ref="0315.g1:A">$A$</span> is of <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>;
I say that, alternately also, whatever part or
parts <span data-ref="0315.g1:A">$A$</span> is of <span data-ref="0315.g1:D">$D$</span>, the same part or parts is <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>
of <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> also.

![Book VII, Proposition 9](0315.g1.svg)

For since, whatever part <span data-ref="0315.g1:A">$A$</span> is of <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, the
same part also is <span data-ref="0315.g1:D">$D$</span> of <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>,
therefore, as many numbers as there are in <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> equal to <span data-ref="0315.g1:A">$A$</span>,
so many also are there in <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> equal to <span data-ref="0315.g1:D">$D$</span>.

Let <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> be divided into the numbers equal to <span data-ref="0315.g1:A">$A$</span>, namely
<span data-ref="0315.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span>, <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ G C /">$GC$</span>,
and <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> into those equal to <span data-ref="0315.g1:D">$D$</span>, namely <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span>, <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ H F /">$HF$</span>;
thus the multitude of <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span>, <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ G C /">$GC$</span> will be equal to the multitude
of <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span>, <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ H F /">$HF$</span>.

Now, since the numbers <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span>, <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ G C /">$GC$</span> are equal to one another,
and the numbers <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span>, <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ H F /">$HF$</span> are also equal to one another,
while the multitude of <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span>, <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ G C /">$GC$</span> is equal to the multitude of
<span data-ref="0315.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span>, <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ H F /">$HF$</span>,
therefore, whatever part or parts <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span> is of <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span>, the same
part or the same parts is <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ G C /">$GC$</span> of <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ H F /">$HF$</span> also;
so that, in addition, whatever part or parts <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span> is of <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span>,
the same part also, or the same parts, is the sum <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> of the
sum <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>. [VII. 5, 6]
