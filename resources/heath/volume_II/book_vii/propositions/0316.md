If $a = \frac{1}{n}b$ and $c = \frac{1}{n}d$, then, whatever fraction ("part" or "parts") $a$ is of<br />
$c$, the same fraction will $b$ be of $d$.

Dividing $b$ into each of its parts equal to $a$, and $d$ into each of its parts<br />
equal to $c$, it is clear that, whatever fraction one of the parts $a$ is of one of the<br />
parts $c$, the same fraction is any other of the parts $a$ of any other of the parts $c$.

And the number of the parts $a$ is equal to the number of the parts $c$, viz. $n$.

Therefore, by VII. 5, 6, $na$ is the same fraction of $nc$ that $a$ is of $c$, i.e. $b$ is<br />
the same fraction of $d$ that $a$ is of $c$.

### PROPOSITION 10.

<p class="enunciation">If a number be parts of a number, and another be the<br />
same parts of another, alternately also, whatever parts or part<br />
the first is of the third, the same parts or the same part will<br />
the second also be of the fourth.</p>

For let the number <span data-ref="0316.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> be parts of the number <span data-ref="0316.g1:C">$C$</span>,<br />
and another, <span data-ref="0316.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>, the same parts of another,<br />
<span data-ref="0316.g1:F">$F$</span>;

I say that, alternately also, whatever parts or<br />
part <span data-ref="0316.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is of <span data-ref="0316.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>, the same parts or the<br />
same part is <span data-ref="0316.g1:C">$C$</span> of <span data-ref="0316.g1:F">$F$</span> also.

<p class="sub-clause">For since, whatever parts <span data-ref="0316.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is of <span data-ref="0316.g1:C">$C$</span>,<br />

<p class="sub-clause">Now since, whatever part <span data-ref="0316.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span> is of <span data-ref="0316.g1:C">$C$</span>, the same part also<br />
