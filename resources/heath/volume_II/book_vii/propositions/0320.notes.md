# Transcription Notes: Folio 0320 (Page 314)

## Summary & Folio Continuity
- **Proposition 14 (concluded)**: Completes the proof carried over from Page 313 (Folio 0319). Cites `[VII. 13]` and `[id.]` for alternate proportion and transitivity; ends without a formal `Q. E. D.`
- **Heath Commentary on Prop 14**:
  - Provides algebraic representation showing simplicity of proof via alternation.
  - Crucial foundational critique explaining why this method fails for Book V, Proposition 22: alternating ratios requires all terms to be of the same kind, which holds for numbers (discrete arithmetic quantities) but not for general geometric magnitudes.
  - Scholarly debate with Heiberg regarding whether Euclid assumed Book V, Proposition 11 (equality of ratios) or regarded ratio transitivity in arithmetic as an immediate axiom arising from numerical equality of fractions.
- **Proposition 15 (enunciation)**: Enunciation printed at the foot of Page 314; demonstration begins on Page 315 (Folio 0321).

## Textual / Lexical / Mathematical Observations
- **Reference shorthand `[id.]`**: Euclid / Heath uses `[id.]` (*idem*) to indicate repetition of the previously cited authority (here VII. 13).
- **Foundational Divergence between Book V and Book VII**: Heath's commentary provides an essential perspective on Euclid's dual development of ratio theory—Eudoxian general magnitude theory versus Pythagorean/arithmetic discrete unit theory.
- **Printing artifact**: Heath's text reads "six numbers" (the scan exhibits slight ink distortion making the 'm' appear like 'in').
