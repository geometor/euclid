### PROPOSITION 16.

<p class="enunciation">If two numbers by multiplying one another make certain<br />
numbers, the numbers so produced[^316_1] will be equal to one another.</p>

Let <span data-ref="0322.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0322.g1:B">$B$</span> be two numbers, and let <span data-ref="0322.g1:A">$A$</span> by multiplying <span data-ref="0322.g1:B">$B$</span><br />
make <span data-ref="0322.g1:C">$C$</span>, and <span data-ref="0322.g1:B">$B$</span> by multiplying<br />
<span data-ref="0322.g1:A">$A$</span> make <span data-ref="0322.g1:D">$D$</span>;<br />
I say that <span data-ref="0322.g1:C">$C$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0322.g1:D">$D$</span>.

![Proposition 16 Diagram](0322.g1.svg)

<p class="sub-clause">For, since <span data-ref="0322.g1:A">$A$</span> by multiply-<br />
ing <span data-ref="0322.g1:B">$B$</span> has made <span data-ref="0322.g1:C">$C$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0322.g1:B">$B$</span> measures <span data-ref="0322.g1:C">$C$</span> ac-<br />
cording to the units in <span data-ref="0322.g1:A">$A$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">But the unit <span data-ref="0322.g1:E">$E$</span> also measures the number <span data-ref="0322.g1:A">$A$</span> according to<br />
the units in it;</p>

<p class="assertion">therefore the unit <span data-ref="0322.g1:E">$E$</span> measures <span data-ref="0322.g1:A">$A$</span> the same number of times<br />
that <span data-ref="0322.g1:B">$B$</span> measures <span data-ref="0322.g1:C">$C$</span>.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore, alternately, the unit <span data-ref="0322.g1:E">$E$</span> measures the number <span data-ref="0322.g1:B">$B$</span><br />
the same number of times that <span data-ref="0322.g1:A">$A$</span> measures <span data-ref="0322.g1:C">$C$</span>. <span class="justification">[VII. 15]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">Again, since <span data-ref="0322.g1:B">$B$</span> by multiplying <span data-ref="0322.g1:A">$A$</span> has made <span data-ref="0322.g1:D">$D$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0322.g1:A">$A$</span> measures <span data-ref="0322.g1:D">$D$</span> according to the units in <span data-ref="0322.g1:B">$B$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">But the unit <span data-ref="0322.g1:E">$E$</span> also measures <span data-ref="0322.g1:B">$B$</span> according to the units<br />
in it;</p>

<p class="assertion">therefore the unit <span data-ref="0322.g1:E">$E$</span> measures the number <span data-ref="0322.g1:B">$B$</span> the same<br />
number of times that <span data-ref="0322.g1:A">$A$</span> measures <span data-ref="0322.g1:D">$D$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">But the unit <span data-ref="0322.g1:E">$E$</span> measured the number <span data-ref="0322.g1:B">$B$</span> the same number<br />
of times that <span data-ref="0322.g1:A">$A$</span> measures <span data-ref="0322.g1:C">$C$</span>;</p>

<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0322.g1:A">$A$</span> measures each of the numbers <span data-ref="0322.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0322.g1:D">$D$</span> the same<br />
number of times.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore <span data-ref="0322.g1:C">$C$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0322.g1:D">$D$</span>.</p>

<p class="qed">Therefore etc.<span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

[^316_1]: **2. The numbers so produced.** The Greek has οἱ γενόμενοι ἐξ αὐτῶν, "the (numbers) produced *from them*." By "from them" Euclid means "from the original numbers," though this is not very clear even in the Greek. I think ambiguity is best avoided by leaving out the words.

---

This proposition proves that, *if any numbers be multiplied together, the order<br />
of multiplication is indifferent*, or $ab = ba$.

It is important to get a clear understanding of what Euclid means when<br />
he speaks of *one number multiplying another*. VII. Def. 15 states that the<br />
effect of "$a$ multiplying $b$" is taking $a$ times $b$. We shall always represent<br />
"$a$ times $b$" by $ab$ and "$b$ times $a$" by $ba$. This being premised, the proof<br />
that $ab = ba$ may be represented as follows in the language of proportions.
