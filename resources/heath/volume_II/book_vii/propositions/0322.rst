PROPOSITION 16.
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

.. enunciation::

   If two numbers by multiplying one another make certain numbers, the numbers so produced\ [#f316_1]_ will be equal to one another.

| Let :g:`$A$ <0322.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0322.g1:B>` be two numbers, and let :g:`$A$ <0322.g1:A>` by multiplying :g:`$B$ <0322.g1:B>`
| make :g:`$C$ <0322.g1:C>`, and :g:`$B$ <0322.g1:B>` by multiplying
| :g:`$A$ <0322.g1:A>` make :g:`$D$ <0322.g1:D>`;
| I say that :g:`$C$ <0322.g1:C>` is equal to :g:`$D$ <0322.g1:D>`.

.. picture:: 0322.g1.svg
   :alt: Proposition 16 Diagram

.. subclause::

   | For, since :g:`$A$ <0322.g1:A>` by multiply-
   | ing :g:`$B$ <0322.g1:B>` has made :g:`$C$ <0322.g1:C>`,

.. assertion::

   | therefore :g:`$B$ <0322.g1:B>` measures :g:`$C$ <0322.g1:C>` ac-
   | cording to the units in :g:`$A$ <0322.g1:A>`.

.. subclause::

   | But the unit :g:`$E$ <0322.g1:E>` also measures the number :g:`$A$ <0322.g1:A>` according to
   | the units in it;

.. assertion::

   | therefore the unit :g:`$E$ <0322.g1:E>` measures :g:`$A$ <0322.g1:A>` the same number of times
   | that :g:`$B$ <0322.g1:B>` measures :g:`$C$ <0322.g1:C>`.

.. assertion::

   | Therefore, alternately, the unit :g:`$E$ <0322.g1:E>` measures the number :g:`$B$ <0322.g1:B>`
   | the same number of times that :g:`$A$ <0322.g1:A>` measures :g:`$C$ <0322.g1:C>`. :just:`[VII. 15] <VII.15>`

.. subclause::

   Again, since :g:`$B$ <0322.g1:B>` by multiplying :g:`$A$ <0322.g1:A>` has made :g:`$D$ <0322.g1:D>`,

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$A$ <0322.g1:A>` measures :g:`$D$ <0322.g1:D>` according to the units in :g:`$B$ <0322.g1:B>`.

.. subclause::

   | But the unit :g:`$E$ <0322.g1:E>` also measures :g:`$B$ <0322.g1:B>` according to the units
   | in it;

.. assertion::

   | therefore the unit :g:`$E$ <0322.g1:E>` measures the number :g:`$B$ <0322.g1:B>` the same
   | number of times that :g:`$A$ <0322.g1:A>` measures :g:`$D$ <0322.g1:D>`.

.. subclause::

   | But the unit :g:`$E$ <0322.g1:E>` measured the number :g:`$B$ <0322.g1:B>` the same number
   | of times that :g:`$A$ <0322.g1:A>` measures :g:`$C$ <0322.g1:C>`;

.. assertion::

   | therefore :g:`$A$ <0322.g1:A>` measures each of the numbers :g:`$C$ <0322.g1:C>`, :g:`$D$ <0322.g1:D>` the same
   | number of times.

.. assertion::

   Therefore :g:`$C$ <0322.g1:C>` is equal to :g:`$D$ <0322.g1:D>`.

.. qed::

   Therefore etc. :just:`Q. E. D.`

.. [#f316_1] **2. The numbers so produced.** The Greek has οἱ γενόμενοι ἐξ αὐτῶν, "the (numbers) produced *from them*." By "from them" Euclid means "from the original numbers," though this is not very clear even in the Greek. I think ambiguity is best avoided by leaving out the words.

----

| This proposition proves that, *if any numbers be multiplied together, the order
| of multiplication is indifferent*, or $ab = ba$.

| It is important to get a clear understanding of what Euclid means when
| he speaks of *one number multiplying another*. VII. Def. 15 states that the
| effect of "$a$ multiplying $b$" is taking $a$ times $b$. We shall always represent
| "$a$ times $b$" by $ab$ and "$b$ times $a$" by $ba$. This being premised, the proof
| that $ab = ba$ may be represented as follows in the language of proportions.
