\text{Therefore, alternately,} \quad & 1 : b = a : ab. \quad \text{[VII. 13]} \\
\text{Again, by VII. Def. 20,} \quad & 1 : b = a : ba. \\

| Euclid does not use the language of proportions but that of fractions or
| their equivalent measures, quoting VII. 15, a particular case of VII. 13
| differently expressed, instead of VII. 13 itself.

PROPOSITION 17.
---------------

.. enunciation::

   If a number by multiplying two numbers make certain numbers, the numbers so produced will have the same ratio as the numbers multiplied.

| For let the number :g:`$A$ <0323.g1:A>` by multiplying the two numbers :g:`$B$ <0323.g1:B>`,
| :g:`$C$ <0323.g1:C>` make :g:`$D$ <0323.g1:D>`, :g:`$E$ <0323.g1:E>`;

I say that, as :g:`$B$ <0323.g1:B>` is to :g:`$C$ <0323.g1:C>`, so is :g:`$D$ <0323.g1:D>` to :g:`$E$ <0323.g1:E>`.

.. subclause::

   For, since :g:`$A$ <0323.g1:A>` by multiplying :g:`$B$ <0323.g1:B>` has made :g:`$D$ <0323.g1:D>`,

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$B$ <0323.g1:B>` measures :g:`$D$ <0323.g1:D>` according to the units in :g:`$A$ <0323.g1:A>`.

.. picture:: 0323.g1.svg
   :alt: Proposition 17 Diagram

.. subclause::

   | But the unit :g:`$F$ <0323.g1:F>` also measures the number :g:`$A$ <0323.g1:A>` according to
   | the units in it;

.. assertion::

   | therefore the unit :g:`$F$ <0323.g1:F>` measures the number :g:`$A$ <0323.g1:A>` the same number
   | of times that :g:`$B$ <0323.g1:B>` measures :g:`$D$ <0323.g1:D>`.

.. assertion:: [VII. Def. 20] <VII.def.20>

   Therefore, as the unit :g:`$F$ <0323.g1:F>` is to the number :g:`$A$ <0323.g1:A>`, so is :g:`$B$ <0323.g1:B>` to :g:`$D$ <0323.g1:D>`.

.. subclause::

   For the same reason,

.. assertion::

   as the unit :g:`$F$ <0323.g1:F>` is to the number :g:`$A$ <0323.g1:A>`, so also is :g:`$C$ <0323.g1:C>` to :g:`$E$ <0323.g1:E>`;

.. assertion::

   therefore also, as :g:`$B$ <0323.g1:B>` is to :g:`$D$ <0323.g1:D>`, so is :g:`$C$ <0323.g1:C>` to :g:`$E$ <0323.g1:E>`.

.. assertion:: [VII. 13] <VII.13>

   Therefore, alternately, as :g:`$B$ <0323.g1:B>` is to :g:`$C$ <0323.g1:C>`, so is :g:`$D$ <0323.g1:D>` to :g:`$E$ <0323.g1:E>`.

.. qed::

   Therefore etc. :just:`Q. E. D.`

.. container:: center

   $b : c = ab : ac$.

| In this case Euclid translates the language of measures into that of
| proportions, and the proof is exactly like that set out in the last note.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{By VII. Def. 20,} \quad & 1 : a = b : ab, \\
\text{and} \quad & 1 : a = c : ac. \\
\text{Therefore} \quad & b : ab = c : ac, \\
\text{and, alternately,} \quad & b : c = ab : ac. \quad \text{[VII. 13]}
\end{aligned}
$$
