(2) Since
$$ad = bc,$$
$$ac : ad = ac : bc.$$
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{But}\qquad ac : ad &= c : d, & \text{[VII. 17]} \\
\text{and}\qquad ac : bc &= a : b. & \text{[VII. 18]} \\
\text{Therefore}\qquad a : b &= c : d.
\end{aligned}$$

As indicated in the note on VII. 14 above, Heiberg regards Euclid as<br />
basing the inferences contained in the last step of part (1) of this proof and<br />
in the first step of part (2) on the propositions v. 9 and v. 7 respectively,<br />
since he has not proved those propositions separately for numbers in this<br />
Book. I prefer to suppose that he regarded the inferences as obvious and<br />
not needing proof, in view of the definition of numbers which are in pro-<br />
portion. E.g., if $ac$ is the same fraction ("part" or "parts") of $ad$ that $ac$ is<br />
of $bc$, it is obvious that $ad$ must be equal to $bc$.

Heiberg omits from his text here, and relegates to an Appendix, a<br />
proposition appearing in the manuscripts V, p, $\phi$ to the effect that, *if three<br />
numbers be proportional, the product of the extremes is equal to the square<br />
of the mean, and conversely*. It does not appear in P in the first hand, B has<br />
it in the margin only, and Campanus omits it, remarking that Euclid does<br />
not give the proposition about *three* proportionals as he does in VI. 17, since<br />
it is easily proved by the proposition just given. Moreover an-Nairīzī quotes<br />
the proposition about three proportionals as an observation on VII. 19 probably<br />
due to Heron (who is mentioned by name in the preceding paragraph).

### PROPOSITION 20.

<p class="enunciation">The least numbers of those which have the same ratio with<br />
them measure those which have the same ratio the same number<br />
of times, the greater the greater and the less the less.</p>

For let <span data-ref="0326.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, <span data-ref="0326.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> be the least numbers of those which have<br />
the same ratio with <span data-ref="0326.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0326.g1:B">$B$</span>;

I say that <span data-ref="0326.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> measures <span data-ref="0326.g1:A">$A$</span> the same number<br />
of times that <span data-ref="0326.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> measures <span data-ref="0326.g1:B">$B$</span>.

![Book VII, Proposition 20](0326.g1.svg)

<p class="assertion">Now <span data-ref="0326.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> is not parts of <span data-ref="0326.g1:A">$A$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">For, if possible, let it be so;</p>

<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0326.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> is also the same parts of <span data-ref="0326.g1:B">$B$</span><br />
that <span data-ref="0326.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> is of <span data-ref="0326.g1:A">$A$</span>. <span class="justification">[VII. 13 and Def. 20]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore, as many parts of <span data-ref="0326.g1:A">$A$</span> as there<br />
are in <span data-ref="0326.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, so many parts of <span data-ref="0326.g1:B">$B$</span> are there also<br />
in <span data-ref="0326.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>.</p>

Let <span data-ref="0326.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> be divided into the parts of <span data-ref="0326.g1:A">$A$</span>, namely <span data-ref="0326.g1:/ C G /">$CG$</span>, <span data-ref="0326.g1:/ G D /">$GD$</span>,<br />
and <span data-ref="0326.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> into the parts of <span data-ref="0326.g1:B">$B$</span>, namely <span data-ref="0326.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span>, <span data-ref="0326.g1:/ H F /">$HF$</span>;

<p class="assertion">thus the multitude of <span data-ref="0326.g1:/ C G /">$CG$</span>, <span data-ref="0326.g1:/ G D /">$GD$</span> will be equal to the multitude<br />
of <span data-ref="0326.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span>, <span data-ref="0326.g1:/ H F /">$HF$</span>.</p>
