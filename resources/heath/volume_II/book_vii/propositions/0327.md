<p class="sub-clause">Now, since the numbers <span data-ref="0326.g1:/ C G /">$CG$</span>, <span data-ref="0326.g1:/ G D /">$GD$</span> are equal to one another,<br />
and the numbers <span data-ref="0326.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span>, <span data-ref="0326.g1:/ H F /">$HF$</span> are also equal to one another,<br />
while the multitude of <span data-ref="0326.g1:/ C G /">$CG$</span>, <span data-ref="0326.g1:/ G D /">$GD$</span> is equal to the multitude of<br />
<span data-ref="0326.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span>, <span data-ref="0326.g1:/ H F /">$HF$</span>,</p>

<p class="assertion">therefore, as <span data-ref="0326.g1:/ C G /">$CG$</span> is to <span data-ref="0326.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0326.g1:/ G D /">$GD$</span> to <span data-ref="0326.g1:/ H F /">$HF$</span>.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore also, as one of the antecedents is to one of<br />
the consequents, so will all the antecedents be to all the<br />
consequents. <span class="justification">[VII. 12]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore, as <span data-ref="0326.g1:/ C G /">$CG$</span> is to <span data-ref="0326.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0326.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> to <span data-ref="0326.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore <span data-ref="0326.g1:/ C G /">$CG$</span>, <span data-ref="0326.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span> are in the same ratio with <span data-ref="0326.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, <span data-ref="0326.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>,<br />
being less than they :<br />
which is impossible, for by hypothesis <span data-ref="0326.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, <span data-ref="0326.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> are the least<br />
numbers of those which have the same ratio with them.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore <span data-ref="0326.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> is not parts of <span data-ref="0326.g1:A">$A$</span> ;<br />
therefore it is a part of it. <span class="justification">[VII. 4]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">And <span data-ref="0326.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> is the same part of <span data-ref="0326.g1:B">$B$</span> that <span data-ref="0326.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> is of <span data-ref="0326.g1:A">$A$</span> ;<br />
<span class="justification">[VII. 13 and Def. 20]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0326.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> measures <span data-ref="0326.g1:A">$A$</span> the same number of times that <span data-ref="0326.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span><br />
measures <span data-ref="0326.g1:B">$B$</span>.</p>

<p class="qed">Therefore etc.<span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

If $a$, $b$ are the least numbers among those which have the same ratio<br />
(i.e. if $a/b$ is a fraction in its lowest terms), and $c$, $d$ are any others in the same<br />
ratio, i.e. if
$$a : b = c : d,$$
then $a = \frac{1}{n} c$ and $b = \frac{1}{n} d$, where $n$ is some integer.

The proof is by *reductio ad absurdum*, thus.<br />
[Since $a < c$, $a$ is some proper fraction ("part" or "parts") of $c$, by VII. 4.]

Now $a$ cannot be equal to $\frac{m}{n} c$, where $m$ is an integer less than $n$ but<br />
greater than 1.

For, if $a = \frac{m}{n} c$, $b = \frac{m}{n} d$ also. <span class="justification">[VII. 13 and Def. 20]</span>

Take each of the $m$ parts of $a$ with each of the $m$ parts of $b$, two and two;<br />
the ratio of the members of all pairs is the same ratio $\frac{1}{m} a : \frac{1}{m} b$.

Therefore
$$\frac{1}{m} a : \frac{1}{m} b = a : b. \qquad \text{[VII. 12]}$$

But $\frac{1}{m} a$ and $\frac{1}{m} b$ are respectively less than $a$, $b$ and they are in the same<br />
ratio : which contradicts the hypothesis.
