.. subclause::

   Now, since the numbers :g:`$CG$ <0326.g1:/ C G />`, :g:`$GD$ <0326.g1:/ G D />` are equal to one another, and the numbers :g:`$EH$ <0326.g1:/ E H />`, :g:`$HF$ <0326.g1:/ H F />` are also equal to one another, while the multitude of :g:`$CG$ <0326.g1:/ C G />`, :g:`$GD$ <0326.g1:/ G D />` is equal to the multitude of :g:`$EH$ <0326.g1:/ E H />`, :g:`$HF$ <0326.g1:/ H F />`,

.. assertion::

   therefore, as :g:`$CG$ <0326.g1:/ C G />` is to :g:`$EH$ <0326.g1:/ E H />`, so is :g:`$GD$ <0326.g1:/ G D />` to :g:`$HF$ <0326.g1:/ H F />`.

.. assertion::

   Therefore also, as one of the antecedents is to one of the consequents, so will all the antecedents be to all the consequents. :just:`[VII. 12] <VII.12>`

.. assertion::

   Therefore, as :g:`$CG$ <0326.g1:/ C G />` is to :g:`$EH$ <0326.g1:/ E H />`, so is :g:`$CD$ <0326.g1:/ C D />` to :g:`$EF$ <0326.g1:/ E F />`.

.. assertion::

   Therefore :g:`$CG$ <0326.g1:/ C G />`, :g:`$EH$ <0326.g1:/ E H />` are in the same ratio with :g:`$CD$ <0326.g1:/ C D />`, :g:`$EF$ <0326.g1:/ E F />`, being less than they : which is impossible, for by hypothesis :g:`$CD$ <0326.g1:/ C D />`, :g:`$EF$ <0326.g1:/ E F />` are the least numbers of those which have the same ratio with them.

.. assertion::

   Therefore :g:`$CD$ <0326.g1:/ C D />` is not parts of :g:`$A$ <0326.g1:A>` ; therefore it is a part of it. :just:`[VII. 4] <VII.4>`

.. subclause::

   And :g:`$EF$ <0326.g1:/ E F />` is the same part of :g:`$B$ <0326.g1:B>` that :g:`$CD$ <0326.g1:/ C D />` is of :g:`$A$ <0326.g1:A>` ; :just:`[VII. 13 and Def. 20] <VII.13, VII.def.20>`

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$CD$ <0326.g1:/ C D />` measures :g:`$A$ <0326.g1:A>` the same number of times that :g:`$EF$ <0326.g1:/ E F />` measures :g:`$B$ <0326.g1:B>`.

.. qed::

   Therefore etc. :just:`Q. E. D.`

----

If $a$, $b$ are the least numbers among those which have the same ratio (i.e. if $a/b$ is a fraction in its lowest terms), and $c$, $d$ are any others in the same ratio, i.e. if

$$a : b = c : d,$$

then $a = \frac{1}{n} c$ and $b = \frac{1}{n} d$, where $n$ is some integer.

The proof is by :term:`*reductio ad absurdum* <reductio-ad-absurdum>`, thus.

[Since $a < c$, $a$ is some proper fraction ("part" or "parts") of $c$, by VII. 4.]

Now $a$ cannot be equal to $\frac{m}{n} c$, where $m$ is an integer less than $n$ but greater than 1.

For, if $a = \frac{m}{n} c$, $b = \frac{m}{n} d$ also. :just:`[VII. 13 and Def. 20] <VII.13, VII.def.20>`

Take each of the $m$ parts of $a$ with each of the $m$ parts of $b$, two and two; the ratio of the members of all pairs is the same ratio $\frac{1}{m} a : \frac{1}{m} b$.

Therefore

$$\frac{1}{m} a : \frac{1}{m} b = a : b. \qquad \text{[VII. 12]}$$

But $\frac{1}{m} a$ and $\frac{1}{m} b$ are respectively less than $a$, $b$ and they are in the same ratio : which contradicts the hypothesis.
