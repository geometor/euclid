---

In other words, if $a$, $b$ are prime to one another, the ratio $a : b$ is "in its<br />
lowest terms."

The proof is equivalent to the following.<br />
If not, suppose that $c$, $d$ are the *least* numbers for which
$$a : b = c : d.$$
[Euclid only supposes *some* numbers $c$, $d$ in the ratio of $a$ to $b$ such that<br />
$c < a$, and (consequently) $d < b$. It is however necessary to suppose that<br />
$c$, $d$ are the *least* numbers in that ratio in order to enable VII. 20 to be<br />
used in the proof.]

Then [VII. 20] $a = mc$, and $b = md$, where $m$ is some integer.<br />
Therefore
$$a = cm, \quad b = dm, \qquad \text{[VII. 16]}$$
and $m$ is a common measure of $a$, $b$, though these are prime to one another :<br />
which is impossible. <span class="justification">[VII. Def. 12]</span>

Thus the least numbers in the ratio of $a$ to $b$ cannot be less than $a$, $b$<br />
themselves.

Where I have quoted VII. 16 Heiberg regards the reference as being to<br />
VII. 15. I think the phraseology of the text combined with that of Def. 15<br />
suggests the former rather than the latter.

### PROPOSITION 22.

<p class="enunciation">The least numbers of those which have the same ratio with<br />
them are prime to one another.</p>

Let <span data-ref="0329.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0329.g1:B">$B$</span> be the least numbers of those which have the<br />
same ratio with them;

I say that <span data-ref="0329.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0329.g1:B">$B$</span> are prime to one<br />
another.

<p class="sub-clause">For, if they are not prime to one<br />
another, some number will measure<br />
them.</p>
