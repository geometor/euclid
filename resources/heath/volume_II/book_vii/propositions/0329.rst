| In other words, if $a$, $b$ are prime to one another, the ratio $a : b$ is "in its
| lowest terms."

| The proof is equivalent to the following.
| If not, suppose that $c$, $d$ are the *least* numbers for which

$$a : b = c : d.$$

| [Euclid only supposes *some* numbers $c$, $d$ in the ratio of $a$ to $b$ such that
| $c < a$, and (consequently) $d < b$. It is however necessary to suppose that
| $c$, $d$ are the *least* numbers in that ratio in order to enable VII. 20 to be
| used in the proof.]

| Then [VII. 20] $a = mc$, and $b = md$, where $m$ is some integer.
| Therefore

$$a = cm, \quad b = dm, \qquad \text{[VII. 16]}$$

| and $m$ is a common measure of $a$, $b$, though these are prime to one another :
| which is impossible. :just:`[VII. Def. 12] <VII.def.12>`

| Thus the least numbers in the ratio of $a$ to $b$ cannot be less than $a$, $b$
| themselves.

| Where I have quoted VII. 16 Heiberg regards the reference as being to
| VII. 15. I think the phraseology of the text combined with that of Def. 15
| suggests the former rather than the latter.

PROPOSITION 22.
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

.. enunciation::

   The least numbers of those which have the same ratio with them are prime to one another.

| Let :g:`$A$ <0329.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0329.g1:B>` be the least numbers of those which have the
| same ratio with them;

| I say that :g:`$A$ <0329.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0329.g1:B>` are prime to one
| another.

.. picture:: 0329.g1.svg
   :alt: Book VII, Proposition 22

.. subclause::

   | For, if they are not prime to one
   | another, some number will measure
   | them.

Let some number measure them, and let it be :g:`$C$ <0329.g1:C>`.
