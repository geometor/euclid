If $a : b$ is "in its lowest terms," $a$, $b$ are prime to one another.
Again the proof is indirect.
If $a$, $b$ are not prime to one another, they have some common measure $c$, and
$$a = mc, \quad b = nc.$$
Therefore
$$m : n = a : b. \qquad \text{[VII. 17 or 18]}$$
But $m$, $n$ are less than $a$, $b$ respectively, so that $a : b$ is not in its lowest terms: which is contrary to the hypothesis.
Therefore etc.

### PROPOSITION 23.

*If two numbers be prime to one another, the number which measures the one of them will be prime to the remaining number.*

Let <span data-ref="0330.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0330.g1:B">$B$</span> be two numbers prime to one another, and let any number <span data-ref="0330.g1:C">$C$</span> measure <span data-ref="0330.g1:A">$A$</span>;
I say that <span data-ref="0330.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0330.g1:B">$B$</span> are also prime to one another.
