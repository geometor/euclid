<p class="assertion">Thus the number <span data-ref="0329.g1:C">$C$</span> by multiplying the two numbers <span data-ref="0329.g1:D">$D$</span>,<br />

<p class="assertion">therefore, as <span data-ref="0329.g1:D">$D$</span> is to <span data-ref="0329.g1:E">$E$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0329.g1:A">$A$</span> to <span data-ref="0329.g1:B">$B$</span> ; <span class="justification">[VII. 17]</span></p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0329.g1:D">$D$</span>, <span data-ref="0329.g1:E">$E$</span> are in the same ratio with <span data-ref="0329.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0329.g1:B">$B$</span>, being less<br />

If $a : b$ is "in its lowest terms," $a$, $b$ are prime to one another.<br />
Again the proof is indirect.<br />
If $a$, $b$ are not prime to one another, they have some common measure $c$,<br />
and
$$a = mc, \quad b = nc.$$
Therefore
$$m : n = a : b. \qquad \text{[VII. 17 or 18]}$$
But $m$, $n$ are less than $a$, $b$ respectively, so that $a : b$ is not in its lowest<br />
terms : which is contrary to the hypothesis.<br />
Therefore etc.

### PROPOSITION 23.

<p class="enunciation">If two numbers be prime to one another, the number which<br />
measures the one of them will be prime to the remaining<br />
number.</p>

Let <span data-ref="0330.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0330.g1:B">$B$</span> be two numbers prime to one another, and let<br />
any number <span data-ref="0330.g1:C">$C$</span> measure <span data-ref="0330.g1:A">$A$</span>;

I say that <span data-ref="0330.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0330.g1:B">$B$</span> are also prime to one another.

<p class="sub-clause">For, if <span data-ref="0330.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0330.g1:B">$B$</span> are not prime to one another,<br />
some number will measure <span data-ref="0330.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0330.g1:B">$B$</span>.</p>

Let a number measure them, and let it be <span data-ref="0330.g1:D">$D$</span>.
