And, as many times as :g:`C <0329.g1:C>` measures :g:`A <0329.g1:A>`, so many units let there be in :g:`D <0329.g1:D>`, and, as many times as :g:`C <0329.g1:C>` measures :g:`B <0329.g1:B>`, so many units let there be in :g:`E <0329.g1:E>`.

.. subclause::

   Since :g:`C <0329.g1:C>` measures :g:`A <0329.g1:A>` according to the units in :g:`D <0329.g1:D>`,

.. assertion:: [VII. Def. 15] <VII.def.15>

   therefore :g:`C <0329.g1:C>` by multiplying :g:`D <0329.g1:D>` has made :g:`A <0329.g1:A>`.

.. subclause::

   For the same reason also

.. assertion::

   :g:`C <0329.g1:C>` by multiplying :g:`E <0329.g1:E>` has made :g:`B <0329.g1:B>`.

.. assertion::

   Thus the number :g:`C <0329.g1:C>` by multiplying the two numbers :g:`D <0329.g1:D>`, :g:`E <0329.g1:E>` has made :g:`A <0329.g1:A>`, :g:`B <0329.g1:B>` ;

.. assertion:: [VII. 17] <VII.17>

   therefore, as :g:`D <0329.g1:D>` is to :g:`E <0329.g1:E>`, so is :g:`A <0329.g1:A>` to :g:`B <0329.g1:B>` ;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`D <0329.g1:D>`, :g:`E <0329.g1:E>` are in the same ratio with :g:`A <0329.g1:A>`, :g:`B <0329.g1:B>`, being less than they : which is impossible.

.. assertion::

   Therefore no number will measure the numbers :g:`A <0329.g1:A>`, :g:`B <0329.g1:B>`.

.. assertion::

   Therefore :g:`A <0329.g1:A>`, :g:`B <0329.g1:B>` are prime to one another.

.. qed::

   :just:`Q. E. D.`

----

If $a : b$ is "in its lowest terms," $a$, $b$ are prime to one another. Again the proof is indirect. If $a$, $b$ are not prime to one another, they have some common measure $c$, and

$$a = mc, \quad b = nc.$$

Therefore

$$m : n = a : b. \qquad \text{[VII. 17 or 18]}$$

But $m$, $n$ are less than $a$, $b$ respectively, so that $a : b$ is not in its lowest terms : which is contrary to the hypothesis.

Therefore etc.

----

PROPOSITION 23.
---------------

.. enunciation::

   If two numbers be prime to one another, the number which measures the one of them will be prime to the remaining number.

Let :g:`A <0330.g1:A>`, :g:`B <0330.g1:B>` be two numbers prime to one another, and let any number :g:`C <0330.g1:C>` measure :g:`A <0330.g1:A>`;

I say that :g:`C <0330.g1:C>`, :g:`B <0330.g1:B>` are also prime to one another.

.. picture:: 0330.g1.svg
   :alt: Book VII, Proposition 23

.. subclause::

   For, if :g:`C <0330.g1:C>`, :g:`B <0330.g1:B>` are not prime to one another, some number will measure :g:`C <0330.g1:C>`, :g:`B <0330.g1:B>`.

Let a number measure them, and let it be :g:`D <0330.g1:D>`.

.. subclause::

   Since :g:`D <0330.g1:D>` measures :g:`C <0330.g1:C>`, and :g:`C <0330.g1:C>` measures :g:`A <0330.g1:A>`,

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`D <0330.g1:D>` also measures :g:`A <0330.g1:A>`.
