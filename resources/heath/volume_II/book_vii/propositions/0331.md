### PROPOSITION 24.

<p class="enunciation">If two numbers be prime to any number, their product also<br />
will be prime to the same.</p>

For let the two numbers <span data-ref="0331.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0331.g1:B">$B$</span> be prime to any number <span data-ref="0331.g1:C">$C$</span>,<br />
and let <span data-ref="0331.g1:A">$A$</span> by multiplying <span data-ref="0331.g1:B">$B$</span> make <span data-ref="0331.g1:D">$D$</span>;

I say that <span data-ref="0331.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0331.g1:D">$D$</span> are prime to one another.

![Book VII, Proposition 24](0331.g1.svg)

<p class="sub-clause">For, if <span data-ref="0331.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0331.g1:D">$D$</span> are not prime to one another,<br />
some number will measure <span data-ref="0331.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0331.g1:D">$D$</span>.</p>

Let a number measure them, and let it<br />
be <span data-ref="0331.g1:E">$E$</span>.

<p class="sub-clause">Now, since <span data-ref="0331.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0331.g1:A">$A$</span> are prime to one<br />
another,<br />
and a certain number <span data-ref="0331.g1:E">$E$</span> measures <span data-ref="0331.g1:C">$C$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0331.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0331.g1:E">$E$</span> are prime to one another. <span class="justification">[VII. 23]</span></p>

As many times, then, as <span data-ref="0331.g1:E">$E$</span> measures <span data-ref="0331.g1:D">$D$</span>, so many units let<br />
there be in <span data-ref="0331.g1:F">$F$</span>;

<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0331.g1:F">$F$</span> also measures <span data-ref="0331.g1:D">$D$</span> according to the units in <span data-ref="0331.g1:E">$E$</span>. <span class="justification">[VII. 16]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore <span data-ref="0331.g1:E">$E$</span> by multiplying <span data-ref="0331.g1:F">$F$</span> has made <span data-ref="0331.g1:D">$D$</span>. <span class="justification">[VII. Def. 15]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But, further, <span data-ref="0331.g1:A">$A$</span> by multiplying <span data-ref="0331.g1:B">$B$</span> has also made <span data-ref="0331.g1:D">$D$</span> ;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the product of <span data-ref="0331.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0331.g1:F">$F$</span> is equal to the product of <span data-ref="0331.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0331.g1:B">$B$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">But, if the product of the extremes be equal to that of the<br />
means, the four numbers are proportional ; <span class="justification">[VII. 19]</span></p>
<p class="assertion">therefore, as <span data-ref="0331.g1:E">$E$</span> is to <span data-ref="0331.g1:A">$A$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0331.g1:B">$B$</span> to <span data-ref="0331.g1:F">$F$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">But <span data-ref="0331.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0331.g1:E">$E$</span> are prime to one another,<br />
numbers which are prime to one another are also the least of<br />
those which have the same ratio, <span class="justification">[VII. 21]</span><br />
and the least numbers of those which have the same ratio<br />
