If $a$, $b$ are both prime to $c$, then $ab$, $c$ are prime to one another.

The proof is again by reductio ad absurdum.

If $ab$, $c$ are not prime to one another, let them be measured by $d$ and be equal to $md$, $nd$, say, respectively.

Now, since $a$, $c$ are prime to one another and $d$ measures $c$,
$$a,\ d\text{ are prime to one another. \qquad [VII. 23]}$$

But, since $ab = md$,
$$d : a = b : m. \qquad\text{[VII. 19]}$$

Therefore [VII. 20] $d$ measures $b$,
or $b = pd$, say.

But $c = nd$.

Therefore $d$ measures both $b$ and $c$, which are therefore not prime to one another: which is impossible.

Therefore etc.

### Proposition 25.

*If two numbers be prime to one another, the product of one of them into itself will be prime to the remaining one.*

![Book VII, Proposition 25](0332.g1.svg)

Let <span data-ref="0332.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0332.g1:B">$B$</span> be two numbers prime to one another, and let <span data-ref="0332.g1:A">$A$</span> by multiplying itself make <span data-ref="0332.g1:C">$C$</span>;
I say that <span data-ref="0332.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0332.g1:C">$C$</span> are prime to one another.

For let <span data-ref="0332.g1:D">$D$</span> be made equal to <span data-ref="0332.g1:A">$A$</span>.

Since <span data-ref="0332.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0332.g1:B">$B$</span> are prime to one another, and <span data-ref="0332.g1:A">$A$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0332.g1:D">$D$</span>, therefore <span data-ref="0332.g1:D">$D$</span>, <span data-ref="0332.g1:B">$B$</span> are also prime to one another.

Therefore each of the two numbers <span data-ref="0332.g1:D">$D$</span>, <span data-ref="0332.g1:A">$A$</span> is prime to <span data-ref="0332.g1:B">$B$</span>; therefore the product of <span data-ref="0332.g1:D">$D$</span>, <span data-ref="0332.g1:A">$A$</span> will also be prime to <span data-ref="0332.g1:B">$B$</span>. [VII. 24]
