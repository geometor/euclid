$$d : a = b : m. \qquad\text{[VII. 19]}$$

| Therefore [VII. 20] $d$ measures $b$,
| or $b = pd$, say.
| But $c = nd$.
| Therefore $d$ measures both $b$ and $c$, which are therefore not prime to one
| another : which is impossible.

PROPOSITION 25.
---------------

.. enunciation::

   If two numbers be prime to one another, the product of one of them into itself will be prime to the remaining one.

| Let :g:`$A$ <0332.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0332.g1:B>` be two numbers prime to one another,
| and let :g:`$A$ <0332.g1:A>` by multiplying itself make :g:`$C$ <0332.g1:C>`:

I say that :g:`$B$ <0332.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0332.g1:C>` are prime to one another.

.. picture:: 0332.g1.svg
   :alt: Book VII, Proposition 25

For let :g:`$D$ <0332.g1:D>` be made equal to :g:`$A$ <0332.g1:A>`.

.. subclause::

   | Since :g:`$A$ <0332.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0332.g1:B>` are prime to one another,
   | and :g:`$A$ <0332.g1:A>` is equal to :g:`$D$ <0332.g1:D>`,

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$D$ <0332.g1:D>`, :g:`$B$ <0332.g1:B>` are also prime to one another.

.. assertion::

   | Therefore each of the two numbers :g:`$D$ <0332.g1:D>`, :g:`$A$ <0332.g1:A>` is
   | prime to :g:`$B$ <0332.g1:B>` ;

.. assertion:: [VII. 24] <VII.24>

   therefore the product of :g:`$D$ <0332.g1:D>`, :g:`$A$ <0332.g1:A>` will also be prime to :g:`$B$ <0332.g1:B>`.
