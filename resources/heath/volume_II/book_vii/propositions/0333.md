[^327_1]. **the product of one of them into itself.** The Greek, ὁ ἐκ τοῦ ἑνὸς αὐτῶν γενόμενος, literally "the number produced from the one of them," leaves "multiplied into itself" to be understood.

If $a$, $b$ are prime to one another,
$$a^2\text{ is prime to }b.$$

Euclid takes $d$ equal to $a$, so that $d$, $a$ are both prime to $b$.
Hence, by VII. 24, $da$, i.e. $a^2$, is prime to $b$.

The proposition is a particular case of the preceding proposition; and the method of proof is by substitution of different numbers in the result of that proposition.

### Proposition 26.

*If two numbers be prime to two numbers, both to each, their products also will be prime to one another.*

![Book VII, Proposition 26](0333.g1.svg)

For let the two numbers <span data-ref="0333.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0333.g1:B">$B$</span> be prime to the two numbers <span data-ref="0333.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0333.g1:D">$D$</span>; both to each, and let <span data-ref="0333.g1:A">$A$</span> by multiplying <span data-ref="0333.g1:B">$B$</span> make <span data-ref="0333.g1:E">$E$</span>, and let <span data-ref="0333.g1:C">$C$</span> by multiplying <span data-ref="0333.g1:D">$D$</span> make <span data-ref="0333.g1:F">$F$</span>;
I say that <span data-ref="0333.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0333.g1:F">$F$</span> are prime to one another.

For, since each of the numbers <span data-ref="0333.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0333.g1:B">$B$</span> is prime to <span data-ref="0333.g1:C">$C$</span>, therefore the product of <span data-ref="0333.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0333.g1:B">$B$</span> will also be prime to <span data-ref="0333.g1:C">$C$</span>. [VII. 24]

But the product of <span data-ref="0333.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0333.g1:B">$B$</span> is <span data-ref="0333.g1:E">$E$</span>; therefore <span data-ref="0333.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0333.g1:C">$C$</span> are prime to one another.

For the same reason
<span data-ref="0333.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0333.g1:D">$D$</span> are also prime to one another.

Therefore each of the numbers <span data-ref="0333.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0333.g1:D">$D$</span> is prime to <span data-ref="0333.g1:E">$E$</span>.

Therefore the product of <span data-ref="0333.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0333.g1:D">$D$</span> will also be prime to <span data-ref="0333.g1:E">$E$</span>. [VII. 24]

But the product of <span data-ref="0333.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0333.g1:D">$D$</span> is <span data-ref="0333.g1:F">$F$</span>.

Therefore <span data-ref="0333.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0333.g1:F">$F$</span> are prime to one another.

$$\text{Q. E. D.}$$

If both $a$ and $b$ are prime to each of two numbers $c$, $d$, then $ab$, $cd$ will be prime to one another.

Since $a$, $b$ are both prime to $c$,
$$ab,\ c\text{ are prime to one another. \qquad [VII. 24]}$$

Similarly $ab$, $d$ are prime to one another.

Therefore $c$, $d$ are both prime to $ab$,
$$\text{and so therefore is }cd.\text{ \qquad [VII. 24]}$$
