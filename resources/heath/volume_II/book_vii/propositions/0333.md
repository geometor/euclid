[^327_1]: **the product of one of them into itself.** The Greek, ὁ ἐκ τοῦ ἑνὸς αὐτῶν γενόμενος, literally "the number produced from the one of them," leaves "multiplied into itself" to be understood.

If $a$, $b$ are prime to one another,
$$a^2\text{ is prime to }b.$$
Euclid takes $d$ equal to $a$, so that $d$, $a$ are both prime to $b$.<br />
Hence, by VII. 24, $da$, i.e. $a^2$, is prime to $b$.<br />
The proposition is a particular case of the preceding proposition ; and the<br />
method of proof is by substitution of different numbers in the result of that<br />
proposition.

### PROPOSITION 26.

<p class="enunciation">If two numbers be prime to two numbers, both to each, their<br />
products also will be prime to one another.</p>

For let the two numbers <span data-ref="0333.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0333.g1:B">$B$</span> be prime to the two<br />
numbers <span data-ref="0333.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0333.g1:D">$D$</span> ; both to each,<br />
and let <span data-ref="0333.g1:A">$A$</span> by multiplying <span data-ref="0333.g1:B">$B$</span><br />
make <span data-ref="0333.g1:E">$E$</span>, and let <span data-ref="0333.g1:C">$C$</span> by multi-<br />
plying <span data-ref="0333.g1:D">$D$</span> make <span data-ref="0333.g1:F">$F$</span>;

I say that <span data-ref="0333.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0333.g1:F">$F$</span> are prime to<br />
one another.

![Book VII, Proposition 26](0333.g1.svg)

<p class="sub-clause">For, since each of the numbers <span data-ref="0333.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0333.g1:B">$B$</span> is prime to <span data-ref="0333.g1:C">$C$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the product of <span data-ref="0333.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0333.g1:B">$B$</span> will also be prime to <span data-ref="0333.g1:C">$C$</span>. <span class="justification">[VII. 24]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But the product of <span data-ref="0333.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0333.g1:B">$B$</span> is <span data-ref="0333.g1:E">$E$</span> ;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0333.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0333.g1:C">$C$</span> are prime to one another.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">For the same reason</p>
<p class="assertion"><span data-ref="0333.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0333.g1:D">$D$</span> are also prime to one another.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore each of the numbers <span data-ref="0333.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0333.g1:D">$D$</span> is prime to <span data-ref="0333.g1:E">$E$</span>.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore the product of <span data-ref="0333.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0333.g1:D">$D$</span> will also be prime to <span data-ref="0333.g1:E">$E$</span>. <span class="justification">[VII. 24]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But the product of <span data-ref="0333.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0333.g1:D">$D$</span> is <span data-ref="0333.g1:F">$F$</span>.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore <span data-ref="0333.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0333.g1:F">$F$</span> are prime to one another.</p>

<p class="qed"><span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

---

If both $a$ and $b$ are prime to each of two numbers $c$, $d$, then $ab$, $cd$ will be<br />
prime to one another.

Since $a$, $b$ are both prime to $c$,
$$ab,\ c\text{ are prime to one another. \qquad [VII. 24]}$$

Similarly
$$ab,\ d\text{ are prime to one another.}$$

Therefore $c$, $d$ are both prime to $ab$,
$$\text{and so therefore is }cd.\text{ \qquad [VII. 24]}$$
