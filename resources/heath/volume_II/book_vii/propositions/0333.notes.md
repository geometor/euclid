## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Footnote 1**: Greek phrase ὁ ἐκ τοῦ ἑνὸς αὐτῶν γενόμενος (literally "the number produced from the one of them"), elliptical for squaring.
- **Proposition VII.26 Theorem**: If $\gcd(A, C) = 1, \gcd(A, D) = 1, \gcd(B, C) = 1, \gcd(B, D) = 1$, then $\gcd(AB, CD) = 1$.
- **Proof Structure**: Elegant two-step application of VII.24:
  1. $A, B$ both coprime to $C \implies AB (= E)$ is coprime to $C$.
  2. $A, B$ both coprime to $D \implies AB (= E)$ is coprime to $D$.
  3. Now $C, D$ are both coprime to $E \implies CD (= F)$ is coprime to $E$. Hence $\gcd(E, F) = 1$.
