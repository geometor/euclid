### PROPOSITION 27.

<p class="enunciation">If two numbers be prime to one another, and each by<br />
multiplying itself make a certain number, the products will be<br />
prime to one another ; and, if the original numbers by multi-<br />
plying the products make certain numbers, the latter will also<br />
be prime to one another [and this is always the case with the<br />
extremes].</p>

Let <span data-ref="0334.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0334.g1:B">$B$</span> be two numbers prime to one another,<br />
let <span data-ref="0334.g1:A">$A$</span> by multiplying itself make <span data-ref="0334.g1:C">$C$</span>, and by<br />
multiplying <span data-ref="0334.g1:C">$C$</span> make <span data-ref="0334.g1:D">$D$</span>,<br />
and let <span data-ref="0334.g1:B">$B$</span> by multiplying itself make <span data-ref="0334.g1:E">$E$</span>, and<br />
by multiplying <span data-ref="0334.g1:E">$E$</span> make <span data-ref="0334.g1:F">$F$</span>;

I say that both <span data-ref="0334.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0334.g1:E">$E$</span> and <span data-ref="0334.g1:D">$D$</span>, <span data-ref="0334.g1:F">$F$</span> are prime<br />
to one another.

![Book VII, Proposition 27](0334.g1.svg)

<p class="sub-clause">For, since <span data-ref="0334.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0334.g1:B">$B$</span> are prime to one another,<br />
and <span data-ref="0334.g1:A">$A$</span> by multiplying itself has made <span data-ref="0334.g1:C">$C$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0334.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0334.g1:B">$B$</span> are prime to one another. <span class="justification">[VII. 25]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">Since then <span data-ref="0334.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0334.g1:B">$B$</span> are prime to one another,<br />
and <span data-ref="0334.g1:B">$B$</span> by multiplying itself has made <span data-ref="0334.g1:E">$E$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0334.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0334.g1:E">$E$</span> are prime to one another. <span class="justification">[id.]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">Again, since <span data-ref="0334.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0334.g1:B">$B$</span> are prime to one another,<br />
and <span data-ref="0334.g1:B">$B$</span> by multiplying itself has made <span data-ref="0334.g1:E">$E$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0334.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0334.g1:E">$E$</span> are prime to one another. <span class="justification">[id.]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">Since then the two numbers <span data-ref="0334.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0334.g1:C">$C$</span> are prime to the two<br />
numbers <span data-ref="0334.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0334.g1:E">$E$</span>, both to each,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore also the product of <span data-ref="0334.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0334.g1:C">$C$</span> is prime to the product of<br />
<span data-ref="0334.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0334.g1:E">$E$</span>. <span class="justification">[VII. 26]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">And the product of <span data-ref="0334.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0334.g1:C">$C$</span> is <span data-ref="0334.g1:D">$D$</span>, and the product of <span data-ref="0334.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0334.g1:E">$E$</span><br />
is <span data-ref="0334.g1:F">$F$</span>.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore <span data-ref="0334.g1:D">$D$</span>, <span data-ref="0334.g1:F">$F$</span> are prime to one another.</p>

<p class="qed"><span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

---

If $a$, $b$ are prime to one another, so are $a^2$, $b^2$ and so are $a^3$, $b^3$ ; and,<br />
generally, $a^n$, $b^n$ are prime to one another.

The words in the enunciation which assert the truth of the proposition for<br />
*any* powers are suspected and bracketed by Heiberg because (1) in περὶ τοὺς<br />
ἄκρους the use of ἄκροι is peculiar, for it can only mean "the last products,"<br />
