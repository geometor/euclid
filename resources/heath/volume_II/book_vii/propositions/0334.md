### Proposition 27.

*If two numbers be prime to one another, and each by multiplying itself make a certain number, the products will be prime to one another; and, if the original numbers by multiplying the products make certain numbers, the latter will also be prime to one another [and this is always the case with the extremes].*

![Book VII, Proposition 27](0334.g1.svg)

Let <span data-ref="0334.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0334.g1:B">$B$</span> be two numbers prime to one another, let <span data-ref="0334.g1:A">$A$</span> by multiplying itself make <span data-ref="0334.g1:C">$C$</span>, and by multiplying <span data-ref="0334.g1:C">$C$</span> make <span data-ref="0334.g1:D">$D$</span>, and let <span data-ref="0334.g1:B">$B$</span> by multiplying itself make <span data-ref="0334.g1:E">$E$</span>, and by multiplying <span data-ref="0334.g1:E">$E$</span> make <span data-ref="0334.g1:F">$F$</span>;
I say that both <span data-ref="0334.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0334.g1:E">$E$</span> and <span data-ref="0334.g1:D">$D$</span>, <span data-ref="0334.g1:F">$F$</span> are prime to one another.

For, since <span data-ref="0334.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0334.g1:B">$B$</span> are prime to one another, and <span data-ref="0334.g1:A">$A$</span> by multiplying itself has made <span data-ref="0334.g1:C">$C$</span>, therefore <span data-ref="0334.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0334.g1:B">$B$</span> are prime to one another. [VII. 25]

Since then <span data-ref="0334.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0334.g1:B">$B$</span> are prime to one another, and <span data-ref="0334.g1:B">$B$</span> by multiplying itself has made <span data-ref="0334.g1:E">$E$</span>, therefore <span data-ref="0334.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0334.g1:E">$E$</span> are prime to one another. [id.]

Again, since <span data-ref="0334.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0334.g1:B">$B$</span> are prime to one another, and <span data-ref="0334.g1:B">$B$</span> by multiplying itself has made <span data-ref="0334.g1:E">$E$</span>, therefore <span data-ref="0334.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0334.g1:E">$E$</span> are prime to one another. [id.]

Since then the two numbers <span data-ref="0334.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0334.g1:C">$C$</span> are prime to the two numbers <span data-ref="0334.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0334.g1:E">$E$</span>, both to each, therefore also the product of <span data-ref="0334.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0334.g1:C">$C$</span> is prime to the product of <span data-ref="0334.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0334.g1:E">$E$</span>. [VII. 26]

And the product of <span data-ref="0334.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0334.g1:C">$C$</span> is <span data-ref="0334.g1:D">$D$</span>, and the product of <span data-ref="0334.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0334.g1:E">$E$</span> is <span data-ref="0334.g1:F">$F$</span>.

Therefore <span data-ref="0334.g1:D">$D$</span>, <span data-ref="0334.g1:F">$F$</span> are prime to one another.

$$\text{Q. E. D.}$$

---

If $a$, $b$ are prime to one another, so are $a^2$, $b^2$ and so are $a^3$, $b^3$; and, generally, $a^n$, $b^n$ are prime to one another.

The words in the enunciation which assert the truth of the proposition for *any* powers are suspected and bracketed by Heiberg because (1) in περὶ τοὺς ἄκρους the use of ἄκροι is peculiar, for it can only mean "the last products," and (2) the words have nothing corresponding to them in the proof, much less is the generalisation proved.
