## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Textual Criticism on Proposition 27**:
  - The phrase `[καὶ ἀεὶ περὶ τοὺς ἄκρους τοῦτο συμβαίνει]` / `[and this is always the case with the extremes]` is bracketed by Heiberg.
  - Greek terms: περὶ τοὺς ἄκρους, ἄκροι. Heath notes that ἄκροι here must mean "the last products" or highest powers, an atypical usage in Euclidean terminology.
  - The Euclidean text only explicitly demonstrates powers up to cubes ($a^2, b^2$ and $a^3, b^3$), omitting any inductive proof for arbitrary $a^n, b^n$.
- **Campanus's Version**: Campanus omits the phrase in the enunciation, though he remarks on its validity for arbitrary powers in his commentary.
