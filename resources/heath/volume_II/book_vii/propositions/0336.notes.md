## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Divisibility Axioms**: Heath and Heiberg highlight that Euclid implicitly assumes the fundamental divisibility property: if $c$ measures both $a$ and $b$, it also measures any integer combination $a \pm pb$.
- **Proposition VII.29 Significance**: For any prime $p$ and integer $b$, either $p \mid b$ or $\gcd(p, b) = 1$. This is the defining characterization of prime elements in unique factorisation domains.
- **Proof Structure of VII.29**: Direct reductio ad absurdum: if a common measure $C$ existed, $C$ would measure the prime $A$ while being distinct from $A$ (since $A$ does not measure $B$), contradicting the definition of a prime number (VII. Def. 11).
