### PROPOSITION 30.

<p class="enunciation">If two numbers by multiplying one another make some<br />
number, and any prime number measure the product, it will<br />
also measure one of the original numbers.</p>

For let the two numbers <span data-ref="0337.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0337.g1:B">$B$</span> by multiplying one another<br />
make <span data-ref="0337.g1:C">$C$</span>, and let any prime number<br />
<span data-ref="0337.g1:D">$D$</span> measure <span data-ref="0337.g1:C">$C$</span>;

I say that <span data-ref="0337.g1:D">$D$</span> measures one of the<br />
numbers <span data-ref="0337.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0337.g1:B">$B$</span>.

![Book VII, Proposition 30](0337.g1.svg)

For let it not measure <span data-ref="0337.g1:A">$A$</span>.

<p class="sub-clause">Now <span data-ref="0337.g1:D">$D$</span> is prime ;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0337.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0337.g1:D">$D$</span> are prime to one<br />
another. <span class="justification">[VII. 29]</span></p>

And, as many times as <span data-ref="0337.g1:D">$D$</span> measures <span data-ref="0337.g1:C">$C$</span>, so many units let<br />
there be in <span data-ref="0337.g1:E">$E$</span>.

<p class="sub-clause">Since then <span data-ref="0337.g1:D">$D$</span> measures <span data-ref="0337.g1:C">$C$</span> according to the units in <span data-ref="0337.g1:E">$E$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0337.g1:D">$D$</span> by multiplying <span data-ref="0337.g1:E">$E$</span> has made <span data-ref="0337.g1:C">$C$</span>. <span class="justification">[VII. Def. 15]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">Further, <span data-ref="0337.g1:A">$A$</span> by multiplying <span data-ref="0337.g1:B">$B$</span> has also made <span data-ref="0337.g1:C">$C$</span> ;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the product of <span data-ref="0337.g1:D">$D$</span>, <span data-ref="0337.g1:E">$E$</span> is equal to the product of<br />
<span data-ref="0337.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0337.g1:B">$B$</span>.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore, as <span data-ref="0337.g1:D">$D$</span> is to <span data-ref="0337.g1:A">$A$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0337.g1:B">$B$</span> to <span data-ref="0337.g1:E">$E$</span>. <span class="justification">[VII. 19]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But <span data-ref="0337.g1:D">$D$</span>, <span data-ref="0337.g1:A">$A$</span> are prime to one another,<br />
primes are also least, <span class="justification">[VII. 21]</span><br />
and the least measure the numbers which have the same<br />
ratio the same number of times, the greater the greater and<br />
the less the less, that is, the antecedent the antecedent and<br />
the consequent the consequent ; <span class="justification">[VII. 20]</span></p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0337.g1:D">$D$</span> measures <span data-ref="0337.g1:B">$B$</span>.</p>

<p class="assertion">Similarly we can also show that, if <span data-ref="0337.g1:D">$D$</span> do not measure <span data-ref="0337.g1:B">$B$</span>,<br />
it will measure <span data-ref="0337.g1:A">$A$</span>.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore <span data-ref="0337.g1:D">$D$</span> measures one of the numbers <span data-ref="0337.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0337.g1:B">$B$</span>.</p>

<p class="qed"><span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>
