## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Euclid's Lemma (VII.30)**: This is the pivotal foundational theorem of Euclidean arithmetic: if a prime number divides a product, it must divide at least one of the factors.
- **Proof Chain**:
  1. Suppose $D \nmid A$. Since $D$ is prime, $\gcd(D, A) = 1$ by VII.29.
  2. Let $D \cdot E = C = A \cdot B$.
  3. By VII.19, $D : A = B : E$.
  4. By VII.21, since $\gcd(D, A) = 1$, the ratio $D : A$ is in lowest terms.
  5. By VII.20, the least numbers measure those in the same ratio: hence $D$ measures $B$.
  6. Symmetrically, if $D \nmid B$, then $D$ measures $A$.
- **Foundational Impact**: Proposition VII.30 is the critical bridge to the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic (unique prime factorisation), culminating in Book IX, Proposition 14.
