## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Mathematical Progression**:
  - Construction: $E, F, G$ are defined such that $A = D \cdot E$, $B = D \cdot F$, $C = D \cdot G$. Thus $E, F, G$ are in the same ratio as $A, B, C$ (VII. Def. 20).
  - Proof of minimality by contradiction: Assume numbers $H, K, L$ less than $E, F, G$ in the same ratio.
  - Let $M$ be the common quotient ($A = H \cdot M$). By commutativity of multiplication (VII. 16), $M$ measures $A, B, C$.
  - Since $E \cdot D = A = H \cdot M$, we have $E : H = M : D$ (VII. 19).
  - Since $E > H$, it follows that $M > D$. But $M$ is a common measure of $A, B, C$, which contradicts $D$ being the *greatest* common measure.
