## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Textual Note on Line 17**: Heath notes the standard Greek distributive phrasing: "each of the numbers $E, F, G$ measures each of the numbers $A, B, C$."
- **Heath's Commentary**:
  - Expresses Euclid's algorithm algebraically: given $a, b, c$, let $g = \gcd(a, b, c)$; then $m = a/g$, $n = b/g$, $p = c/g$ give the minimal integers in the given ratio.
  - Critical analysis of Euclid's proof: Heath observes an unstated assumption in Euclid's text. Euclid assumes $x, y, z$ are simply *smaller* numbers in the same ratio, but to invoke VII. 20 ($a = kx, b = ky, c = kz$), $x, y, z$ must actually be the *least* numbers in that ratio.
  - Disagreement with Heiberg on foundational independence: Heiberg claimed that the inference $m > x \implies k > g$ from $m : x = k : g$ relies on V. 14 (importing Book V general magnitude theory into Book VII). Heath argues persuasively that Book VII arithmetic is completely self-contained via VII. Def. 20 (part/parts definition of numerical proportion: if $x/m = g/k$, then $x < m \implies g < k$, so $k > g$).
