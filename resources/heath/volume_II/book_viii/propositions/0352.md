Therefore <span data-ref="0351.g1:A">$A$</span> measures <span data-ref="0351.g1:E">$E$</span>, the greater the less : which is impossible.

Therefore <span data-ref="0351.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0351.g1:F">$F$</span>, <span data-ref="0351.g1:G">$G$</span>, <span data-ref="0351.g1:H">$H$</span> which are less than <span data-ref="0351.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0351.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0351.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0351.g1:D">$D$</span> are not in the same ratio with them.

Therefore <span data-ref="0351.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0351.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0351.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0351.g1:D">$D$</span> are the least of those which have the same ratio with them.

<div style="text-align: right;">Q. E. D.</div>

---

What we call a geometrical progression is with Euclid a series of terms "in continued proportion" (ἑξῆς ἀνάλογον).

This proposition proves that, if $a, b, c, \dots k$ are a series of numbers in geometrical progression, and if $a, k$ are prime to one another, the series is in the lowest terms possible with the same common ratio.

The proof is in *form* by *reductio ad absurdum*. We should no doubt desert this *form* while retaining the substance. If $a', b', c', \dots k'$ be any other series of numbers in G.P. with the same common ratio as before, we have, *ex aequali*,
$$a : k = a' : k',$$
<div style="text-align: right;">[VII. 14]</div>
whence, since $a, k$ are prime to one another, $a, k$ measure $a', k'$ respectively, so that $a', k'$ are *greater* than $a, k$ respectively.

## PROPOSITION 2.

*To find numbers in continued proportion, as many as may be prescribed, and the least that are in a given ratio.*

Let the ratio of <span data-ref="0352.g1:A">$A$</span> to <span data-ref="0352.g1:B">$B$</span> be the given ratio in least numbers;  
thus it is required to find numbers in continued proportion, as many as may be prescribed, and the least that are in the ratio of <span data-ref="0352.g1:A">$A$</span> to <span data-ref="0352.g1:B">$B$</span>.

Let four be prescribed;  
let <span data-ref="0352.g1:A">$A$</span> by multiplying itself make <span data-ref="0352.g1:C">$C$</span>, and by multiplying <span data-ref="0352.g1:B">$B$</span> let it make <span data-ref="0352.g1:D">$D$</span>;  
let <span data-ref="0352.g1:B">$B$</span> by multiplying itself make <span data-ref="0352.g1:E">$E$</span>;

and let <span data-ref="0352.g1:B">$B$</span> by multiplying <span data-ref="0352.g1:E">$E$</span> make <span data-ref="0352.g1:K">$K$</span>.
