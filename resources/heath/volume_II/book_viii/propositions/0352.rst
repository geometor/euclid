.. assertion::

   Therefore :g:`$A$ <0351.g1:A>` measures :g:`$E$ <0351.g1:E>`, the greater the less : which is impossible.

.. assertion::

   Therefore :g:`$E$ <0351.g1:E>`, :g:`$F$ <0351.g1:F>`, :g:`$G$ <0351.g1:G>`, :g:`$H$ <0351.g1:H>` which are less than :g:`$A$ <0351.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0351.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0351.g1:C>`, :g:`$D$ <0351.g1:D>` are not in the same ratio with them.

.. assertion::

   Therefore :g:`$A$ <0351.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0351.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0351.g1:C>`, :g:`$D$ <0351.g1:D>` are the least of those which have the same ratio with them.

.. qed::

   :just:`Q. E. D.`

----

What we call a *geometrical progression* is with Euclid a series of terms "in continued proportion" (ἑξῆς :term:`ἀνάλογον <proportional>`).

This proposition proves that, if $a, b, c, \dots k$ are a series of numbers in geometrical progression, and if $a, k$ are prime to one another, the series is in the lowest terms possible with the same common ratio.

The proof is in *form* by :term:`*reductio ad absurdum* <reductio-ad-absurdum>`. We should no doubt desert this *form* while retaining the substance. If $a', b', c', \dots k'$ be any other series of numbers in G.P. with the same common ratio as before, we have, :term:`*ex aequali* <ex-aequali>`,

$$a : k = a' : k', \qquad \text{[VII. 14]}$$

whence, since $a, k$ are prime to one another, $a, k$ measure $a', k'$ respectively, so that $a', k'$ are *greater* than $a, k$ respectively.

PROPOSITION 2.
--------------

.. enunciation::

   To find numbers in continued proportion, as many as may be prescribed, and the least that are in a given ratio.

Let the ratio of :g:`$A$ <0352.g1:A>` to :g:`$B$ <0352.g1:B>` be the given ratio in least numbers ;

thus it is required to find numbers in continued proportion, as many as may be prescribed, and the least that are in the ratio of :g:`$A$ <0352.g1:A>` to :g:`$B$ <0352.g1:B>`.

.. picture:: 0352.g1.svg
   :alt: Proposition 2 Diagram

Let four be prescribed ; let :g:`$A$ <0352.g1:A>` by multiplying itself make :g:`$C$ <0352.g1:C>`, and by multiplying :g:`$B$ <0352.g1:B>` let it make :g:`$D$ <0352.g1:D>` ; let :g:`$B$ <0352.g1:B>` by multiplying itself make :g:`$E$ <0352.g1:E>` ; further, let :g:`$A$ <0352.g1:A>` by multiplying :g:`$C$ <0352.g1:C>`, :g:`$D$ <0352.g1:D>`, :g:`$E$ <0352.g1:E>` make :g:`$F$ <0352.g1:F>`, :g:`$G$ <0352.g1:G>`, :g:`$H$ <0352.g1:H>`, and let :g:`$B$ <0352.g1:B>` by multiplying :g:`$E$ <0352.g1:E>` make :g:`$K$ <0352.g1:K>`.
