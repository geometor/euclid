---

I say that the extremes of them *A*, *D* are prime to one another.

For let two numbers *E*, *F*, the least that are in the ratio of *A*, *B*, *C*, *D*, be taken,
<div style="text-align: right;">[VII. 33]</div>
then three others *G*, *H*, *K* with the same property;  
and others, more by one continually,
<div style="text-align: right;">[VIII. 2]</div>
until the multitude taken becomes equal to the multitude of the numbers *A*, *B*, *C*, *D*.

Let them be taken, and let them be *L*, *M*, *N*, *O*.

Now, since *E*, *F* are the least of those which have the same ratio with them, they are prime to one another.
<div style="text-align: right;">[VII. 22]</div>

And, since the numbers *E*, *F* by multiplying themselves respectively have made the numbers *G*, *K*, and by multiplying the numbers *G*, *K* respectively have made the numbers *L*, *O*,
<div style="text-align: right;">[VIII. 2, Por.]</div>
therefore both *G*, *K* and *L*, *O* are prime to one another.
<div style="text-align: right;">[VII. 27]</div>

And, since *A*, *B*, *C*, *D* are the least of those which have the same ratio with them, while *L*, *M*, *N*, *O* are the least that are in the same ratio with *A*, *B*, *C*, *D*, and the multitude of the numbers *A*, *B*, *C*, *D* is equal to the multitude of the numbers *L*, *M*, *N*, *O*,  
therefore the numbers *A*, *B*, *C*, *D* are equal to the numbers *L*, *M*, *N*, *O* respectively;  
therefore *A* is equal to *L*, and *D* to *O*.

And *L*, *O* are prime to one another.

Therefore *A*, *D* are also prime to one another.

<div style="text-align: right;">Q. E. D.</div>

---

The proof consists in merely equating the given numbers to the terms of a series found in the manner of VIII. 2.

If $a, b, c, \dots k$ ($n$ terms) be a geometrical progression in the lowest terms having a given common ratio, the terms must respectively be of the form
$$a^{n-1},\, a^{n-2}\beta,\, \dots a^2\beta^{n-3},\, a\beta^{n-2},\, \beta^{n-1}$$
found by VIII. 2, where $a : \beta$ is the ratio $a : b$ expressed in its lowest terms, so that $a, \beta$ are prime to one another [VII. 22], and hence $a^{n-1}, \beta^{n-1}$ are prime to one another [VII. 27].

But the two series must be the same, so that
$$a = a^{n-1},\quad b = \beta^{n-1}$$
