I say that the extremes of them <span data-ref="0354.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:D">$D$</span> are prime to one another.

For let two numbers <span data-ref="0354.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:F">$F$</span>, the least that are in the ratio of <span data-ref="0354.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:D">$D$</span>, be taken,
<div style="text-align: right;">[VII. 33]</div>
then three others <span data-ref="0354.g1:G">$G$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:H">$H$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:K">$K$</span> with the same property;  
and others, more by one continually,
<div style="text-align: right;">[VIII. 2]</div>
until the multitude taken becomes equal to the multitude of the numbers <span data-ref="0354.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:D">$D$</span>.

Let them be taken, and let them be <span data-ref="0354.g1:L">$L$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:M">$M$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:N">$N$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:O">$O$</span>.

Now, since <span data-ref="0354.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:F">$F$</span> are the least of those which have the same ratio with them, they are prime to one another.
<div style="text-align: right;">[VII. 22]</div>

And, since the numbers <span data-ref="0354.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:F">$F$</span> by multiplying themselves respectively have made the numbers <span data-ref="0354.g1:G">$G$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:K">$K$</span>, and by multiplying the numbers <span data-ref="0354.g1:G">$G$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:K">$K$</span> respectively have made the numbers <span data-ref="0354.g1:L">$L$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:O">$O$</span>,
<div style="text-align: right;">[VIII. 2, Por.]</div>
therefore both <span data-ref="0354.g1:G">$G$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:K">$K$</span> and <span data-ref="0354.g1:L">$L$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:O">$O$</span> are prime to one another.
<div style="text-align: right;">[VII. 27]</div>

And, since <span data-ref="0354.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:D">$D$</span> are the least of those which have the same ratio with them, while <span data-ref="0354.g1:L">$L$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:M">$M$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:N">$N$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:O">$O$</span> are the least that are in the same ratio with <span data-ref="0354.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:D">$D$</span>, and the multitude of the numbers <span data-ref="0354.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:D">$D$</span> is equal to the multitude of the numbers <span data-ref="0354.g1:L">$L$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:M">$M$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:N">$N$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:O">$O$</span>,  
therefore the numbers <span data-ref="0354.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:D">$D$</span> are equal to the numbers <span data-ref="0354.g1:L">$L$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:M">$M$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:N">$N$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:O">$O$</span> respectively;  
therefore <span data-ref="0354.g1:A">$A$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0354.g1:L">$L$</span>, and <span data-ref="0354.g1:D">$D$</span> to <span data-ref="0354.g1:O">$O$</span>.

And <span data-ref="0354.g1:L">$L$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:O">$O$</span> are prime to one another.

Therefore <span data-ref="0354.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:D">$D$</span> are also prime to one another.

<div style="text-align: right;">Q. E. D.</div>

---

The proof consists in merely equating the given numbers to the terms of a series found in the manner of VIII. 2.

If $a, b, c, \dots k$ ($n$ terms) be a geometrical progression in the lowest terms having a given common ratio, the terms must respectively be of the form
$$a^{n-1},\, a^{n-2}\beta,\, \dots a^2\beta^{n-3},\, a\beta^{n-2},\, \beta^{n-1}$$
found by VIII. 2, where $a : \beta$ is the ratio $a : b$ expressed in its lowest terms, so that $a, \beta$ are prime to one another [VII. 22], and hence $a^{n-1}, \beta^{n-1}$ are prime to one another [VII. 27].

But the two series must be the same, so that
$$a = a^{n-1},\quad b = \beta^{n-1}$$
