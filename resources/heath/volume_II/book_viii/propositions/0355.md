I say that the extremes of them <span data-ref="0354.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:D">$D$</span> are prime to one<br />
another.

For let two numbers <span data-ref="0354.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:F">$F$</span>, the least that are in the ratio<br />
of <span data-ref="0354.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:D">$D$</span>, be taken, <span class="justification">[VII. 33]</span><br />
then three others <span data-ref="0354.g1:G">$G$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:H">$H$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:K">$K$</span> with the same property;<br />
and others, more by one continually, <span class="justification">[VIII. 2]</span><br />
until the multitude taken becomes equal to the multitude of<br />
the numbers <span data-ref="0354.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:D">$D$</span>.

Let them be taken, and let them be <span data-ref="0354.g1:L">$L$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:M">$M$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:N">$N$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:O">$O$</span>.

<p class="assertion">Now, since <span data-ref="0354.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:F">$F$</span> are the least of those which have the<br />
same ratio with them, they are prime to one another. <span class="justification">[VII. 22]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">And, since the numbers <span data-ref="0354.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:F">$F$</span> by multiplying themselves<br />
respectively have made the numbers <span data-ref="0354.g1:G">$G$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:K">$K$</span>, and by multiplying<br />
the numbers <span data-ref="0354.g1:G">$G$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:K">$K$</span> respectively have made the numbers <span data-ref="0354.g1:L">$L$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:O">$O$</span>,<br />
<span class="justification">[VIII. 2, Por.]</span></p>
<p class="assertion">therefore both <span data-ref="0354.g1:G">$G$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:K">$K$</span> and <span data-ref="0354.g1:L">$L$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:O">$O$</span> are prime to one another. <span class="justification">[VII. 27]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">And, since <span data-ref="0354.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:D">$D$</span> are the least of those which have<br />
the same ratio with them,<br />
while <span data-ref="0354.g1:L">$L$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:M">$M$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:N">$N$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:O">$O$</span> are the least that are in the same ratio with<br />
<span data-ref="0354.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:D">$D$</span>,<br />
and the multitude of the numbers <span data-ref="0354.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:D">$D$</span> is equal to the<br />
multitude of the numbers <span data-ref="0354.g1:L">$L$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:M">$M$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:N">$N$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:O">$O$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the numbers <span data-ref="0354.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:D">$D$</span> are equal to the numbers<br />
<span data-ref="0354.g1:L">$L$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:M">$M$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:N">$N$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:O">$O$</span> respectively ;<br />
therefore <span data-ref="0354.g1:A">$A$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0354.g1:L">$L$</span>, and <span data-ref="0354.g1:D">$D$</span> to <span data-ref="0354.g1:O">$O$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">And <span data-ref="0354.g1:L">$L$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:O">$O$</span> are prime to one another.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore <span data-ref="0354.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0354.g1:D">$D$</span> are also prime to one another.</p>

<p class="qed"><span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

---

The proof consists in merely equating the given numbers to the terms of<br />
a series found in the manner of VIII. 2.

If $a, b, c, \dots k$ ($n$ terms) be a geometrical progression in the lowest terms<br />
having a given common ratio, the terms must respectively be of the form
$$a^{n-1},\, a^{n-2}\beta,\, \dots a^2\beta^{n-3},\, a\beta^{n-2},\, \beta^{n-1}$$
found by VIII. 2, where $a : \beta$ is the ratio $a : b$ expressed in its lowest terms, so<br />
that $a, \beta$ are prime to one another [VII. 22], and hence $a^{n-1}, \beta^{n-1}$ are prime<br />
to one another [VII. 27].

But the two series must be the same, so that
$$a = a^{n-1},\quad b = \beta^{n-1}$$
