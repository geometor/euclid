:title: Proposition 3

I say that the extremes of them :g:`$A$ <0354.g1:A>`, :g:`$D$ <0354.g1:D>` are prime to one another.

For let two numbers :g:`$E$ <0354.g1:E>`, :g:`$F$ <0354.g1:F>`, the least that are in the ratio of :g:`$A$ <0354.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0354.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0354.g1:C>`, :g:`$D$ <0354.g1:D>`, be taken, :just:`[VII. 33] <VII.33>`

then three others :g:`$G$ <0354.g1:G>`, :g:`$H$ <0354.g1:H>`, :g:`$K$ <0354.g1:K>` with the same property; and others, more by one continually, :just:`[VIII. 2] <VIII.2>`

until the multitude taken becomes equal to the multitude of the numbers :g:`$A$ <0354.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0354.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0354.g1:C>`, :g:`$D$ <0354.g1:D>`.

Let them be taken, and let them be :g:`$L$ <0354.g1:L>`, :g:`$M$ <0354.g1:M>`, :g:`$N$ <0354.g1:N>`, :g:`$O$ <0354.g1:O>`.

.. assertion::

   Now, since :g:`$E$ <0354.g1:E>`, :g:`$F$ <0354.g1:F>` are the least of those which have the same ratio with them, they are prime to one another. :just:`[VII. 22] <VII.22>`

.. subclause::

   And, since the numbers :g:`$E$ <0354.g1:E>`, :g:`$F$ <0354.g1:F>` by multiplying themselves respectively have made the numbers :g:`$G$ <0354.g1:G>`, :g:`$K$ <0354.g1:K>`, and by multiplying the numbers :g:`$G$ <0354.g1:G>`, :g:`$K$ <0354.g1:K>` respectively have made the numbers :g:`$L$ <0354.g1:L>`, :g:`$O$ <0354.g1:O>`, :just:`[VIII. 2, Por.]`

.. assertion:: [VII. 27] <VII.27>

   therefore both :g:`$G$ <0354.g1:G>`, :g:`$K$ <0354.g1:K>` and :g:`$L$ <0354.g1:L>`, :g:`$O$ <0354.g1:O>` are prime to one another.

.. subclause::

   And, since :g:`$A$ <0354.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0354.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0354.g1:C>`, :g:`$D$ <0354.g1:D>` are the least of those which have the same ratio with them, while :g:`$L$ <0354.g1:L>`, :g:`$M$ <0354.g1:M>`, :g:`$N$ <0354.g1:N>`, :g:`$O$ <0354.g1:O>` are the least that are in the same ratio with :g:`$A$ <0354.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0354.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0354.g1:C>`, :g:`$D$ <0354.g1:D>`, and the multitude of the numbers :g:`$A$ <0354.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0354.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0354.g1:C>`, :g:`$D$ <0354.g1:D>` is equal to the multitude of the numbers :g:`$L$ <0354.g1:L>`, :g:`$M$ <0354.g1:M>`, :g:`$N$ <0354.g1:N>`, :g:`$O$ <0354.g1:O>`,

.. assertion::

   therefore the numbers :g:`$A$ <0354.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0354.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0354.g1:C>`, :g:`$D$ <0354.g1:D>` are :eq:`equal <0354.g1:A = 0354.g1:L; 0354.g1:B = 0354.g1:M; 0354.g1:C = 0354.g1:N; 0354.g1:D = 0354.g1:O>` to the numbers :g:`$L$ <0354.g1:L>`, :g:`$M$ <0354.g1:M>`, :g:`$N$ <0354.g1:N>`, :g:`$O$ <0354.g1:O>` respectively ; therefore :g:`$A$ <0354.g1:A>` is :eq:`equal <0354.g1:A = 0354.g1:L; 0354.g1:D = 0354.g1:O>` to :g:`$L$ <0354.g1:L>`, and :g:`$D$ <0354.g1:D>` to :g:`$O$ <0354.g1:O>`.

.. subclause::

   And :g:`$L$ <0354.g1:L>`, :g:`$O$ <0354.g1:O>` are prime to one another.

.. assertion::

   Therefore :g:`$A$ <0354.g1:A>`, :g:`$D$ <0354.g1:D>` are also prime to one another.

.. qed::

   :just:`Q. E. D.`

----

The proof consists in merely equating the given numbers to the terms of a series found in the manner of VIII. 2.

If $a, b, c, \dots k$ ($n$ terms) be a geometrical progression in the lowest terms having a given common ratio, the terms must respectively be of the form

$$a^{n-1},\, a^{n-2}\beta,\, \dots a^2\beta^{n-3},\, a\beta^{n-2},\, \beta^{n-1}$$

found by VIII. 2, where $a : \beta$ is the ratio $a : b$ expressed in its lowest terms, so that $a, \beta$ are prime to one another [VII. 22], and hence $a^{n-1}, \beta^{n-1}$ are prime to one another [VII. 27].

But the two series must be the same, so that

$$a = a^{n-1},\quad b = \beta^{n-1}$$
