# BOOK VIII.

## PROPOSITION 4.

*Given as many ratios as we please in least numbers, to find numbers in continued proportion which are the least in the given ratios.*

Let the given ratios in least numbers be that of *A* to *B*,  
<sup>5</sup> that of *C* to *D*, and that of *E* to *F*;  
thus it is required to find numbers in continued proportion which are the least that are in the ratio of *A* to *B*, in the ratio of *C* to *D*, and in the ratio of *E* to *F*.

![Proposition 4 Diagram 1](0356.g1.svg)

Let *G*, the least number measured by *B*, *C*, be taken.
<div style="text-align: right;">[VII. 34]</div>
<sup>10</sup> And, as many times as *B* measures *G*, so many times also let *A* measure *H*,  
and, as many times as *C* measures *G*, so many times also let *D* measure *K*.  
Now *E* either measures or does not measure *K*.  
<sup>15</sup> First, let it measure it.  
And, as many times as *E* measures *K*, so many times let *F* measure *L* also.  
Now, since *A* measures *H* the same number of times that *B* measures *G*,  
<sup>20</sup> therefore, as *A* is to *B*, so is *H* to *G*.
<div style="text-align: right;">[VII. Def. 20, VII. 13]</div>
For the same reason also,
$$\text{as } C \text{ is to } D\text{, so is } G \text{ to } K\text{,}$$
and further, as *E* is to *F*, so is *K* to *L*;  
therefore *H*, *G*, *K*, *L* are continuously proportional in the  
<sup>25</sup> ratio of *A* to *B*, in the ratio of *C* to *D*, and in the ratio of *E* to *F*.  
I say next that they are also the least that have this property.
