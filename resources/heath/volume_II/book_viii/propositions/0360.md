### PROPOSITION 5.

*Plane numbers have to one another the ratio compounded of the ratios of their sides.*

Let <span data-ref="0360.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0360.g1:B">$B$</span> be plane numbers, and let the numbers <span data-ref="0360.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0360.g1:D">$D$</span> be the sides of <span data-ref="0360.g1:A">$A$</span>, and <span data-ref="0360.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0360.g1:F">$F$</span> of <span data-ref="0360.g1:B">$B$</span>;
<sup>5</sup> I say that <span data-ref="0360.g1:A">$A$</span> has to <span data-ref="0360.g1:B">$B$</span> the ratio compounded of the ratios of the sides.

![Book VIII Proposition 5](0360.g1.svg)

For, the ratios being given which <span data-ref="0360.g1:C">$C$</span> has to <span data-ref="0360.g1:E">$E$</span> and <span data-ref="0360.g1:D">$D$</span> to <span data-ref="0360.g1:F">$F$</span>, let the least numbers <span data-ref="0360.g1:G">$G$</span>, <span data-ref="0360.g1:H">$H$</span>, <span data-ref="0360.g1:K">$K$</span> that are continuously
<sup>10</sup> in the ratios <span data-ref="0360.g1:C">$C$</span> : <span data-ref="0360.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0360.g1:D">$D$</span> : <span data-ref="0360.g1:F">$F$</span> be taken, so that,
<div style="text-align: center;">as <span data-ref="0360.g1:C">$C$</span> is to <span data-ref="0360.g1:E">$E$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0360.g1:G">$G$</span> to <span data-ref="0360.g1:H">$H$</span>,</div>
and,
<div style="text-align: center;">as <span data-ref="0360.g1:D">$D$</span> is to <span data-ref="0360.g1:F">$F$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0360.g1:H">$H$</span> to <span data-ref="0360.g1:K">$K$</span>.</div>
<div style="text-align: right;">[VIII. 4]</div>

And let <span data-ref="0360.g1:D">$D$</span> by multiplying <span data-ref="0360.g1:E">$E$</span> make <span data-ref="0360.g1:L">$L$</span>.

<sup>15</sup> Now, since <span data-ref="0360.g1:D">$D$</span> by multiplying <span data-ref="0360.g1:C">$C$</span> has made <span data-ref="0360.g1:A">$A$</span>, and by multiplying <span data-ref="0360.g1:E">$E$</span> has made <span data-ref="0360.g1:L">$L$</span>,
therefore, as <span data-ref="0360.g1:C">$C$</span> is to <span data-ref="0360.g1:E">$E$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0360.g1:A">$A$</span> to <span data-ref="0360.g1:L">$L$</span>. [VII. 17]

But, as <span data-ref="0360.g1:C">$C$</span> is to <span data-ref="0360.g1:E">$E$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0360.g1:G">$G$</span> to <span data-ref="0360.g1:H">$H$</span>;
therefore also, as <span data-ref="0360.g1:G">$G$</span> is to <span data-ref="0360.g1:H">$H$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0360.g1:A">$A$</span> to <span data-ref="0360.g1:L">$L$</span>.

<sup>20</sup> Again, since <span data-ref="0360.g1:E">$E$</span> by multiplying <span data-ref="0360.g1:D">$D$</span> has made <span data-ref="0360.g1:L">$L$</span>, and further by multiplying <span data-ref="0360.g1:F">$F$</span> has made <span data-ref="0360.g1:B">$B$</span>,
therefore, as <span data-ref="0360.g1:D">$D$</span> is to <span data-ref="0360.g1:F">$F$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0360.g1:L">$L$</span> to <span data-ref="0360.g1:B">$B$</span>. [VII. 17]

But, as <span data-ref="0360.g1:D">$D$</span> is to <span data-ref="0360.g1:F">$F$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0360.g1:H">$H$</span> to <span data-ref="0360.g1:K">$K$</span>;
therefore also, as <span data-ref="0360.g1:H">$H$</span> is to <span data-ref="0360.g1:K">$K$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0360.g1:L">$L$</span> to <span data-ref="0360.g1:B">$B$</span>.

<sup>25</sup> But it was also proved that,
<div style="text-align: center;">as <span data-ref="0360.g1:G">$G$</span> is to <span data-ref="0360.g1:H">$H$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0360.g1:A">$A$</span> to <span data-ref="0360.g1:L">$L$</span>;</div>
therefore, *ex aequali*,
<div style="text-align: center;">as <span data-ref="0360.g1:G">$G$</span> is to <span data-ref="0360.g1:K">$K$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0360.g1:A">$A$</span> to <span data-ref="0360.g1:B">$B$</span>.</div>
<div style="text-align: right;">[VII. 14]</div>

But <span data-ref="0360.g1:G">$G$</span> has to <span data-ref="0360.g1:K">$K$</span> the ratio compounded of the ratios of the
<sup>30</sup> sides;
therefore <span data-ref="0360.g1:A">$A$</span> also has to <span data-ref="0360.g1:B">$B$</span> the ratio compounded of the ratios of the sides.

Q. E. D.

---

**1, 5, 29, 31. compounded of the ratios of their sides.** As in VI. 23, the Greek has the less exact phrase, "compounded of their sides."

If
$$a = cd,\quad b = ef,$$
then $a$ has to $b$ the ratio compounded of $c : e$ and $d : f$.

Take three numbers the least which are continuously in the given ratios.
