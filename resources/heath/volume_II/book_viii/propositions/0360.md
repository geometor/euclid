### PROPOSITION 5.

<p class="enunciation">Plane numbers have to one another the ratio compounded<br />
of the ratios of their sides.</p>

Let <span data-ref="0360.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0360.g1:B">$B$</span> be plane numbers, and let the numbers <span data-ref="0360.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0360.g1:D">$D$</span><br />
be the sides of <span data-ref="0360.g1:A">$A$</span>, and <span data-ref="0360.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0360.g1:F">$F$</span> of <span data-ref="0360.g1:B">$B$</span>;

<sup>5</sup> I say that <span data-ref="0360.g1:A">$A$</span> has to <span data-ref="0360.g1:B">$B$</span> the ratio com-<br />
pounded of the ratios of the sides.

![Book VIII Proposition 5](0360.g1.svg)

For, the ratios being given which <span data-ref="0360.g1:C">$C$</span><br />
has to <span data-ref="0360.g1:E">$E$</span> and <span data-ref="0360.g1:D">$D$</span> to <span data-ref="0360.g1:F">$F$</span>, let the least<br />
numbers <span data-ref="0360.g1:G">$G$</span>, <span data-ref="0360.g1:H">$H$</span>, <span data-ref="0360.g1:K">$K$</span> that are continuously<br />
<sup>10</sup> in the ratios <span data-ref="0360.g1:C">$C$</span> : <span data-ref="0360.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0360.g1:D">$D$</span> : <span data-ref="0360.g1:F">$F$</span> be taken, so<br />
that,<br />
as <span data-ref="0360.g1:C">$C$</span> is to <span data-ref="0360.g1:E">$E$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0360.g1:G">$G$</span> to <span data-ref="0360.g1:H">$H$</span>,<br />
and, as <span data-ref="0360.g1:D">$D$</span> is to <span data-ref="0360.g1:F">$F$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0360.g1:H">$H$</span> to <span data-ref="0360.g1:K">$K$</span>. <span class="justification">[VIII. 4]</span>

And let <span data-ref="0360.g1:D">$D$</span> by multiplying <span data-ref="0360.g1:E">$E$</span> make <span data-ref="0360.g1:L">$L$</span>.

<p class="sub-clause"><sup>15</sup> Now, since <span data-ref="0360.g1:D">$D$</span> by multiplying <span data-ref="0360.g1:C">$C$</span> has made <span data-ref="0360.g1:A">$A$</span>, and by<br />
multiplying <span data-ref="0360.g1:E">$E$</span> has made <span data-ref="0360.g1:L">$L$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore, as <span data-ref="0360.g1:C">$C$</span> is to <span data-ref="0360.g1:E">$E$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0360.g1:A">$A$</span> to <span data-ref="0360.g1:L">$L$</span>. <span class="justification">[VII. 17]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But, as <span data-ref="0360.g1:C">$C$</span> is to <span data-ref="0360.g1:E">$E$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0360.g1:G">$G$</span> to <span data-ref="0360.g1:H">$H$</span>;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore also, as <span data-ref="0360.g1:G">$G$</span> is to <span data-ref="0360.g1:H">$H$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0360.g1:A">$A$</span> to <span data-ref="0360.g1:L">$L$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause"><sup>20</sup> Again, since <span data-ref="0360.g1:E">$E$</span> by multiplying <span data-ref="0360.g1:D">$D$</span> has made <span data-ref="0360.g1:L">$L$</span>, and further<br />
by multiplying <span data-ref="0360.g1:F">$F$</span> has made <span data-ref="0360.g1:B">$B$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore, as <span data-ref="0360.g1:D">$D$</span> is to <span data-ref="0360.g1:F">$F$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0360.g1:L">$L$</span> to <span data-ref="0360.g1:B">$B$</span>. <span class="justification">[VII. 17]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But, as <span data-ref="0360.g1:D">$D$</span> is to <span data-ref="0360.g1:F">$F$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0360.g1:H">$H$</span> to <span data-ref="0360.g1:K">$K$</span>;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore also, as <span data-ref="0360.g1:H">$H$</span> is to <span data-ref="0360.g1:K">$K$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0360.g1:L">$L$</span> to <span data-ref="0360.g1:B">$B$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause"><sup>25</sup> But it was also proved that,<br />
as <span data-ref="0360.g1:G">$G$</span> is to <span data-ref="0360.g1:H">$H$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0360.g1:A">$A$</span> to <span data-ref="0360.g1:L">$L$</span>;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore, *ex aequali*,<br />
as <span data-ref="0360.g1:G">$G$</span> is to <span data-ref="0360.g1:K">$K$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0360.g1:A">$A$</span> to <span data-ref="0360.g1:B">$B$</span>. <span class="justification">[VII. 14]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But <span data-ref="0360.g1:G">$G$</span> has to <span data-ref="0360.g1:K">$K$</span> the ratio compounded of the ratios of the<br />
<sup>30</sup> sides;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0360.g1:A">$A$</span> also has to <span data-ref="0360.g1:B">$B$</span> the ratio compounded of the ratios<br />
of the sides.</p>

<p class="qed"><span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

---

**1, 5, 29, 31. compounded of the ratios of their sides.** As in VI. 23, the Greek has the less exact phrase, "compounded of their sides."

If
$$a = cd,\quad b = ef,$$
then $a$ has to $b$ the ratio compounded of $c : e$ and $d : f$.

Take three numbers the least which are continuously in the given ratios.
