---

Let $a, b, c \dots k$ be a geometrical progression in which $a$ does not measure $b$.
Suppose, if possible, that $a$ measures some term of the series, as $f$.
Take $x, y, z, u, v, w$ the *least* numbers in the ratio $a, b, c, d, e, f$.
Since
$$x : y = a : b,$$
and $a$ does not measure $b$,
$x$ does not measure $y$; therefore $x$ cannot be unity.
And, *ex aequali*,
$$x : w = a : f.$$
Now $x$, $w$ are prime to one another. [VIII. 3]
Therefore $a$ does not measure $f$.

We can of course prove that an intermediate term, as $b$, does not measure a later term $f$ by using the series $b, c, d, e, f$ and remembering that, since $b : c = a : b$, $b$ does not measure $c$.

### PROPOSITION 7.

*If there be as many numbers as we please in continued proportion, and the first measure the last, it will measure the second also.*

Let there be as many numbers as we please, <span data-ref="0362.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0362.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0362.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0362.g1:D">$D$</span>, in continued proportion; and
let <span data-ref="0362.g1:A">$A$</span> measure <span data-ref="0362.g1:D">$D$</span>;
I say that <span data-ref="0362.g1:A">$A$</span> also measures <span data-ref="0362.g1:B">$B$</span>.

![Book VIII Proposition 7](0362.g1.svg)

For, if <span data-ref="0362.g1:A">$A$</span> does not measure <span data-ref="0362.g1:B">$B$</span>, neither will any other of the numbers measure any other. [VIII. 6]
But <span data-ref="0362.g1:A">$A$</span> measures <span data-ref="0362.g1:D">$D$</span>.
Therefore <span data-ref="0362.g1:A">$A$</span> also measures <span data-ref="0362.g1:B">$B$</span>.

Q. E. D.

---

An obvious proof by *reductio ad absurdum* from VIII. 6.
