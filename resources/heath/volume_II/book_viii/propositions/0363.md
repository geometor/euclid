### PROPOSITION 8.

<p class="enunciation">If between two numbers there fall numbers in continued<br />
proportion with them, then, however many numbers fall between<br />
them in continued proportion, so many will also fall in con-<br />
tinued proportion between the numbers which have the same<br />
ratio with the original numbers.</p>

Let the numbers <span data-ref="0363.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0363.g1:D">$D$</span> fall between the two numbers <span data-ref="0363.g1:A">$A$</span>,<br />
<span data-ref="0363.g1:B">$B$</span> in continued proportion with them, and let <span data-ref="0363.g1:E">$E$</span> be made in<br />
the same ratio to <span data-ref="0363.g1:F">$F$</span> as <span data-ref="0363.g1:A">$A$</span> is to <span data-ref="0363.g1:B">$B$</span>;

I say that, as many numbers as have fallen between <span data-ref="0363.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0363.g1:B">$B$</span> in<br />
continued proportion, so many will also fall between <span data-ref="0363.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0363.g1:F">$F$</span> in<br />
continued proportion.

![Book VIII Proposition 8](0363.g1.svg)

For, as many as <span data-ref="0363.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0363.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0363.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0363.g1:D">$D$</span> are in multitude, let so many<br />
numbers <span data-ref="0363.g1:G">$G$</span>, <span data-ref="0363.g1:H">$H$</span>, <span data-ref="0363.g1:K">$K$</span>, <span data-ref="0363.g1:L">$L$</span>, the least of those which have the same<br />
ratio with <span data-ref="0363.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0363.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0363.g1:D">$D$</span>, <span data-ref="0363.g1:B">$B$</span>, be taken ; <span class="justification">[VII. 33]</span>

<p class="assertion">therefore the extremes of them <span data-ref="0363.g1:G">$G$</span>, <span data-ref="0363.g1:L">$L$</span> are prime to one another. <span class="justification">[VIII. 3]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">Now, since <span data-ref="0363.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0363.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0363.g1:D">$D$</span>, <span data-ref="0363.g1:B">$B$</span> are in the same ratio with <span data-ref="0363.g1:G">$G$</span>, <span data-ref="0363.g1:H">$H$</span>,<br />
<span data-ref="0363.g1:K">$K$</span>, <span data-ref="0363.g1:L">$L$</span>,<br />
and the multitude of the numbers <span data-ref="0363.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0363.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0363.g1:D">$D$</span>, <span data-ref="0363.g1:B">$B$</span> is equal to the<br />
multitude of the numbers <span data-ref="0363.g1:G">$G$</span>, <span data-ref="0363.g1:H">$H$</span>, <span data-ref="0363.g1:K">$K$</span>, <span data-ref="0363.g1:L">$L$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore, *ex aequali*, as <span data-ref="0363.g1:A">$A$</span> is to <span data-ref="0363.g1:B">$B$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0363.g1:G">$G$</span> to <span data-ref="0363.g1:L">$L$</span>. <span class="justification">[VII. 14]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But, as <span data-ref="0363.g1:A">$A$</span> is to <span data-ref="0363.g1:B">$B$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0363.g1:E">$E$</span> to <span data-ref="0363.g1:F">$F$</span>;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore also, as <span data-ref="0363.g1:G">$G$</span> is to <span data-ref="0363.g1:L">$L$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0363.g1:E">$E$</span> to <span data-ref="0363.g1:F">$F$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">But <span data-ref="0363.g1:G">$G$</span>, <span data-ref="0363.g1:L">$L$</span> are prime,<br />
primes are also least, <span class="justification">[VII. 21]</span><br />
and the least numbers measure those which have the same<br />
ratio the same number of times, the greater the greater and<br />
the less the less, that is, the antecedent the antecedent and the<br />
consequent the consequent. <span class="justification">[VII. 20]</span></p>
