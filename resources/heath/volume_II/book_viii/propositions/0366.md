<p class="sub-clause">But the unit <span data-ref="0365.g1:E">$E$</span> also measures the number <span data-ref="0365.g1:F">$F$</span> according to<br />

<p class="assertion">therefore the unit <span data-ref="0365.g1:E">$E$</span> measures the number <span data-ref="0365.g1:F">$F$</span> the same number<br />

Suppose there are $n$ geometric means between $a$, $b$, two numbers prime to<br />
one another; there are the same number ($n$) of geometric means between $1$<br />
and $a$ and between $1$ and $b$.

If $c, d\dots$ are the $n$ means between $a$, $b$,
$$a,\quad c,\quad d\dots b$$
are the least numbers in that ratio, since $a$, $b$ are prime to one another. <span class="justification">[VIII. 1]</span>

The terms are therefore respectively identical with
$$\alpha^{n+1},\quad \alpha^n\beta,\quad \alpha^{n-1}\beta^2\dots \alpha\beta^n,\quad \beta^{n+1},$$
where $\alpha$, $\beta$ is the common ratio in its lowest terms. <span class="justification">[VIII. 2, Por.]</span>

Thus
$$a = \alpha^{n+1},\quad b = \beta^{n+1}.$$
Now
$$1 : \alpha = \alpha : \alpha^2 = \alpha^2 : \alpha^3 \dots = \alpha^n : \alpha^{n+1},$$
and
$$1 : \beta = \beta : \beta^2 = \beta^2 : \beta^3 \dots = \beta^n : \beta^{n+1};$$
whence there are $n$ geometric means between $1$, $a$, and between $1$, $b$.

### PROPOSITION 10.

<p class="enunciation">If numbers fall between each of two numbers and an unit<br />
in continued proportion, however many numbers fall between<br />
each of them and an unit in continued proportion, so many<br />
also will fall between the numbers themselves in continued<br />
proportion.</p>
