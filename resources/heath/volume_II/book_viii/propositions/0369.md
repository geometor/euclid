### PROPOSITION 11.

<p class="enunciation">Between two square numbers there is one mean proportional<br />
number, and the square has to the square the ratio duplicate<br />
of that which the side has to the side.</p>

Let <span data-ref="0369.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0369.g1:B">$B$</span> be square numbers,<br />
and let <span data-ref="0369.g1:C">$C$</span> be the side of <span data-ref="0369.g1:A">$A$</span>, and <span data-ref="0369.g1:D">$D$</span> of <span data-ref="0369.g1:B">$B$</span>;<br />
I say that between <span data-ref="0369.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0369.g1:B">$B$</span> there is one mean proportional<br />
number, and <span data-ref="0369.g1:A">$A$</span> has to <span data-ref="0369.g1:B">$B$</span> the ratio<br />
duplicate of that which <span data-ref="0369.g1:C">$C$</span> has to <span data-ref="0369.g1:D">$D$</span>.

For let <span data-ref="0369.g1:C">$C$</span> by multiplying <span data-ref="0369.g1:D">$D$</span> make <span data-ref="0369.g1:E">$E$</span>.

![Book VIII, Proposition 11](0369.g1.svg)

<p class="sub-clause">Now, since <span data-ref="0369.g1:A">$A$</span> is a square and <span data-ref="0369.g1:C">$C$</span> is<br />
its side,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0369.g1:C">$C$</span> by multiplying itself has<br />
made <span data-ref="0369.g1:A">$A$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">For the same reason also</p>
<p class="assertion"><span data-ref="0369.g1:D">$D$</span> by multiplying itself has made <span data-ref="0369.g1:B">$B$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">Since then <span data-ref="0369.g1:C">$C$</span> by multiplying the numbers <span data-ref="0369.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0369.g1:D">$D$</span> has made<br />
<span data-ref="0369.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0369.g1:E">$E$</span> respectively,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore, as <span data-ref="0369.g1:C">$C$</span> is to <span data-ref="0369.g1:D">$D$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0369.g1:A">$A$</span> to <span data-ref="0369.g1:E">$E$</span>. <span class="justification">[VII. 17]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">For the same reason also,</p>
<p class="assertion">as <span data-ref="0369.g1:C">$C$</span> is to <span data-ref="0369.g1:D">$D$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0369.g1:E">$E$</span> to <span data-ref="0369.g1:B">$B$</span>. <span class="justification">[VII. 18]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore also, as <span data-ref="0369.g1:A">$A$</span> is to <span data-ref="0369.g1:E">$E$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0369.g1:E">$E$</span> to <span data-ref="0369.g1:B">$B$</span>.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore between <span data-ref="0369.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0369.g1:B">$B$</span> there is one mean proportional<br />
number.</p>

I say next that <span data-ref="0369.g1:A">$A$</span> also has to <span data-ref="0369.g1:B">$B$</span> the ratio duplicate of<br />
that which <span data-ref="0369.g1:C">$C$</span> has to <span data-ref="0369.g1:D">$D$</span>.

<p class="sub-clause">For, since <span data-ref="0369.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0369.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0369.g1:B">$B$</span> are three numbers in proportion,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0369.g1:A">$A$</span> has to <span data-ref="0369.g1:B">$B$</span> the ratio duplicate of that which <span data-ref="0369.g1:A">$A$</span> has<br />
to <span data-ref="0369.g1:E">$E$</span>. <span class="justification">[V. Def. 9]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But, as <span data-ref="0369.g1:A">$A$</span> is to <span data-ref="0369.g1:E">$E$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0369.g1:C">$C$</span> to <span data-ref="0369.g1:D">$D$</span>.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore <span data-ref="0369.g1:A">$A$</span> has to <span data-ref="0369.g1:B">$B$</span> the ratio duplicate of that which<br />
the side <span data-ref="0369.g1:C">$C$</span> has to <span data-ref="0369.g1:D">$D$</span>.</p>

<p class="qed"><span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

According to Nicomachus the theorems in this proposition and the next,<br />
that two squares have *one* geometric mean, and two cubes *two* geometric<br />
means, between them are Platonic. Cf. *Timaeus*, 32 A sqq. and the note<br />
thereon, p. 294 above.
