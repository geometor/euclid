$a^2$, $b^2$ being two squares, it is only necessary to form the product $ab$ and<br />
to prove that
$$a^2,\quad ab,\quad b^2$$
are in geometrical progression. Euclid proves that
$$a^2 : ab = ab : b^2$$
by means of VII. 17, 18, as usual.

In assuming that, since $a^2$ is to $b^2$ in the duplicate ratio of $a^2$ to $ab$, $a^2$ is<br />
to $b^2$ in the duplicate ratio of $a$ to $b$, Euclid assumes that ratios which are<br />
the duplicates of equal ratios are equal. This, an obvious inference from<br />
V. 22, can be inferred just as easily for numbers from VII. 14.

### PROPOSITION 12.

<p class="enunciation">Between two cube numbers there are two mean proportional<br />
numbers, and the cube has to the cube the ratio triplicate of that<br />
which the side has to the side.</p>

Let <span data-ref="0370.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0370.g1:B">$B$</span> be cube numbers,<br />
and let <span data-ref="0370.g1:C">$C$</span> be the side of <span data-ref="0370.g1:A">$A$</span>, and <span data-ref="0370.g1:D">$D$</span> of <span data-ref="0370.g1:B">$B$</span>;<br />
I say that between <span data-ref="0370.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0370.g1:B">$B$</span> there are two mean proportional<br />
numbers, and <span data-ref="0370.g1:A">$A$</span> has to <span data-ref="0370.g1:B">$B$</span> the ratio triplicate of that which <span data-ref="0370.g1:C">$C$</span><br />
has to <span data-ref="0370.g1:D">$D$</span>.

![Book VIII, Proposition 12](0370.g1.svg)

For let <span data-ref="0370.g1:C">$C$</span> by multiplying itself make <span data-ref="0370.g1:E">$E$</span>, and by multiplying<br />
<span data-ref="0370.g1:D">$D$</span> let it make <span data-ref="0370.g1:F">$F$</span>;<br />
let <span data-ref="0370.g1:D">$D$</span> by multiplying itself make <span data-ref="0370.g1:G">$G$</span>,<br />
and let the numbers <span data-ref="0370.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0370.g1:D">$D$</span> by multiplying <span data-ref="0370.g1:F">$F$</span> make <span data-ref="0370.g1:H">$H$</span>, <span data-ref="0370.g1:K">$K$</span><br />
respectively.

<p class="sub-clause">Now, since <span data-ref="0370.g1:A">$A$</span> is a cube, and <span data-ref="0370.g1:C">$C$</span> its side,<br />
and <span data-ref="0370.g1:C">$C$</span> by multiplying itself has made <span data-ref="0370.g1:E">$E$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0370.g1:C">$C$</span> by multiplying itself has made <span data-ref="0370.g1:E">$E$</span> and by multiply-<br />
ing <span data-ref="0370.g1:E">$E$</span> has made <span data-ref="0370.g1:A">$A$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">For the same reason also</p>
<p class="assertion"><span data-ref="0370.g1:D">$D$</span> by multiplying itself has made <span data-ref="0370.g1:G">$G$</span> and by multiplying <span data-ref="0370.g1:G">$G$</span> has<br />
made <span data-ref="0370.g1:B">$B$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">And, since <span data-ref="0370.g1:C">$C$</span> by multiplying the numbers <span data-ref="0370.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0370.g1:D">$D$</span> has made<br />
<span data-ref="0370.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0370.g1:F">$F$</span> respectively,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore, as <span data-ref="0370.g1:C">$C$</span> is to <span data-ref="0370.g1:D">$D$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0370.g1:E">$E$</span> to <span data-ref="0370.g1:F">$F$</span>. <span class="justification">[VII. 17]</span></p>
