The cube numbers $a^3$, $b^3$ being given, Euclid forms the products $a^2b$, $ab^2$<br />
and then proves, as usual, by means of VII. 17, 18 that
$$a^3,\quad a^2b,\quad ab^2,\quad b^3$$
are in continued proportion.

He assumes that, since $a^3$ has to $b^3$ the ratio triplicate of $a^3 : a^2b$, the<br />
ratio $a^3 : b^3$ is triplicate of the ratio $a : b$ which is equal to $a^3 : a^2b$. This<br />
is again an obvious inference from VII. 14.

### PROPOSITION 13.

<p class="enunciation">If there be as many numbers as we please in continued<br />
proportion, and each by multiplying itself make some number,<br />
the products will be proportional; and, if the original numbers<br />
by multiplying the products make certain numbers, the latter<br />
will also be proportional.</p>
