If $a^2$ measures $b^2$, $a$ measures $b$; and, if $a$ measures $b$, $a^2$ measures $b^2$.

(1) $a^2$, $ab$, $b^2$ are in continued proportion in the ratio of $a$ to $b$.

$$\begin{array}{ll}
\text{Therefore, since} & a^2 \text{ measures } b^2\text{,} \\
& a^2 \text{ measures } ab\text{.} & \text{[VIII. 7]} \\
\text{But} & a^2 : ab = a : b\text{.} \\
\text{Therefore} & a \text{ measures } b\text{.}
\end{array}$$

(2) Since $a$ measures $b$, $a^2$ measures $ab$.

And $a^2$, $ab$, $b^2$ are continuously proportional.

$$\begin{array}{ll}
\text{Thus} & ab \text{ measures } b^2\text{.} \\
\text{And} & a^2 \text{ measures } ab\text{.} \\
\text{Therefore} & a^2 \text{ measures } b^2\text{.}
\end{array}$$

It will be seen that Euclid puts the last step shortly, saying that, since $a^2$ measures $ab$, and $a^2$, $ab$, $b^2$ are in continued proportion, $a^2$ measures $b^2$. The same thing happens in VIII. 15, where the series of terms is one more than here.

PROPOSITION 15.
---------------

.. enunciation::

   If a cube number measure a cube number, the side will also measure the side; and, if the side measure the side, the cube will also measure the cube.

For let the cube number :g:`$A$ <0375.g1:A>` measure the cube :g:`$B$ <0375.g1:B>`, and let :g:`$C$ <0375.g1:C>` be the side of :g:`$A$ <0375.g1:A>` and :g:`$D$ <0375.g1:D>` of :g:`$B$ <0375.g1:B>`;

I say that :g:`$C$ <0375.g1:C>` measures :g:`$D$ <0375.g1:D>`.
