<p class="sub-clause">But, as <span data-ref="0377.g1:C">$C$</span> is to <span data-ref="0377.g1:E">$E$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0377.g1:D">$D$</span> to <span data-ref="0377.g1:F">$F$</span>;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore also, as <span data-ref="0377.g1:D">$D$</span> is to <span data-ref="0377.g1:F">$F$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0377.g1:A">$A$</span> to <span data-ref="0377.g1:G">$G$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">Again, since <span data-ref="0377.g1:E">$E$</span> by multiplying <span data-ref="0377.g1:D">$D$</span> has made <span data-ref="0377.g1:G">$G$</span>, and by<br />
multiplying <span data-ref="0377.g1:F">$F$</span> has made <span data-ref="0377.g1:B">$B$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore, as <span data-ref="0377.g1:D">$D$</span> is to <span data-ref="0377.g1:F">$F$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0377.g1:G">$G$</span> to <span data-ref="0377.g1:B">$B$</span>. <span class="justification">[VII. 17]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But it was also proved that,<br />
as <span data-ref="0377.g1:D">$D$</span> is to <span data-ref="0377.g1:F">$F$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0377.g1:A">$A$</span> to <span data-ref="0377.g1:G">$G$</span>;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore also, as <span data-ref="0377.g1:A">$A$</span> is to <span data-ref="0377.g1:G">$G$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0377.g1:G">$G$</span> to <span data-ref="0377.g1:B">$B$</span>.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore <span data-ref="0377.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0377.g1:G">$G$</span>, <span data-ref="0377.g1:B">$B$</span> are in continued proportion.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore between <span data-ref="0377.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0377.g1:B">$B$</span> there is one mean proportional<br />
number.</p>

I say next that <span data-ref="0377.g1:A">$A$</span> also has to <span data-ref="0377.g1:B">$B$</span> the ratio duplicate of<br />
that which the corresponding side has to the corresponding<br />
side, that is, of that which <span data-ref="0377.g1:C">$C$</span> has to <span data-ref="0377.g1:E">$E$</span> or <span data-ref="0377.g1:D">$D$</span> to <span data-ref="0377.g1:F">$F$</span>.

<p class="sub-clause">For, since <span data-ref="0377.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0377.g1:G">$G$</span>, <span data-ref="0377.g1:B">$B$</span> are in continued proportion,</p>
<p class="assertion"><span data-ref="0377.g1:A">$A$</span> has to <span data-ref="0377.g1:B">$B$</span> the ratio duplicate of that which it has to <span data-ref="0377.g1:G">$G$</span>. <span class="justification">[V. Def. 9]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">And, as <span data-ref="0377.g1:A">$A$</span> is to <span data-ref="0377.g1:G">$G$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0377.g1:C">$C$</span> to <span data-ref="0377.g1:E">$E$</span>, and so is <span data-ref="0377.g1:D">$D$</span> to <span data-ref="0377.g1:F">$F$</span>.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore <span data-ref="0377.g1:A">$A$</span> also has to <span data-ref="0377.g1:B">$B$</span> the ratio duplicate of that which<br />
<span data-ref="0377.g1:C">$C$</span> has to <span data-ref="0377.g1:E">$E$</span> or <span data-ref="0377.g1:D">$D$</span> to <span data-ref="0377.g1:F">$F$</span>.</p>

<p class="qed"><span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

If $ab$, $cd$ be "similar plane numbers," i.e. products of factors such that
$$a : b = c : d,$$
there is one mean proportional between $ab$ and $cd$; and $ab$ is to $cd$ in the<br />
duplicate ratio of $a$ to $c$ or of $b$ to $d$.

Form the product $bc$ (or $ad$, which is equal to it, by VII. 19).

Then
$$\left.\begin{array}{r} ab,\ bc \\ = ad \end{array}\right\},\ cd$$
is a series of terms in geometrical progression.

For
$$a : b = c : d.$$

Therefore
$$a : c = b : d. \qquad \text{[VII. 13]}$$

Therefore
$$ab : bc = bc : cd. \qquad \text{[VII. 17 and 16]}$$

Thus $bc$ (or $ad$) is a geometric mean between $ab$, $cd$.

And $ab$ is to $cd$ in the duplicate ratio of $ab$ to $bc$ or of $bc$ to $cd$, that is, of<br />
$a$ to $c$ or of $b$ to $d$.
