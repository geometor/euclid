### PROPOSITION 19.

*Between two similar solid numbers there fall two mean proportional numbers; and the solid number has to the similar solid number the ratio triplicate of that which the corresponding side has to the corresponding side.*

Let <span data-ref="0379.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0379.g1:B">$B$</span> be two similar solid numbers, and let <span data-ref="0379.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0379.g1:D">$D$</span>, <span data-ref="0379.g1:E">$E$</span> be the sides of <span data-ref="0379.g1:A">$A$</span>, and <span data-ref="0379.g1:F">$F$</span>, <span data-ref="0379.g1:G">$G$</span>, <span data-ref="0379.g1:H">$H$</span> of <span data-ref="0379.g1:B">$B$</span>.

Now, since similar solid numbers are those which have their sides proportional, [VII. Def. 21] therefore, as <span data-ref="0379.g1:C">$C$</span> is to <span data-ref="0379.g1:D">$D$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0379.g1:F">$F$</span> to <span data-ref="0379.g1:G">$G$</span>, and, as <span data-ref="0379.g1:D">$D$</span> is to <span data-ref="0379.g1:E">$E$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0379.g1:G">$G$</span> to <span data-ref="0379.g1:H">$H$</span>.

I say that between <span data-ref="0379.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0379.g1:B">$B$</span> there fall two mean proportional numbers, and <span data-ref="0379.g1:A">$A$</span> has to <span data-ref="0379.g1:B">$B$</span> the ratio triplicate of that which <span data-ref="0379.g1:C">$C$</span> has to <span data-ref="0379.g1:F">$F$</span>, <span data-ref="0379.g1:D">$D$</span> to <span data-ref="0379.g1:G">$G$</span>, and also <span data-ref="0379.g1:E">$E$</span> to <span data-ref="0379.g1:H">$H$</span>.

![Book VIII, Proposition 19](0379.g1.svg)

For let <span data-ref="0379.g1:C">$C$</span> by multiplying <span data-ref="0379.g1:D">$D$</span> make <span data-ref="0379.g1:K">$K$</span>, and let <span data-ref="0379.g1:F">$F$</span> by multiplying <span data-ref="0379.g1:G">$G$</span> make <span data-ref="0379.g1:L">$L$</span>.

Now, since <span data-ref="0379.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0379.g1:D">$D$</span> are in the same ratio with <span data-ref="0379.g1:F">$F$</span>, <span data-ref="0379.g1:G">$G$</span>, and <span data-ref="0379.g1:K">$K$</span> is the product of <span data-ref="0379.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0379.g1:D">$D$</span>, and <span data-ref="0379.g1:L">$L$</span> the product of <span data-ref="0379.g1:F">$F$</span>, <span data-ref="0379.g1:G">$G$</span>, <span data-ref="0379.g1:K">$K$</span>, <span data-ref="0379.g1:L">$L$</span> are similar plane numbers; [VII. Def. 21] therefore between <span data-ref="0379.g1:K">$K$</span>, <span data-ref="0379.g1:L">$L$</span> there is one mean proportional number. [VIII. 18]

Let it be <span data-ref="0379.g1:M">$M$</span>.

Therefore <span data-ref="0379.g1:M">$M$</span> is the product of <span data-ref="0379.g1:D">$D$</span>, <span data-ref="0379.g1:F">$F$</span>, as was proved in the theorem preceding this. [VIII. 18]

Now, since <span data-ref="0379.g1:D">$D$</span> by multiplying <span data-ref="0379.g1:C">$C$</span> has made <span data-ref="0379.g1:K">$K$</span>, and by multiplying <span data-ref="0379.g1:F">$F$</span> has made <span data-ref="0379.g1:M">$M$</span>, therefore, as <span data-ref="0379.g1:C">$C$</span> is to <span data-ref="0379.g1:F">$F$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0379.g1:K">$K$</span> to <span data-ref="0379.g1:M">$M$</span>. [VII. 17]

But, as <span data-ref="0379.g1:K">$K$</span> is to <span data-ref="0379.g1:M">$M$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0379.g1:M">$M$</span> to <span data-ref="0379.g1:L">$L$</span>.

Therefore <span data-ref="0379.g1:K">$K$</span>, <span data-ref="0379.g1:M">$M$</span>, <span data-ref="0379.g1:L">$L$</span> are continuously proportional in the ratio of <span data-ref="0379.g1:C">$C$</span> to <span data-ref="0379.g1:F">$F$</span>.
