PROPOSITION 19.
---------------

.. enunciation::

   Between two similar solid numbers there fall two mean proportional numbers; and the solid number has to the similar solid number the ratio triplicate of that which the corresponding side has to the corresponding side.

Let :g:`$A$ <0379.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0379.g1:B>` be two similar solid numbers, and let :g:`$C$ <0379.g1:C>`, :g:`$D$ <0379.g1:D>`, :g:`$E$ <0379.g1:E>` be the sides of :g:`$A$ <0379.g1:A>`, and :g:`$F$ <0379.g1:F>`, :g:`$G$ <0379.g1:G>`, :g:`$H$ <0379.g1:H>` of :g:`$B$ <0379.g1:B>`.

.. subclause::

   Now, since similar solid numbers are those which have their sides proportional, :just:`[VII. Def. 21] <VII.def.21>`

.. assertion::

   therefore, as :g:`$C$ <0379.g1:C>` is to :g:`$D$ <0379.g1:D>`, so is :g:`$F$ <0379.g1:F>` to :g:`$G$ <0379.g1:G>`, and, as :g:`$D$ <0379.g1:D>` is to :g:`$E$ <0379.g1:E>`, so is :g:`$G$ <0379.g1:G>` to :g:`$H$ <0379.g1:H>`.

I say that between :g:`$A$ <0379.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0379.g1:B>` there fall two mean proportional numbers, and :g:`$A$ <0379.g1:A>` has to :g:`$B$ <0379.g1:B>` the ratio triplicate of that which :g:`$C$ <0379.g1:C>` has to :g:`$F$ <0379.g1:F>`, :g:`$D$ <0379.g1:D>` to :g:`$G$ <0379.g1:G>`, and also :g:`$E$ <0379.g1:E>` to :g:`$H$ <0379.g1:H>`.

.. picture:: 0379.g1.svg
   :alt: Book VIII, Proposition 19

For let :g:`$C$ <0379.g1:C>` by multiplying :g:`$D$ <0379.g1:D>` make :g:`$K$ <0379.g1:K>`, and let :g:`$F$ <0379.g1:F>` by multiplying :g:`$G$ <0379.g1:G>` make :g:`$L$ <0379.g1:L>`.

.. subclause::

   Now, since :g:`$C$ <0379.g1:C>`, :g:`$D$ <0379.g1:D>` are in the same ratio with :g:`$F$ <0379.g1:F>`, :g:`$G$ <0379.g1:G>`, and :g:`$K$ <0379.g1:K>` is the product of :g:`$C$ <0379.g1:C>`, :g:`$D$ <0379.g1:D>`, and :g:`$L$ <0379.g1:L>` the product of :g:`$F$ <0379.g1:F>`, :g:`$G$ <0379.g1:G>`, :g:`$K$ <0379.g1:K>`, :g:`$L$ <0379.g1:L>` are similar plane numbers; :just:`[VII. Def. 21] <VII.def.21>`

.. assertion::

   therefore between :g:`$K$ <0379.g1:K>`, :g:`$L$ <0379.g1:L>` there is one mean proportional number. :just:`[VIII. 18] <VIII.18>`

Let it be :g:`$M$ <0379.g1:M>`.

.. assertion::

   Therefore :g:`$M$ <0379.g1:M>` is the product of :g:`$D$ <0379.g1:D>`, :g:`$F$ <0379.g1:F>`, as was proved in the theorem preceding this. :just:`[VIII. 18] <VIII.18>`

.. subclause::

   Now, since :g:`$D$ <0379.g1:D>` by multiplying :g:`$C$ <0379.g1:C>` has made :g:`$K$ <0379.g1:K>`, and by multiplying :g:`$F$ <0379.g1:F>` has made :g:`$M$ <0379.g1:M>`,

.. assertion:: [VII. 17] <VII.17>

   therefore, as :g:`$C$ <0379.g1:C>` is to :g:`$F$ <0379.g1:F>`, so is :g:`$K$ <0379.g1:K>` to :g:`$M$ <0379.g1:M>`.

.. subclause::

   But, as :g:`$K$ <0379.g1:K>` is to :g:`$M$ <0379.g1:M>`, so is :g:`$M$ <0379.g1:M>` to :g:`$L$ <0379.g1:L>`.

.. assertion::

   Therefore :g:`$K$ <0379.g1:K>`, :g:`$M$ <0379.g1:M>`, :g:`$L$ <0379.g1:L>` are continuously proportional in the ratio of :g:`$C$ <0379.g1:C>` to :g:`$F$ <0379.g1:F>`.
