.. subclause::

   For, since :g:`$A$ <0379.g1:A>`, :g:`$N$ <0379.g1:N>`, :g:`$O$ <0379.g1:O>`, :g:`$B$ <0379.g1:B>` are four numbers in continued proportion,

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$A$ <0379.g1:A>` has to :g:`$B$ <0379.g1:B>` the ratio triplicate of that which :g:`$A$ <0379.g1:A>` has to :g:`$N$ <0379.g1:N>`. :just:`[v. Def. 10] <V.def.10>`

.. subclause::

   But, as :g:`$A$ <0379.g1:A>` is to :g:`$N$ <0379.g1:N>`, so it was proved that :g:`$C$ <0379.g1:C>` is to :g:`$F$ <0379.g1:F>`, :g:`$D$ <0379.g1:D>` to :g:`$G$ <0379.g1:G>`, and also :g:`$E$ <0379.g1:E>` to :g:`$H$ <0379.g1:H>`.

.. assertion::

   Therefore :g:`$A$ <0379.g1:A>` also has to :g:`$B$ <0379.g1:B>` the ratio triplicate of that which the corresponding side has to the corresponding side, that is, of the ratio which the number :g:`$C$ <0379.g1:C>` has to :g:`$F$ <0379.g1:F>`, :g:`$D$ <0379.g1:D>` to :g:`$G$ <0379.g1:G>`, and also :g:`$E$ <0379.g1:E>` to :g:`$H$ <0379.g1:H>`.

.. qed::

   :just:`Q. E. D.`

----

In other words, if $a : b : c = d : e : f$, then there are two geometric means between $abc$, $def$; and $abc$ is to $def$ in the triplicate ratio of $a$ to $d$, or $b$ to $e$, or $c$ to $f$.

Euclid first takes the plane numbers $ab$, $de$ (leaving out $c$, $f$) and forms the product $bd$. Thus, as in VIII. 18,

$$\left.\begin{array}{r} ab,\ bd \\ = ea \end{array}\right\},\ de$$

are three terms in geometrical progression in the ratio of $a$ to $d$, or of $b$ to $e$.

He next forms the products of $c$, $f$ respectively into the mean $bd$.

Then

$$abc,\ cbd,\ fbd,\ def$$

are in geometrical progression in the ratio of $a$ to $d$ etc.

For

$$\left.\begin{aligned} abc : cbd &= ab : bd = a : d \\ bd : fbd &= c : f \\ fbd : def &= bd : de = b : e \end{aligned}\right\} \qquad \text{[VII. 17]}$$

And

$$a : d = b : e = c : f.$$

The ratio of $abc$ to $def$ is the ratio triplicate of that of $abc$ to $cbd$, i.e. of that of $a$ to $d$ etc.

PROPOSITION 20.
---------------

.. enunciation::

   If one mean proportional number fall between two numbers, the numbers will be similar plane numbers.

For let one mean proportional number :g:`$C$ <0382.g1:C>` fall between the two numbers :g:`$A$ <0382.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0382.g1:B>`;

I say that :g:`$A$ <0382.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0382.g1:B>` are similar plane numbers.

Let :g:`$D$ <0382.g1:D>`, :g:`$E$ <0382.g1:E>`, the least numbers of those which have the same ratio with :g:`$A$ <0382.g1:A>`, :g:`$C$ <0382.g1:C>`, be taken; :just:`[VII. 33] <VII.33>`

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$D$ <0382.g1:D>` measures :g:`$A$ <0382.g1:A>` the same number of times that :g:`$E$ <0382.g1:E>` measures :g:`$C$ <0382.g1:C>`. :just:`[VII. 20] <VII.20>`

Now, as many times as :g:`$D$ <0382.g1:D>` measures :g:`$A$ <0382.g1:A>`, so many units let there be in :g:`$F$ <0382.g1:F>`;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$F$ <0382.g1:F>` by multiplying :g:`$D$ <0382.g1:D>` has made :g:`$A$ <0382.g1:A>`, so that :g:`$A$ <0382.g1:A>` is plane, and :g:`$D$ <0382.g1:D>`, :g:`$F$ <0382.g1:F>` are its sides.
