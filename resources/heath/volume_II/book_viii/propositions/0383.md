### PROPOSITION 21.

<p class="enunciation">If two mean proportional numbers fall between two numbers,<br />
the numbers are similar solid numbers.</p>

For let two mean proportional numbers <span data-ref="0383.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0383.g1:D">$D$</span> fall between<br />
the two numbers <span data-ref="0383.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0383.g1:B">$B$</span>;<br />
I say that <span data-ref="0383.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0383.g1:B">$B$</span> are similar solid numbers.

![Proposition 21](0383.g1.svg)

For let three numbers <span data-ref="0383.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0383.g1:F">$F$</span>, <span data-ref="0383.g1:G">$G$</span>, the least of those which<br />
have the same ratio with <span data-ref="0383.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0383.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0383.g1:D">$D$</span>, be taken; <span class="justification">[VII. 33 or VIII. 2]</span>
<p class="assertion">therefore the extremes of them <span data-ref="0383.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0383.g1:G">$G$</span> are prime to one another.<br />
<span class="justification">[VIII. 3]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">Now, since one mean proportional number <span data-ref="0383.g1:F">$F$</span> has fallen<br />

<p class="assertion">Therefore it is manifest from the theorem before this that<br />

and that of <span data-ref="0383.g1:K">$K$</span> to <span data-ref="0383.g1:M">$M$</span>.</p>
