Now, *ex aequali*,
$$a : d = \alpha : \gamma, \qquad \text{[VII. 14]}$$
and, since $\alpha, \gamma$ are prime to one another,
$$a = r\alpha, \quad d = r\gamma, \quad \text{where } r \text{ is an integer.}$$
But $\alpha = mn$:
therefore $a = rmn$, and therefore $a$ is "solid."
Again, *ex aequali*,
$$c : b = \alpha : \gamma,$$
and therefore
$$c = s\alpha, \quad b = s\gamma, \quad \text{where } s \text{ is an integer.}$$
Thus $b = spq$, and $b$ is therefore "solid."
Now
$$\begin{aligned}
\alpha : \beta = a : c &= r\alpha : s\alpha \\
&= r : s.
\end{aligned} \qquad \text{[VII. 18]}$$
And, from above,
$$\alpha : \beta = m : p = n : q.$$
Therefore
$$r : s = m : p = n : q,$$
and hence $a, b$ are similar solid numbers.

### PROPOSITION 22.

<p class="enunciation">If three numbers be in continued proportion, and the first<br />
be square, the third will also be square.</p>

Let <span data-ref="0385.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0385.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0385.g1:C">$C$</span> be three numbers in continued proportion,<br />
and let <span data-ref="0385.g1:A">$A$</span> the first be square;<br />
I say that <span data-ref="0385.g1:C">$C$</span> the third is also square.

![Proposition 22](0385.g1.svg)

<p class="sub-clause">For, since between <span data-ref="0385.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0385.g1:C">$C$</span> there is one<br />
mean proportional number, <span data-ref="0385.g1:B">$B$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0385.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0385.g1:C">$C$</span> are similar plane numbers. <span class="justification">[VIII. 20]</span></p>
<p class="sub-clause">But <span data-ref="0385.g1:A">$A$</span> is square;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0385.g1:C">$C$</span> is also square.</p>

<p class="qed"><span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

A mere application of VIII. 20 to the particular case where one of the<br />
"similar plane numbers" is square.
