Then, by the proof of VIII. 20,

$$\alpha : \beta = m : p = n : q.$$

Now, *ex aequali*,

$$a : d = \alpha : \gamma, \qquad \text{[VII. 14]}$$

and, since $\alpha, \gamma$ are prime to one another,

$$a = r\alpha, \quad d = r\gamma, \quad \text{where } r \text{ is an integer.}$$

But $\alpha = mn$:
therefore $a = rmn$, and therefore $a$ is "solid."
Again, *ex aequali*,

$$c : b = \alpha : \gamma,$$

and therefore

$$c = s\alpha, \quad b = s\gamma, \quad \text{where } s \text{ is an integer.}$$

Thus $b = spq$, and $b$ is therefore "solid."
Now

$$\begin{aligned}
\alpha : \beta = a : c &= r\alpha : s\alpha \\
&= r : s.
\end{aligned} \qquad \text{[VII. 18]}$$

And, from above,

$$\alpha : \beta = m : p = n : q.$$

Therefore

$$r : s = m : p = n : q,$$

and hence $a, b$ are similar solid numbers.

PROPOSITION 22.
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

.. enunciation::

   If three numbers be in continued proportion, and the first be square, the third will also be square.

| Let :g:`$A$ <0385.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0385.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0385.g1:C>` be three numbers in continued proportion,
| and let :g:`$A$ <0385.g1:A>` the first be square;
| I say that :g:`$C$ <0385.g1:C>` the third is also square.

   | mean proportional number, :g:`$B$ <0385.g1:B>`,

   therefore :g:`$A$ <0385.g1:A>`, :g:`$C$ <0385.g1:C>` are similar plane numbers.

.. subclause::

   But :g:`$A$ <0385.g1:A>` is square;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$C$ <0385.g1:C>` is also square.

.. qed::

   :just:`Q. E. D.`

| A mere application of VIII. 20 to the particular case where one of the
