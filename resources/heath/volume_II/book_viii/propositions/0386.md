### PROPOSITION 24.

*If two numbers have to one another the ratio which a square number has to a square number, and the first be square, the second will also be square.*

For let the two numbers <span data-ref="0386.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0386.g1:B">$B$</span> have to one another the ratio which the square number <span data-ref="0386.g1:C">$C$</span> has to the square number <span data-ref="0386.g1:D">$D$</span>, and let <span data-ref="0386.g1:A">$A$</span> be square;
I say that <span data-ref="0386.g1:B">$B$</span> is also square.

![Proposition 24](0386.g1.svg)

For, since <span data-ref="0386.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0386.g1:D">$D$</span> are square,
<span data-ref="0386.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0386.g1:D">$D$</span> are similar plane numbers.
Therefore one mean proportional number falls between <span data-ref="0386.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0386.g1:D">$D$</span>. [VIII. 18]
And, as <span data-ref="0386.g1:C">$C$</span> is to <span data-ref="0386.g1:D">$D$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0386.g1:A">$A$</span> to <span data-ref="0386.g1:B">$B$</span>;
therefore one mean proportional number falls between <span data-ref="0386.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0386.g1:B">$B$</span> also. [VIII. 8]
And <span data-ref="0386.g1:A">$A$</span> is square;
therefore <span data-ref="0386.g1:B">$B$</span> is also square. [VIII. 22]
Q. E. D.

---

If $a : b = c^2 : d^2$, and $a$ is a square, then $b$ is also a square.
For $c^2, d^2$ have one mean proportional $cd$. [VIII. 18]
Therefore $a, b$, which are in the same ratio, have one mean proportional. [VIII. 8]
And, since $a$ is square, $b$ must also be a square. [VIII. 22]
