If $a, b$ are similar "plane numbers," let $c$ be the mean proportional between them. [VIII. 18]
Take $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$ the smallest numbers in the ratio of $a, c, b$. [VII. 33 or VIII. 2]
Then $\alpha, \gamma$ are squares. [VIII. 2, Por.]
Therefore $a, b$ are in the ratio of a square to a square.

### PROPOSITION 27.

*Similar solid numbers have to one another the ratio which a cube number has to a cube number.*

Let <span data-ref="0388.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0388.g1:B">$B$</span> be similar solid numbers;
I say that <span data-ref="0388.g1:A">$A$</span> has to <span data-ref="0388.g1:B">$B$</span> the ratio which a cube number has to a cube number.

![Proposition 27](0388.g1.svg)

For, since <span data-ref="0388.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0388.g1:B">$B$</span> are similar solid numbers,
therefore two mean proportional numbers fall between <span data-ref="0388.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0388.g1:B">$B$</span>. [VIII. 19]
Let <span data-ref="0388.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0388.g1:D">$D$</span> so fall,
and let <span data-ref="0388.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0388.g1:F">$F$</span>, <span data-ref="0388.g1:G">$G$</span>, <span data-ref="0388.g1:H">$H$</span>, the least numbers of those which have the same ratio with <span data-ref="0388.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0388.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0388.g1:D">$D$</span>, <span data-ref="0388.g1:B">$B$</span>, and equal with them in multitude, be taken; [VII. 33 or VIII. 2]
therefore the extremes of them <span data-ref="0388.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0388.g1:H">$H$</span> are cube. [VIII. 2, Por.]
And, as <span data-ref="0388.g1:E">$E$</span> is to <span data-ref="0388.g1:H">$H$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0388.g1:A">$A$</span> to <span data-ref="0388.g1:B">$B$</span>;
therefore <span data-ref="0388.g1:A">$A$</span> also has to <span data-ref="0388.g1:B">$B$</span> the ratio which a cube number has to a cube number.
Q. E. D.
