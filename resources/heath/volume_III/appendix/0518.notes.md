# Transcription Notes: Folio 0518 (Page 514)

## Summary & Folio Continuity
- **Folio Range**: Folio 0518 corresponds to Page 514 of Heath's *Euclid's Elements*, Volume III.
- **Section**: Appendix, Section I: "The Contents of the So-Called Book XIV. By Hypsicles."
- **Continuity**: Completes the proof of the Lemma ($BA^2 + AC^2 = 5 DE^2$, where $BA$ is the chord subtending two sides, $AC$ is the side of the pentagon, and $DE$ is the radius), then begins the proof of Proposition 2 comparing the circumscribed circles of the dodecahedron's pentagonal face and the icosahedron's triangular face.
- **Folio Transition**: The proof of Proposition 2 continues onto page 515 (Folio 0519).

## Textual / Lexical / Mathematical Observations
- **Mathematical Steps in Lemma**:
  - Diameter $BE = 2 ED \implies BE^2 = 4 ED^2$.
  - In right triangle $BAE$, $BE^2 = BA^2 + AE^2 \implies BA^2 + AE^2 + ED^2 = 5 ED^2$.
  - By XIII. 10, $AC^2 = DE^2 + EA^2$ (square on side of pentagon equals square on side of hexagon plus square on side of decagon).
  - Hence $BA^2 + AC^2 = 5 ED^2 = 5 r^2$.
- **Proposition 2 Construction**:
  - $DG$ is the side of the cube ($d_{\text{pentagon}}$).
  - $MN$ is the radius of the small circle through five vertices of the icosahedron ($AB^2 = 5 MN^2$).
  - $MO$ is the greater segment of $MN$ cut in extreme and mean ratio, hence the side of the decagon in the circle of radius $MN$.
  - Refers forward to the lemma on extreme and mean ratio on pp. 518–9.
