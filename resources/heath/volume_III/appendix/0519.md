[Prop. 3.] "*If there be an equilateral and equiangular pentagon and a circle circumscribed about it, and if a perpendicular be drawn from the centre to one side, then
30 times the rectangle contained by the side and the perpendicular is equal to the surface of the dodecahedron.*"

Let <span data-ref="0519.g1:< A B C D E >">$ABCDE$</span> be the pentagon, <span data-ref="0519.g1:F">$F$</span> the centre of the circle, <span data-ref="0519.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span> the perpendicular on a side <span data-ref="0519.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>.

I say that
$30 $ <span data-ref="0519.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> $\cdot $ <span data-ref="0519.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span> $= 12 (\text{area of pentagon})$.

Let <span data-ref="0519.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span>, <span data-ref="0519.g1:/ F D /">$FD$</span> be joined.

Then, since
<span data-ref="0519.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> $\cdot $ <span data-ref="0519.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span> $= 2 (\triangle $ <span data-ref="0519.g1:< C D F >">$CDF$</span>$)$,
$5 $ <span data-ref="0519.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> $\cdot $ <span data-ref="0519.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span> $= 10 (\triangle $ <span data-ref="0519.g1:< C D F >">$CDF$</span>$)$,
whence
$30 $ <span data-ref="0519.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> $\cdot $ <span data-ref="0519.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span> $= 12 (\text{area of pentagon})$.

Similarly we can prove that,

[Prop. 4] *If <span data-ref="0519.g2:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> be an equilateral triangle in a circle, <span data-ref="0519.g2:D">$D$</span> the centre, and <span data-ref="0519.g2:/ D E /">$DE$</span> perpendicular to <span data-ref="0519.g2:/ B C /">$BC$</span>,*
$30 $ <span data-ref="0519.g2:/ B C /">$BC$</span> $\cdot $ <span data-ref="0519.g2:/ D E /">$DE$</span> $= (\text{surface of icosahedron})$.

For
<span data-ref="0519.g2:/ D E /">$DE$</span> $\cdot $ <span data-ref="0519.g2:/ B C /">$BC$</span> $= 2 (\triangle $ <span data-ref="0519.g2:< D B C >">$DBC$</span>$)$;
therefore
$3 $ <span data-ref="0519.g2:/ D E /">$DE$</span> $\cdot $ <span data-ref="0519.g2:/ B C /">$BC$</span> $= 6 (\triangle $ <span data-ref="0519.g2:< D B C >">$DBC$</span>$) = 2 (\triangle $ <span data-ref="0519.g2:< A B C >">$ABC$</span>$)$,
whence
$30 $ <span data-ref="0519.g2:/ D E /">$DE$</span> $\cdot $ <span data-ref="0519.g2:/ B C /">$BC$</span> $= 20 (\triangle $ <span data-ref="0519.g2:< A B C >">$ABC$</span>$)$.

![Book XIV, Prop. 4](0519.g2.svg)

It follows that [Prop. 5]
$$\text{(surface of dodecahedron)} : \text{(surface of icosahedron)} = \text{(side of pentagon)} \cdot \text{(its perpendicular)} : \text{(side of triangle)} \cdot \text{(its perp.)}.$$
