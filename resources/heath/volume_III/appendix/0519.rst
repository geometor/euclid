[Prop. 3.] "*If there be an equilateral and equiangular pentagon and a circle circumscribed about it, and if a perpendicular be drawn from the centre to one side, then
30 times the rectangle contained by the side and the perpendicular is equal to the surface of the dodecahedron.*"

Let :g:`ABCDE <0519.g1:< A B C D E >>` be the pentagon, :g:`F <0519.g1:F>` the centre of the circle, :g:`FG <0519.g1:/ F G />` the perpendicular on a side :g:`CD <0519.g1:/ C D />`.

I say that
$30 $ :g:`CD <0519.g1:/ C D />` $\cdot $ :g:`FG <0519.g1:/ F G />` $= 12 (\text{area of pentagon})$.

Let :g:`CF <0519.g1:/ C F />`, :g:`FD <0519.g1:/ F D />` be joined.

Then, since
:g:`CD <0519.g1:/ C D />` $\cdot $ :g:`FG <0519.g1:/ F G />` $= 2 (\triangle $ :g:`CDF <0519.g1:< C D F >>` $)$,
$5 $ :g:`CD <0519.g1:/ C D />` $\cdot $ :g:`FG <0519.g1:/ F G />` $= 10 (\triangle $ :g:`CDF <0519.g1:< C D F >>` $)$,
whence
$30 $ :g:`CD <0519.g1:/ C D />` $\cdot $ :g:`FG <0519.g1:/ F G />` $= 12 (\text{area of pentagon})$.

   :alt: Book XIV, Prop. 3

Similarly we can prove that,

[Prop. 4] *If* :g:`ABC <0519.g2:< A B C >>` *be an equilateral triangle in a circle*, :g:`D <0519.g2:D>` *the centre, and* :g:`DE <0519.g2:/ D E />` *perpendicular to* :g:`BC <0519.g2:/ B C />`,
$30 $ :g:`BC <0519.g2:/ B C />` $\cdot $ :g:`DE <0519.g2:/ D E />` $= (\text{surface of icosahedron})$.

For
:g:`DE <0519.g2:/ D E />` $\cdot $ :g:`BC <0519.g2:/ B C />` $= 2 (\triangle $ :g:`DBC <0519.g2:< D B C >>` $)$;
therefore
$3 $ :g:`DE <0519.g2:/ D E />` $\cdot $ :g:`BC <0519.g2:/ B C />` $= 6 (\triangle $ :g:`DBC <0519.g2:< D B C >>` $) = 2 (\triangle $ :g:`ABC <0519.g2:< A B C >>` $)$,
whence
$30 $ :g:`DE <0519.g2:/ D E />` $\cdot $ :g:`BC <0519.g2:/ B C />` $= 20 (\triangle $ :g:`ABC <0519.g2:< A B C >>` $)$.

.. picture:: 0519.g2.svg
   :alt: Book XIV, Prop. 4

It follows that [Prop. 5]

$$\text{(surface of dodecahedron)} : \text{(surface of icosahedron)} = \text{(side of pentagon)} \cdot \text{(its perpendicular)} : \text{(side of triangle)} \cdot \text{(its perp.)}.$$
