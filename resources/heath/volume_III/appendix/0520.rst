Let :g:`ABC <0520.g1:( A B C )>` be the circle circumscribing the pentagon of the dodecahedron and the triangle of the icosahedron, and let :g:`CD <0520.g1:/ C D />` be the side of the triangle, :g:`AC <0520.g1:/ A C />` that of the pentagon.

Let :g:`E <0520.g1:E>` be the centre, and :g:`EF <0520.g1:/ E F />`, :g:`EG <0520.g1:/ E G />` perpendiculars to :g:`CD <0520.g1:/ C D />`, :g:`AC <0520.g1:/ A C />`.

Produce :g:`EG <0520.g1:/ E G />` to meet the circle in :g:`B <0520.g1:B>` and join :g:`BC <0520.g1:/ B C />`.

Set out :g:`H <0520.g1:H>` equal to the side of the cube inscribed in the same sphere.

I say that
$(\text{surface of dodecahedron}) : (\text{surface of icosahedron}) = $ :g:`H <0520.g1:H>` $: $ :g:`CD <0520.g1:/ C D />`.

.. picture:: 0520.g1.svg
   :alt: Book XIV, Prop. 6

For, since the sum of :g:`EB <0520.g1:/ E B />`, :g:`BC <0520.g1:/ B C />` is divided at :g:`B <0520.g1:B>` in extreme and mean ratio, and :g:`BE <0520.g1:/ B E />` is the greater segment, [XIII. 9]
and :g:`EG <0520.g1:/ E G />` $= \frac{1}{2}($ :g:`EB <0520.g1:/ E B />` $+ $ :g:`BC <0520.g1:/ B C />` $)$, [Prop. 1]
while :g:`EF <0520.g1:/ E F />` $= \frac{1}{2}$ :g:`BE <0520.g1:/ B E />`, [see p. 513 above]
therefore, if :g:`EG <0520.g1:/ E G />` is divided in extreme and mean ratio, the greater segment is equal to :g:`EF <0520.g1:/ E F />` [that is to say, since :g:`EB <0520.g1:/ E B />` is the greater segment of :g:`EB <0520.g1:/ E B />` $+ $ :g:`BC <0520.g1:/ B C />` divided in extreme and mean ratio, $\frac{1}{2}$ :g:`EB <0520.g1:/ E B />` is the greater segment of $\frac{1}{2}($ :g:`EB <0520.g1:/ E B />` $+ $ :g:`BC <0520.g1:/ B C />` $)$ similarly divided].

But, if :g:`H <0520.g1:H>` is also divided in extreme and mean ratio, the greater segment is equal to :g:`CA <0520.g1:/ C A />`. [XIII. 17, Por.]

Therefore
:g:`H <0520.g1:H>` $: $ :g:`CA <0520.g1:/ C A />` $= $ :g:`EG <0520.g1:/ E G />` $: $ :g:`EF <0520.g1:/ E F />`,
or
:g:`FE <0520.g1:/ F E />` $\cdot $ :g:`H <0520.g1:H>` $= $ :g:`CA <0520.g1:/ C A />` $\cdot $ :g:`EG <0520.g1:/ E G />`.

And, since
:g:`H <0520.g1:H>` $: $ :g:`CD <0520.g1:/ C D />` $= $ :g:`FE <0520.g1:/ F E />` $\cdot $ :g:`H <0520.g1:H>` $: $ :g:`FE <0520.g1:/ F E />` $\cdot $ :g:`CD <0520.g1:/ C D />`,
and
:g:`FE <0520.g1:/ F E />` $\cdot $ :g:`H <0520.g1:H>` $= $ :g:`CA <0520.g1:/ C A />` $\cdot $ :g:`EG <0520.g1:/ E G />`,
therefore
:g:`H <0520.g1:H>` $: $ :g:`CD <0520.g1:/ C D />` $= $ :g:`CA <0520.g1:/ C A />` $\cdot $ :g:`EG <0520.g1:/ E G />` $: $ :g:`FE <0520.g1:/ F E />` $\cdot $ :g:`CD <0520.g1:/ C D />` $= (\text{surface of dodecahedron}) : (\text{surf. of icos.})$. [Prop. 5]

*Another proof of the same theorem.*

*Preliminary.*

Let :g:`ABC <0520.g2:( A B C )>` be a circle and :g:`AB <0520.g2:/ A B />`, :g:`AC <0520.g2:/ A C />` sides of an inscribed regular pentagon. Join :g:`BC <0520.g2:/ B C />`; take :g:`D <0520.g2:D>` the centre of the circle, join :g:`AD <0520.g2:/ A D />` and produce it to meet the circle at :g:`E <0520.g2:E>`. Join :g:`BD <0520.g2:/ B D />`.

Let :g:`DF <0520.g2:/ D F />` be made equal to $\frac{1}{2}$ :g:`AD <0520.g2:/ A D />`, and :g:`CH <0520.g2:/ C H />` equal to $\frac{1}{3}$ :g:`CG <0520.g2:/ C G />`.

I say that
$\text{rect. }$ :g:`AF <0520.g2:/ A F />`, :g:`BH <0520.g2:/ B H />` $= (\text{area of pentagon})$.

.. picture:: 0520.g2.svg
   :alt: Book XIV, Prop. 6 Alternative Proof Preliminary

For, since :g:`AD <0520.g2:/ A D />` $= 2 $ :g:`DF <0520.g2:/ D F />`,
:g:`AF <0520.g2:/ A F />` $= \frac{3}{2} $ :g:`AD <0520.g2:/ A D />`.

And, since :g:`GC <0520.g2:/ G C />` $= 3 $ :g:`HC <0520.g2:/ H C />`,
:g:`GC <0520.g2:/ G C />` $= \frac{3}{2} $ :g:`GH <0520.g2:/ G H />`.

Therefore
:g:`FA <0520.g2:/ F A />` $: $ :g:`AD <0520.g2:/ A D />` $= $ :g:`CG <0520.g2:/ C G />` $: $ :g:`GH <0520.g2:/ G H />`,
so that
:g:`AF <0520.g2:/ A F />` $\cdot $ :g:`GH <0520.g2:/ G H />` $= $ :g:`AD <0520.g2:/ A D />` $\cdot $ :g:`CG <0520.g2:/ C G />` $= $ :g:`AD <0520.g2:/ A D />` $\cdot $ :g:`BG <0520.g2:/ B G />` $= 2 (\triangle $ :g:`ABD <0520.g2:< A B D >>` $)$.
