## Textual & Mathematical Content
- Opens Heath's substantial historical and mathematical introduction to Book X of Euclid's *Elements*.
- Cites the first scholium on Book X regarding Pythagorean discovery of incommensurability through investigation of numbers.
- Distinguishes commensurable/incommensurable (which the Pythagoreans regarded as *natural* kinds, φύσει) from rational/irrational (which rested on convention/assumption, θέσει: ῥητά, ἄλογα, ὡς πρός τι).
- Quotes the famous Pythagorean legend of the shipwreck of the disciple who disclosed irrationality, with its Neoplatonic allegorical interpretation ("carried away into the sea of becoming").
- Connects the crisis of irrationals to the collapse of the early Pythagorean numerical theory of proportion and Tannery's thesis regarding the recasting of geometry (which culminated in Eudoxus' general theory in Book V).
- Introduces Theodorus of Cyrene's investigations recorded in Plato's *Theaetetus* (147 D).
- Bottom sentence carries over to Folio 0006: "...proving that the square roots of / [p. 2] three square feet and five square feet...".

## Greek Lexical Verification
- `ῥητά` (expressible / rational)
- `ἄλογα` (irrational / without ratio)
- `ὡς πρός τι` (relative / in relation to something)
- `φύσει` (by nature)
- `θέσει` (by convention / hypothesis)
- `δυνάμεις` (powers / square roots / sides whose squares are given)
