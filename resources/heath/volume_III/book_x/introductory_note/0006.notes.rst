Mathematical Content & Proof Analysis
-------------------------------------

- Detailed historical analysis of Theodorus' proof of incommensurability of $\sqrt{3}, \sqrt{5}, \dots, \sqrt{17}$ and Pythagoras' side- and diagonal-numbers (Theon of Smyrna, Eucl. II. 9, 10).
- Reproduction of the traditional Pythagorean proof of the incommensurability of $\sqrt{2}$ with unity cited by Aristotle (*Prior Analytics* I. 23, 41a26—27) and interpolated as Euclid X. 117:

  - Assumes diagonal $AC$ is commensurable with side $AB$, with irreducible ratio $\alpha : \beta$.
  - Shows that $\alpha^2 = 2\beta^2 \implies \alpha$ is even.
  - Since $\alpha : \beta$ is in lowest terms, $\beta$ must be odd.
  - Substituting $\alpha = 2\gamma$ gives $\beta^2 = 2\gamma^2 \implies \beta$ is even, yielding contradiction.
