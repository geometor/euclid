Historical & Textual Analysis
-----------------------------

- Documents the role of Theaetetus in establishing Euclid X. 9 (squares not having to one another the ratio of a square number to a square number have sides incommensurable in length).
- Analyzes Plato's dialogue *Theaetetus* (147 D sqq.):

  - Classification of numbers into equilateral/square (ἴσον ἰσάκις) and oblong.
  - Definition of lines commensurate in length (μῆκος) versus commensurable in square only (δυνάμεις).

- Analysis of the Arabic commentary on Book X discovered by Franz Woepcke (1856):

  - Attribution by Woepcke to Vettius Valens, corrected by Heiberg and Heinrich Suter to Pappus of Alexandria.
  - Pappus' account of Theaetetus classifying irrational lines according to the classical means:

    - Medial $\leftrightarrow$ Geometric mean
    - Binomial $\leftrightarrow$ Arithmetic mean
    - Apotome $\leftrightarrow$ Harmonic mean

  - Cites Eudemus the Peripatetic and Apollonius of Perga's extensions.

- Bottom text continues directly onto Folio 0008: "As for Euclid, he set himself to give rigorous rules, which he established, / [p. 4] relative to commensurability and incommensurability in general...".

Greek Lexical Observations
--------------------------

- ``δυνάμεις`` (square roots, powers, French *puissances*)
- ``ἴσον ἰσάκις`` (equal multiplied by equal, square number)
- ``μῆκος`` (length)
