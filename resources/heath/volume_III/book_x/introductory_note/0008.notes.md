## Textual & Mathematical Observations
- Continuation of Pappus' summary on Euclid's systematization of irrationals and infinite orders (X. 115).
- Explains the mathematical rationale linking the three means to Euclid's irrational classes:
  - Geometric mean $\sqrt{xy}$ $\longleftrightarrow$ Medial straight line.
  - Arithmetic mean $\frac{1}{2}(x+y)$ $\longleftrightarrow$ Binomial straight line.
  - Harmonic mean $\frac{2xy}{x+y} = \frac{2xy}{x^2 - y^2}\cdot(x - y)$ $\longleftrightarrow$ Apotome straight line (rational rectangle divided by binomial gives apotome, Eucl. X. 111—114).
- Scholarly critique of Democritus' lost work on irrationals:
  - Title recorded by Diogenes Laertius: *περὶ ἀλόγων γραμμῶν καὶ ναστῶν βʹ* ("two Books on irrational straight lines and solids/atoms").
  - Hultsch's emendation to *κλαστῶν* ("broken lines") rejected by Heath, defending the transmitted text and Democritean terminology (*ναστόν* vs. *κενόν*, solid vs. void, as attested in Aristotle).
- Quotation from H. G. Zeuthen (*Geschichte der Mathematik im Altertum und Mittelalter*, p. 56) on Greek geometric algebra: representing incommensurable quadratic roots by exact geometric straight lines rather than numerical approximations.

## Greek Lexical Verification
- `περὶ ἀλόγων γραμμῶν καὶ ναστῶν βʹ` (On irrational lines and solids, 2 books)
- `περὶ ἀλόγων γραμμῶν κλαστῶν` (Hultsch's conjectured reading)
- `κλαστός` (broken / bent)
- `ναστόν` (solid / compact / filled space)
- `κενόν` (void / empty space)
