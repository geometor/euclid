Transcription Notes: Folio 0013 (Page 9)
========================================

Summary & Folio Continuity
--------------------------

- **Section**: Book X Introductory Note (Part 2)
- **Preceding Context**: Folio 0012 (Page 8) surveyed early algebraists and introduced Simon Stevin's *Appendice des incommensurables grandeurs*.
- **Current Content**:

  - Stevin's famous French quotation designating Book X as "la croix des mathématiciens" (the cross of mathematicians).
  - Geometrical purpose of Book X: its indispensable application in Book XIII to the regular polyhedra (regular pentagon side = minor, icosahedron side = minor, dodecahedron side = apotome, extreme and mean ratio parts = apotomes).
  - Applications of the positive irrationals (binomials) by Pappus of Alexandria (*Collection*, Book IV, pp. 178, 182) with geometric figures and radical calculations.

- **Succeeding Context**: Folio 0014 (Page 10) completes the Pappus enunciation and moves to Proclus's comments on Apollonius before presenting Definitions 1–4.

Textual & Mathematical Observations
-----------------------------------

- **French Text**: Stevin quote printed in original French with 17th-century orthography (*dixiesme*, *matière*, *n'aperçoivent*).
- **Geometric Applications**: Heath clarifies why Euclid needed both additive (binomial) and subtractive (apotome) irrationals: the apotomes govern regular solids in Book XIII, while Pappus utilized both in circle tangent constructions.
- **Formulas**: Calculation of $CE = \sqrt{\frac{5+\sqrt{13}}{2}} - \sqrt{\frac{5-\sqrt{13}}{2}}$ for unit radius.
