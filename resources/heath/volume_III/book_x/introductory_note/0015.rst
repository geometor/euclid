DEFINITION 1.
-------------

.. container:: center

   Σύμμετρα μεγέθη λέγεται τὰ τῷ αὐτῷ μέτρῳ μετρούμενα, ἀσύμμετρα δέ, ὧν μηδὲν ἐνδέχεται κοινὸν μέτρον γενέσθαι.

DEFINITION 2.
-------------

.. container:: center

   Εὐθεῖαι :term:`δυνάμει <in-square>` σύμμετροί εἰσιν, ὅταν τὰ ἀπ᾿ αὐτῶν τετράγωνα τῷ αὐτῷ χωρίῳ μετρῆται, ἀσύμμετροι δέ, ὅταν τοῖς ἀπ᾿ αὐτῶν τετραγώνοις μηδὲν ἐνδέχεται χωρίον κοινὸν μέτρον γενέσθαι.

*Commensurable in square* is in the Greek :term:`δυνάμει <in-square>` :term:`σύμμετρος <commensurable>`. In earlier translations (e.g. Williamson's) :term:`δυνάμει <in-square>` has been translated "in power," but, as the particular *power* represented by :term:`δύναμις <dynamis>` in Greek geometry is *square*, I have thought it best to use the latter word throughout. It will be observed that Euclid's expression :term:`*commensurable in square only* <commensurable-in-square-only>` (used in Def. 3 and constantly) corresponds to what :person:`Plato <plato>` makes :person:`Theaetetus <theaetetus>` call a *square root* (:term:`δύναμις <dynamis>`) in the sense of a *surd*. If $a$ is any straight line, $a$ and $a\sqrt{m}$, or $a\sqrt{m}$ and $a\sqrt{n}$ (where $m, n$ are integers or arithmetical fractions in their lowest terms, proper or improper, but not square) are :term:`*commensurable in square only* <commensurable-in-square-only>`. Of course (as explained in the Porism to x. 10) all straight lines :term:`*commensurable in length* <commensurable-in-length>` (:term:`μήκει <in-length>`), in Euclid's phrase, are commensurable in square also; but not all straight lines which are *commensurable in square* are :term:`*commensurable in length* <commensurable-in-length>` as well. On the other hand, straight lines *incommensurable in square* are necessarily :term:`*incommensurable in length* <incommensurable-in-length>` also; but not all straight lines which are :term:`*incommensurable in length* <incommensurable-in-length>` are incommensurable in square. In fact, straight lines which are :term:`*commensurable in square only* <commensurable-in-square-only>` are incommensurable in length, but obviously not incommensurable in square.

DEFINITION 3.
-------------

.. container:: center

   Τούτων ὑποκειμένων δείκνυται, ὅτι τῇ προτεθείσῃ εὐθείᾳ ὑπάρχουσιν εὐθεῖαι πλήθει ἄπειροι σύμμετροί τε καὶ ἀσύμμετροι αἱ μὲν :term:`μήκει <in-length>` μόνον, αἱ δὲ καὶ :term:`δυνάμει <in-square>`. καλείσθω οὖν ἡ μὲν προτεθεῖσα :term:`εὐθεῖα <straight-line>` ῥητή, καὶ αἱ ταύτῃ σύμμετροι εἴτε :term:`μήκει <in-length>` καὶ :term:`δυνάμει <in-square>` εἴτε :term:`δυνάμει <in-square>` μόνον ῥηταί, αἱ δὲ ταύτῃ ἀσύμμετροι ἄλογοι καλείσθωσαν.

The first sentence of the definition is decidedly elliptical. It should, strictly speaking, assert that "with a given straight line there are an infinite number of straight lines which are (1) commensurable either ($a$) in square only or ($b$) in square and in length also, and (2) incommensurable, either ($a$) in length only or ($b$) in length and in square also."

The relativity of the terms *rational* and *irrational* is well brought out in this definition. We may set out *any straight line* and call it rational, and it is then with reference to this assumed rational straight line that others are called *rational* or *irrational*.

We should carefully note that the signification of *rational* in Euclid is wider than in our terminology. With him, not only is a straight line commensurable in *length* with a rational straight line rational, but a straight line is rational which is commensurable with a rational straight line *in square only*.
