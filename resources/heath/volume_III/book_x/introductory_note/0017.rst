αἱ δυνάμεναι αὐτά are the straight lines the squares on which are equal to the areas, in accordance with the regular meaning of :term:`δύνασθαι <dynasthai>`. It is scarcely possible, in a book written in geometrical language, to translate :term:`δυναμένη <dynamene>` as the square root (of an area) and :term:`δύνασθαι <dynasthai>` as to be the square root (of an area), although I can use the term "square root" when in my notes I am using an algebraical expression to represent an area; I shall therefore hereafter use the word "side" for :term:`δυναμένη <dynamene>` and "to be the side of" for :term:`δύνασθαι <dynasthai>`, so that "side" will in such expressions be a short way of expressing the "*side of a square equal to (an area)*." In this particular passage it is not quite practicable to use the words "side of" or "straight line the square on which is equal to," for these expressions occur just afterwards for two alternatives which the word :term:`δυναμένη <dynamene>` covers. I have therefore exceptionally translated "the straight lines which produce them" (i.e. if squares are described upon them as sides).

αἱ ἴσα αὐτοῖς τετράγωνα ἀναγράφουσαι, literally "the (straight lines) which describe squares equal to them": a peculiar use of the active of ἀναγράφειν, the meaning being of course "the straight lines on which are described the squares" which are equal to the rectilineal figures.
