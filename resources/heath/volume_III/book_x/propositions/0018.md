# BOOK X. PROPOSITIONS.

### PROPOSITION 1.

<p class="enunciation">Two unequal magnitudes being set out, if from the greater<br />
there be subtracted a magnitude greater than its half, and from<br />
that which is left a magnitude greater than its half, and if<br />
this process be repeated continually, there will be left some<br />
magnitude which will be less than the lesser magnitude set out.</p>

Let <span data-ref="0018.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0018.g1:C">$C$</span> be two unequal magnitudes of which <span data-ref="0018.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is<br />
the greater :<br />
I say that, if from <span data-ref="0018.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> there be<br />
subtracted a magnitude greater<br />
than its half, and from that which<br />
is left a magnitude greater than its half, and if this process be<br />
repeated continually, there will be left some magnitude which<br />
will be less than the magnitude <span data-ref="0018.g1:C">$C$</span>.

![Book X, Proposition 1](0018.g1.svg)

For <span data-ref="0018.g1:C">$C$</span> if multiplied will sometime be greater than <span data-ref="0018.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>.<br />
<span class="justification">[cf. v. Def. 4]</span>

Let it be multiplied, and let <span data-ref="0018.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> be a multiple of <span data-ref="0018.g1:C">$C$</span>, and<br />
greater than <span data-ref="0018.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>;<br />
let <span data-ref="0018.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> be divided into the parts <span data-ref="0018.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span>, <span data-ref="0018.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span>, <span data-ref="0018.g1:/ G E /">$GE$</span> equal to <span data-ref="0018.g1:C">$C$</span>,<br />
from <span data-ref="0018.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> let there be subtracted <span data-ref="0018.g1:/ B H /">$BH$</span> greater than its half,<br />
and, from <span data-ref="0018.g1:/ A H /">$AH$</span>, <span data-ref="0018.g1:/ H K /">$HK$</span> greater than its half,<br />
and let this process be repeated continually until the divisions<br />
in <span data-ref="0018.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> are equal in multitude with the divisions in <span data-ref="0018.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>.

Let, then, <span data-ref="0018.g1:/ A K /">$AK$</span>, <span data-ref="0018.g1:/ K H /">$KH$</span>, <span data-ref="0018.g1:/ H B /">$HB$</span> be divisions which are equal in<br />
multitude with <span data-ref="0018.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span>, <span data-ref="0018.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span>, <span data-ref="0018.g1:/ G E /">$GE$</span>.

<p class="sub-clause">Now, since <span data-ref="0018.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> is greater than <span data-ref="0018.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>,<br />
and from <span data-ref="0018.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> there has been subtracted <span data-ref="0018.g1:/ E G /">$EG$</span> less than its<br />
half,<br />
and, from <span data-ref="0018.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0018.g1:/ B H /">$BH$</span> greater than its half,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the remainder <span data-ref="0018.g1:/ G D /">$GD$</span> is greater than the remainder <span data-ref="0018.g1:/ H A /">$HA$</span>.</p>
