in XII. 2 (:cite:`*On the Sphere and Cylinder*, I. 6 <archimedes:sphere-and-cylinder>`). As I have before suggested (:cite:`*The Works of Archimedes*, p. xlviii <heath:works-of-archimedes>`), the apparent difficulty caused by the mention of *two* lemmas in connexion with the theorem of Eucl. XII. 2 may be explained by reference to the proof of X. 1. Euclid there takes the lesser magnitude and says that it is possible, by multiplying it, to make it some time exceed the greater, and this statement he clearly bases on the 4th definition of Book V., to the effect that "magnitudes are said to bear a ratio to one another which can, if multiplied, exceed one another." Since then the smaller magnitude in X. 1 may be regarded as the difference between some two unequal magnitudes, it is clear that the lemma stated by :person:`Archimedes <archimedes>` is in substance used to prove the lemma in X. 1, which appears to play so much larger a part in the investigations of quadrature and cubature which have come down to us.

Besides being employed in Eucl. X. 1, the "Axiom of :person:`Archimedes <archimedes>`" appears in :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>`, who also practically quotes the result of X. 1 itself. Thus he says, :cite:`*Physics* VIII. 10, 266 b 2 <aristotle:phys:266.b.2>`, "By continually adding to a finite (magnitude) I shall exceed any definite (magnitude), and similarly by 'continually subtracting from it I shall arrive at something less than it," and *ibid.* III. 7, 207 b 10 "For bisections of a magnitude are endless." It is thus somewhat misleading to use the term ":person:`Archimedes <archimedes>`' Axiom" for the "lemma" quoted by him, since he makes no claim to be the discoverer of it, and it was obviously much earlier.

:person:`Stolz <stolz>` (see :person:`G. Vitali <vitali>` in :cite:`*Questioni riguardanti le matematiche elementari* <vitali:questioni>`, I., pp. 129—30) showed how to prove the so-called Axiom or Postulate of :person:`Archimedes <archimedes>` by means of the Postulate of :person:`Dedekind <dedekind>`, thus. Suppose the two magnitudes to be straight lines. It is required to prove that, *given two straight lines, there always exists a multiple of the smaller which is greater than the other.*

Let the straight lines be so placed that they have a common extremity and the smaller lies along the other on the same side of the common extremity.

If :g:`$AC$ <0020.g1:/ A C />` be the greater and :g:`$AB$ <0020.g1:/ A B />` the smaller, we have to prove that there exists an integral number $n$ such that $n \cdot$ :g:`$AB$ <0020.g1:/ A B />` $>$ :g:`$AC$ <0020.g1:/ A C />`.

Suppose that this is not true but that there are some points, like :g:`$B$ <0020.g1:B>`, not coincident with the extremity :g:`$A$ <0020.g1:A>`, and such that, $n$ being any integer however great, $n \cdot$ :g:`$AB$ <0020.g1:/ A B />` $<$ :g:`$AC$ <0020.g1:/ A C />`; and we have to prove that this assumption leads to an absurdity.

.. picture:: 0020.g1.svg
   :alt: Dedekind's Postulate Diagram

The points of :g:`$AC$ <0020.g1:/ A C />` may be regarded as distributed into two "parts," namely

(1) points :g:`$H$ <0020.g1:H>` for which there exists no integer $n$ such that $n \cdot$ :g:`$AH$ <0020.g1:/ A H />` $>$ :g:`$AC$ <0020.g1:/ A C />`,
(2) points :g:`$K$ <0020.g1:K>` for which an integer $n$ does exist such that $n \cdot$ :g:`$AK$ <0020.g1:/ A K />` $>$ :g:`$AC$ <0020.g1:/ A C />`.

This division into parts satisfies the conditions for the application of :person:`Dedekind <dedekind>`'s Postulate, and therefore there exists a point :g:`$M$ <0020.g1:M>` such that the points of :g:`$AM$ <0020.g1:/ A M />` belong to the first part and those of :g:`$MC$ <0020.g1:/ M C />` to the second part.

Take now a point :g:`$Y$ <0020.g1:Y>` on :g:`$MC$ <0020.g1:/ M C />` such that :g:`$MY$ <0020.g1:/ M Y />` $<$ :g:`$AM$ <0020.g1:/ A M />`. The middle point (:g:`$X$ <0020.g1:X>`) of :g:`$AY$ <0020.g1:/ A Y />` will fall between :g:`$A$ <0020.g1:A>` and :g:`$M$ <0020.g1:M>` and will therefore belong to the first part; but, since there exists an integer $n$ such that $n \cdot$ :g:`$AY$ <0020.g1:/ A Y />` $>$ :g:`$AC$ <0020.g1:/ A C />`, it follows that $2n \cdot$ :g:`$AX$ <0020.g1:/ A X />` $>$ :g:`$AC$ <0020.g1:/ A C />`: which is contrary to the hypothesis.
