### PROPOSITION 2.

<p class="enunciation">If, when the less of two unequal magnitudes is continually<br />
subtracted in turn from the greater, that which is left never<br />
measures the one before it, the magnitudes will be incom-<br />
mensurable.</p>

For, there being two unequal magnitudes <span data-ref="0021.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0021.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, and<br />
<span data-ref="0021.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> being the less, when the less is continually subtracted<br />
in turn from the greater, let that which is left over never<br />
measure the one before it;<br />
I say that the magnitudes <span data-ref="0021.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0021.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> are incommensurable.

![Book X, Proposition 2](0021.g1.svg)

For, if they are commensurable, some magnitude will<br />
measure them.

Let a magnitude measure them, if possible, and let it be <span data-ref="0021.g1:E">$E$</span>;<br />
let <span data-ref="0021.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, measuring <span data-ref="0021.g1:/ F D /">$FD$</span>, leave <span data-ref="0021.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span> less than itself,<br />
let <span data-ref="0021.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span> measuring <span data-ref="0021.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span>, leave <span data-ref="0021.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span> less than itself,<br />
and let this process be repeated continually, until there is left<br />
some magnitude which is less than <span data-ref="0021.g1:E">$E$</span>.

Suppose this done, and let there be left <span data-ref="0021.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span> less than <span data-ref="0021.g1:E">$E$</span>.

<p class="sub-clause">Then, since <span data-ref="0021.g1:E">$E$</span> measures <span data-ref="0021.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>,<br />
while <span data-ref="0021.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> measures <span data-ref="0021.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0021.g1:E">$E$</span> will also measure <span data-ref="0021.g1:/ F D /">$FD$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">But it measures the whole <span data-ref="0021.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> also;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore it will also measure the remainder <span data-ref="0021.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">But <span data-ref="0021.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span> measures <span data-ref="0021.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span>;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0021.g1:E">$E$</span> also measures <span data-ref="0021.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">But it measures the whole <span data-ref="0021.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> also;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore it will also measure the remainder <span data-ref="0021.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, the greater<br />
the less :</p>
<p class="assertion">which is impossible.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore no magnitude will measure the magnitudes <span data-ref="0021.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>,<br />
<span data-ref="0021.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>;<br />
therefore the magnitudes <span data-ref="0021.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0021.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> are incommensurable. <span class="justification">[x. Def. 1]</span></p>

<p class="qed">Therefore etc.<span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>
