:title: Book X: Proposition 4 (cont.)

| Now let their greatest common measure be taken, and let
| it be :g:`$E$ <0026.g1:E>`. :just:`[X. 3] <X.3>`

.. subclause::

   | Since then :g:`$E$ <0026.g1:E>` measures :g:`$D$ <0026.g1:D>`,
   | while :g:`$D$ <0026.g1:D>` measures :g:`$A$ <0026.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0026.g1:B>`,

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$E$ <0026.g1:E>` will also measure :g:`$A$ <0026.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0026.g1:B>`.

.. subclause::

   But it measures :g:`$C$ <0026.g1:C>` also ;

.. assertion::

   | therefore :g:`$E$ <0026.g1:E>` measures :g:`$A$ <0026.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0026.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0026.g1:C>` ;
   | therefore :g:`$E$ <0026.g1:E>` is a common measure of :g:`$A$ <0026.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0026.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0026.g1:C>`.

I say next that it is also the greatest.

| For, if possible, let there be some magnitude :g:`$F$ <0026.g1:F>` greater than
| :g:`$E$ <0026.g1:E>`, and let it measure :g:`$A$ <0026.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0026.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0026.g1:C>`.

.. subclause::

   | Now, since :g:`$F$ <0026.g1:F>` measures :g:`$A$ <0026.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0026.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0026.g1:C>`,
   | it will also measure :g:`$A$ <0026.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0026.g1:B>`,
   | and will measure the greatest common measure of :g:`$A$ <0026.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0026.g1:B>`. :just:`[X. 3, Por.]`

.. subclause::

   But the greatest common measure of :g:`$A$ <0026.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0026.g1:B>` is :g:`$D$ <0026.g1:D>` ;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$F$ <0026.g1:F>` measures :g:`$D$ <0026.g1:D>`.

.. subclause::

   But it measures :g:`$C$ <0026.g1:C>` also ;

.. assertion::

   | therefore :g:`$F$ <0026.g1:F>` measures :g:`$C$ <0026.g1:C>`, :g:`$D$ <0026.g1:D>` ;
   | therefore :g:`$F$ <0026.g1:F>` will also measure the greatest common measure
   | of :g:`$C$ <0026.g1:C>`, :g:`$D$ <0026.g1:D>`. :just:`[X. 3, Por.]`

.. subclause::

   But that is :g:`$E$ <0026.g1:E>` ;

.. assertion::

   | therefore :g:`$F$ <0026.g1:F>` will measure :g:`$E$ <0026.g1:E>`, the greater the less :
   | which is impossible.

.. assertion::

   | Therefore no magnitude greater than the magnitude :g:`$E$ <0026.g1:E>`
   | will measure :g:`$A$ <0026.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0026.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0026.g1:C>` ;
   | therefore :g:`$E$ <0026.g1:E>` is the greatest common measure of :g:`$A$ <0026.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0026.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0026.g1:C>` if :g:`$D$ <0026.g1:D>`
   | do not measure :g:`$C$ <0026.g1:C>`,
   | and, if it measure it, :g:`$D$ <0026.g1:D>` is itself the greatest common measure.

.. assertion::

   | Therefore the greatest common measure of the three given
   | commensurable magnitudes has been found.

.. assertion::

   | PORISM. From this it is manifest that, if a magnitude
   | measure three magnitudes, it will also measure their greatest
   | common measure.

.. qed::

   | Similarly too, with more magnitudes, the greatest common
   | measure can be found, and the porism can be extended. :just:`Q. E. D.`
