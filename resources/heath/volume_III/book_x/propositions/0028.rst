:title: Book X: Proposition 4 (commentary), Proposition 5

| This proposition again corresponds exactly to VII. 3 for numbers. As
| there Euclid thinks it necessary to prove that, $a, b, c$ not being prime to one
| another, $d$ and $c$ are also not prime to one another, so here he thinks it
| necessary to prove that $d, c$ are commensurable, as they must be since any
| common measure of $a, b$ must be a measure of their greatest common
| measure $d$ (X. 3, Por.).

| The argument in the proof that $e$, the greatest common measure of $d, c$, is
| the greatest common measure of $a, b, c$, is the same as that in VII. 3 and X. 3.

| The Porism contains the extension of the process to the case of four
| or more magnitudes, corresponding to Heron's remark with regard to the
| similar extension of VII. 3 to the case of four or more *numbers*.

----

PROPOSITION 5.
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

.. enunciation::

   Commensurable magnitudes have to one another the ratio which a number has to a number.

| Let :g:`$A$ <0028.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0028.g1:B>` be commensurable magnitudes;
| I say that :g:`$A$ <0028.g1:A>` has to :g:`$B$ <0028.g1:B>` the ratio which a number has to a
| number.

| For, since :g:`$A$ <0028.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0028.g1:B>` are commensurable, some magnitude will
| measure them.
| Let it measure them, and let it be :g:`$C$ <0028.g1:C>`.

.. picture:: 0028.g1.svg
   :alt: Proposition 5 diagram

| And, as many times as :g:`$C$ <0028.g1:C>` measures :g:`$A$ <0028.g1:A>`, so many units let
| there be in :g:`$D$ <0028.g1:D>`;
| and, as many times as :g:`$C$ <0028.g1:C>` measures :g:`$B$ <0028.g1:B>`, so many units let there
| be in :g:`$E$ <0028.g1:E>`.

.. subclause::

   | Since then :g:`$C$ <0028.g1:C>` measures :g:`$A$ <0028.g1:A>` according to the units in :g:`$D$ <0028.g1:D>`,
   | while the unit also measures :g:`$D$ <0028.g1:D>` according to the units in it,

.. assertion::

   | therefore the unit measures the number :g:`$D$ <0028.g1:D>` the same number
   | of times as the magnitude :g:`$C$ <0028.g1:C>` measures :g:`$A$ <0028.g1:A>`;

.. assertion:: [VII. Def. 20] <VII.def.20>

   therefore, as :g:`$C$ <0028.g1:C>` is to :g:`$A$ <0028.g1:A>`, so is the unit to :g:`$D$ <0028.g1:D>`;

.. assertion::

   | therefore, inversely, as :g:`$A$ <0028.g1:A>` is to :g:`$C$ <0028.g1:C>`, so is :g:`$D$ <0028.g1:D>` to the unit.
   | :just:`[cf. V. 7, Por.]`

   | while the unit also measures :g:`$E$ <0028.g1:E>` according to the units in it,
