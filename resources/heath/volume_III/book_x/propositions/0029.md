measures <span data-ref="0028.g1:B">$B$</span>;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore, as <span data-ref="0028.g1:C">$C$</span> is to <span data-ref="0028.g1:B">$B$</span>, so is the unit to <span data-ref="0028.g1:E">$E$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">But it was also proved that,<br />

<p class="assertion">therefore, <em>ex aequali</em>,<br />
as <span data-ref="0028.g1:A">$A$</span> is to <span data-ref="0028.g1:B">$B$</span>, so is the number <span data-ref="0028.g1:D">$D$</span> to <span data-ref="0028.g1:E">$E$</span>. <span class="justification">[V. 22]</span></p>

<p class="qed">Therefore the commensurable magnitudes <span data-ref="0028.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0028.g1:B">$B$</span> have to<br />
one another the ratio which the number <span data-ref="0028.g1:D">$D$</span> has to the number <span data-ref="0028.g1:E">$E$</span>.<span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

The argument is as follows. If $a, b$ be commensurable magnitudes, they<br />
have some common measure $c$, and
$$a = mc,$$
$$b = nc,$$
where $m, n$ are integers.

It follows that
$$c : a = 1 : m \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(1),$$
or, inversely,
$$a : c = m : 1;$$
and also that
$$c : b = 1 : n,$$
so that, _ex aequali_,
$$a : b = m : n.$$

It will be observed that, in stating the proportion (1), Euclid is merely<br />
expressing the fact that $a$ is the same multiple of $c$ that $m$ is of $1$. In other<br />
words, he rests the statement on the definition of proportion in VII. Def. 20.<br />
This, however, is applicable only to four *numbers*, and $c, a$ are not numbers but<br />
*magnitudes*. Hence the statement of the proportion is not legitimate unless<br />
it is proved that it is true in the sense of V. Def. 5 with regard to magnitudes<br />
in general, the numbers $1, m$ being *magnitudes*. Similarly with regard to the<br />
other proportions in the proposition.

There is, therefore, a hiatus. Euclid ought to have proved that magnitudes<br />
which are proportional in the sense of VII. Def. 20 are also proportional in the<br />
sense of V. Def. 5, or that the proportion of numbers is included in the<br />
proportion of magnitudes as a particular case. Simson has proved this in his<br />
Proposition C inserted in Book V. (see Vol. II. pp. 126—8). The portion of<br />
that proposition which is required here is the proof that,<br />
if
$$
\left.\begin{aligned}
a &= mb \\
c &= md
\end{aligned}\right\},
$$
then
$$a : b = c : d\text{, in the sense of V. Def. 5.}$$

Take any equimultiples $pa, pc$ of $a, c$ and any equimultiples $qb, qd$ of $b, d$.

Now
$$
\left.\begin{aligned}
pa &= pmb \\
pc &= pmd
\end{aligned}\right\}.
$$

But, according as $pmb \gtreqless qb$, $pmd \gtreqless qd$.

Therefore, according as $pa \gtreqless qb$, $pa \gtreqless qd$.[^25_1]

And $pa, pc$ are any equimultiples of $a, c$, and $qb, qd$ any equimultiples<br />
of $b, d$.

Therefore
$$a : b = c : d. \qquad \text{[V. Def. 5.]}$$

---

[^25_1]: In the printed text, the second relation is given as $pa \gtreqless qd$, an obvious misprint for $pc \gtreqless qd$, since $pc = pmd$.
