PROPOSITION 6.
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

.. enunciation::

   If two magnitudes have to one another the ratio which a number has to a number, the magnitudes will be commensurable.

| For let the two magnitudes :g:`$A$ <0030.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0030.g1:B>` have to one another the
| ratio which the number :g:`$D$ <0030.g1:D>` has to the number :g:`$E$ <0030.g1:E>`;
| :line:`5` I say that the magnitudes :g:`$A$ <0030.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0030.g1:B>` are commensurable.

.. picture:: 0030.g1.svg
   :alt: Proposition 6 diagram

| For let :g:`$A$ <0030.g1:A>` be divided into as many equal parts as there
| are units in :g:`$D$ <0030.g1:D>`,
| and let :g:`$C$ <0030.g1:C>` be equal to one of them;
| and let :g:`$F$ <0030.g1:F>` be made up of as many magnitudes equal to :g:`$C$ <0030.g1:C>` as
| :line:`10` there are units in :g:`$E$ <0030.g1:E>`.

.. subclause::

   | Since then there are in :g:`$A$ <0030.g1:A>` as many magnitudes equal to :g:`$C$ <0030.g1:C>`
   | as there are units in :g:`$D$ <0030.g1:D>`,
   | whatever part the unit is of :g:`$D$ <0030.g1:D>`, the same part is :g:`$C$ <0030.g1:C>` of :g:`$A$ <0030.g1:A>` also;

.. assertion:: [VII. Def. 20] <VII.def.20>

   therefore, as :g:`$C$ <0030.g1:C>` is to :g:`$A$ <0030.g1:A>`, so is the unit to :g:`$D$ <0030.g1:D>`.

.. subclause::

   :line:`15` But the unit measures the number :g:`$D$ <0030.g1:D>`;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$C$ <0030.g1:C>` also measures :g:`$A$ <0030.g1:A>`.\ [#f27_1]_

.. subclause::

   And since, as :g:`$C$ <0030.g1:C>` is to :g:`$A$ <0030.g1:A>`, so is the unit to :g:`$D$ <0030.g1:D>`,

.. assertion::

   | therefore, inversely, as :g:`$A$ <0030.g1:A>` is to :g:`$C$ <0030.g1:C>`, so is the number :g:`$D$ <0030.g1:D>` to the
   | unit. :just:`[cf. V. 7, Por.]`

.. subclause::

   | :line:`20` Again, since there are in :g:`$F$ <0030.g1:F>` as many magnitudes equal
   | to :g:`$C$ <0030.g1:C>` as there are units in :g:`$E$ <0030.g1:E>`,

.. assertion:: [VII. Def. 20] <VII.def.20>

   therefore, as :g:`$C$ <0030.g1:C>` is to :g:`$F$ <0030.g1:F>`, so is the unit to :g:`$E$ <0030.g1:E>`.

.. subclause::

   | But it was also proved that,
   | as :g:`$A$ <0030.g1:A>` is to :g:`$C$ <0030.g1:C>`, so is :g:`$D$ <0030.g1:D>` to the unit;

.. assertion:: [V. 22] <V.22>

   :line:`25` therefore, *ex aequali*, as :g:`$A$ <0030.g1:A>` is to :g:`$F$ <0030.g1:F>`, so is :g:`$D$ <0030.g1:D>` to :g:`$E$ <0030.g1:E>`.

.. subclause::

   But, as :g:`$D$ <0030.g1:D>` is to :g:`$E$ <0030.g1:E>`, so is :g:`$A$ <0030.g1:A>` to :g:`$B$ <0030.g1:B>`;

.. assertion:: [V. 11] <V.11>

   therefore also, as :g:`$A$ <0030.g1:A>` is to :g:`$B$ <0030.g1:B>`, so is it to :g:`$F$ <0030.g1:F>` also.

.. assertion::

   | Therefore :g:`$A$ <0030.g1:A>` has the same ratio to each of the magnitudes
   | :g:`$B$ <0030.g1:B>`, :g:`$F$ <0030.g1:F>`;

.. assertion:: [V. 9] <V.9>

   :line:`30` therefore :g:`$B$ <0030.g1:B>` is equal to :g:`$F$ <0030.g1:F>`.

.. subclause::

   But :g:`$C$ <0030.g1:C>` measures :g:`$F$ <0030.g1:F>`;

.. assertion::

   therefore it measures :g:`$B$ <0030.g1:B>` also.

.. subclause::

   Further it measures :g:`$A$ <0030.g1:A>` also;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$C$ <0030.g1:C>` measures :g:`$A$ <0030.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0030.g1:B>`.
