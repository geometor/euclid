**15.** **But the unit measures the number <span data-ref="0030.g1:D">$D$</span>; therefore <span data-ref="0030.g1:C">$C$</span> also measures <span data-ref="0030.g1:A">$A$</span>.**<br />
These words are redundant, though they are apparently found in all the MSS.[^27_1]

The same link to connect the proportion of numbers with the proportion<br />
of magnitudes as was necessary in the last proposition is necessary here. This<br />
being premised, the argument is as follows.

Suppose
$$a : b = m : n,$$
where $m, n$ are (integral) numbers.

Divide $a$ into $m$ parts, each equal to $c$, say, so that
$$a = mc.$$

Now take $d$ such that
$$d = nc.$$

Therefore we have
$$a : c = m : 1,$$
and
$$c : d = 1 : n,$$
so that, _ex aequali_,
$$
\begin{aligned}
a : d &= m : n \\
&= a : b\text{, by hypothesis.}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore $b = d = nc$,<br />
so that $c$ measures $b$ $n$ times, and $a, b$ are commensurable.

The Porism is often used in the later propositions. It follows (1) that, if<br />
$a$ be a given straight line, and $m, n$ any numbers, a straight line $x$ can be<br />
found such that
$$a : x = m : n.$$

(2) We can find a straight line $y$ such that
$$a^2 : y^2 = m : n.$$

For we have only to take $y$, a mean proportional between $a$ and $x$, as<br />
previously found, in which case $a, y, x$ are in continued proportion and<br />
<span class="justification">[V. Def. 9]</span>
$$
\begin{aligned}
a^2 : y^2 &= a : x \\
&= m : n.
\end{aligned}
$$
