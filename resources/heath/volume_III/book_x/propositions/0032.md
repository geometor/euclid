### PROPOSITION 7.

<p class="enunciation">Incommensurable magnitudes have not to one another the<br />
ratio which a number has to a number.</p>

Let <span data-ref="0032.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0032.g1:B">$B$</span> be incommensurable magnitudes;<br />
I say that <span data-ref="0032.g1:A">$A$</span> has not to <span data-ref="0032.g1:B">$B$</span> the ratio which a number has to a<br />
number.

<p class="sub-clause">For, if <span data-ref="0032.g1:A">$A$</span> has to <span data-ref="0032.g1:B">$B$</span> the ratio which a number has to a<br />
number, <span data-ref="0032.g1:A">$A$</span> will be commensurable with <span data-ref="0032.g1:B">$B$</span>. <span class="justification">[X. 6]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But it is not;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0032.g1:A">$A$</span> has not to <span data-ref="0032.g1:B">$B$</span> the ratio which a<br />
number has to a number.</p>

### PROPOSITION 8.

<p class="enunciation">If two magnitudes have not to one another the ratio which<br />
a number has to a number, the magnitudes will be incom-<br />
mensurable.</p>

For let the two magnitudes <span data-ref="0032.g2:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0032.g2:B">$B$</span> not have to one another<br />
the ratio which a number has to a number;<br />
I say that the magnitudes <span data-ref="0032.g2:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0032.g2:B">$B$</span> are incom-<br />
mensurable.

![Proposition 8 diagram](0032.g2.svg)

<p class="sub-clause">For, if they are commensurable, <span data-ref="0032.g2:A">$A$</span> will have to <span data-ref="0032.g2:B">$B$</span> the<br />
ratio which a number has to a number. <span class="justification">[X. 5]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But it has not;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the magnitudes <span data-ref="0032.g2:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0032.g2:B">$B$</span> are incommensurable.</p>

<p class="qed">Therefore etc.</p>

### PROPOSITION 9.

<p class="enunciation">The squares on straight lines commensurable in length have<br />
to one another the ratio which a square number has to a square<br />
number; and squares which have to one another the ratio<br />
which a square number has to a square number will also have<br />
their sides commensurable in length. But the squares on<br />
straight lines incommensurable in length have not to one<br />
another the ratio which a square number has to a square<br />
number; and squares which have not to one another the ratio<br />
which a square number has to a square number will not have<br />
their sides commensurable in length either.</p>
