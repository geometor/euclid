PROPOSITION 7.
--------------

.. enunciation::

   Incommensurable magnitudes have not to one another the ratio which a number has to a number.

Let :g:`$A$ <0032.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0032.g1:B>` be incommensurable magnitudes; I say that :g:`$A$ <0032.g1:A>` has not to :g:`$B$ <0032.g1:B>` the ratio which a number has to a number.

.. subclause::

   For, if :g:`$A$ <0032.g1:A>` has to :g:`$B$ <0032.g1:B>` the ratio which a number has to a number, :g:`$A$ <0032.g1:A>` will be commensurable with :g:`$B$ <0032.g1:B>`. :just:`[X. 6] <X.6>`

.. subclause::

   But it is not;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$A$ <0032.g1:A>` has not to :g:`$B$ <0032.g1:B>` the ratio which a number has to a number.

.. picture:: 0032.g1.svg
   :alt: Proposition 7 diagram

.. qed::

   Therefore etc.

PROPOSITION 8.
--------------

.. enunciation::

   If two magnitudes have not to one another the ratio which a number has to a number, the magnitudes will be incommensurable.

For let the two magnitudes :g:`$A$ <0032.g2:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0032.g2:B>` not have to one another the ratio which a number has to a number; I say that the magnitudes :g:`$A$ <0032.g2:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0032.g2:B>` are incommensurable.

.. picture:: 0032.g2.svg
   :alt: Proposition 8 diagram

.. subclause::

   For, if they are commensurable, :g:`$A$ <0032.g2:A>` will have to :g:`$B$ <0032.g2:B>` the ratio which a number has to a number. :just:`[X. 5] <X.5>`

.. subclause::

   But it has not;

.. assertion::

   therefore the magnitudes :g:`$A$ <0032.g2:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0032.g2:B>` are incommensurable.

.. qed::

   Therefore etc.

PROPOSITION 9.
--------------

.. enunciation::

   The squares on straight lines commensurable in length have to one another the ratio which a square number has to a square number; and squares which have to one another the ratio which a square number has to a square number will also have their sides commensurable in length. But the squares on straight lines incommensurable in length have not to one another the ratio which a square number has to a square number; and squares which have not to one another the ratio which a square number has to a square number will not have their sides commensurable in length either.
