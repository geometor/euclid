<p class="assertion">PORISM. And it is manifest from what has been proved<br />
that straight lines commensurable in length are always com-<br />
mensurable in square also, but those commensurable in square<br />
are not always commensurable in length also.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">[LEMMA. It has been proved in the arithmetical books<br />
that similar plane numbers have to one another the ratio<br />
which a square number has to a square number, <span class="justification">[VIII. 26]</span><br />
and that, if two numbers have to one another the ratio which<br />
a square number has to a square number, they are similar<br />
plane numbers. <span class="justification">[Converse of VIII. 26]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">And it is manifest from these propositions that numbers<br />
which are not similar plane numbers, that is, those which<br />
have not their sides proportional, have not to one another<br />
the ratio which a square number has to a square number.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">For, if they have, they will be similar plane numbers :<br />
which is contrary to the hypothesis.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore numbers which are not similar plane numbers<br />

A scholium to this proposition (Schol. x. No. 62) says categorically that<br />
the theorem proved in it was the discovery of Theaetetus.

If $a$, $b$ be straight lines, and
$$a : b = m : n,$$
where $m$, $n$ are numbers,
then
$$a^2 : b^2 = m^2 : n^2;$$
and conversely.
