I shall henceforth, for the sake of brevity, use symbols for the terms<br />
"commensurable (with)" and "incommensurable (with)" according to the<br />
varieties described in X. Deff. 1—4. The symbols are taken from Lorenz<br />
and seem convenient.

*Commensurable* and *commensurable with*, in relation to areas, and *com-<br />
mensurable in length* and *commensurable in length with*, in relation to straight<br />
lines, will be denoted by $\frown$.

*Commensurable in square only* or *commensurable in square only with* (terms<br />
applicable only to straight lines) will be denoted by $\sim$.

*Incommensurable (with)*, of areas, and *incommensurable (with)*, of straight<br />
lines will be denoted by $\smile$.

*Incommensurable in square (with)* (a term applicable to straight lines only)<br />
will be denoted by $\sim\hspace{-0.5em}-$.

Suppose $a$, $b$, $c$, $d$ to be four magnitudes such that
$$a : b = c : d.$$
Then (1), if $a \frown b$,
$$a : b = m : n, \quad \text{where } m, n \text{ are integers, [x. 5]}$$
whence
$$c : d = m : n,$$
and therefore
$$c \frown d. \quad \text{[x. 6]}$$
(2) If $a \smile b$,
$$a : b \neq m : n, \quad \text{[x. 7]}$$
so that
$$c : d \neq m : n,$$
whence
$$c \smile d. \quad \text{[x. 8]}$$

### PROPOSITION 12.

<p class="enunciation">Magnitudes commensurable with the same magnitude are<br />
commensurable with one another also.</p>

For let each of the magnitudes <span data-ref="0038.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0038.g1:B">$B$</span> be commensurable<br />
with <span data-ref="0038.g1:C">$C$</span>;<br />
I say that <span data-ref="0038.g1:A">$A$</span> is also commensurable with <span data-ref="0038.g1:B">$B$</span>.

<p class="sub-clause">For, since <span data-ref="0038.g1:A">$A$</span> is commensurable with <span data-ref="0038.g1:C">$C$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0038.g1:A">$A$</span> has to <span data-ref="0038.g1:C">$C$</span> the ratio which a number has to a<br />
number. <span class="justification">[X. 5]</span></p>
