## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations

- **Introduction of Lorenz Notation**:
  - Heath adopts the shorthand notation introduced by Johann Friedrich Lorenz (*Euklids Elemente*, 1781) to represent the four types of commensurability / incommensurability defined in Book X Definitions 1–4:
    1. $\frown$ (arc / cap): Commensurable in length (straight lines) or area.
    2. $\sim$ (tilde): Commensurable in square only (straight lines).
    3. $\smile$ (smile / cup): Incommensurable in length (straight lines) or area.
    4. $\sim\hspace{-0.5em}-$ (tilde with bar): Incommensurable in square only (straight lines).
  - Heath demonstrates the compactness of this notation by restating the proof of Proposition 11 in four lines.
- **Proposition 12 Enunciation**: Establishes the equivalence relation (transitivity) of commensurability: $A \commensurablesymbol C \land B \commensurablesymbol C \implies A \commensurablesymbol B$.
