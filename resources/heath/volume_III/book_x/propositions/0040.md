### PROPOSITION 13.

<p class="enunciation">If two magnitudes be commensurable, and the one of them<br />
be incommensurable with any magnitude, the remaining one<br />
will also be incommensurable with the same.</p>

Let <span data-ref="0040.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0040.g1:B">$B$</span> be two commensurable magnitudes, and let one<br />
of them, <span data-ref="0040.g1:A">$A$</span>, be incommensurable with<br />
any other magnitude <span data-ref="0040.g1:C">$C$</span>;<br />
I say that the remaining one, <span data-ref="0040.g1:B">$B$</span>, will<br />
also be incommensurable with <span data-ref="0040.g1:C">$C$</span>.

![Proposition 13 Diagram](0040.g1.svg)

<p class="sub-clause">For, if <span data-ref="0040.g1:B">$B$</span> is commensurable with <span data-ref="0040.g1:C">$C$</span>,<br />
while <span data-ref="0040.g1:A">$A$</span> is also commensurable with <span data-ref="0040.g1:B">$B$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion"><span data-ref="0040.g1:A">$A$</span> is also commensurable with <span data-ref="0040.g1:C">$C$</span>. <span class="justification">[X. 12]</span></p>
<p class="sub-clause">But it is also incommensurable with it :</p>
<p class="assertion">which is impossible.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore <span data-ref="0040.g1:B">$B$</span> is not commensurable with <span data-ref="0040.g1:C">$C$</span>;<br />
therefore it is incommensurable with it.</p>

<p class="qed">Therefore etc.</p>

### LEMMA.

<p class="enunciation">Given two unequal straight lines, to find by what square the<br />
square on the greater is greater than the square on the less.</p>

Let <span data-ref="0040.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0040.g2:C">$C$</span> be the given two unequal straight lines, and<br />
let <span data-ref="0040.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span> be the greater of them;<br />
thus it is required to find by what<br />
square the square on <span data-ref="0040.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is greater<br />
than the square on <span data-ref="0040.g2:C">$C$</span>.

![Lemma Diagram](0040.g2.svg)

Let the semicircle <span data-ref="0040.g2:( A D B )">$ADB$</span> be de-<br />
scribed on <span data-ref="0040.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span>,<br />
and let <span data-ref="0040.g2:/ A D /">$AD$</span> be fitted into it equal to <span data-ref="0040.g2:C">$C$</span>; <span class="justification">[IV. 1]</span><br />
let <span data-ref="0040.g2:/ D B /">$DB$</span> be joined.

<p class="assertion">It is then manifest that the angle <span data-ref="0040.g2:< A D B )">$ADB$</span> is right, <span class="justification">[III. 31]</span><br />
and that the square on <span data-ref="0040.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is greater than the square on<br />
<span data-ref="0040.g2:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, that is, <span data-ref="0040.g2:C">$C$</span>, by the square on <span data-ref="0040.g2:/ D B /">$DB$</span>. <span class="justification">[I. 47]</span></p>

Similarly also, if two straight lines be given, the straight<br />
line the square on which is equal to the sum of the squares<br />
on them is found in this manner.
