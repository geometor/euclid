### PROPOSITION 13.

*If two magnitudes be commensurable, and the one of them
be incommensurable with any magnitude, the remaining one
will also be incommensurable with the same.*

Let <span data-ref="0040.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0040.g1:B">$B$</span> be two commensurable magnitudes, and let one
of them, <span data-ref="0040.g1:A">$A$</span>, be incommensurable with
any other magnitude <span data-ref="0040.g1:C">$C$</span>;
I say that the remaining one, <span data-ref="0040.g1:B">$B$</span>, will
also be incommensurable with <span data-ref="0040.g1:C">$C$</span>.

![Proposition 13 Diagram](0040.g1.svg)

For, if <span data-ref="0040.g1:B">$B$</span> is commensurable with <span data-ref="0040.g1:C">$C$</span>,
while <span data-ref="0040.g1:A">$A$</span> is also commensurable with <span data-ref="0040.g1:B">$B$</span>,
<span data-ref="0040.g1:A">$A$</span> is also commensurable with <span data-ref="0040.g1:C">$C$</span>. [x. 12]
But it is also incommensurable with it:
which is impossible.

Therefore <span data-ref="0040.g1:B">$B$</span> is not commensurable with <span data-ref="0040.g1:C">$C$</span>;
therefore it is incommensurable with it.

Therefore etc.

### LEMMA.

*Given two unequal straight lines, to find by what square the
square on the greater is greater than the square on the less.*

Let <span data-ref="0040.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0040.g2:C">$C$</span> be the given two unequal straight lines, and
let <span data-ref="0040.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span> be the greater of them;
thus it is required to find by what
square the square on <span data-ref="0040.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is greater
than the square on <span data-ref="0040.g2:C">$C$</span>.

![Lemma Diagram](0040.g2.svg)

Let the semicircle <span data-ref="0040.g2:( A D B )">$ADB$</span> be de-
scribed on <span data-ref="0040.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span>,
and let <span data-ref="0040.g2:/ A D /">$AD$</span> be fitted into it equal to <span data-ref="0040.g2:C">$C$</span>; [iv. 1]
let <span data-ref="0040.g2:/ D B /">$DB$</span> be joined.

It is then manifest that the angle <span data-ref="0040.g2:< A D B )">$ADB$</span> is right, [iii. 31]
and that the square on <span data-ref="0040.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is greater than the square on
<span data-ref="0040.g2:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, that is, <span data-ref="0040.g2:C">$C$</span>, by the square on <span data-ref="0040.g2:/ D B /">$DB$</span>. [i. 47]

Similarly also, if two straight lines be given, the straight
line the square on which is equal to the sum of the squares
on them is found in this manner.
