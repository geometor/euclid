Let <span data-ref="0040.g2:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0040.g2:/ D B /">$DB$</span> be the given two straight lines, and let it be
required to find the straight line the square on which is equal
to the sum of the squares on them.

Let them be placed so as to contain a right angle, that
formed by <span data-ref="0040.g2:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0040.g2:/ D B /">$DB$</span>;
and let <span data-ref="0040.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span> be joined.

It is again manifest that the straight line the square on
which is equal to the sum of the squares on <span data-ref="0040.g2:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0040.g2:/ D B /">$DB$</span> is <span data-ref="0040.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span>.
[i. 47]

<p align="right">Q. E. D.</p>

---

The lemma gives an obvious method of finding a straight line ($c$) equal to
$\sqrt{a^2 \mp b^2}$, where $a$, $b$ are given straight lines of which $a$ is the greater.

### PROPOSITION 14.

*If four straight lines be proportional, and the square on
the first be greater than the square on the second by the square
on a straight line commensurable with the first, the square on
the third will also be greater than the square on the fourth by*
<sup>5</sup> *the square on a straight line commensurable with the third.*

*And, if the square on the first be greater than the square
on the second by the square on a straight line incommensurable
with the first, the square on the third will also be greater than
the square on the fourth by the square on a straight line in-*
<sup>10</sup> *commensurable with the third.*

Let <span data-ref="0041.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0041.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0041.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0041.g1:D">$D$</span> be four straight lines in proportion, so
that, as <span data-ref="0041.g1:A">$A$</span> is to <span data-ref="0041.g1:B">$B$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0041.g1:C">$C$</span> to <span data-ref="0041.g1:D">$D$</span>;
and let the square on <span data-ref="0041.g1:A">$A$</span> be greater than
the square on <span data-ref="0041.g1:B">$B$</span> by the square on <span data-ref="0041.g1:E">$E$</span>, and
<sup>15</sup> let the square on <span data-ref="0041.g1:C">$C$</span> be greater than the
square on <span data-ref="0041.g1:D">$D$</span> by the square on <span data-ref="0041.g1:F">$F$</span>;
I say that, if <span data-ref="0041.g1:A">$A$</span> is commensurable with <span data-ref="0041.g1:E">$E$</span>,
<span data-ref="0041.g1:C">$C$</span> is also commensurable with <span data-ref="0041.g1:F">$F$</span>,
and, if <span data-ref="0041.g1:A">$A$</span> is incommensurable with <span data-ref="0041.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0041.g1:C">$C$</span> is
<sup>20</sup> also incommensurable with <span data-ref="0041.g1:F">$F$</span>.

![Proposition 14 Diagram](0041.g1.svg)

For since, as <span data-ref="0041.g1:A">$A$</span> is to <span data-ref="0041.g1:B">$B$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0041.g1:C">$C$</span> to <span data-ref="0041.g1:D">$D$</span>,
therefore also, as the square on <span data-ref="0041.g1:A">$A$</span> is to the square on <span data-ref="0041.g1:B">$B$</span>, so is
the square on <span data-ref="0041.g1:C">$C$</span> to the square on <span data-ref="0041.g1:D">$D$</span>. [vi. 22]

But the squares on <span data-ref="0041.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0041.g1:B">$B$</span> are equal to the square on <span data-ref="0041.g1:A">$A$</span>,
<sup>25</sup> and the squares on <span data-ref="0041.g1:D">$D$</span>, <span data-ref="0041.g1:F">$F$</span> are equal to the square on <span data-ref="0041.g1:C">$C$</span>.
