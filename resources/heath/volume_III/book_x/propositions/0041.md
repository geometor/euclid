Let <span data-ref="0040.g2:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0040.g2:/ D B /">$DB$</span> be the given two straight lines, and let it be<br />
required to find the straight line the square on which is equal<br />
to the sum of the squares on them.

Let them be placed so as to contain a right angle, that<br />
formed by <span data-ref="0040.g2:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0040.g2:/ D B /">$DB$</span>;<br />
and let <span data-ref="0040.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span> be joined.

<p class="assertion">It is again manifest that the straight line the square on<br />
which is equal to the sum of the squares on <span data-ref="0040.g2:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0040.g2:/ D B /">$DB$</span> is <span data-ref="0040.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span>.<br />
<span class="justification">[I. 47]</span></p>

<p class="qed"><span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

---

The lemma gives an obvious method of finding a straight line ($c$) equal to<br />
$\sqrt{a^2 \mp b^2}$, where $a$, $b$ are given straight lines of which $a$ is the greater.

### PROPOSITION 14.

<p class="enunciation">If four straight lines be proportional, and the square on<br />
the first be greater than the square on the second by the square<br />
on a straight line commensurable with the first, the square on<br />
the third will also be greater than the square on the fourth by<br />
<sup>5</sup> the square on a straight line commensurable with the third.</p>

<p class="enunciation">And, if the square on the first be greater than the square<br />
on the second by the square on a straight line incommensurable<br />
with the first, the square on the third will also be greater than<br />
the square on the fourth by the square on a straight line in-<br />
<sup>10</sup> commensurable with the third.</p>

Let <span data-ref="0041.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0041.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0041.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0041.g1:D">$D$</span> be four straight lines in proportion, so<br />
that, as <span data-ref="0041.g1:A">$A$</span> is to <span data-ref="0041.g1:B">$B$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0041.g1:C">$C$</span> to <span data-ref="0041.g1:D">$D$</span>;<br />
and let the square on <span data-ref="0041.g1:A">$A$</span> be greater than<br />
the square on <span data-ref="0041.g1:B">$B$</span> by the square on <span data-ref="0041.g1:E">$E$</span>, and<br />
<sup>15</sup> let the square on <span data-ref="0041.g1:C">$C$</span> be greater than the<br />
square on <span data-ref="0041.g1:D">$D$</span> by the square on <span data-ref="0041.g1:F">$F$</span>;<br />
I say that, if <span data-ref="0041.g1:A">$A$</span> is commensurable with <span data-ref="0041.g1:E">$E$</span>,<br />
<span data-ref="0041.g1:C">$C$</span> is also commensurable with <span data-ref="0041.g1:F">$F$</span>,<br />
and, if <span data-ref="0041.g1:A">$A$</span> is incommensurable with <span data-ref="0041.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0041.g1:C">$C$</span> is<br />
<sup>20</sup> also incommensurable with <span data-ref="0041.g1:F">$F$</span>.

![Proposition 14 Diagram](0041.g1.svg)

<p class="sub-clause">For since, as <span data-ref="0041.g1:A">$A$</span> is to <span data-ref="0041.g1:B">$B$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0041.g1:C">$C$</span> to <span data-ref="0041.g1:D">$D$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore also, as the square on <span data-ref="0041.g1:A">$A$</span> is to the square on <span data-ref="0041.g1:B">$B$</span>, so is<br />
the square on <span data-ref="0041.g1:C">$C$</span> to the square on <span data-ref="0041.g1:D">$D$</span>. <span class="justification">[VI. 22]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But the squares on <span data-ref="0041.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0041.g1:B">$B$</span> are equal to the square on <span data-ref="0041.g1:A">$A$</span>,<br />
<sup>25</sup> and the squares on <span data-ref="0041.g1:D">$D$</span>, <span data-ref="0041.g1:F">$F$</span> are equal to the square on <span data-ref="0041.g1:C">$C$</span>.</p>
