### LEMMA.

*If to any straight line there be applied a parallelogram
deficient by a square figure, the applied parallelogram is equal
to the rectangle contained by the segments of the straight line
resulting from the application.*

For let there be applied to the straight line <span data-ref="0045.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> the
parallelogram <span data-ref="0045.g1:< A C D E >">$AD$</span> deficient by the
square figure <span data-ref="0045.g1:< C B F D >">$DB$</span>;
I say that <span data-ref="0045.g1:< A C D E >">$AD$</span> is equal to the rectangle
contained by <span data-ref="0045.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0045.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span>.

![Lemma Diagram](0045.g1.svg)

This is indeed at once manifest;
for, since <span data-ref="0045.g1:< C B F D >">$DB$</span> is a square,
<span data-ref="0045.g1:/ C D /">$DC$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0045.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span>;
and <span data-ref="0045.g1:< A C D E >">$AD$</span> is the rectangle <span data-ref="0045.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0045.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, that is, the rectangle <span data-ref="0045.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>,
<span data-ref="0045.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span>.

Therefore etc.

If $a$ be the given straight line, and $x$ the side of the square by which the
applied rectangle is to be deficient, the rectangle is equal to $ax - x^2$, which is
of course equal to $x(a - x)$. The rectangle may be written $xy$, where
$x + y = a$. Given the area $x(a - x)$, or $xy$ (where $x + y = a$), two different
*applications* will give rectangles equal to this area, the sides of the defect
being $x$ or $a - x$ ($x$ or $y$) respectively; but the second mode of expression
shows that the rectangles do not differ in form but only in position.
