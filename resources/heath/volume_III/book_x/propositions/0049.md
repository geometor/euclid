It is often more convenient to use the symmetrical form of equation in<br />
this and similar cases, viz.
$$\left.\begin{aligned} xy &= \frac{b^2}{4} \\ x + y &= a \end{aligned}\right\}$$
The result with this mode of expression is that<br />
(1) if $x \mathrel{⌢} y$, then $a \mathrel{⌢} \sqrt{a^2 - b^2}$; and<br />
(2) if $a \mathrel{⌢} \sqrt{a^2 - b^2}$, then $x \mathrel{⌢} y$.<br />
The truth of the proposition is even easier to see in this case, since<br />
$(x - y)^2 = (a^2 - b^2)$.

### PROPOSITION 18.

<p class="enunciation">If there be two unequal straight lines, and to the greater<br />
there be applied a parallelogram equal to the fourth part of<br />
the square on the less and deficient by a square figure, and<br />
if it divide it into parts which are incommensurable, the square<br />
<sup>5</sup> on the greater will be greater than the square on the less by<br />
the square on a straight line incommensurable with the greater.</p>

<p class="enunciation">And, if the square on the greater be greater than the square<br />
on the less by the square on a straight line incommensurable<br />
with the greater, and if there be applied to the greater a<br />
<sup>10</sup> parallelogram equal to the fourth part of the square on the<br />
less and deficient by a square figure, it divides it into parts<br />
which are incommensurable.</p>

Let <span data-ref="0049.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0049.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> be two unequal straight lines, of which <span data-ref="0049.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is<br />
the greater,<br />
and to <span data-ref="0049.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> let there be applied a parallelogram equal<br />
to the fourth part of the square on the less, <span data-ref="0049.g1:A">$A$</span>, and<br />
deficient by a square figure. Let this be the rect-<br />
angle <span data-ref="0049.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>, <span data-ref="0049.g1:/ D C /">$DC$</span>, <span class="justification">[cf. Lemma before X. 17]</span><br />
and let <span data-ref="0049.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> be incommensurable in length with <span data-ref="0049.g1:/ D C /">$DC$</span>;<br />
I say that the square on <span data-ref="0049.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is greater than the<br />
square on <span data-ref="0049.g1:A">$A$</span> by the square on a straight line incom-<br />
mensurable with <span data-ref="0049.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>.

![Proposition 18](0049.g1.svg)

For, with the same construction as before, we can prove<br />
similarly that the square on <span data-ref="0049.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is greater than the square on<br />
<span data-ref="0049.g1:A">$A$</span> by the square on <span data-ref="0049.g1:/ F D /">$FD$</span>.

It is to be proved that <span data-ref="0049.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is incommensurable in length<br />
with <span data-ref="0049.g1:/ F D /">$FD$</span>.
