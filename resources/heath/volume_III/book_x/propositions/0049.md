It is often more convenient to use the symmetrical form of equation in this and similar cases, viz.
$$\left.\begin{aligned} xy &= \frac{b^2}{4} \\ x + y &= a \end{aligned}\right\}$$
The result with this mode of expression is that
(1) if $x \mathrel{⌢} y$, then $a \mathrel{⌢} \sqrt{a^2 - b^2}$; and
(2) if $a \mathrel{⌢} \sqrt{a^2 - b^2}$, then $x \mathrel{⌢} y$.
The truth of the proposition is even easier to see in this case, since $(x - y)^2 = (a^2 - b^2)$.

### PROPOSITION 18.

*If there be two unequal straight lines, and to the greater there be applied a parallelogram equal to the fourth part of the square on the less and deficient by a square figure, and if it divide it into parts which are incommensurable, the square* <sup>5</sup> *on the greater will be greater than the square on the less by the square on a straight line incommensurable with the greater.*
*And, if the square on the greater be greater than the square on the less by the square on a straight line incommensurable with the greater, and if there be applied to the greater a* <sup>10</sup> *parallelogram equal to the fourth part of the square on the less and deficient by a square figure, it divides it into parts which are incommensurable.*

Let <span data-ref="0049.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0049.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> be two unequal straight lines, of which <span data-ref="0049.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is the greater, and to <span data-ref="0049.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> let there be applied a parallelogram equal to the fourth part of the square on the less, <span data-ref="0049.g1:A">$A$</span>, and deficient by a square figure. Let this be the rectangle <span data-ref="0049.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>, <span data-ref="0049.g1:/ D C /">$DC$</span>, [cf. Lemma before X. 17] and let <span data-ref="0049.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> be incommensurable in length with <span data-ref="0049.g1:/ D C /">$DC$</span>;
I say that the square on <span data-ref="0049.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is greater than the square on <span data-ref="0049.g1:A">$A$</span> by the square on a straight line incommensurable with <span data-ref="0049.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>.

![Proposition 18](0049.g1.svg)

For, with the same construction as before, we can prove similarly that the square on <span data-ref="0049.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is greater than the square on <span data-ref="0049.g1:A">$A$</span> by the square on <span data-ref="0049.g1:/ F D /">$FD$</span>.
It is to be proved that <span data-ref="0049.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is incommensurable in length with <span data-ref="0049.g1:/ F D /">$FD$</span>.
