I say that <span data-ref="0054.g1:< A C >">$AC$</span> is irrational, and the side of the square equal<br />
to it is irrational;<br />
and let the latter be called **medial**.

![Proposition 21](0054.g1.svg)

For on <span data-ref="0054.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> let the square <span data-ref="0054.g1:< A D >">$AD$</span> be described;

<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0054.g1:< A D >">$AD$</span> is rational. <span class="justification">[X. Def. 4]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">And, since <span data-ref="0054.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is incommensurable in length<br />
with <span data-ref="0054.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>,<br />
for by hypothesis they are commensurable in<br />
square only,<br />
while <span data-ref="0054.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0054.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>,</p>

<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0054.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> is also incommensurable in length with <span data-ref="0054.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">And, as <span data-ref="0054.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0054.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0054.g1:< A D >">$AD$</span> to <span data-ref="0054.g1:< A C >">$AC$</span>; <span class="justification">[VI. 1]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0054.g1:< D A >">$DA$</span> is incommensurable with <span data-ref="0054.g1:< A C >">$AC$</span>. <span class="justification">[X. 11]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But <span data-ref="0054.g1:< D A >">$DA$</span> is rational;</p>

<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0054.g1:< A C >">$AC$</span> is irrational,<br />
so that the side of the square equal to <span data-ref="0054.g1:< A C >">$AC$</span> is also irrational. <span class="justification">[X. Def. 4]</span></p>

<p class="qed">And let the latter be called **medial**.<span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

---

A *medial* straight line, now defined for the first time, is so called because<br />
it is a mean proportional between two rational straight lines commensurable<br />
in square only. Such straight lines can be denoted by $\rho$, $\rho \sqrt{k}$. A medial<br />
straight line is therefore of the form $\sqrt{\rho^2 \sqrt{k}}$ or $k^{\frac{1}{4}} \rho$. Euclid's proof that this is<br />
irrational is equivalent to the following. Take $\rho$, $\rho \sqrt{k}$ commensurable in<br />
square only, so that they are incommensurable in length.

Now
$$\rho : \rho \sqrt{k} = \rho^2 : \rho^2 \sqrt{k},$$
whence [X. 11] $\rho^2 \sqrt{k}$ is incommensurable with $\rho^2$ and therefore irrational<br />
[X. Def. 4], so that $\sqrt{\rho^2 \sqrt{k}}$ is also irrational [ibid.].

A medial straight line may evidently take either of the forms $\sqrt{a\sqrt{B}}$ or<br />
$\sqrt[4]{AB}$, where of course $B$ is not of the form $k^2 A$.

#### Lemma.

<p class="enunciation">If there be two straight lines, then, as the first is to the<br />
second, so is the square on the first<br />
to the rectangle contained by the<br />
two straight lines.</p>

![Lemma](0054.g2.svg)

Let <span data-ref="0054.g2:/ F E /">$FE$</span>, <span data-ref="0054.g2:/ E G /">$EG$</span> be two straight<br />
lines.

I say that, as <span data-ref="0054.g2:/ F E /">$FE$</span> is to <span data-ref="0054.g2:/ E G /">$EG$</span>, so is the square on <span data-ref="0054.g2:/ F E /">$FE$</span> to<br />
the rectangle <span data-ref="0054.g2:/ F E /">$FE$</span>, <span data-ref="0054.g2:/ E G /">$EG$</span>.
