I say that <span data-ref="0054.g1:< A C >">$AC$</span> is irrational, and the side of the square equal to it is irrational;
and let the latter be called **medial**.

![Proposition 21](0054.g1.svg)

For on <span data-ref="0054.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> let the square <span data-ref="0054.g1:< A D >">$AD$</span> be described;
therefore <span data-ref="0054.g1:< A D >">$AD$</span> is rational. [x. Def. 4]
And, since <span data-ref="0054.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is incommensurable in length with <span data-ref="0054.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>,
for by hypothesis they are commensurable in square only,
while <span data-ref="0054.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0054.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>,
therefore <span data-ref="0054.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> is also incommensurable in length with <span data-ref="0054.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>.
And, as <span data-ref="0054.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0054.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0054.g1:< A D >">$AD$</span> to <span data-ref="0054.g1:< A C >">$AC$</span>; [VI. 1]
therefore <span data-ref="0054.g1:< D A >">$DA$</span> is incommensurable with <span data-ref="0054.g1:< A C >">$AC$</span>. [x. 11]
But <span data-ref="0054.g1:< D A >">$DA$</span> is rational;
therefore <span data-ref="0054.g1:< A C >">$AC$</span> is irrational,
so that the side of the square equal to <span data-ref="0054.g1:< A C >">$AC$</span> is also irrational. [x. Def. 4]
And let the latter be called **medial**.

Q. E. D.

---

A *medial* straight line, now defined for the first time, is so called because it is a mean proportional between two rational straight lines commensurable in square only. Such straight lines can be denoted by $\rho$, $\rho \sqrt{k}$. A medial straight line is therefore of the form $\sqrt{\rho^2 \sqrt{k}}$ or $k^{\frac{1}{4}} \rho$. Euclid's proof that this is irrational is equivalent to the following. Take $\rho$, $\rho \sqrt{k}$ commensurable in square only, so that they are incommensurable in length.

Now
$$\rho : \rho \sqrt{k} = \rho^2 : \rho^2 \sqrt{k},$$
whence [x. 11] $\rho^2 \sqrt{k}$ is incommensurable with $\rho^2$ and therefore irrational [x. Def. 4], so that $\sqrt{\rho^2 \sqrt{k}}$ is also irrational [ibid.].

A medial straight line may evidently take either of the forms $\sqrt{a\sqrt{B}}$ or $\sqrt[4]{AB}$, where of course $B$ is not of the form $k^2 A$.

#### Lemma.

*If there be two straight lines, then, as the first is to the second, so is the square on the first to the rectangle contained by the two straight lines.*

![Lemma](0054.g2.svg)

Let <span data-ref="0054.g2:/ F E /">$FE$</span>, <span data-ref="0054.g2:/ E G /">$EG$</span> be two straight lines.
I say that, as <span data-ref="0054.g2:/ F E /">$FE$</span> is to <span data-ref="0054.g2:/ E G /">$EG$</span>, so is the square on <span data-ref="0054.g2:/ F E /">$FE$</span> to the rectangle <span data-ref="0054.g2:/ F E /">$FE$</span>, <span data-ref="0054.g2:/ E G /">$EG$</span>.
