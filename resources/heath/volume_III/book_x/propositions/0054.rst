:title: Proposition 21 (conclusion), Lemma

I say that :g:`$AC$ <0054.g1:< A C >>` is irrational, and the side of the square equal to it is irrational; and let the latter be called :term:`**medial** <medial>`.

.. picture:: 0054.g1.svg
   :alt: Proposition 21

For on :g:`$AB$ <0054.g1:/ A B />` let the square :g:`$AD$ <0054.g1:< A D >>` be described;

.. assertion:: [X. Def. 4] <X.def.4>

   therefore :g:`$AD$ <0054.g1:< A D >>` is rational.

.. subclause::

   And, since :g:`$AB$ <0054.g1:/ A B />` is incommensurable in length with :g:`$BC$ <0054.g1:/ B C />`, for by hypothesis they are commensurable in square only, while :g:`$AB$ <0054.g1:/ A B />` is :eq:`equal <0054.g1:/ A B / = 0054.g1:/ B D />` to :g:`$BD$ <0054.g1:/ B D />`,

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$DB$ <0054.g1:/ D B />` is also incommensurable in length with :g:`$BC$ <0054.g1:/ B C />`.

.. subclause:: [VI. 1] <VI.1>

   And, as :g:`$DB$ <0054.g1:/ D B />` is to :g:`$BC$ <0054.g1:/ B C />`, so is :g:`$AD$ <0054.g1:< A D >>` to :g:`$AC$ <0054.g1:< A C >>`;

.. assertion:: [X. 11] <X.11>

   therefore :g:`$DA$ <0054.g1:< D A >>` is incommensurable with :g:`$AC$ <0054.g1:< A C >>`.

.. subclause::

   But :g:`$DA$ <0054.g1:< D A >>` is rational;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$AC$ <0054.g1:< A C >>` is irrational, so that the side of the square equal to :g:`$AC$ <0054.g1:< A C >>` is also irrational. :just:`[X. Def. 4] <X.def.4>`

.. qed::

   And let the latter be called :term:`**medial** <medial>`. :just:`Q. E. D.`

----

A *medial* straight line, now defined for the first time, is so called because it is a mean proportional between two rational straight lines commensurable in square only. Such straight lines can be denoted by $\rho$, $\rho \sqrt{k}$. A medial straight line is therefore of the form $\sqrt{\rho^2 \sqrt{k}}$ or $k^{\frac{1}{4}} \rho$. Euclid's proof that this is irrational is equivalent to the following. Take $\rho$, $\rho \sqrt{k}$ commensurable in square only, so that they are incommensurable in length.

Now

$$\rho : \rho \sqrt{k} = \rho^2 : \rho^2 \sqrt{k},$$

whence [X. 11] $\rho^2 \sqrt{k}$ is incommensurable with $\rho^2$ and therefore irrational [X. Def. 4], so that $\sqrt{\rho^2 \sqrt{k}}$ is also irrational [ibid.].

A medial straight line may evidently take either of the forms $\sqrt{a\sqrt{B}}$ or $\sqrt[4]{AB}$, where of course $B$ is not of the form $k^2 A$.

Lemma.
~~~~~~

.. enunciation::

   If there be two straight lines, then, as the first is to the second, so is the square on the first to the rectangle contained by the two straight lines.

.. picture:: 0054.g2.svg
   :alt: Lemma

Let :g:`$FE$ <0054.g2:/ F E />`, :g:`$EG$ <0054.g2:/ E G />` be two straight lines.

I say that, as :g:`$FE$ <0054.g2:/ F E />` is to :g:`$EG$ <0054.g2:/ E G />`, so is the square on :g:`$FE$ <0054.g2:/ F E />` to the rectangle :g:`$FE$ <0054.g2:/ F E />`, :g:`$EG$ <0054.g2:/ E G />`.
