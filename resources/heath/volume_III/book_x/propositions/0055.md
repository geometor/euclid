### Proposition 22.

*The square on a medial straight line, if applied to a rational straight line, produces as breadth a straight line rational and incommensurable in length with that to which it is applied.*

![Proposition 22](0055.g1.svg)

Let <span data-ref="0055.g1:A">$A$</span> be medial and <span data-ref="0055.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span> rational,
and let a rectangular area <span data-ref="0055.g1:< B D >">$BD$</span> equal to the square on <span data-ref="0055.g1:A">$A$</span> be applied to <span data-ref="0055.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, producing <span data-ref="0055.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> as breadth;
I say that <span data-ref="0055.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> is rational and incommensurable in length with <span data-ref="0055.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span>.
For, since <span data-ref="0055.g1:A">$A$</span> is medial, the square on it is equal to a rectangular area contained by rational straight lines commensurable in square only. [x. 21]
Let the square on it be equal to <span data-ref="0055.g1:< G F >">$GF$</span>.
But the square on it is also equal to <span data-ref="0055.g1:< B D >">$BD$</span>;
therefore <span data-ref="0055.g1:< B D >">$BD$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0055.g1:< G F >">$GF$</span>.
But it is also equiangular with it;
and in equal and equiangular parallelograms the sides about the equal angles are reciprocally proportional; [VI. 14]
therefore, proportionally, as <span data-ref="0055.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is to <span data-ref="0055.g1:/ E G /">$EG$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0055.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> to <span data-ref="0055.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>.
Therefore also, as the square on <span data-ref="0055.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is to the square on <span data-ref="0055.g1:/ E G /">$EG$</span>, so is the square on <span data-ref="0055.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> to the square on <span data-ref="0055.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>. [VI. 22]
