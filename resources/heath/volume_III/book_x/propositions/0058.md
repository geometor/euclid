---

As explained in the bracketed passage following this proposition, a straight<br />
line commensurable with a medial straight line in square only, as well as a<br />
straight line commensurable with it in length, is medial.

Algebraical notation shows this easily.

If $k^{\frac{1}{4}} \rho$ be the given straight line, $\lambda k^{\frac{1}{4}} \rho$ is a straight line commensurable<br />
in length with it and $\sqrt{\lambda} \cdot k^{\frac{1}{4}} \rho$ a straight line commensurable with it in square<br />
only.

But $\lambda \rho$ and $\sqrt{\lambda} \cdot \rho$ are both rational [X. Def. 3] and therefore can be<br />
expressed by $\rho'$, and we thus arrive at $k^{\frac{1}{4}} \rho'$, which is clearly medial.

Euclid's proof amounts to the following.

Apply both the areas $\sqrt{k} \cdot \rho^2$ and $\lambda^2 \sqrt{k} \cdot \rho^2$ (or $\lambda \sqrt{k} \cdot \rho^2$) to a rational<br />
straight line $\sigma$.

The breadths $\sqrt{k} \cdot \frac{\rho^2}{\sigma}$ and $\lambda^2 \sqrt{k} \cdot \frac{\rho^2}{\sigma}$ (or $\lambda \sqrt{k} \cdot \frac{\rho^2}{\sigma}$) are in the ratio of the<br />
areas $\sqrt{k} \cdot \rho^2$ and $\lambda^2 \sqrt{k} \cdot \rho^2$ (or $\lambda \sqrt{k} \cdot \rho^2$) themselves and are therefore com-<br />
mensurable.

Now [X. 22] $\sqrt{k} \cdot \frac{\rho^2}{\sigma}$ is rational but incommensurable with $\sigma$.
