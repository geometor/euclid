### Proposition 24.

<p class="enunciation">The rectangle contained by medial straight lines commen-<br />
surable in length is medial.</p>

![Proposition 24](0059.g1.svg)

For let the rectangle <span data-ref="0059.g1:< A C >">$AC$</span> be contained by the medial<br />
straight lines <span data-ref="0059.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0059.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> which are commensurable<br />
in length;<br />
I say that <span data-ref="0059.g1:< A C >">$AC$</span> is medial.

For on <span data-ref="0059.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> let the square <span data-ref="0059.g1:< A D >">$AD$</span> be described;
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0059.g1:< A D >">$AD$</span> is medial.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">And, since <span data-ref="0059.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is commensurable in length<br />
with <span data-ref="0059.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>,<br />
while <span data-ref="0059.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0059.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0059.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> is also commensurable in length<br />
with <span data-ref="0059.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>;<br />
so that <span data-ref="0059.g1:< D A >">$DA$</span> is also commensurable with <span data-ref="0059.g1:< A C >">$AC$</span>. <span class="justification">[VI. 1, X. 11]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But <span data-ref="0059.g1:< D A >">$DA$</span> is medial;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0059.g1:< A C >">$AC$</span> is also medial. <span class="justification">[X. 23, Por.]</span></p>

<p class="qed"><span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

There is the same difficulty in the text of this enunciation as in that of<br />
X. 19. The Greek says "medial straight lines commensurable in length in<br />
any of the aforesaid ways"; but straight lines can only be *commensurable in*<br />
*length* in one way, though they can be medial in two ways, as explained in the<br />
addition to the preceding proposition, i.e. they can be either commensurable<br />
in length or commensurable in square only with a *given* medial straight line.<br />
For the same reason as that explained in the note on X. 19 I have omitted<br />
"in any of the aforesaid ways" in the enunciation and bracketed the addition<br />
to X. 23 to which it refers.

$k^{\frac{1}{4}} \rho$ and $\lambda k^{\frac{1}{4}} \rho$ are medial straight lines commensurable in length. The<br />
rectangle contained by them is $\lambda k^{\frac{1}{2}} \rho^2$, which may be written $k'^{\frac{1}{2}} \rho'^2$ and is there-<br />
fore clearly medial.

Euclid's proof proceeds thus. Let $x$, $\lambda x$ be the two medial straight lines<br />
commensurable in length.<br />
Therefore
$$x^2 : x \cdot \lambda x = x : \lambda x.$$
