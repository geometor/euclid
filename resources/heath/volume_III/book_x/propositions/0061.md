Two medial straight lines commensurable in square only are of the form<br />
$k^{\frac{1}{4}}\rho$, $\sqrt{\lambda} \,.\, k^{\frac{1}{4}}\rho$.

The rectangle contained by them is $\sqrt{\lambda} \,.\, k^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho^2$. Now this is in general<br />
*medial*; but, if $\sqrt{\lambda} = k' \sqrt{k}$, the rectangle is $k k'\rho^2$, which is *rational*.

Euclid's argument is as follows. Let us, for convenience, put $x$ for $k^{\frac{1}{4}}\rho$, so<br />
that the medial straight lines are $x$, $\sqrt{\lambda} \,.\, x$.

Form the areas $x^2$, $x \,.\, \sqrt{\lambda} \,.\, x$, $\lambda x^2$,<br />
and let these be respectively equal to $\sigma u$, $\sigma v$, $\sigma w$, where $\sigma$ is a rational<br />
straight line.

Since $x^2$, $\lambda x^2$ are *medial areas*,<br />
so are $\sigma u$, $\sigma w$,<br />
whence $u$, $w$ are respectively rational and $\smile \sigma$.
