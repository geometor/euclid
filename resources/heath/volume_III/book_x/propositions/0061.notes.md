## Textual / Lexical / Mathematical Observations
- **Euclidean Text**: Shows that the rectangle $FH, KL$ being rational implies that $HK^2$ is rational, whence $HK$ is rational. The rectangle $HN$ is either rational or medial according as $HK$ is commensurable or incommensurable in length with $FG$. Since $HN = AC$, $AC$ is either rational or medial.
- **Marginal References**: Euclid's text contains bracketed cross-references to previous books/propositions: `[X. 19]`, `[VI. 1]`, `[id.]`, `[VI. 1, V. 11]`, `[VI. 17]`, `[X. 19]`, and `[X. 21]`.
- **Algebraic Notation**: Heath introduces standard modern parameters $k^{\frac{1}{4}}\rho$ and $\sqrt{\lambda} \cdot k^{\frac{1}{4}}\rho$ for two medial straight lines commensurable in square only. He defines areas applied to a rational straight line $\sigma$: $\sigma u = x^2$, $\sigma v = x \cdot \sqrt{\lambda} \cdot x$, $\sigma w = \lambda x^2$.
- **Symbols**: The relation $\smile \sigma$ denotes incommensurability in length with $\sigma$ (arising from Proposition X. 22).
