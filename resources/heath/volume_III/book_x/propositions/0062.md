But
$$x^2 \frown \lambda x^2,$$
so that
$$\sigma u \frown \sigma w,$$
or
$$u \frown w \ \text{................................................(1).}$$

Therefore, $u$, $w$ being both rational, $u w$ is rational $\text{..........................(2).}$

Now
$$x^2 : \sqrt{\lambda} \,.\, x^2 = \sqrt{\lambda} \,.\, x^2 : \lambda x^2$$
or
$$\sigma u : \sigma v = \sigma v : \sigma w,$$
so that
$$u : v = v : w,$$
and
$$u w = v^2.$$

Hence, by (2), $v^2$, and therefore $v$, is rational $\text{.............................(3).}$

Now ($\alpha$) if $v \frown \sigma$, $\sigma v$ or $\sqrt{\lambda} \,.\, x^2$ is *rational*;
<br />
($\beta$) if $v \smile \sigma$, so that $v \sim \sigma$, $\sigma v$ or $\sqrt{\lambda} \,.\, x^2$ is *medial*.

### PROPOSITION 26.

<p class="enunciation">A medial area does not exceed a medial area by a rational<br />
area.</p>

For, if possible, let the medial area <span data-ref="0062.g1:< A B >">$AB$</span> exceed the medial<br />
area <span data-ref="0062.g1:< A C >">$AC$</span> by the rational area<br />
<span data-ref="0062.g1:< D B >">$DB$</span>,<br />
and let a rational straight line<br />
<span data-ref="0062.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> be set out;<br />
to <span data-ref="0062.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> let there be applied the<br />
rectangular parallelogram <span data-ref="0062.g1:< F H >">$FH$</span><br />
equal to <span data-ref="0062.g1:< A B >">$AB$</span>, producing <span data-ref="0062.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span> as<br />
breadth,<br />
and let the rectangle <span data-ref="0062.g1:< F G >">$FG$</span> equal to <span data-ref="0062.g1:< A C >">$AC$</span> be subtracted;
<p class="assertion">therefore the remainder <span data-ref="0062.g1:< B D >">$BD$</span> is equal to the remainder <span data-ref="0062.g1:< K H >">$KH$</span>.</p>

![Proposition 26 diagram](0062.g1.svg)

<p class="sub-clause">But <span data-ref="0062.g1:< D B >">$DB$</span> is rational;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0062.g1:< K H >">$KH$</span> is also rational.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">Since, then, each of the rectangles <span data-ref="0062.g1:< A B >">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0062.g1:< A C >">$AC$</span> is medial,<br />
and <span data-ref="0062.g1:< A B >">$AB$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0062.g1:< F H >">$FH$</span>, and <span data-ref="0062.g1:< A C >">$AC$</span> to <span data-ref="0062.g1:< F G >">$FG$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore each of the rectangles <span data-ref="0062.g1:< F H >">$FH$</span>, <span data-ref="0062.g1:< F G >">$FG$</span> is also medial.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">And they are applied to the rational straight line <span data-ref="0062.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore each of the straight lines <span data-ref="0062.g1:/ H E /">$HE$</span>, <span data-ref="0062.g1:/ E G /">$EG$</span> is rational and<br />
incommensurable in length with <span data-ref="0062.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>. <span class="justification">[X. 22]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">And, since [<span data-ref="0062.g1:< D B >">$DB$</span> is rational and is equal to <span data-ref="0062.g1:< K H >">$KH$</span>,<br />
therefore] <span data-ref="0062.g1:< K H >">$KH$</span> is [also] rational;<br />
and it is applied to the rational straight line <span data-ref="0062.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>;</p>
