"Apply" the two given medial areas to one and the same rational straight<br />
line $\rho$. They can then be written in the form $\rho \,.\, k^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho$, $\rho \,.\, \lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho$.

The difference is then $(\sqrt{k} - \sqrt{\lambda})\rho^2$; and the proposition asserts that this<br />
cannot be rational, i.e. $(\sqrt{k} - \sqrt{\lambda})$ cannot be equal to $k'$. Cf. the proposition<br />
corresponding to this in algebraical text-books.

To make Euclid's proof clear we will put $x$ for $k^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho$ and $y$ for $\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho$.
Suppose
$$\rho(x - y) = \rho z,$$
and, if possible, let $\rho z$ be rational, so that $z$ must be rational and $\frown \rho \text{ ...(1).}$

Since $\rho x$, $\rho y$ are medial,
$$x \text{ and } y \text{ are respectively rational and } \smile \rho \text{ .................(2).}$$
From (1) and (2),
$$y \smile z.$$
Now
$$y : z = y^2 : yz,$$
so that
$$y^2 \smile yz.$$
