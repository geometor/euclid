Euclid takes two rational straight lines commensurable in square only, say
$\rho$, $k^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho$.
Find the mean proportional, i.e. $k^{\frac{1}{4}}\rho$.
Take $x$ such that
$$\rho : k^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho = k^{\frac{1}{4}}\rho : x \ \text{.........................................(1).}$$
This gives $x = k^{\frac{3}{4}}\rho$,
and the lines required are $k^{\frac{1}{4}}\rho$, $k^{\frac{3}{4}}\rho$.
For ($\alpha$) $k^{\frac{1}{4}}\rho$ is medial.
And ($\beta$), by (1), since $\rho \sim k^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho$,
$$k^{\frac{1}{4}}\rho \sim k^{\frac{3}{4}}\rho,$$
whence [addition to X. 23], since $k^{\frac{1}{4}}\rho$ is medial,
$k^{\frac{3}{4}}\rho$ is also medial.

The medial straight lines thus found may take either of the forms
$$(1) \quad \sqrt{a\sqrt{B}}, \quad \sqrt{\frac{B\sqrt{B}}{a}} \quad \text{or} \quad (2) \quad \sqrt[4]{AB}, \quad \sqrt{B\sqrt{\frac{B}{A}}}.$$

### PROPOSITION 28.

*To find medial straight lines commensurable in square only
which contain a medial rectangle.*

Let the rational straight lines <span data-ref="0065.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0065.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0065.g1:C">$C$</span> commensurable in
square only be set out;
let <span data-ref="0065.g1:D">$D$</span> be taken a mean proportional between <span data-ref="0065.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0065.g1:B">$B$</span>, [VI. 13]
and let it be contrived that,
<p style="text-align: center;">as <span data-ref="0065.g1:B">$B$</span> is to <span data-ref="0065.g1:C">$C$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0065.g1:D">$D$</span> to <span data-ref="0065.g1:E">$E$</span>. <span style="float: right;">[VI. 12]</span></p>

![Proposition 28 diagram](0065.g1.svg)

Since <span data-ref="0065.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0065.g1:B">$B$</span> are rational straight lines commensurable in
square only,
therefore the rectangle <span data-ref="0065.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0065.g1:B">$B$</span>, that is, the square on <span data-ref="0065.g1:D">$D$</span> [VI. 17],
is medial. [x. 21]
