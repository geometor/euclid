<p class="assertion">Therefore <span data-ref="0065.g1:D">$D$</span> is medial. <span class="justification">[X. 21]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">And since <span data-ref="0065.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0065.g1:C">$C$</span> are commensurable in square only,<br />
and, as <span data-ref="0065.g1:B">$B$</span> is to <span data-ref="0065.g1:C">$C$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0065.g1:D">$D$</span> to <span data-ref="0065.g1:E">$E$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0065.g1:D">$D$</span>, <span data-ref="0065.g1:E">$E$</span> are also commensurable in square only. <span class="justification">[X. 11]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But <span data-ref="0065.g1:D">$D$</span> is medial;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0065.g1:E">$E$</span> is also medial. <span class="justification">[X. 23, addition]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore <span data-ref="0065.g1:D">$D$</span>, <span data-ref="0065.g1:E">$E$</span> are medial straight lines commensurable<br />
in square only.</p>

I say next that they also contain a medial rectangle.

<p class="sub-clause">For since, as <span data-ref="0065.g1:B">$B$</span> is to <span data-ref="0065.g1:C">$C$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0065.g1:D">$D$</span> to <span data-ref="0065.g1:E">$E$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore, alternately, as <span data-ref="0065.g1:B">$B$</span> is to <span data-ref="0065.g1:D">$D$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0065.g1:C">$C$</span> to <span data-ref="0065.g1:E">$E$</span>. <span class="justification">[V. 16]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But, as <span data-ref="0065.g1:B">$B$</span> is to <span data-ref="0065.g1:D">$D$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0065.g1:D">$D$</span> to <span data-ref="0065.g1:A">$A$</span>;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore also, as <span data-ref="0065.g1:D">$D$</span> is to <span data-ref="0065.g1:A">$A$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0065.g1:C">$C$</span> to <span data-ref="0065.g1:E">$E$</span>;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the rectangle <span data-ref="0065.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0065.g1:C">$C$</span> is equal to the rectangle <span data-ref="0065.g1:D">$D$</span>, <span data-ref="0065.g1:E">$E$</span>.<br />
<span class="justification">[VI. 16]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But the rectangle <span data-ref="0065.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0065.g1:C">$C$</span> is medial; <span class="justification">[X. 21]</span></p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the rectangle <span data-ref="0065.g1:D">$D$</span>, <span data-ref="0065.g1:E">$E$</span> is also medial.</p>

<p class="qed">Therefore medial straight lines commensurable in square<br />
only have been found which contain a medial rectangle.<span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

Euclid takes three straight lines commensurable in square only, i.e. of the<br />
form $\rho$, $k^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho$, $\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho$, and proceeds as follows.<br />
Take the mean proportional to $\rho$, $k^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho$, i.e. $k^{\frac{1}{4}}\rho$.<br />
Then take $x$ such that
$$k^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho : \lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho = k^{\frac{1}{4}}\rho : x \ \text{.........................................(1),}$$
so that $x = \lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho / k^{\frac{1}{4}}$.<br />
$k^{\frac{1}{4}}\rho$, $\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho / k^{\frac{1}{4}}$ are the required medial straight lines.<br />
For $k^{\frac{1}{4}}\rho$ is medial.<br />
Now, by (1), since $k^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho \sim \lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho$,
$$k^{\frac{1}{4}}\rho \sim x,$$
whence $x$ is also medial [X. 23, addition], while $\sim k^{\frac{1}{4}}\rho$.<br />
Next, by (1),
$$\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho : x = k^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho : k^{\frac{1}{4}}\rho$$
$$= k^{\frac{1}{4}}\rho : \rho,$$
whence
$$x \,.\, k^{\frac{1}{4}}\rho = \lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho^2\text{, which is medial.}$$

The straight lines $k^{\frac{1}{4}}\rho$, $\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho / k^{\frac{1}{4}}$ of course take different forms according as<br />
the original straight lines are of the forms (1) $a$, $\sqrt{B}$, $\sqrt{C}$, (2) $\sqrt{A}$, $\sqrt{B}$, $\sqrt{C}$,<br />
(3) $\sqrt{A}$, $b$, $\sqrt{C}$, and (4) $\sqrt{A}$, $\sqrt{B}$, $c$.
