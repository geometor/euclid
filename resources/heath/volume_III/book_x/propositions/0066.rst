   Therefore :g:`$D$ <0065.g1:D>` is medial.

.. subclause::

   And since :g:`$B$ <0065.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0065.g1:C>` are commensurable in square only, and, as :g:`$B$ <0065.g1:B>` is to :g:`$C$ <0065.g1:C>`, so is :g:`$D$ <0065.g1:D>` to :g:`$E$ <0065.g1:E>`,

.. assertion:: [X. 11] <X.11>

   therefore :g:`$D$ <0065.g1:D>`, :g:`$E$ <0065.g1:E>` are also commensurable in square only.

.. subclause::

   But :g:`$D$ <0065.g1:D>` is medial;

.. assertion:: [X. 23, addition]

   therefore :g:`$E$ <0065.g1:E>` is also medial.

.. assertion::

   Therefore :g:`$D$ <0065.g1:D>`, :g:`$E$ <0065.g1:E>` are medial straight lines commensurable in square only.

I say next that they also contain a medial rectangle.

.. subclause::

   For since, as :g:`$B$ <0065.g1:B>` is to :g:`$C$ <0065.g1:C>`, so is :g:`$D$ <0065.g1:D>` to :g:`$E$ <0065.g1:E>`,

.. assertion:: [V. 16] <V.16>

   therefore, alternately, as :g:`$B$ <0065.g1:B>` is to :g:`$D$ <0065.g1:D>`, so is :g:`$C$ <0065.g1:C>` to :g:`$E$ <0065.g1:E>`.

.. subclause::

   But, as :g:`$B$ <0065.g1:B>` is to :g:`$D$ <0065.g1:D>`, so is :g:`$D$ <0065.g1:D>` to :g:`$A$ <0065.g1:A>`;

.. assertion::

   therefore also, as :g:`$D$ <0065.g1:D>` is to :g:`$A$ <0065.g1:A>`, so is :g:`$C$ <0065.g1:C>` to :g:`$E$ <0065.g1:E>`;

.. assertion::

   therefore the rectangle :g:`$A$ <0065.g1:A>`, :g:`$C$ <0065.g1:C>` is equal to the rectangle :g:`$D$ <0065.g1:D>`, :g:`$E$ <0065.g1:E>`. :just:`[VI. 16] <VI.16>`

.. subclause:: [X. 21] <X.21>

   But the rectangle :g:`$A$ <0065.g1:A>`, :g:`$C$ <0065.g1:C>` is medial;

.. assertion::

   therefore the rectangle :g:`$D$ <0065.g1:D>`, :g:`$E$ <0065.g1:E>` is also medial.

.. qed::

   Therefore medial straight lines commensurable in square only have been found which contain a medial rectangle. :just:`Q. E. D.`

----

Euclid takes three straight lines commensurable in square only, i.e. of the form $\rho$, $k^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho$, $\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho$, and proceeds as follows. Take the mean proportional to $\rho$, $k^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho$, i.e. $k^{\frac{1}{4}}\rho$. Then take $x$ such that

$$k^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho : \lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho = k^{\frac{1}{4}}\rho : x \ \text{.........................................(1),}$$

so that $x = \lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho / k^{\frac{1}{4}}$. $k^{\frac{1}{4}}\rho$, $\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho / k^{\frac{1}{4}}$ are the required medial straight lines. For $k^{\frac{1}{4}}\rho$ is medial. Now, by (1), since $k^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho \sim \lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho$,

$$k^{\frac{1}{4}}\rho \sim x,$$

whence $x$ is also medial [X. 23, addition], while $\sim k^{\frac{1}{4}}\rho$. Next, by (1),

$$\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho : x = k^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho : k^{\frac{1}{4}}\rho$$

$$= k^{\frac{1}{4}}\rho : \rho,$$

whence

$$x \,.\, k^{\frac{1}{4}}\rho = \lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho^2\text{, which is medial.}$$

The straight lines $k^{\frac{1}{4}}\rho$, $\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho / k^{\frac{1}{4}}$ of course take different forms according as the original straight lines are of the forms (1) $a$, $\sqrt{B}$, $\sqrt{C}$, (2) $\sqrt{A}$, $\sqrt{B}$, $\sqrt{C}$,

(3) $\sqrt{A}$, $b$, $\sqrt{C}$, and (4) $\sqrt{A}$, $\sqrt{B}$, $c$.
