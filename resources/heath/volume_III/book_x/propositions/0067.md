### LEMMA I.

*To find two square numbers such that their sum is also
square.*

Let two numbers <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> be set out, and let them be
either both even or both odd.

Then since, whether an even number is subtracted from an
even number, or an odd number from an odd number, the
remainder is even, [IX. 24, 26]
therefore the remainder <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is even.

![Lemma 1 diagram](0067.g1.svg)

Let <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> be bisected at <span data-ref="0067.g1:D">$D$</span>.

Let <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> also be either similar plane numbers, or
square numbers, which are themselves also similar plane
numbers.

Now the product of <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> together with the square on
<span data-ref="0067.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> is equal to the square on <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>. [II. 6]

And the product of <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is square, inasmuch as it
was proved that, if two similar plane numbers by multiplying
one another make some number the product is square. [IX. 1]

Therefore two square numbers, the product of <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>,
and the square on <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, have been found which, when added
together, make the square on <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>.

And it is manifest that two square numbers, the square
on <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> and the square on <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, have again been found such
that their difference, the product of <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, is a square,
whenever <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> are similar plane numbers.

But when they are not similar plane numbers, two square
numbers, the square on <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> and the square on <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ D C /">$DC$</span>, have been
found such that their difference, the product of <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, is
not square.

Q. E. D.
