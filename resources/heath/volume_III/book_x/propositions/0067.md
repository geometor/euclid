### LEMMA I.

<p class="enunciation">To find two square numbers such that their sum is also<br />
square.</p>

Let two numbers <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> be set out, and let them be<br />
either both even or both odd.

<p class="sub-clause">Then since, whether an even number is subtracted from an<br />
even number, or an odd number from an odd number, the<br />
remainder is even, <span class="justification">[IX. 24, 26]</span></p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the remainder <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is even.</p>

![Lemma 1 diagram](0067.g1.svg)

Let <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> be bisected at <span data-ref="0067.g1:D">$D$</span>.

Let <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> also be either similar plane numbers, or<br />
square numbers, which are themselves also similar plane<br />
numbers.

<p class="sub-clause">Now the product of <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> together with the square on<br />
<span data-ref="0067.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> is equal to the square on <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>. <span class="justification">[II. 6]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">And the product of <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is square, inasmuch as it<br />
was proved that, if two similar plane numbers by multiplying<br />
one another make some number the product is square. <span class="justification">[IX. 1]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore two square numbers, the product of <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>,<br />
and the square on <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, have been found which, when added<br />
together, make the square on <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>.</p>

<p class="assertion">And it is manifest that two square numbers, the square<br />
on <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> and the square on <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, have again been found such<br />
that their difference, the product of <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, is a square,<br />
whenever <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> are similar plane numbers.</p>

<p class="assertion">But when they are not similar plane numbers, two square<br />
numbers, the square on <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> and the square on <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ D C /">$DC$</span>, have been<br />
found such that their difference, the product of <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0067.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, is<br />
not square.</p>

<p class="qed"><span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>
