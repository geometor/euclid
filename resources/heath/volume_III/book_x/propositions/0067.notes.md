## Textual / Lexical / Mathematical Observations
- **Lemma 1 Enunciation**: "To find two square numbers such that their sum is also square."
- **Pythagorean Triples Formulation**: Euclid uses numbers represented by line segments: $AB, BC$ both even or both odd, with remainder $AC$ even, bisected at $D$. By II. 6 (applied arithmetically), $AB \cdot BC + CD^2 = BD^2$. If $AB, BC$ are similar plane numbers, $AB \cdot BC$ is a square (IX. 1), yielding $CD^2 + (AB \cdot BC) = BD^2$, two square numbers whose sum is square.
- **Porism / Corollaries**: The lemma also establishes how to find two squares whose difference is a square, or whose difference is not a square.
- **Heath's Commentary**: Connects this construction to the arithmetic method of generating right-angled triangles in integers (Pythagorean triples), citing I. 47 and introducing parameterization $mnp^2, mnq^2$.
