### Lemma 2.

<p class="enunciation">To find two square numbers such that their sum is not<br />
square.</p>

For let the product of <span data-ref="0068.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0068.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, as we said, be square,<br />
and <span data-ref="0068.g1:/ C A /">$CA$</span> even,<br />
and let <span data-ref="0068.g1:/ C A /">$CA$</span> be bisected by <span data-ref="0068.g1:D">$D$</span>.

![Lemma 2](0068.g1.svg)

<p class="assertion">It is then manifest that the square product of <span data-ref="0068.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0068.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span><br />
together with the square on <span data-ref="0068.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> is equal to the square on <span data-ref="0068.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>. <span class="justification">[See Lemma 1]</span></p>

Let the unit <span data-ref="0068.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> be subtracted;

<p class="assertion">therefore the product of <span data-ref="0068.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0068.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> together with the square on<br />
<span data-ref="0068.g1:/ C E /">$CE$</span> is less than the square on <span data-ref="0068.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>.</p>

I say then that the square product of <span data-ref="0068.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0068.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> together<br />
with the square on <span data-ref="0068.g1:/ C E /">$CE$</span> will not be square.

<p class="sub-clause">For, if it is square, it is either equal to the square on <span data-ref="0068.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span>,<br />
or less than the square on <span data-ref="0068.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span>, but cannot any more be<br />
greater, lest the unit be divided.</p>

First, if possible, let the product of <span data-ref="0068.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0068.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> together<br />
with the square on <span data-ref="0068.g1:/ C E /">$CE$</span> be equal to the square on <span data-ref="0068.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span>,<br />
and let <span data-ref="0068.g1:/ G A /">$GA$</span> be double of the unit <span data-ref="0068.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>.

<p class="sub-clause">Since then the whole <span data-ref="0068.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is double of the whole <span data-ref="0068.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>,<br />
and in them <span data-ref="0068.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span> is double of <span data-ref="0068.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>,</p>

<p class="assertion">therefore the remainder <span data-ref="0068.g1:/ G C /">$GC$</span> is also double of the remainder <span data-ref="0068.g1:/ E C /">$EC$</span>;<br />
therefore <span data-ref="0068.g1:/ G C /">$GC$</span> is bisected by <span data-ref="0068.g1:E">$E$</span>.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore the product of <span data-ref="0068.g1:/ G B /">$GB$</span>, <span data-ref="0068.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> together with the square<br />
on <span data-ref="0068.g1:/ C E /">$CE$</span> is equal to the square on <span data-ref="0068.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span>. <span class="justification">[II. 6]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But the product of <span data-ref="0068.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0068.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> together with the square on<br />
<span data-ref="0068.g1:/ C E /">$CE$</span> is also, by hypothesis, equal to the square on <span data-ref="0068.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span>;</p>
