Lemma 2.
~~~~~~~~

.. enunciation::

   To find two square numbers such that their sum is not square.

| For let the product of :g:`$AB$ <0068.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$BC$ <0068.g1:/ B C />`, as we said, be square,
| and :g:`$CA$ <0068.g1:/ C A />` even,
| and let :g:`$CA$ <0068.g1:/ C A />` be bisected by :g:`$D$ <0068.g1:D>`.

.. picture:: 0068.g1.svg
   :alt: Lemma 2

.. assertion::

   | It is then manifest that the square product of :g:`$AB$ <0068.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$BC$ <0068.g1:/ B C />`
   | together with the square on :g:`$CD$ <0068.g1:/ C D />` is equal to the square on :g:`$BD$ <0068.g1:/ B D />`. :just:`[See Lemma 1]`

Let the unit :g:`$DE$ <0068.g1:/ D E />` be subtracted;

.. assertion::

   | therefore the product of :g:`$AB$ <0068.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$BC$ <0068.g1:/ B C />` together with the square on
   | :g:`$CE$ <0068.g1:/ C E />` is less than the square on :g:`$BD$ <0068.g1:/ B D />`.

| I say then that the square product of :g:`$AB$ <0068.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$BC$ <0068.g1:/ B C />` together
| with the square on :g:`$CE$ <0068.g1:/ C E />` will not be square.

.. subclause::

   | For, if it is square, it is either equal to the square on :g:`$BE$ <0068.g1:/ B E />`,
   | or less than the square on :g:`$BE$ <0068.g1:/ B E />`, but cannot any more be
   | greater, lest the unit be divided.

| First, if possible, let the product of :g:`$AB$ <0068.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$BC$ <0068.g1:/ B C />` together
| with the square on :g:`$CE$ <0068.g1:/ C E />` be equal to the square on :g:`$BE$ <0068.g1:/ B E />`,
| and let :g:`$GA$ <0068.g1:/ G A />` be double of the unit :g:`$DE$ <0068.g1:/ D E />`.

.. subclause::

   | Since then the whole :g:`$AC$ <0068.g1:/ A C />` is double of the whole :g:`$CD$ <0068.g1:/ C D />`,
   | and in them :g:`$AG$ <0068.g1:/ A G />` is double of :g:`$DE$ <0068.g1:/ D E />`,

.. assertion::

   | therefore the remainder :g:`$GC$ <0068.g1:/ G C />` is also double of the remainder :g:`$EC$ <0068.g1:/ E C />`;
   | therefore :g:`$GC$ <0068.g1:/ G C />` is bisected by :g:`$E$ <0068.g1:E>`.

.. assertion::

   | Therefore the product of :g:`$GB$ <0068.g1:/ G B />`, :g:`$BC$ <0068.g1:/ B C />` together with the square
   | on :g:`$CE$ <0068.g1:/ C E />` is equal to the square on :g:`$BE$ <0068.g1:/ B E />`. :just:`[II. 6] <II.6>`

.. subclause::

   | But the product of :g:`$AB$ <0068.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$BC$ <0068.g1:/ B C />` together with the square on
   | :g:`$CE$ <0068.g1:/ C E />` is also, by hypothesis, equal to the square on :g:`$BE$ <0068.g1:/ B E />`;
