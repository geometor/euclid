and let it be contrived that, as :g:`DC <0071.g1:/ D C />` is to :g:`CE <0071.g1:/ C E />`, so is the square on :g:`BA <0071.g1:/ B A />` to the square on :g:`AF <0071.g1:/ A F />`. :just:`[X. 6, Por.]` Let :g:`FB <0071.g1:/ F B />` be joined.

   :alt: Proposition 29

.. subclause::

   Since, as the square on :g:`BA <0071.g1:/ B A />` is to the square on :g:`AF <0071.g1:/ A F />`, so is :g:`DC <0071.g1:/ D C />` to :g:`CE <0071.g1:/ C E />`,

.. assertion::

   therefore the square on :g:`BA <0071.g1:/ B A />` has to the square on :g:`AF <0071.g1:/ A F />` the ratio which the number :g:`DC <0071.g1:/ D C />` has to the number :g:`CE <0071.g1:/ C E />`; therefore the square on :g:`BA <0071.g1:/ B A />` is commensurable with the square on :g:`AF <0071.g1:/ A F />`. :just:`[X. 6] <X.6>`

.. subclause:: [X. Def. 4] <X.def.4>

   But the square on :g:`AB <0071.g1:/ A B />` is rational;

.. assertion::

   therefore the square on :g:`AF <0071.g1:/ A F />` is also rational; :just:`[id.]` therefore :g:`AF <0071.g1:/ A F />` is also rational.

.. subclause::

   And, since :g:`DC <0071.g1:/ D C />` has not to :g:`CE <0071.g1:/ C E />` the ratio which a square number has to a square number,

.. assertion::

   neither has the square on :g:`BA <0071.g1:/ B A />` to the square on :g:`AF <0071.g1:/ A F />` the ratio which a square number has to a square number; therefore :g:`AB <0071.g1:/ A B />` is incommensurable in length with :g:`AF <0071.g1:/ A F />`. :just:`[X. 9] <X.9>`

.. assertion::

   Therefore :g:`BA <0071.g1:/ B A />`, :g:`AF <0071.g1:/ A F />` are rational straight lines commensurable in square only.

.. subclause::

   And since, as :g:`DC <0071.g1:/ D C />` is to :g:`CE <0071.g1:/ C E />`, so is the square on :g:`BA <0071.g1:/ B A />` to the square on :g:`AF <0071.g1:/ A F />`,

.. assertion::

   therefore, :term:`*convertendo* <convertendo>`, as :g:`CD <0071.g1:/ C D />` is to :g:`DE <0071.g1:/ E D />`, so is the square on :g:`AB <0071.g1:/ A B />` to the square on :g:`BF <0071.g1:/ B F />`. :just:`[V. 19, Por., III. 31, I. 47]`

.. subclause::

   But :g:`CD <0071.g1:/ C D />` has to :g:`DE <0071.g1:/ E D />` the ratio which a square number has to a square number;

.. assertion::

   therefore also the square on :g:`AB <0071.g1:/ A B />` has to the square on :g:`BF <0071.g1:/ B F />` the ratio which a square number has to a square number; therefore :g:`AB <0071.g1:/ A B />` is commensurable in length with :g:`BF <0071.g1:/ B F />`. :just:`[X. 9] <X.9>`

.. subclause::

   And the square on :g:`AB <0071.g1:/ A B />` is equal to the squares on :g:`AF <0071.g1:/ A F />`, :g:`FB <0071.g1:/ F B />`;

.. assertion::

   therefore the square on :g:`AB <0071.g1:/ A B />` is greater than the square on :g:`AF <0071.g1:/ A F />` by the square on :g:`BF <0071.g1:/ B F />` commensurable with :g:`AB <0071.g1:/ A B />`.

.. qed::

   Therefore there have been found two rational straight lines :g:`BA <0071.g1:/ B A />`, :g:`AF <0071.g1:/ A F />` commensurable in square only and such that the square on the greater :g:`AB <0071.g1:/ A B />` is greater than the square on the less :g:`AF <0071.g1:/ A F />` by the square on :g:`BF <0071.g1:/ B F />` commensurable in length with :g:`AB <0071.g1:/ A B />`.
