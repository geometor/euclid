| Take a rational straight line $\rho$ and two numbers $m^2$, $n^2$ such that $(m^2 - n^2)$
| is not a square.

Take a straight line $x$ such that

$$m^2 : m^2 - n^2 = \rho^2 : x^2 \tag{1},$$

whence

$$x^2 = \frac{m^2 - n^2}{m^2}\rho^2,$$

and

$$x = \rho\sqrt{1 - k^2}, \quad \text{where } k = \frac{n}{m}.$$

Then $\rho$, $\rho\sqrt{1 - k^2}$ are the straight lines required.

It follows from (1) that

$$x^2 \sim \rho^2,$$

and $x$ is rational, but

$$x \smile \rho.$$

By (1), :term:`*convertendo* <convertendo>`,

$$m^2 : n^2 = \rho^2 : \rho^2 - x^2,$$

so that $\sqrt{\rho^2 - x^2} \sim \rho$, and in fact $= k\rho$.

| According as $\rho$ is of the form $a$ or $\sqrt{A}$, the straight lines are (1) $a$, $\sqrt{a^2 - b^2}$
| or (2) $\sqrt{A}$, $\sqrt{A - k^2 A}$.

PROPOSITION 30.
---------------

.. enunciation::

   To find two rational straight lines commensurable in square only and such that the square on the greater is greater than the square on the less by the square on a straight line incom- mensurable in length with the greater.

| Let there be set out a rational straight line :g:`$AB$ <0072.g1:/ A B />`,
| and two square numbers :g:`$CE$ <0072.g1:/ C E />`, :g:`$ED$ <0072.g1:/ E D />`
| such that their sum :g:`$CD$ <0072.g1:/ C D />` is not
| square; :just:`[Lemma 2]`
| let there be described on :g:`$AB$ <0072.g1:/ A B />` the
| semicircle :g:`$AFB$ <0072.g1:( A F B )>`,
| let it be contrived that,
| as :g:`$DC$ <0072.g1:/ D C />` is to :g:`$CE$ <0072.g1:/ C E />`, so is the square
| on :g:`$BA$ <0072.g1:/ B A />` to the square on :g:`$AF$ <0072.g1:/ A F />`,
