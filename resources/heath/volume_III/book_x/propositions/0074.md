Let the rectangle <span data-ref="0073.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0073.g1:D">$D$</span> be equal to the square on <span data-ref="0073.g1:B">$B$</span>.

Now the square on <span data-ref="0073.g1:B">$B$</span> is rational; therefore the rectangle <span data-ref="0073.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0073.g1:D">$D$</span> is also rational.

And since, as <span data-ref="0073.g1:A">$A$</span> is to <span data-ref="0073.g1:B">$B$</span>, so is the rectangle <span data-ref="0073.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0073.g1:B">$B$</span> to the square on <span data-ref="0073.g1:B">$B$</span>, while the square on <span data-ref="0073.g1:C">$C$</span> is equal to the rectangle <span data-ref="0073.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0073.g1:B">$B$</span>, and the rectangle <span data-ref="0073.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0073.g1:D">$D$</span> is equal to the square on <span data-ref="0073.g1:B">$B$</span>, therefore, as <span data-ref="0073.g1:A">$A$</span> is to <span data-ref="0073.g1:B">$B$</span>, so is the square on <span data-ref="0073.g1:C">$C$</span> to the rectangle <span data-ref="0073.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0073.g1:D">$D$</span>.

But, as the square on <span data-ref="0073.g1:C">$C$</span> is to the rectangle <span data-ref="0073.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0073.g1:D">$D$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0073.g1:C">$C$</span> to <span data-ref="0073.g1:D">$D$</span>; therefore also, as <span data-ref="0073.g1:A">$A$</span> is to <span data-ref="0073.g1:B">$B$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0073.g1:C">$C$</span> to <span data-ref="0073.g1:D">$D$</span>.

But <span data-ref="0073.g1:A">$A$</span> is commensurable with <span data-ref="0073.g1:B">$B$</span> in square only; therefore <span data-ref="0073.g1:C">$C$</span> is also commensurable with <span data-ref="0073.g1:D">$D$</span> in square only. [x. 11]

And <span data-ref="0073.g1:C">$C$</span> is medial; therefore <span data-ref="0073.g1:D">$D$</span> is also medial. [x. 23, addition]

And since, as <span data-ref="0073.g1:A">$A$</span> is to <span data-ref="0073.g1:B">$B$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0073.g1:C">$C$</span> to <span data-ref="0073.g1:D">$D$</span>, and the square on <span data-ref="0073.g1:A">$A$</span> is greater than the square on <span data-ref="0073.g1:B">$B$</span> by the square on a straight line commensurable with <span data-ref="0073.g1:A">$A$</span>, therefore also the square on <span data-ref="0073.g1:C">$C$</span> is greater than the square on <span data-ref="0073.g1:D">$D$</span> by the square on a straight line commensurable with <span data-ref="0073.g1:C">$C$</span>. [x. 14]

Therefore two medial straight lines <span data-ref="0073.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0073.g1:D">$D$</span>, commensurable in square only and containing a rational rectangle, have been found, and the square on <span data-ref="0073.g1:C">$C$</span> is greater than the square on <span data-ref="0073.g1:D">$D$</span> by the square on a straight line commensurable in length with <span data-ref="0073.g1:C">$C$</span>.

Similarly also it can be proved that the square on <span data-ref="0073.g1:C">$C$</span> exceeds the square on <span data-ref="0073.g1:D">$D$</span> by the square on a straight line incommensurable with <span data-ref="0073.g1:C">$C$</span>, when the square on <span data-ref="0073.g1:A">$A$</span> is greater than the square on <span data-ref="0073.g1:B">$B$</span> by the square on a straight line incommensurable with <span data-ref="0073.g1:A">$A$</span>. [x. 30]

Take the mean proportional $\rho(1 - k^2)^\frac{1}{4}$ and $x$ such that
$$\rho(1 - k^2)^\frac{1}{4} : \rho\sqrt{1 - k^2} = \rho\sqrt{1 - k^2} : x.$$

Then $\rho(1 - k^2)^\frac{1}{4}$, $x$, or $\rho(1 - k^2)^\frac{1}{4}$, $\rho(1 - k^2)^\frac{3}{4}$ are straight lines satisfying the given conditions.
