Let the rectangle <span data-ref="0073.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0073.g1:D">$D$</span> be equal to the square on <span data-ref="0073.g1:B">$B$</span>.

<p class="sub-clause">Now the square on <span data-ref="0073.g1:B">$B$</span> is rational ;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the rectangle <span data-ref="0073.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0073.g1:D">$D$</span> is also rational.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">And since, as <span data-ref="0073.g1:A">$A$</span> is to <span data-ref="0073.g1:B">$B$</span>, so is the rectangle <span data-ref="0073.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0073.g1:B">$B$</span> to the<br />
square on <span data-ref="0073.g1:B">$B$</span>,<br />
while the square on <span data-ref="0073.g1:C">$C$</span> is equal to the rectangle <span data-ref="0073.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0073.g1:B">$B$</span>,<br />
and the rectangle <span data-ref="0073.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0073.g1:D">$D$</span> is equal to the square on <span data-ref="0073.g1:B">$B$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore, as <span data-ref="0073.g1:A">$A$</span> is to <span data-ref="0073.g1:B">$B$</span>, so is the square on <span data-ref="0073.g1:C">$C$</span> to the rectangle<br />
<span data-ref="0073.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0073.g1:D">$D$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">But, as the square on <span data-ref="0073.g1:C">$C$</span> is to the rectangle <span data-ref="0073.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0073.g1:D">$D$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0073.g1:C">$C$</span><br />
to <span data-ref="0073.g1:D">$D$</span> ;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore also, as <span data-ref="0073.g1:A">$A$</span> is to <span data-ref="0073.g1:B">$B$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0073.g1:C">$C$</span> to <span data-ref="0073.g1:D">$D$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">But <span data-ref="0073.g1:A">$A$</span> is commensurable with <span data-ref="0073.g1:B">$B$</span> in square only ;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0073.g1:C">$C$</span> is also commensurable with <span data-ref="0073.g1:D">$D$</span> in square only. <span class="justification">[X. 11]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">And <span data-ref="0073.g1:C">$C$</span> is medial ;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0073.g1:D">$D$</span> is also medial. <span class="justification">[X. 23, addition]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">And since, as <span data-ref="0073.g1:A">$A$</span> is to <span data-ref="0073.g1:B">$B$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0073.g1:C">$C$</span> to <span data-ref="0073.g1:D">$D$</span>,<br />
and the square on <span data-ref="0073.g1:A">$A$</span> is greater than the square on <span data-ref="0073.g1:B">$B$</span> by the<br />
square on a straight line commensurable with <span data-ref="0073.g1:A">$A$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore also the square on <span data-ref="0073.g1:C">$C$</span> is greater than the square on<br />
<span data-ref="0073.g1:D">$D$</span> by the square on a straight line commensurable with <span data-ref="0073.g1:C">$C$</span>.<br />
<span class="justification">[X. 14]</span></p>

<p class="qed">Therefore two medial straight lines <span data-ref="0073.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0073.g1:D">$D$</span>, commensurable<br />
in square only and containing a rational rectangle, have been<br />

by the square on a straight line commensurable in length<br />
with <span data-ref="0073.g1:C">$C$</span>.</p>

<p class="assertion">Similarly also it can be proved that the square on <span data-ref="0073.g1:C">$C$</span><br />

I. Take the rational straight lines commensurable in square only found<br />
in x. 29, i.e. $\rho$, $\rho\sqrt{1 - k^2}$.

Take the mean proportional $\rho(1 - k^2)^\frac{1}{4}$ and $x$ such that
$$\rho(1 - k^2)^\frac{1}{4} : \rho\sqrt{1 - k^2} = \rho\sqrt{1 - k^2} : x.$$

Then $\rho(1 - k^2)^\frac{1}{4}$, $x$, or $\rho(1 - k^2)^\frac{1}{4}$, $\rho(1 - k^2)^\frac{3}{4}$ are straight lines satisfying the<br />
given conditions.
