## 2. Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Mathematical Steps in Proposition 31**:
  - $C \cdot D = B^2$ (rational).
  - $A : B = (A \cdot B) : B^2 = C^2 : (C \cdot D) = C : D$.
  - Since $A$ is commensurable with $B$ in square only, $C$ is commensurable with $D$ in square only (X. 11).
  - Since $C$ is medial, $D$ is also medial (X. 23, addition).
  - Since $A^2 - B^2$ is the square on a line commensurable with $A$, by X. 14 $C^2 - D^2$ is the square on a line commensurable with $C$.
  - Adds the counterpart observation: if $A, B$ were chosen via X. 30 (incommensurable difference), $C^2 - D^2$ would be incommensurable with $C$.
- **Heath's Commentary**:
  - Formulates algebraically: starting from $\rho$ and $\rho\sqrt{1 - k^2}$, takes $C = \rho(1 - k^2)^{1/4}$ and finds $D = \rho(1 - k^2)^{3/4}$.
  - Note the fractional powers $(1 - k^2)^{1/4}$ and $(1 - k^2)^{3/4}$.
- **Citations**: `[X. 11]`, `[X. 23, addition]`, `[X. 14]`, `[X. 30]`, `[X. 29]`.
