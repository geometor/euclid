:title: Book X: Proposition 31 (cont.)

Let the rectangle :g:`$C$ <0073.g1:C>`, :g:`$D$ <0073.g1:D>` be equal to the square on :g:`$B$ <0073.g1:B>`.

.. subclause::

   Now the square on :g:`$B$ <0073.g1:B>` is rational ;

.. assertion::

   therefore the rectangle :g:`$C$ <0073.g1:C>`, :g:`$D$ <0073.g1:D>` is also rational.

.. subclause::

   And since, as :g:`$A$ <0073.g1:A>` is to :g:`$B$ <0073.g1:B>`, so is the rectangle :g:`$A$ <0073.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0073.g1:B>` to the square on :g:`$B$ <0073.g1:B>`, while the square on :g:`$C$ <0073.g1:C>` is equal to the rectangle :g:`$A$ <0073.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0073.g1:B>`, and the rectangle :g:`$C$ <0073.g1:C>`, :g:`$D$ <0073.g1:D>` is equal to the square on :g:`$B$ <0073.g1:B>`,

.. assertion::

   therefore, as :g:`$A$ <0073.g1:A>` is to :g:`$B$ <0073.g1:B>`, so is the square on :g:`$C$ <0073.g1:C>` to the rectangle :g:`$C$ <0073.g1:C>`, :g:`$D$ <0073.g1:D>`.

.. subclause::

   But, as the square on :g:`$C$ <0073.g1:C>` is to the rectangle :g:`$C$ <0073.g1:C>`, :g:`$D$ <0073.g1:D>`, so is :g:`$C$ <0073.g1:C>` to :g:`$D$ <0073.g1:D>` ;

.. assertion::

   therefore also, as :g:`$A$ <0073.g1:A>` is to :g:`$B$ <0073.g1:B>`, so is :g:`$C$ <0073.g1:C>` to :g:`$D$ <0073.g1:D>`.

.. subclause::

   But :g:`$A$ <0073.g1:A>` is commensurable with :g:`$B$ <0073.g1:B>` in square only ;

.. assertion:: [X. 11] <X.11>

   therefore :g:`$C$ <0073.g1:C>` is also commensurable with :g:`$D$ <0073.g1:D>` in square only.

.. subclause::

   And :g:`$C$ <0073.g1:C>` is medial ;

.. assertion:: [X. 23, addition]

   therefore :g:`$D$ <0073.g1:D>` is also medial.

.. subclause::

   And since, as :g:`$A$ <0073.g1:A>` is to :g:`$B$ <0073.g1:B>`, so is :g:`$C$ <0073.g1:C>` to :g:`$D$ <0073.g1:D>`, and the square on :g:`$A$ <0073.g1:A>` is greater than the square on :g:`$B$ <0073.g1:B>` by the square on a straight line commensurable with :g:`$A$ <0073.g1:A>`,

.. assertion::

   therefore also the square on :g:`$C$ <0073.g1:C>` is greater than the square on :g:`$D$ <0073.g1:D>` by the square on a straight line commensurable with :g:`$C$ <0073.g1:C>`. :just:`[X. 14] <X.14>`

.. qed::

   Therefore two medial straight lines :g:`$C$ <0073.g1:C>`, :g:`$D$ <0073.g1:D>`, commensurable in square only and containing a rational rectangle, have been found, and the square on :g:`$C$ <0073.g1:C>` is greater than the square on :g:`$D$ <0073.g1:D>` by the square on a straight line commensurable in length with :g:`$C$ <0073.g1:C>`.

.. assertion::

   Similarly also it can be proved that the square on :g:`$C$ <0073.g1:C>` exceeds the square on :g:`$D$ <0073.g1:D>` by the square on a straight line incommensurable with :g:`$C$ <0073.g1:C>`, when the square on :g:`$A$ <0073.g1:A>` is greater than the square on :g:`$B$ <0073.g1:B>` by the square on a straight line incommensurable with :g:`$A$ <0073.g1:A>`. :just:`[X. 30] <X.30>`

----

I. Take the rational straight lines commensurable in square only found in x. 29, i.e. $\rho$, $\rho\sqrt{1 - k^2}$.

Take the mean proportional $\rho(1 - k^2)^\frac{1}{4}$ and $x$ such that

$$\rho(1 - k^2)^\frac{1}{4} : \rho\sqrt{1 - k^2} = \rho\sqrt{1 - k^2} : x.$$

Then $\rho(1 - k^2)^\frac{1}{4}$, $x$, or $\rho(1 - k^2)^\frac{1}{4}$, $\rho(1 - k^2)^\frac{3}{4}$ are straight lines satisfying the given conditions.
