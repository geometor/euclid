:title: Proposition 32 (cont.)

.. qed::

   | square on the less by the square on a straight line commen-
   | surable with the greater.

.. assertion::

   | Similarly again it can be proved that the square on :g:`$D$ <0076.g1:D>`
   | is greater than the square on :g:`$E$ <0076.g1:E>` by the square on a straight
   | line *incommensurable* with :g:`$D$ <0076.g1:D>`, when the square on :g:`$A$ <0076.g1:A>` is
   | greater than the square on :g:`$C$ <0076.g1:C>` by the square on a straight line
   | incommensurable with :g:`$A$ <0076.g1:A>`. :just:`[X. 30] <X.30>`

----

| I. Euclid takes three straight lines of the form $\rho$, $\rho\sqrt{\lambda}$, $\rho\sqrt{1-k^2}$,
| takes the mean proportional $\rho\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}}$ between the first two ....................(1),
| and then finds $x$ such that

$$\rho\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}} : \rho\sqrt{\lambda} = \rho\sqrt{1-k^2} : x \text{ .........................................(2),}$$

| whence $x = \rho\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}}\sqrt{1-k^2}$,
| and the straight lines $\rho\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}}$, $\rho\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}}\sqrt{1-k^2}$ satisfy the given conditions.

Now ($\alpha$) $\rho\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}}$ is medial.

($\beta$) We have, from (1) and (2),

$$\rho : \rho\sqrt{1-k^2} = \rho\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}} : x \text{ .........................................(3),}$$

whence $x \mathbin{\sim} \rho\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}}$ ; and $x$ is therefore medial and $\mathbin{\sim} \rho\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}}$.

| ($\gamma$) $x\ .\ \rho\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}} = \rho\sqrt{\lambda}\ .\ \rho\sqrt{1-k^2}$.
| But the latter is medial ; [x. 21]
| therefore $x\ .\ \rho\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}}$, or $\rho\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}}\ .\ \rho\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}}\sqrt{1-k^2}$, is medial.

| Lastly ($\delta$) $\rho$, $\rho\sqrt{1-k^2}$ have the remaining property in the enunciation ;
| therefore $\rho\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}}$, $\rho\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}}\sqrt{1-k^2}$ have it also. [x. 14]

| (Euclid has not the assistance of symbols to prove the proportion (3) above.
| He therefore uses the lemmas $ab : bc = a : c$ and $d^2 : de = d : e$ to deduce from
| the relations

$$\left.\begin{array}{r} ab = d^2 \\ d : b = c : e \end{array}\right\}$$

and that $a : c = d : e$.)

| The straight lines $\rho\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}}$, $\rho\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}}\sqrt{1-k^2}$ may take any of the following forms
| according as the straight lines first taken are
| (1) $a$, $\sqrt{B}$, $\sqrt{a^2-c^2}$, (2) $\sqrt{A}$, $\sqrt{B}$, $\sqrt{A-k^2A}$, (3) $\sqrt{A}$, $b$, $\sqrt{A-k^2A}$.

$$\begin{array}{lcc}
(1) & \sqrt{a\sqrt{B}}, & \dfrac{\sqrt{B(a^2-c^2)}}{\sqrt{a\sqrt{B}}} ; \\
(2) & \sqrt[4]{AB}, & \dfrac{\sqrt{B(A-k^2A)}}{\sqrt[4]{AB}} ; \\
(3) & \sqrt{b\sqrt{A}}, & \dfrac{b\sqrt{A-k^2A}}{\sqrt{b\sqrt{A}}}.
\end{array}$$
