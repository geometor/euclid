<p class="sub-clause">Since <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span> is incommensurable in length with <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ F B /">$FB$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the rectangle <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span>, <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span> is also incommensurable with<br />
the rectangle <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ B F /">$BF$</span>. <span class="justification">[X. 11]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But the rectangle <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span>, <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span> is equal to the square on <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>,<br />
and the rectangle <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ B F /">$BF$</span> to the square on <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span>;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the square on <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> is also incommensurable with the<br />
square on <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">And, since the square on <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is medial,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the sum of the squares on <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> is also medial.<br />
<span class="justification">[III. 31, I. 47]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">And, since <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is double of <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the rectangle <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is also double of the rectangle<br />
<span data-ref="0082.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ F D /">$FD$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">But the rectangle <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is rational;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the rectangle <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ F D /">$FD$</span> is also rational. <span class="justification">[X. 6]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But the rectangle <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ F D /">$FD$</span> is equal to the rectangle <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>,<br />
<span data-ref="0082.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span>; <span class="justification">[Lemma]</span></p>
<p class="assertion">so that the rectangle <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> is also rational.</p>

<p class="qed">Therefore two straight lines <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0082.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> incommensurable<br />
in square have been found which make the sum of the squares<br />
on them medial, but the rectangle contained by them rational.<span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

In this case we take [x. 31, 2nd part] the medial straight lines
$$\frac{\rho}{(1 + k^2)^{\frac{1}{4}}}, \quad \frac{\rho}{(1 + k^2)^{\frac{3}{4}}}.$$

Solve the equations
$$\left. \begin{aligned} x + y &= \frac{\rho}{(1 + k^2)^{\frac{1}{4}}} \\ xy &= \frac{\rho^2}{4(1 + k^2)^{\frac{3}{2}}} \end{aligned} \right\} \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(1).$$

Take $u$, $v$ such that, if $x$, $y$ be the result of the solution,
$$\left. \begin{aligned} u^2 &= \frac{\rho}{(1 + k^2)^{\frac{1}{4}}} \cdot x \\ v^2 &= \frac{\rho}{(1 + k^2)^{\frac{1}{4}}} \cdot y \end{aligned} \right\} \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(2),$$
and $u$, $v$ are straight lines satisfying the given conditions.

Euclid's proof is similar to the preceding.

($\alpha$) From (1) it follows [x. 18] that
$$x \mathbin{\smile} y,$$
whence
$$u^2 \mathbin{\smile} v^2,$$
and $u$, $v$ are thus incommensurable in square.
