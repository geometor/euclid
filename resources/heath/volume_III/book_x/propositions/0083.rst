:title: Proposition 34 (conclusion)

.. subclause::

   Since :g:`AF <0082.g1:/ A F />` is incommensurable in length with :g:`FB <0082.g1:/ F B />`,

.. assertion::

   therefore the rectangle :g:`BA <0082.g1:/ B A />`, :g:`AF <0082.g1:/ A F />` is also incommensurable with the rectangle :g:`AB <0082.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`BF <0082.g1:/ B F />`. :just:`[X. 11] <X.11>`

.. subclause::

   But the rectangle :g:`BA <0082.g1:/ B A />`, :g:`AF <0082.g1:/ A F />` is equal to the square on :g:`AD <0082.g1:/ A D />`, and the rectangle :g:`AB <0082.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`BF <0082.g1:/ B F />` to the square on :g:`DB <0082.g1:/ D B />`;

.. assertion::

   therefore the square on :g:`AD <0082.g1:/ A D />` is also incommensurable with the square on :g:`DB <0082.g1:/ D B />`.

.. subclause::

   And, since the square on :g:`AB <0082.g1:/ A B />` is medial,

.. assertion::

   therefore the sum of the squares on :g:`AD <0082.g1:/ A D />`, :g:`DB <0082.g1:/ D B />` is also medial. :just:`[III. 31, I. 47] <III.31, I.47>`

.. subclause::

   And, since :g:`BC <0082.g1:/ B C />` is double of :g:`DF <0082.g1:/ D F />`,

.. assertion::

   therefore the rectangle :g:`AB <0082.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`BC <0082.g1:/ B C />` is also double of the rectangle :g:`AB <0082.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`FD <0082.g1:/ F D />`.

.. subclause::

   But the rectangle :g:`AB <0082.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`BC <0082.g1:/ B C />` is rational;

.. assertion:: [X. 6] <X.6>

   therefore the rectangle :g:`AB <0082.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`FD <0082.g1:/ F D />` is also rational.

.. subclause::

   But the rectangle :g:`AB <0082.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`FD <0082.g1:/ F D />` is equal to the rectangle :g:`AD <0082.g1:/ A D />`, :g:`DB <0082.g1:/ D B />`; :just:`[Lemma]`

.. assertion::

   so that the rectangle :g:`AD <0082.g1:/ A D />`, :g:`DB <0082.g1:/ D B />` is also rational.

.. qed::

   Therefore two straight lines :g:`AD <0082.g1:/ A D />`, :g:`DB <0082.g1:/ D B />` incommensurable in square have been found which make the sum of the squares on them medial, but the rectangle contained by them rational. :just:`Q. E. D.`

----

In this case we take [x. 31, 2nd part] the medial straight lines

$$\frac{\rho}{(1 + k^2)^{\frac{1}{4}}}, \quad \frac{\rho}{(1 + k^2)^{\frac{3}{4}}}.$$

Solve the equations

$$\left. \begin{aligned} x + y &= \frac{\rho}{(1 + k^2)^{\frac{1}{4}}} \\ xy &= \frac{\rho^2}{4(1 + k^2)^{\frac{3}{2}}} \end{aligned} \right\} \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(1).$$

Take $u$, $v$ such that, if $x$, $y$ be the result of the solution,

$$\left. \begin{aligned} u^2 &= \frac{\rho}{(1 + k^2)^{\frac{1}{4}}} \cdot x \\ v^2 &= \frac{\rho}{(1 + k^2)^{\frac{1}{4}}} \cdot y \end{aligned} \right\} \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(2),$$

and $u$, $v$ are straight lines satisfying the given conditions.

Euclid's proof is similar to the preceding.

($\alpha$) From (1) it follows [x. 18] that

$$x \mathbin{\smile} y,$$

whence

$$u^2 \mathbin{\smile} v^2,$$

and $u$, $v$ are thus incommensurable in square.
