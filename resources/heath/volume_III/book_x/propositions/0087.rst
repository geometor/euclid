| According as $\rho$ is of the form $a$ or $\sqrt{A}$, we have a variety of forms for
| $u$, $v$, arrived at by using the same transformations as in the notes on x. 30
| and x. 32 (second part), e.g.

$$(1) \quad \sqrt{\frac{(a + \sqrt{C})\sqrt{B}}{2}}, \quad \sqrt{\frac{(a - \sqrt{C})\sqrt{B}}{2}};$$

$$(2) \quad \sqrt{\frac{(\sqrt{A} + \sqrt{C})\sqrt{B}}{2}}, \quad \sqrt{\frac{(\sqrt{A} - \sqrt{C})\sqrt{B}}{2}};$$

$$(3) \quad \sqrt{\frac{(\sqrt{A} + c)\sqrt{B}}{2}}, \quad \sqrt{\frac{(\sqrt{A} - c)\sqrt{B}}{2}};$$

and the expressions in (2), (3) with $b$ in place of $\sqrt{B}$.

PROPOSITION 36.
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

.. enunciation::

   If two rational straight lines commensurable in square only be added together, the whole is irrational; and let it be called **binomial**.

| For let two rational straight lines :g:`$AB$ <0087.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$BC$ <0087.g1:/ B C />` commen-
| :line:`5` surable in square only be added
| together;

| I say that the whole :g:`$AC$ <0087.g1:/ A C />` is ir-
| rational.

.. picture:: 0087.g1.svg
   :alt: Proposition 36

.. subclause::

   | For, since :g:`$AB$ <0087.g1:/ A B />` is incommensurable in length with :g:`$BC$ <0087.g1:/ B C />`—
   | :line:`10` for they are commensurable in square only—
   | and, as :g:`$AB$ <0087.g1:/ A B />` is to :g:`$BC$ <0087.g1:/ B C />`, so is the rectangle :g:`$AB$ <0087.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$BC$ <0087.g1:/ B C />` to the
   | square on :g:`$BC$ <0087.g1:/ B C />`,

.. assertion::

   | therefore the rectangle :g:`$AB$ <0087.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$BC$ <0087.g1:/ B C />` is incommensurable with the
   | square on :g:`$BC$ <0087.g1:/ B C />`. :just:`[X. 11] <X.11>`

.. subclause::

   | :line:`15` But twice the rectangle :g:`$AB$ <0087.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$BC$ <0087.g1:/ B C />` is commensurable with
   | the rectangle :g:`$AB$ <0087.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$BC$ <0087.g1:/ B C />` :just:`[X. 6] <X.6>`, and the squares on :g:`$AB$ <0087.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$BC$ <0087.g1:/ B C />` are
   | commensurable with the square on :g:`$BC$ <0087.g1:/ B C />`—for :g:`$AB$ <0087.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$BC$ <0087.g1:/ B C />` are
   | rational straight lines commensurable in square only— :just:`[X. 15] <X.15>`

.. assertion::

   | therefore twice the rectangle :g:`$AB$ <0087.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$BC$ <0087.g1:/ B C />` is incommensurable
   | :line:`20` with the squares on :g:`$AB$ <0087.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$BC$ <0087.g1:/ B C />`. :just:`[X. 13] <X.13>`

.. assertion::

   | And, *componendo*, twice the rectangle :g:`$AB$ <0087.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$BC$ <0087.g1:/ B C />` together
   | with the squares on :g:`$AB$ <0087.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$BC$ <0087.g1:/ B C />`, that is, the square on :g:`$AC$ <0087.g1:/ A C />` :just:`[II. 4] <II.4>`,
   | is incommensurable with the sum of the squares on :g:`$AB$ <0087.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$BC$ <0087.g1:/ B C />`.
   | :just:`[X. 16] <X.16>`

.. subclause::

   But the sum of the squares on :g:`$AB$ <0087.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$BC$ <0087.g1:/ B C />` is rational;

.. assertion::

   | :line:`25` therefore the square on :g:`$AC$ <0087.g1:/ A C />` is irrational,
   | so that :g:`$AC$ <0087.g1:/ A C />` is also irrational. :just:`[X. Def. 4] <X.def.4>`

.. qed::

   And let it be called **binomial**.
