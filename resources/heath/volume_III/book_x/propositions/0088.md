Here begins the first hexad of propositions relating to compound irrational<br />
straight lines. The six compound irrational straight lines are formed by<br />
*adding* two parts, as the corresponding six in Props. 73—78 are formed by<br />
*subtraction*. The relation between the six irrational straight lines in this and<br />
the next five propositions with those described in Definitions II. and the<br />
Props. 48—53 following thereon (the *first, second, third, fourth, fifth* and<br />
*sixth binomials*) will be seen when we come to Props. 54—59; but it may be<br />
stated here that the six compound irrationals in Props. 36—41 can be found<br />
by means of the equivalent of extracting the square root of the compound<br />
irrationals in x. 48—53 (the process being, strictly speaking, the finding of the<br />
sides of the squares equal to the rectangles contained by the latter irrationals<br />
respectively and a rational straight line as the other side), and it is therefore<br />
the further removed compound irrational, so to speak, which is treated first.

In reproducing the proofs of the propositions, I shall for the sake of<br />
simplicity call the two parts of the compound irrational straight line $x$, $y$,<br />
explaining at the outset the forms which $x$, $y$ really have in each case; $x$ will<br />
always be supposed to be the greater segment.

In this proposition $x$, $y$ are of the form $\rho$, $\sqrt{k} \cdot \rho$, and $(x + y)$ is proved to<br />
be irrational thus.

$x \sim y$, so that $x \mathbin{\smile} y$.

Now
$$x : y = x^2 : xy,$$
so that
$$x^2 \mathbin{\smile} xy.$$

But $x^2 \mathbin{\sim} (x^2 + y^2)$, and $xy \mathbin{\sim} 2xy$;<br />
therefore
$$(x^2 + y^2) \mathbin{\smile} 2xy,$$
and hence
$$(x^2 + y^2 + 2xy) \mathbin{\smile} (x^2 + y^2).$$

But $(x^2 + y^2)$ is rational;<br />
therefore $(x + y)^2$, and therefore $(x + y)$, is irrational.

This irrational straight line, $\rho + \sqrt{k} \cdot \rho$, is called a **binomial** straight line.

This and the corresponding apotome ($\rho - \sqrt{k} \cdot \rho$) found in x. 73 are the<br />
positive roots of the equation
$$x^4 - 2(1 + k)\rho^2 \cdot x^2 + (1 - k)^2\rho^4 = 0.$$

### PROPOSITION 37.

<p class="enunciation">If two medial straight lines commensurable in square only<br />
and containing a rational rectangle be added together, the<br />
whole is irrational; and let it be called a <strong>first bimedial<br />
straight line</strong>.</p>

For let two medial straight lines <span data-ref="0088.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0088.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> commensurable<br />
in square only and containing<br />
a rational rectangle be added<br />
together;<br />
I say that the whole <span data-ref="0088.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is irrational.
