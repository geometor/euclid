After proving (l. 21) that each of the squares on <span data-ref="0089.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0089.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is medial, Euclid<br />
states (ll. 24, 26) that <span data-ref="0089.g1:< E H >">$EH$</span>, which is equal to the *sum* of the squares, is a<br />
medial area, but does not explain why. It is because, by hypothesis, the<br />
squares on <span data-ref="0089.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0089.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> are commensurable, so that the sum of the squares is<br />
commensurable with either [x. 15] and is therefore a medial area [x. 23, Por.].

In this case [x. 28, note] $x$, $y$ are of the forms $k^{\frac{1}{4}}\rho$, $\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho/k^{\frac{1}{4}}$ respectively.<br />
Apply each of the areas $(x^2 + y^2)$ and $2xy$ to a rational straight line $\sigma$, i.e.<br />
suppose
$$x^2 + y^2 = \sigma u,$$
$$2xy = \sigma v.$$

Now it follows from the hypothesis, x. 15 and x. 23, Por. that $(x^2 + y^2)$ is<br />
a medial area; and so is $2xy$, by hypothesis;<br />
therefore $\sigma u$, $\sigma v$ are medial areas.<br />
Therefore each of the straight lines $u$, $v$ is rational and $\mathbin{\smile} \sigma$ ........ (1).<br />
Again
$$x \mathbin{\smile} y;$$
therefore
$$x^2 \mathbin{\smile} xy.$$
But
$$x^2 \mathbin{\sim} x^2 + y^2 \text{ and } xy \mathbin{\sim} 2xy;$$
therefore
$$x^2 + y^2 \mathbin{\smile} 2xy,$$
or
$$\sigma u \mathbin{\smile} \sigma v,$$
whence
$$u \mathbin{\smile} v \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(2).$$
Therefore, by (1), (2), $u$, $v$ are rational and $\sim$.<br />
It follows, by x. 36, that $(u + v)$ is irrational.<br />
Therefore $(u + v)\,\sigma$ is an irrational area [this can be deduced from x. 20<br />
by *reductio ad absurdum*],<br />
whence $(x + y)^2$, and consequently $(x + y)$, is irrational.

The irrational straight line $k^{\frac{1}{4}}\rho + \frac{\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho}{k^{\frac{1}{4}}}$ is called a **second bimedial straight<br />
line**.

This and the corresponding *second apotome of a medial* $\left(k^{\frac{1}{4}}\rho - \frac{\sqrt{\lambda}}{k^{\frac{1}{4}}}\rho\right)$<br />
found in x. 75 are the positive roots of the equation
$$x^4 - 2\,\frac{k+\lambda}{\sqrt{k}}\,\rho^2 \cdot x^2 + \frac{(k-\lambda)^2}{k}\,\rho^4 = 0.$$

### PROPOSITION 39.

<p class="enunciation">If two straight lines incommensurable in square which<br />
make the sum of the squares on them rational, but the rectangle<br />
contained by them medial, be added together, the whole straight<br />
line is irrational : and let it be called <strong>major</strong>.</p>
