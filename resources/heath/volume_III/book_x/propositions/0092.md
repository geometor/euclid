For let two straight lines <span data-ref="0092.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0092.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> incommensurable in<br />
square, and fulfilling the given con-<br />
ditions <span class="justification">[x. 33]</span>, be added together;<br />
I say that <span data-ref="0092.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is irrational.

![Proposition 39 diagram](0092.g1.svg)

<p class="sub-clause">For, since the rectangle <span data-ref="0092.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0092.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is medial,</p>
<p class="assertion">twice the rectangle <span data-ref="0092.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0092.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is also medial. <span class="justification">[x. 6 and 23, Por.]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But the sum of the squares on <span data-ref="0092.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0092.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is rational;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore twice the rectangle <span data-ref="0092.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0092.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is incommensurable<br />
with the sum of the squares on <span data-ref="0092.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0092.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>,<br />
so that the squares on <span data-ref="0092.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0092.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> together with twice the rect-<br />
angle <span data-ref="0092.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0092.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, that is, the square on <span data-ref="0092.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, is also incommen-<br />
surable with the sum of the squares on <span data-ref="0092.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0092.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>; <span class="justification">[x. 16]</span><br />
therefore the square on <span data-ref="0092.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is irrational,<br />
so that <span data-ref="0092.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is also irrational. <span class="justification">[x. Def. 4]</span></p>

<p class="qed">And let it be called <strong>major</strong>.<span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

Here $x$, $y$ are of the form found in x. 33, viz.
$$\frac{\rho}{\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{1+\frac{k}{\sqrt{1+k^2}}},\quad \frac{\rho}{\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{1-\frac{k}{\sqrt{1+k^2}}}.$$

By hypothesis, the rectangle $xy$ is medial;<br />
therefore $2xy$ is medial.

Also $(x^2+y^2)$ is a rational area.

Therefore
$$x^2+y^2 \mathbin{\smile} 2xy,$$
whence
$$(x+y)^2 \mathbin{\smile} (x^2+y^2),$$
so that $(x+y)^2$, and therefore $(x+y)$, is irrational.

The irrational straight line $\frac{\rho}{\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{1+\frac{k}{\sqrt{1+k^2}}} + \frac{\rho}{\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{1-\frac{k}{\sqrt{1+k^2}}}$ is<br />
called a **major** (irrational) straight line.

This and the corresponding *minor* irrational found in x. 76 are the<br />
positive roots of the equation
$$x^4 - 2\rho^2 \cdot x^2 + \frac{k^2}{1+k^2}\rho^4 = 0.$$

### PROPOSITION 40.

<p class="enunciation">If two straight lines incommensurable in square which<br />
make the sum of the squares on them medial, but the rectangle<br />
contained by them rational, be added together, the whole straight<br />
line is irrational; and let it be called the <strong>side of a rational<br />
plus a medial area</strong>.</p>
