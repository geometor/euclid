For let two straight lines <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> incommensurable in square, and fulfilling the given conditions [x. 34], be added together;
I say that <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is irrational.

![Proposition 40 diagram](0093.g1.svg)

For, since the sum of the squares on <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is medial, while twice the rectangle <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is rational,
therefore the sum of the squares on <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is incommensurable with twice the rectangle <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>;
so that the square on <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is also incommensurable with twice the rectangle <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>. [x. 16]

But twice the rectangle <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is rational;
therefore the square on <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is irrational.
<sup>5</sup> Therefore <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is irrational. [x. Def. 4]

And let it be called the **side of a rational plus a medial area**.

Q. E. D.

---

Here $x$, $y$ have [x. 34] the forms
$$\frac{\rho}{\sqrt{2(1+k^2)}}\sqrt{\sqrt{1+k^2}+k},\quad \frac{\rho}{\sqrt{2(1+k^2)}}\sqrt{\sqrt{1+k^2}-k}.$$

In this case $(x^2+y^2)$ is a medial, and $2xy$ a rational, area; thus
$$x^2+y^2 \cup 2xy.$$

Therefore
$$(x+y)^2 \cup 2xy,$$
whence, since $2xy$ is rational,
$(x+y)^2$, and consequently $(x+y)$, is irrational.

The irrational straight line
$$\frac{\rho}{\sqrt{2(1+k^2)}}\sqrt{\sqrt{1+k^2}+k} + \frac{\rho}{\sqrt{2(1+k^2)}}\sqrt{\sqrt{1+k^2}-k}$$
is called (for an obvious reason) the “*side*” of a rational plus a medial (area).

This and the corresponding irrational with a minus sign found in x. 77 are the positive roots of the equation
$$x^4 - \frac{2}{\sqrt{1+k^2}}\rho^2 \cdot x^2 + \frac{k^2}{(1+k^2)^2}\rho^4 = 0.$$

### PROPOSITION 41.

*If two straight lines incommensurable in square which make the sum of the squares on them medial, and the rectangle contained by them medial and also incommensurable with the sum of the squares on them, be added together, the whole straight line is irrational; and let it be called the* **side of the sum of two medial areas.**
