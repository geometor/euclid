For let two straight lines <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> incommensurable in<br />
square, and fulfilling the given con-<br />
ditions <span class="justification">[X. 34]</span>, be added together;<br />
I say that <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is irrational.

![Proposition 40 diagram](0093.g1.svg)

<p class="sub-clause">For, since the sum of the squares on <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is medial,<br />
while twice the rectangle <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is rational,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the sum of the squares on <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is incommen-<br />
surable with twice the rectangle <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>;<br />
so that the square on <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is also incommensurable with twice<br />
the rectangle <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>. <span class="justification">[X. 16]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But twice the rectangle <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is rational;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the square on <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is irrational.</p>
<p class="assertion"><sup>5</sup> Therefore <span data-ref="0093.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is irrational. <span class="justification">[X. Def. 4]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">And let it be called the **side of a rational plus a<br />
medial area**.</p>

<p class="qed"><span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

---

Here $x$, $y$ have <span class="justification">[X. 34]</span> the forms
$$\frac{\rho}{\sqrt{2(1+k^2)}}\sqrt{\sqrt{1+k^2}+k},\quad \frac{\rho}{\sqrt{2(1+k^2)}}\sqrt{\sqrt{1+k^2}-k}.$$

In this case $(x^2+y^2)$ is a medial, and $2xy$ a rational, area; thus
$$x^2+y^2 \cup 2xy.$$

Therefore
$$(x+y)^2 \cup 2xy,$$
whence, since $2xy$ is rational,<br />
$(x+y)^2$, and consequently $(x+y)$, is irrational.

The irrational straight line
$$\frac{\rho}{\sqrt{2(1+k^2)}}\sqrt{\sqrt{1+k^2}+k} + \frac{\rho}{\sqrt{2(1+k^2)}}\sqrt{\sqrt{1+k^2}-k}$$
is called (for an obvious reason) the “*side*” of a rational plus a medial (area).

This and the corresponding irrational with a minus sign found in x. 77<br />
are the positive roots of the equation
$$x^4 - \frac{2}{\sqrt{1+k^2}}\rho^2 \cdot x^2 + \frac{k^2}{(1+k^2)^2}\rho^4 = 0.$$

### PROPOSITION 41.

<p class="enunciation">If two straight lines incommensurable in square which<br />
make the sum of the squares on them medial, and the rectangle<br />
contained by them medial and also incommensurable with the<br />
sum of the squares on them, be added together, the whole straight<br />
line is irrational; and let it be called the **side of the sum<br />
of two medial areas.**</p>
