For let two straight lines <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> incommensurable in square and satisfying the given conditions [x. 35] be added together;
I say that <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is irrational.

![Proposition 41 diagram](0094.g1.svg)

Let a rational straight line <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> be set out, and let there be applied to <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> the rectangle <span data-ref="0094.g1:< D F >">$DF$</span> equal to the squares on <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, and the rectangle <span data-ref="0094.g1:< G H >">$GH$</span> equal to twice the rectangle <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>;
therefore the whole <span data-ref="0094.g1:< D H >">$DH$</span> is equal to the square on <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>. [II. 4]

Now, since the sum of the squares on <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is medial,
and is equal to <span data-ref="0094.g1:< D F >">$DF$</span>,
therefore <span data-ref="0094.g1:< D F >">$DF$</span> is also medial.

And it is applied to the rational straight line <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>;
therefore <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ D G /">$DG$</span> is rational and incommensurable in length with <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>. [x. 22]

For the same reason <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ G K /">$GK$</span> is also rational and incommensurable in length with <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ G F /">$GF$</span>, that is, <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>.

And, since the squares on <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> are incommensurable with twice the rectangle <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>,
<span data-ref="0094.g1:< D F >">$DF$</span> is incommensurable with <span data-ref="0094.g1:< G H >">$GH$</span>;
so that <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ D G /">$DG$</span> is also incommensurable with <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ G K /">$GK$</span>. [VI. 1, x. 11]

And they are rational;
therefore <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ D G /">$DG$</span>, <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ G K /">$GK$</span> are rational straight lines commensurable in square only;
therefore <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ D K /">$DK$</span> is irrational and what is called binomial. [x. 36]

But <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> is rational;
therefore <span data-ref="0094.g1:< D H >">$DH$</span> is irrational, and the side of the square which is equal to it is irrational. [x. Def. 4]

But <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is the side of the square equal to <span data-ref="0094.g1:< H D >">$HD$</span>;
therefore <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is irrational.

And let it be called the **side of the sum of two medial areas**.

Q. E. D.
