For let two straight lines <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> incommensurable in<br />
square and satisfying the given conditions<br />
<span class="justification">[X. 35]</span> be added together;<br />
I say that <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is irrational.

![Proposition 41 diagram](0094.g1.svg)

Let a rational straight line <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> be set out,<br />
and let there be applied to <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> the rectangle<br />
<span data-ref="0094.g1:< D F >">$DF$</span> equal to the squares on <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, and<br />
the rectangle <span data-ref="0094.g1:< G H >">$GH$</span> equal to twice the rectangle<br />
<span data-ref="0094.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>;
<p class="assertion">therefore the whole <span data-ref="0094.g1:< D H >">$DH$</span> is equal to the square<br />
on <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>. <span class="justification">[II. 4]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">Now, since the sum of the squares on<br />
<span data-ref="0094.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is medial,<br />
and is equal to <span data-ref="0094.g1:< D F >">$DF$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0094.g1:< D F >">$DF$</span> is also medial.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">And it is applied to the rational straight line <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ D G /">$DG$</span> is rational and incommensurable in length with<br />
<span data-ref="0094.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>. <span class="justification">[X. 22]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">For the same reason <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ G K /">$GK$</span> is also rational and incommen-<br />
surable in length with <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ G F /">$GF$</span>, that is, <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">And, since the squares on <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> are incommensurable<br />
with twice the rectangle <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion"><span data-ref="0094.g1:< D F >">$DF$</span> is incommensurable with <span data-ref="0094.g1:< G H >">$GH$</span>;<br />
so that <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ D G /">$DG$</span> is also incommensurable with <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ G K /">$GK$</span>. <span class="justification">[VI. 1, X. 11]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">And they are rational;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ D G /">$DG$</span>, <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ G K /">$GK$</span> are rational straight lines commensurable<br />
in square only;<br />
therefore <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ D K /">$DK$</span> is irrational and what is called binomial. <span class="justification">[X. 36]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> is rational;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0094.g1:< D H >">$DH$</span> is irrational, and the side of the square which<br />
is equal to it is irrational. <span class="justification">[X. Def. 4]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is the side of the square equal to <span data-ref="0094.g1:< H D >">$HD$</span>;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0094.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is irrational.</p>

<p class="assertion">And let it be called the **side of the sum of two medial<br />
areas**.</p>

<p class="qed"><span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>
