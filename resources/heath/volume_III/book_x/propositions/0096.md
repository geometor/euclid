This proves that, if $x + y = u + v$, and if $u$, $v$ are more nearly equal than $x$, $y$ (i.e. if the straight line is divided in the second case nearer to the point of bisection), then
$$(x^2 + y^2) > (u^2 + v^2).$$

It is first proved by means of II. 5 that
$$2xy < 2uv,$$
whence, since $(x+y)^2 = (u+v)^2$, the required result follows.

### PROPOSITION 42.

*A binomial straight line is divided into its terms at one point only.*

Let <span data-ref="0096.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> be a binomial straight line divided into its terms at <span data-ref="0096.g1:C">$C$</span>;
therefore <span data-ref="0096.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0096.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span> are rational straight lines commensurable in square only. [x. 36]

![Proposition 42 diagram](0096.g1.svg)

I say that <span data-ref="0096.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is not divided at another point into two rational straight lines commensurable in square only.
