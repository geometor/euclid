This proves that, if $x + y = u + v$, and if $u$, $v$ are more nearly equal than<br />
$x$, $y$ (i.e. if the straight line is divided in the second case nearer to the point<br />
of bisection), then

It is first proved by means of II. 5 that
$$2xy < 2uv,$$
whence, since $(x+y)^2 = (u+v)^2$, the required result follows.

### PROPOSITION 42.

<p class="enunciation">A binomial straight line is divided into its terms at one<br />
point only.</p>

Let <span data-ref="0096.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> be a binomial straight line divided into its terms<br />
at <span data-ref="0096.g1:C">$C$</span>;
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0096.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0096.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span> are rational<br />
straight lines commensurable in<br />
square only. <span class="justification">[X. 36]</span></p>

![Proposition 42 diagram](0096.g1.svg)

I say that <span data-ref="0096.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is not divided at another point into two<br />
rational straight lines commensurable in square only.
