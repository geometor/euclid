In this case, with the same hypothesis, viz. that
$$x + y = x' + y',$$
and $x', y'$ are more nearly equal than $x, y$,<br />
we have as before
$$(x^2 + y^2) - (x'^2 + y'^2) = 2x'y' - 2xy.$$
But, from the given properties of $x, y$, and $x', y'$, it follows that $2xy, 2x'y'$<br />
are *rational*, and $(x^2 + y^2), (x'^2 + y'^2)$ medial, areas.

Therefore the difference between two medial areas is rational :<br />
which is impossible. [x. 26]

<p class="enunciation">A second bimedial straight line is divided at one point only.</p>
