<p class="sub-clause">And, since <span data-ref="0099.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0099.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span> are medial straight lines commen-<br />

<p class="sub-clause">But, as <span data-ref="0099.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is to <span data-ref="0099.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span>, so is the square on <span data-ref="0099.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> to the rect-<br />

<p class="sub-clause">But <span data-ref="0099.g1:< E G >">$EG$</span> is equal to the squares on <span data-ref="0099.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0099.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span>,<br />

<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0099.g1:< E G >">$EG$</span> is incommensurable with <span data-ref="0099.g1:< H K >">$HK$</span>,<br />

<span class="justification">[VI. 1, X. 11]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">And they are rational ;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0099.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span>, <span data-ref="0099.g1:/ H N /">$HN$</span> are rational straight lines commensurable<br />
in square only.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">But, if two rational straight lines commensurable in square<br />
only be added together, the whole is the irrational which is<br />
called binomial. <span class="justification">[X. 36]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore <span data-ref="0099.g1:/ E N /">$EN$</span> is a binomial straight line divided at <span data-ref="0099.g1:H">$H$</span>.</p>
