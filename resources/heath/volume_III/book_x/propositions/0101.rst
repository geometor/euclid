.. subclause::

   And :g:`$EH$ <0099.g1:/ E H />` is not the same with :g:`$MN$ <0099.g1:/ M N />`.

.. subclause::

   | For the squares on :g:`$AC$ <0099.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$CB$ <0099.g1:/ C B />` are greater than the squares
   | on :g:`$AD$ <0099.g1:/ A D />`, :g:`$DB$ <0099.g1:/ D B />`.

.. subclause::

   | But the squares on :g:`$AD$ <0099.g1:/ A D />`, :g:`$DB$ <0099.g1:/ D B />` are greater than twice the
   | rectangle :g:`$AD$ <0099.g1:/ A D />`, :g:`$DB$ <0099.g1:/ D B />`;

.. assertion::

   | therefore also the squares on :g:`$AC$ <0099.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$CB$ <0099.g1:/ C B />`, that is, :g:`$EG$ <0099.g1:< E G >>`, are much
   | greater than twice the rectangle :g:`$AD$ <0099.g1:/ A D />`, :g:`$DB$ <0099.g1:/ D B />`, that is, :g:`$MK$ <0099.g1:< M K >>`,
   | so that :g:`$EH$ <0099.g1:/ E H />` is also greater than :g:`$MN$ <0099.g1:/ M N />`.

.. qed::

   Therefore :g:`$EH$ <0099.g1:/ E H />` is not the same with :g:`$MN$ <0099.g1:/ M N />`. :just:`Q. E. D.`

| As the irrationality of the *second bimedial* straight line [x. 38] is proved by
| means of the irrationality of the binomial straight line [x. 36], so the present
| theorem is reduced to that of x. 42.

| Suppose, if possible, that the second bimedial straight line can be divided
| into its terms as such in two ways, i.e. that

$$x + y = x' + y',$$

where $x', y'$ are nearer equality than $x, y$.

Apply $x^2 + y^2$, $2xy$ to a rational straight line $\sigma$, i.e. let

$$\begin{aligned}
x^2 + y^2 &= \sigma u, \\
2xy &= \sigma v.
\end{aligned}$$

| Then, as in x. 38, the areas $x^2 + y^2$, $2xy$ are medial, so that $\sigma u, \sigma v$ are
| medial ;
| therefore $u, v$ are both rational and $\smile \sigma$ .....................................................(1).

| Again, by hypothesis, $x, y$ are medial straight lines commensurable in
| square only;
| therefore

$$x \smile y.$$

Hence

$$x^2 \smile xy.$$

And $x^2 \sim (x^2 + y^2)$, while $xy \sim 2xy$;
therefore

$$(x^2 + y^2) \smile 2xy,$$

or

$$\sigma u \smile \sigma v,$$

and hence

$$u \smile v$$

.................................................................(2).

| Therefore, by (1) and (2), $u, v$ are rational straight lines commensurable
| in square only;
| therefore $u + v$ is a binomial straight line.

| Similarly, if $x'^2 + y'^2 = \sigma u'$ and $2x'y' = \sigma v'$,
| $u' + v'$ will be proved to be a binomial straight line.

| And, since $(x + y)^2 = (x' + y')^2$, and therefore $(u + v) = (u' + v')$, it follows that
| a binomial straight line is divided as such in two ways :
| which is impossible. [x. 42]

| Therefore $x + y$, the given second bimedial straight line, can only be so
| divided in one way.

| In order to prove that $u + v$, $u' + v'$ represent a *different* division of the
| same straight line, Euclid assumes that $x^2 + y^2 > 2xy$. This is of course an
| easy inference from II. 7; but the assumption of it here renders it probable
| that the Lemma after x. 59 is interpolated.
