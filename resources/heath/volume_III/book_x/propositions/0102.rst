PROPOSITION 45.
---------------

.. enunciation::

   A major straight line is divided at one and the same point only.

Let :g:`$AB$ <0102.g1:/ A B />` be a major straight line divided at :g:`$C$ <0102.g1:C>`, so that :g:`$AC$ <0102.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$CB$ <0102.g1:/ C B />` are incommensurable in square and make the sum of the squares on :g:`$AC$ <0102.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$CB$ <0102.g1:/ C B />` rational, but the rectangle :g:`$AC$ <0102.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$CB$ <0102.g1:/ C B />` medial; :just:`[X. 39] <X.39>`

.. picture:: 0102.g1.svg
   :alt: Proposition 45 diagram

I say that :g:`$AB$ <0102.g1:/ A B />` is not so divided at another point.

For, if possible, let it be divided at :g:`$D$ <0102.g1:D>` also, so that :g:`$AD$ <0102.g1:/ A D />`, :g:`$DB$ <0102.g1:/ D B />` are also incommensurable in square and make the sum of the squares on :g:`$AD$ <0102.g1:/ A D />`, :g:`$DB$ <0102.g1:/ D B />` rational, but the rectangle contained by them medial.

.. subclause::

   Then, since that by which the squares on :g:`$AC$ <0102.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$CB$ <0102.g1:/ C B />` differ from the squares on :g:`$AD$ <0102.g1:/ A D />`, :g:`$DB$ <0102.g1:/ D B />` is also that by which twice the rectangle :g:`$AD$ <0102.g1:/ A D />`, :g:`$DB$ <0102.g1:/ D B />` differs from twice the rectangle :g:`$AC$ <0102.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$CB$ <0102.g1:/ C B />`, while the squares on :g:`$AC$ <0102.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$CB$ <0102.g1:/ C B />` exceed the squares on :g:`$AD$ <0102.g1:/ A D />`, :g:`$DB$ <0102.g1:/ D B />` by a rational area—for both are rational—

.. assertion::

   therefore twice the rectangle :g:`$AD$ <0102.g1:/ A D />`, :g:`$DB$ <0102.g1:/ D B />` also exceeds twice the rectangle :g:`$AC$ <0102.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$CB$ <0102.g1:/ C B />` by a rational area, though they are medial : which is impossible. :just:`[X. 26] <X.26>`

.. qed::

   Therefore a major straight line is not divided at different points ; therefore it is only divided at one and the same point. :just:`Q. E. D.`

----

If possible, let the major irrational straight line be divided into terms in two ways, viz. as $(x + y)$ and $(x' + y')$, where $x', y'$ are supposed to be nearer equality than $x, y$.

We have then, as in x. 42, 43,

$$(x^2 + y^2) - (x'^2 + y'^2) = 2x'y' - 2xy.$$

But, by hypothesis, $(x^2 + y^2), (x'^2 + y'^2)$ are both *rational*, so that their difference is *rational*.

Also, by hypothesis, $2x'y', 2xy$ are both *medial* areas; therefore the difference of two medial areas is a rational area: which is impossible. [x. 26]

Therefore etc.
