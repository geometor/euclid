### PROPOSITION 46.

<p class="enunciation">The side of a rational plus a medial area is divided at one<br />
point only.</p>

Let <span data-ref="0103.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> be the side of a rational plus a medial area<br />
divided at <span data-ref="0103.g1:C">$C$</span>, so that <span data-ref="0103.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0103.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span> are<br />
incommensurable in square and make<br />
the sum of the squares on <span data-ref="0103.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0103.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span><br />
medial, but twice the rectangle <span data-ref="0103.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0103.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span> rational; <span class="justification">[X. 40]</span><br />
I say that <span data-ref="0103.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is not so divided at another point.

![Proposition 46 diagram](0103.g1.svg)

For, if possible, let it be divided at <span data-ref="0103.g1:D">$D$</span> also, so that <span data-ref="0103.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>,<br />
<span data-ref="0103.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> are also incommensurable in square and make the sum<br />
of the squares on <span data-ref="0103.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0103.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> medial, but twice the rectangle<br />
<span data-ref="0103.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0103.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> rational.

<p class="sub-clause">Since then that by which twice the rectangle <span data-ref="0103.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0103.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span><br />
differs from twice the rectangle <span data-ref="0103.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0103.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> is also that by<br />
which the squares on <span data-ref="0103.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0103.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> differ from the squares on<br />
<span data-ref="0103.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0103.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span>,</p>
<p class="sub-clause">while twice the rectangle <span data-ref="0103.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0103.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span> exceeds twice the rectangle<br />
<span data-ref="0103.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0103.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> by a rational area,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the squares on <span data-ref="0103.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0103.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> also exceed the squares<br />
on <span data-ref="0103.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0103.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span> by a rational area, though they are medial:</p>
<p class="assertion">which is impossible. <span class="justification">[X. 26]</span></p>

<p class="qed">Therefore the side of a rational plus a medial area is not<br />
divided at different points;<br />
therefore it is divided at one point only.<span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

---

Here, as before, if we use the same notation,
$$(x^2 + y^2) - (x'^2 + y'^2) = 2x'y' - 2xy,$$
and the areas on the left side are, by hypothesis, both medial, while the areas<br />
on the right side are both rational.

Thus the result of X. 26 is contradicted, as before.

Therefore etc.
