Using the same notation as in the note on X. 44, we suppose that, if<br />
possible,
$$x + y = x' + y',$$
and we put
$$\left. \begin{array}{r} x^2 + y^2 = \sigma u \\ 2xy = \sigma v \end{array} \right\} \quad \text{and} \quad \left. \begin{array}{r} x'^2 + y'^2 = \sigma u' \\ 2x'y' = \sigma v' \end{array} \right\}.$$

Then, since $x^2 + y^2, 2xy$ are medial areas, and $\sigma$ rational,<br />
$u, v$ are both rational and $\smile \sigma$ ........................................(1).

Also, by hypothesis,
$$x^2 + y^2 \smile 2xy,$$
whence
$$u \smile v \text{ .................................................................(2).}$$

Therefore, by (1) and (2), $u, v$ are rational and $\sim$.<br />
Hence $u + v$ is a binomial straight line. <span class="justification">[X. 36]</span><br />
Similarly $u' + v'$ is a binomial straight line.<br />
But
$$u + v = u' + v';$$
therefore a binomial straight line is divided into terms in two ways:<br />
which is impossible. <span class="justification">[X. 42]</span>

Therefore etc.

### DEFINITIONS II.

**1.** Given a rational straight line and a binomial, divided<br />
into its terms, such that the square on the greater term is<br />
greater than the square on the lesser by the square on a<br />
straight line commensurable in length with the greater, then,<br />
if the greater term be commensurable in length with the<br />
rational straight line set out, let the whole be called a **first<br />
binomial straight line**;

with the rational straight line set out, let the whole be called<br />
