   And :g:`EH <0104.g1:/ E H />` is not the same with :g:`MN <0104.g1:/ M N />`;

.. assertion::

   therefore a binomial has been divided at different points :

   which is absurd.

.. qed::

   Therefore a side of the sum of two medial areas is not divided at different points; therefore it is divided at one point only.

----

Using the same notation as in the note on X. 44, we suppose that, if possible,

$$x + y = x' + y',$$

and we put

$$\left. \begin{array}{r} x^2 + y^2 = \sigma u \\ 2xy = \sigma v \end{array} \right\} \quad \text{and} \quad \left. \begin{array}{r} x'^2 + y'^2 = \sigma u' \\ 2x'y' = \sigma v' \end{array} \right\}.$$

Then, since $x^2 + y^2, 2xy$ are medial areas, and $\sigma$ rational, $u, v$ are both rational and $\smile \sigma$ ........................................(1).

Also, by hypothesis,

$$x^2 + y^2 \smile 2xy,$$

whence

$$u \smile v \text{ .................................................................(2).}$$

Therefore, by (1) and (2), $u, v$ are rational and $\sim$. Hence $u + v$ is a binomial straight line. :just:`[X. 36] <X.36>` Similarly $u' + v'$ is a binomial straight line. But

$$u + v = u' + v';$$

therefore a binomial straight line is divided into terms in two ways: which is impossible. :just:`[X. 42] <X.42>`

Therefore etc.

DEFINITIONS II.
---------------

**1.** Given a rational straight line and a binomial, divided into its terms, such that the square on the greater term is greater than the square on the lesser by the square on a straight line commensurable in length with the greater, then, if the greater term be commensurable in length with the rational straight line set out, let the whole be called a :term:`**first binomial straight line** <first-binomial-straight-line>`;

**2.** but if the lesser term be commensurable in length with the rational straight line set out, let the whole be called a :term:`**second binomial** <second-binomial>`;
