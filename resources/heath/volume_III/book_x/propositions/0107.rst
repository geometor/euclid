.. assertion::

   | neither, therefore, has the square on :g:`$EF$ <0106.g1:/ E F />` to the square on :g:`$FG$ <0106.g1:/ F G />`
   | the ratio which a square number has to a square number;
   | therefore :g:`$EF$ <0106.g1:/ E F />` is incommensurable in length with :g:`$FG$ <0106.g1:/ F G />`. :just:`[X. 9] <X.9>`

.. assertion::

   | Therefore :g:`$EF$ <0106.g1:/ E F />`, :g:`$FG$ <0106.g1:/ F G />` are rational straight lines commen-
   | surable in square only;
   | therefore :g:`$EG$ <0106.g1:/ E G />` is binomial. :just:`[X. 36] <X.36>`

I say that it is also a first binomial straight line.

.. subclause::

   | For since, as the number :g:`$BA$ <0106.g1:/ A B />` is to :g:`$AC$ <0106.g1:/ A C />`, so is the square
   | on :g:`$EF$ <0106.g1:/ E F />` to the square on :g:`$FG$ <0106.g1:/ F G />`,
   | while :g:`$BA$ <0106.g1:/ A B />` is greater than :g:`$AC$ <0106.g1:/ A C />`,

.. assertion::

   | therefore the square on :g:`$EF$ <0106.g1:/ E F />` is also greater than the square
   | on :g:`$FG$ <0106.g1:/ F G />`.

| Let then the squares on :g:`$FG$ <0106.g1:/ F G />`, :g:`$H$ <0106.g1:H>` be equal to the square on
| :g:`$EF$ <0106.g1:/ E F />`.

.. subclause::

   | Now since, as :g:`$BA$ <0106.g1:/ A B />` is to :g:`$AC$ <0106.g1:/ A C />`, so is the square on :g:`$EF$ <0106.g1:/ E F />` to the
   | square on :g:`$FG$ <0106.g1:/ F G />`,

.. assertion::

   | therefore, *convertendo*,
   | as :g:`$AB$ <0106.g1:/ A B />` is to :g:`$BC$ <0106.g1:/ C B />`, so is the square on :g:`$EF$ <0106.g1:/ E F />` to the square on :g:`$H$ <0106.g1:H>`.
   | :just:`[V. 19, Por.]`

.. subclause::

   | But :g:`$AB$ <0106.g1:/ A B />` has to :g:`$BC$ <0106.g1:/ C B />` the ratio which a square number has
   | to a square number;

.. assertion::

   | therefore the square on :g:`$EF$ <0106.g1:/ E F />` also has to the square on :g:`$H$ <0106.g1:H>` the
   | ratio which a square number has to a square number.

.. assertion::

   | Therefore :g:`$EF$ <0106.g1:/ E F />` is commensurable in length with :g:`$H$ <0106.g1:H>`; :just:`[X. 9] <X.9>`
   | therefore the square on :g:`$EF$ <0106.g1:/ E F />` is greater than the square on :g:`$FG$ <0106.g1:/ F G />`
   | by the square on a straight line commensurable with :g:`$EF$ <0106.g1:/ E F />`.

.. subclause::

   | And :g:`$EF$ <0106.g1:/ E F />`, :g:`$FG$ <0106.g1:/ F G />` are rational, and :g:`$EF$ <0106.g1:/ E F />` is commensurable in
   | length with :g:`$D$ <0106.g1:D>`.

.. qed::

   Therefore :g:`$EF$ <0106.g1:/ E F />` is a first binomial straight line. :just:`Q. E. D.`

| Let $kp$ be a straight line commensurable in length with $\rho$, a given rational
| straight line.

| The two numbers taken may be written $p(m^2 - n^2)$, $pn^2$, where $(m^2 - n^2)$ is
| not a square.

Take $x$ such that

$$pm^2 : p(m^2 - n^2) = k^2 p^2 : x^2 \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(1),$$

whence

$$x = kp \frac{\sqrt{m^2 - n^2}}{m}.$$

Then $kp + x$, or $kp + kp \frac{\sqrt{m^2 - n^2}}{m}$, is a *first binomial* straight line $\dots\dots(2)$.
