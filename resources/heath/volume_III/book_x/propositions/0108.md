To prove this we have, from (1),
$$x^2 \sim k^2 p^2,$$
and $x$ is rational, but $x \cup kp$;<br />
that is, $x$ is rational and $\sim kp$,<br />
so that $kp + x$ is a binomial straight line.

Also, $k^2 p^2$ being greater than $x^2$, suppose $k^2 p^2 - x^2 = y^2$.

Then, from (1),
$$pm^2 : pn^2 = k^2 p^2 : y^2,$$
whence $y$ is rational and $\sim kp$.

Therefore $kp + x$ is a *first binomial* straight line [x. Deff. II. 1].

This binomial straight line may be written thus,
$$kp + kp \sqrt{1 - \lambda^2}.$$

When we come to x. 85, we shall find that the corresponding straight line<br />
with a negative sign is the *first apotome*,
$$kp - kp \sqrt{1 - \lambda^2}.$$

Consider now the equation of which these two expressions are the roots.<br />
The equation is
$$x^2 - 2kp \cdot x + \lambda^2 k^2 p^2 = 0.$$

In other words, the first binomial and the first apotome correspond to the<br />
roots of the equation
$$x^2 - 2ax + \lambda^2 a^2 = 0,$$
where $a = kp$.
