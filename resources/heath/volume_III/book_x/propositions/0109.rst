.. subclause::

   For since, inversely, as the number :g:`$BA$ <0108.g1:/ A B />` is to :g:`$AC$ <0108.g1:/ A C />`, so is the square on :g:`$GF$ <0108.g1:/ F G />` to the square on :g:`$FE$ <0108.g1:/ E F />`, while :g:`$BA$ <0108.g1:/ A B />` is greater than :g:`$AC$ <0108.g1:/ A C />`,

Let the squares on :g:`$EF$ <0108.g1:/ E F />`, :g:`$H$ <0108.g1:H>` be equal to the square on :g:`$GF$ <0108.g1:/ F G />`;

.. assertion::

   therefore, :term:`*convertendo* <convertendo>`, as :g:`$AB$ <0108.g1:/ A B />` is to :g:`$BC$ <0108.g1:/ C B />`, so is the square on :g:`$FG$ <0108.g1:/ F G />` to the square on :g:`$H$ <0108.g1:H>`. :just:`[V. 19, Por.]`

.. subclause::

   But :g:`$AB$ <0108.g1:/ A B />` has to :g:`$BC$ <0108.g1:/ C B />` the ratio which a square number has to a square number;

.. assertion::

   therefore the square on :g:`$FG$ <0108.g1:/ F G />` also has to the square on :g:`$H$ <0108.g1:H>` the ratio which a square number has to a square number.

.. assertion::

   Therefore :g:`$FG$ <0108.g1:/ F G />` is commensurable in length with :g:`$H$ <0108.g1:H>`; :just:`[X. 9] <X.9>` so that the square on :g:`$FG$ <0108.g1:/ F G />` is greater than the square on :g:`$FE$ <0108.g1:/ E F />` by the square on a straight line commensurable with :g:`$FG$ <0108.g1:/ F G />`.

.. subclause::

   And :g:`$FG$ <0108.g1:/ F G />`, :g:`$FE$ <0108.g1:/ E F />` are rational straight lines commensurable in square only, and :g:`$EF$ <0108.g1:/ E F />`, the lesser term, is commensurable in length with the rational straight line :g:`$D$ <0108.g1:D>` set out.

.. qed::

   Therefore :g:`$EG$ <0108.g1:/ E G />` is a second binomial straight line. :just:`Q. E. D.`

Taking a rational straight line $kp$ commensurable in length with $\rho$, and selecting numbers of the same form as before, viz. $p(m^2 - n^2)$, $pn^2$, we put

$$p(m^2 - n^2) : pm^2 = k^2 p^2 : x^2 \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(1),$$

so that

$$x = kp \frac{m}{\sqrt{m^2 - n^2}}$$

$$= kp \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - \lambda^2}}, \text{ say} \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(2).$$

Just as before, $x$ is rational and $\sim kp$, whence $kp + x$ is a binomial straight line.

By (1),

$$x^2 > k^2 p^2.$$
