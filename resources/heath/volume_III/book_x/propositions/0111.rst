:title: Proposition 50 (cont.)

.. subclause::

   And, since :g:`$D$ <0110.g1:D>` has not to :g:`$AB$ <0110.g1:/ A B />` the ratio which a square number has to a square number,

.. assertion::

   neither has the square on :g:`$E$ <0110.g1:E>` to the square on :g:`$FG$ <0110.g1:/ F G />` the ratio which a square number has to a square number; therefore :g:`$E$ <0110.g1:E>` is incommensurable in length with :g:`$FG$ <0110.g1:/ F G />`. :just:`[X. 9] <X.9>`

Next let it be contrived that, as the number :g:`$BA$ <0110.g1:/ A B />` is to :g:`$AC$ <0110.g1:/ A C />`, so is the square on :g:`$FG$ <0110.g1:/ F G />` to the square on :g:`$GH$ <0110.g1:/ G H />`; :just:`[X. 6, Por.]`

.. assertion::

   therefore the square on :g:`$FG$ <0110.g1:/ F G />` is commensurable with the square on :g:`$GH$ <0110.g1:/ G H />`. :just:`[X. 6] <X.6>`

.. subclause::

   But :g:`$FG$ <0110.g1:/ F G />` is rational;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$GH$ <0110.g1:/ G H />` is also rational.

.. subclause::

   And, since :g:`$BA$ <0110.g1:/ A B />` has not to :g:`$AC$ <0110.g1:/ A C />` the ratio which a square number has to a square number,

.. assertion::

   neither has the square on :g:`$FG$ <0110.g1:/ F G />` to the square on :g:`$HG$ <0110.g1:/ G H />` the ratio which a square number has to a square number; therefore :g:`$FG$ <0110.g1:/ F G />` is incommensurable in length with :g:`$GH$ <0110.g1:/ G H />`. :just:`[X. 9] <X.9>`

.. assertion::

   Therefore :g:`$FG$ <0110.g1:/ F G />`, :g:`$GH$ <0110.g1:/ G H />` are rational straight lines commensurable in square only; therefore :g:`$FH$ <0110.g1:/ F H />` is binomial. :just:`[X. 36] <X.36>`

I say next that it is also a third binomial straight line.

.. subclause::

   For since, as :g:`$D$ <0110.g1:D>` is to :g:`$AB$ <0110.g1:/ A B />`, so is the square on :g:`$E$ <0110.g1:E>` to the square on :g:`$FG$ <0110.g1:/ F G />`, and, as :g:`$BA$ <0110.g1:/ A B />` is to :g:`$AC$ <0110.g1:/ A C />`, so is the square on :g:`$FG$ <0110.g1:/ F G />` to the square on :g:`$GH$ <0110.g1:/ G H />`,

.. assertion::

   therefore, :term:`*ex aequali* <ex-aequali>`, as :g:`$D$ <0110.g1:D>` is to :g:`$AC$ <0110.g1:/ A C />`, so is the square on :g:`$E$ <0110.g1:E>` to the square on :g:`$GH$ <0110.g1:/ G H />`. :just:`[V. 22] <V.22>`

.. subclause::

   But :g:`$D$ <0110.g1:D>` has not to :g:`$AC$ <0110.g1:/ A C />` the ratio which a square number has to a square number;

.. assertion::

   therefore neither has the square on :g:`$E$ <0110.g1:E>` to the square on :g:`$GH$ <0110.g1:/ G H />` the ratio which a square number has to a square number; therefore :g:`$E$ <0110.g1:E>` is incommensurable in length with :g:`$GH$ <0110.g1:/ G H />`. :just:`[X. 9] <X.9>`

.. subclause::

   And since, as :g:`$BA$ <0110.g1:/ A B />` is to :g:`$AC$ <0110.g1:/ A C />`, so is the square on :g:`$FG$ <0110.g1:/ F G />` to the square on :g:`$GH$ <0110.g1:/ G H />`,

.. assertion::

   therefore the square on :g:`$FG$ <0110.g1:/ F G />` is greater than the square on :g:`$GH$ <0110.g1:/ G H />`.

Let then the squares on :g:`$GH$ <0110.g1:/ G H />`, :g:`$K$ <0110.g1:K>` be equal to the square on :g:`$FG$ <0110.g1:/ F G />`;
