<p class="assertion">therefore, <em>convertendo</em>, as <span data-ref="0110.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0110.g1:/ C B /">$BC$</span>, so is the square on <span data-ref="0110.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span><br />
to the square on <span data-ref="0110.g1:K">$K$</span>. <span class="justification">[V. 19, Por.]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But <span data-ref="0110.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> has to <span data-ref="0110.g1:/ C B /">$BC$</span> the ratio which a square number has<br />
to a square number;</p>

<p class="assertion">therefore the square on <span data-ref="0110.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span> also has to the square on <span data-ref="0110.g1:K">$K$</span> the<br />
ratio which a square number has to a square number;<br />
therefore <span data-ref="0110.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span> is commensurable in length with <span data-ref="0110.g1:K">$K$</span>. <span class="justification">[X. 9]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore the square on <span data-ref="0110.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span> is greater than the square on<br />
<span data-ref="0110.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span> by the square on a straight line commensurable with <span data-ref="0110.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">And <span data-ref="0110.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span>, <span data-ref="0110.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span> are rational straight lines commensurable<br />
in square only, and neither of them is commensurable in length<br />
with <span data-ref="0110.g1:E">$E$</span>.</p>

<p class="qed">Therefore <span data-ref="0110.g1:/ F H /">$FH$</span> is a third binomial straight line.<span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

Let $\rho$ be a rational straight line.

Take the numbers $q(m^2 - n^2)$, $qn^2$,<br />
and let $p$ be a third number which is not a square and which has not to $qm^2$<br />
or $q(m^2 - n^2)$ the ratio of square to square.

Take $x$ such that
$$p : qm^2 = \rho^2 : x^2 \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(1).$$

Thus $x$ is rational and $\sim \rho$ $\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(2)$.

Next suppose that
$$qm^2 : q(m^2 - n^2) = x^2 : y^2 \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(3).$$

It follows that $y$ is rational and $\sim x$ $\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(4)$.

Thus $(x + y)$ is a binomial straight line.

Again, from (1) and (3), *ex aequali*,
$$p : q(m^2 - n^2) = \rho^2 : y^2 \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(5),$$
whence
$$y \cup \rho \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(6).$$

Suppose that
$$x^2 - y^2 = z^2.$$

Then, from (3), *convertendo*,
$$qm^2 : qn^2 = x^2 : z^2,$$
whence
$$z \sim x.$$

Thus
$$\sqrt{x^2 - y^2} \sim x,$$
and $x, y$ are both $\cup\ \rho$;<br />
therefore $x + y$ is a *third binomial* straight line.

Now, from (1),
$$x = \rho \cdot \frac{m\sqrt{q}}{\sqrt{p}},$$
and, by (5),
$$y = \rho \cdot \frac{\sqrt{m^2 - n^2} \cdot \sqrt{q}}{\sqrt{p}}.$$

Thus the *third binomial* is
$$\sqrt{\frac{q}{p}} \cdot \rho(m + \sqrt{m^2 - n^2}),$$
which we may write in the form
$$m\sqrt{k} \cdot \rho + m\sqrt{k} \cdot \rho\sqrt{1 - \lambda^2}.$$
