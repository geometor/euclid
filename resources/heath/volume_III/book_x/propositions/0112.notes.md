## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Proposition 50 Completion**:
  - The excess of $FG^2$ over $GH^2$ is $K^2$, commensurable in length with $FG$.
  - Neither $FG$ nor $GH$ is commensurable in length with $E$, satisfying the definition of the third binomial (Definitions II. 3).
- **Heath's Commentary**:
  - Formulates the algebraic conditions for a third binomial:
    $x = \rho \cdot \frac{m\sqrt{q}}{\sqrt{p}}$, $y = \rho \cdot \frac{\sqrt{m^2-n^2}\sqrt{q}}{\sqrt{p}}$.
  - The third binomial takes the form $\sqrt{\frac{q}{p}} \cdot \rho(m + \sqrt{m^2 - n^2})$, or $m\sqrt{k}\cdot\rho + m\sqrt{k}\cdot\rho\sqrt{1-\lambda^2}$.
  - Note on notation: Uses $\sim$ for commensurability (here in square with $\rho$, since $\rho^2 : x^2$ is rational) and $\cup$ for incommensurability in length ($y \cup \rho$ and $x, y$ both $\cup\ \rho$).
