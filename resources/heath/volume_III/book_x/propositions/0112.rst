:title: Proposition 50 (cont. & commentary)

.. assertion::

   | therefore, *convertendo*, as :g:`$AB$ <0110.g1:/ A B />` is to :g:`$BC$ <0110.g1:/ C B />`, so is the square on :g:`$FG$ <0110.g1:/ F G />`
   | to the square on :g:`$K$ <0110.g1:K>`. :just:`[V. 19, Por.]`

.. subclause::

   | But :g:`$AB$ <0110.g1:/ A B />` has to :g:`$BC$ <0110.g1:/ C B />` the ratio which a square number has
   | to a square number;

.. assertion::

   | therefore the square on :g:`$FG$ <0110.g1:/ F G />` also has to the square on :g:`$K$ <0110.g1:K>` the
   | ratio which a square number has to a square number;
   | therefore :g:`$FG$ <0110.g1:/ F G />` is commensurable in length with :g:`$K$ <0110.g1:K>`. :just:`[X. 9] <X.9>`

.. assertion::

   | Therefore the square on :g:`$FG$ <0110.g1:/ F G />` is greater than the square on
   | :g:`$GH$ <0110.g1:/ G H />` by the square on a straight line commensurable with :g:`$FG$ <0110.g1:/ F G />`.

.. subclause::

   | And :g:`$FG$ <0110.g1:/ F G />`, :g:`$GH$ <0110.g1:/ G H />` are rational straight lines commensurable
   | in square only, and neither of them is commensurable in length
   | with :g:`$E$ <0110.g1:E>`.

.. qed::

   Therefore :g:`$FH$ <0110.g1:/ F H />` is a third binomial straight line. :just:`Q. E. D.`

----

Let $\rho$ be a rational straight line.

| Take the numbers $q(m^2 - n^2)$, $qn^2$,
| and let $p$ be a third number which is not a square and which has not to $qm^2$
| or $q(m^2 - n^2)$ the ratio of square to square.

Take $x$ such that

$$p : qm^2 = \rho^2 : x^2 \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(1).$$

Thus $x$ is rational and $\sim \rho$ $\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(2)$.

Next suppose that

$$qm^2 : q(m^2 - n^2) = x^2 : y^2 \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(3).$$

It follows that $y$ is rational and $\sim x$ $\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(4)$.

Thus $(x + y)$ is a binomial straight line.

Again, from (1) and (3), *ex aequali*,

$$p : q(m^2 - n^2) = \rho^2 : y^2 \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(5),$$

whence

$$y \cup \rho \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(6).$$

Suppose that

$$x^2 - y^2 = z^2.$$

Then, from (3), *convertendo*,

$$qm^2 : qn^2 = x^2 : z^2,$$

whence

$$z \sim x.$$

Thus

$$\sqrt{x^2 - y^2} \sim x,$$

| and $x, y$ are both $\cup\ \rho$;
| therefore $x + y$ is a *third binomial* straight line.

Now, from (1),

$$x = \rho \cdot \frac{m\sqrt{q}}{\sqrt{p}},$$

and, by (5),

$$y = \rho \cdot \frac{\sqrt{m^2 - n^2} \cdot \sqrt{q}}{\sqrt{p}}.$$

Thus the *third binomial* is

$$\sqrt{\frac{q}{p}} \cdot \rho(m + \sqrt{m^2 - n^2}),$$

which we may write in the form

$$m\sqrt{k} \cdot \rho + m\sqrt{k} \cdot \rho\sqrt{1 - \lambda^2}.$$
