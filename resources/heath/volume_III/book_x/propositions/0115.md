Let it be contrived that, as <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ A C /">$CA$</span> is to <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, so is the square on <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> to the square on <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span>. [x. 6, Por.]

But <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ A C /">$CA$</span> has not to <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> the ratio which a square number has to a square number; therefore neither has the square on <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> to the square on <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span> the ratio which a square number has to a square number.

Therefore <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span> are rational straight lines commensurable in square only; [x. 9] therefore <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ E G /">$EG$</span> is binomial. [x. 36]

I say next that it is also a fifth binomial straight line.

For since, as <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ A C /">$CA$</span> is to <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, so is the square on <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> to the square on <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span>, inversely, as <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ A B /">$BA$</span> is to <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, so is the square on <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span> to the square on <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ E F /">$FE$</span>; therefore the square on <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ F G /">$GF$</span> is greater than the square on <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ E F /">$FE$</span>.

Let then the squares on <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, <span data-ref="0115.g1:H">$H$</span> be equal to the square on <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ F G /">$GF$</span>; therefore, *convertendo*, as the number <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ C B /">$BC$</span>, so is the square on <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ F G /">$GF$</span> to the square on <span data-ref="0115.g1:H">$H$</span>. [v. 19, Por.]

But <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> has not to <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ C B /">$BC$</span> the ratio which a square number has to a square number; therefore neither has the square on <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span> to the square on <span data-ref="0115.g1:H">$H$</span> the ratio which a square number has to a square number.

Therefore <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span> is incommensurable in length with <span data-ref="0115.g1:H">$H$</span>; [x. 9] so that the square on <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span> is greater than the square on <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ E F /">$FE$</span> by the square on a straight line incommensurable with <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span>.

And <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ F G /">$GF$</span>, <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ E F /">$FE$</span> are rational straight lines commensurable in square only, and the lesser term <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> is commensurable in length with the rational straight line <span data-ref="0115.g1:D">$D$</span> set out.

Therefore <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ E G /">$EG$</span> is a fifth binomial straight line.

Q. E. D.
