Let it be contrived that, as <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ A C /">$CA$</span> is to <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, so is the<br />
square on <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> to the square on <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span>. <span class="justification">[x. 6, Por.]</span>

<p class="sub-clause">But <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ A C /">$CA$</span> has not to <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> the ratio which a<br />
square number has to a square number;</p>

<p class="assertion">therefore neither has the square on <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> to the<br />
square on <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span> the ratio which a square number<br />
has to a square number.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span> are rational straight<br />
lines commensurable in square only; <span class="justification">[x. 9]</span><br />
therefore <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ E G /">$EG$</span> is binomial. <span class="justification">[x. 36]</span></p>

I say next that it is also a fifth binomial straight line.

<p class="sub-clause">For since, as <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ A C /">$CA$</span> is to <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, so is the square on <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> to<br />
the square on <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span>,<br />
inversely, as <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ A B /">$BA$</span> is to <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, so is the square on <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span> to the<br />
square on <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ E F /">$FE$</span>;</p>

<p class="assertion">therefore the square on <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ F G /">$GF$</span> is greater than the square on <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ E F /">$FE$</span>.</p>

Let then the squares on <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, <span data-ref="0115.g1:H">$H$</span> be equal to the square<br />
on <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ F G /">$GF$</span>;

<p class="assertion">therefore, *convertendo*, as the number <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ C B /">$BC$</span>, so is the<br />
square on <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ F G /">$GF$</span> to the square on <span data-ref="0115.g1:H">$H$</span>. <span class="justification">[v. 19, Por.]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> has not to <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ C B /">$BC$</span> the ratio which a square number<br />
has to a square number;</p>

<p class="assertion">therefore neither has the square on <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span> to the square on <span data-ref="0115.g1:H">$H$</span><br />
the ratio which a square number has to a square number.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span> is incommensurable in length with <span data-ref="0115.g1:H">$H$</span>; <span class="justification">[x. 9]</span><br />
so that the square on <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span> is greater than the square on <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ E F /">$FE$</span><br />
by the square on a straight line incommensurable with <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">And <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ F G /">$GF$</span>, <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ E F /">$FE$</span> are rational straight lines commensurable<br />
in square only, and the lesser term <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> is commensurable in<br />
length with the rational straight line <span data-ref="0115.g1:D">$D$</span> set out.</p>

<p class="qed">Therefore <span data-ref="0115.g1:/ E G /">$EG$</span> is a fifth binomial straight line.<span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>
