<p class="sub-clause">But <span data-ref="0116.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> has not to <span data-ref="0116.g1:/ C B /">$BC$</span> the ratio which a square number<br />

Take numbers $m$, $n$ such that $(m + n)$ has not to either of the numbers<br />
$m$, $n$ the ratio of square to square; take also a third number $p$, which is not<br />
square, and which has not to either of the numbers $(m + n)$, $m$ the ratio of<br />
square to square.

Let
$$p : (m + n) = p^2 : x^2 \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(1)$$
and
$$(m + n) : m = x^2 : y^2 \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(2).$$

Then shall $(x + y)$ be a sixth binomial straight line.

For, by (1), $x$ is rational and $\smile p$.

By (2), since $x$ is rational,<br />
$y$ is rational and $\smile x$.

Hence $x$, $y$ are rational and commensurable in square only, so that $(x + y)$<br />
is a binomial straight line.

Again, *ex aequali*, from (1) and (2),
$$p : m = p^2 : y^2 \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(3),$$
whence $y \smile p$.

Thus $x$, $y$ are both incommensurable in length with $p$.

Lastly, from (2), *convertendo*,
$$(m + n) : n = x^2 : (x^2 - y^2),$$
so that $\sqrt{x^2 - y^2} \smile x$.

Therefore $(x + y)$ is a sixth binomial straight line.

Now, from (1) and (3),
$$x = p \cdot \sqrt{\frac{m + n}{p}} = p\sqrt{k}\text{, say,}$$
$$y = p \cdot \sqrt{\frac{m}{p}} = p\sqrt{\lambda}\text{, say,}$$
and the sixth binomial straight line may be written
$$\sqrt{k}\cdot p + \sqrt{\lambda}\cdot p.$$

The corresponding *sixth apotome* is [X. 90]
$$\sqrt{k}\cdot p - \sqrt{\lambda}\cdot p;$$
