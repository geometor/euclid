Secondly, it results from (1), [by x. 17], that

so that $u$, $v$ are both $\cap (u + v)$, and therefore $\cap \rho \ldots\ldots\ldots\ldots\ldots\ldots(4);$<br />

Therefore $x^2$, $y^2$ are rational and commensurable $\ldots\ldots\ldots\ldots\ldots\ldots(5).$<br />
Next, $k\rho \cup k\rho\sqrt{1 - \lambda^2},$<br />
and $k\rho \cap u$, while $k\rho\sqrt{1 - \lambda^2} \cap \frac{1}{2}k\rho\sqrt{1 - \lambda^2};$<br />
therefore
$$u \cup \frac{1}{2}k\rho\sqrt{1 - \lambda^2},$$
whence
$$\rho u \cup \frac{1}{2}k\rho^2\sqrt{1 - \lambda^2},$$
or
$$x^2 \cup xy,$$
so that
$$x \cup y.$$
By this and (5), $x$, $y$ are rational and $\sim$, so that $(x + y)$ is a binomial<br />
straight line. <span class="justification">[X. 36]</span>

x. 91 will prove in like manner that a like theorem holds for *apotomes*,<br />
viz. that
$$\rho\sqrt{\frac{k}{2}(1 + \lambda)} - \rho\sqrt{\frac{k}{2}(1 - \lambda)} = \sqrt{\rho(k\rho - k\rho\sqrt{1 - \lambda^2})}.$$
Since the *first binomial* straight line and the *first apotome* are the roots of<br />
the equation
$$x^2 - 2k\rho \cdot x + \lambda^2 k^2 \rho^2 = 0,$$
this proposition and x. 91 give us the solution of the biquadratic equation
$$x^4 - 2k\rho^2 \cdot x^2 + \lambda^2 k^2 \rho^4 = 0.$$
