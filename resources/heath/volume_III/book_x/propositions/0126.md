And, since <span data-ref="0125.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> is incommensurable in length with <span data-ref="0125.g1:/ E D /">$ED$</span>,
while <span data-ref="0125.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> is commensurable with <span data-ref="0125.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>,
<sup>50</sup> and <span data-ref="0125.g1:/ E D /">$ED$</span> is commensurable with <span data-ref="0125.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>,
therefore <span data-ref="0125.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span> is incommensurable with <span data-ref="0125.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>; [x. 13]
so that <span data-ref="0125.g1:< A H >">$AH$</span> is also incommensurable with <span data-ref="0125.g1:< E L >">$EL$</span>,
that is, <span data-ref="0125.g1:< S N >">$SN$</span> is incommensurable with <span data-ref="0125.g1:< M R >">$MR$</span>,
that is, <span data-ref="0125.g1:/ N P /">$PN$</span> with <span data-ref="0125.g1:/ R N /">$NR$</span>, [VI. 1, x. 11]
<sup>55</sup> that is, <span data-ref="0125.g1:/ M N /">$MN$</span> is incommensurable in length with <span data-ref="0125.g1:/ N O /">$NO$</span>.
But <span data-ref="0125.g1:/ M N /">$MN$</span>, <span data-ref="0125.g1:/ N O /">$NO$</span> were proved to be both medial and commensurable in square;
therefore <span data-ref="0125.g1:/ M N /">$MN$</span>, <span data-ref="0125.g1:/ N O /">$NO$</span> are medial straight lines commensurable in square only.
<sup>60</sup> I say next that they also contain a rational rectangle.
For, since <span data-ref="0125.g1:/ E D /">$DE$</span> is, by hypothesis, commensurable with each of the straight lines <span data-ref="0125.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0125.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>,
therefore <span data-ref="0125.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> is also commensurable with <span data-ref="0125.g1:/ E K /">$EK$</span>. [x. 12]
And each of them is rational;
<sup>65</sup> therefore <span data-ref="0125.g1:< E L >">$EL$</span>, that is, <span data-ref="0125.g1:< M R >">$MR$</span> is rational, [x. 19]
and <span data-ref="0125.g1:< M R >">$MR$</span> is the rectangle <span data-ref="0125.g1:/ M N /">$MN$</span>, <span data-ref="0125.g1:/ N O /">$NO$</span>.
But, if two medial straight lines commensurable in square only and containing a rational rectangle be added together, the whole is irrational and is called a first bimedial straight line. [x. 37]
<sup>70</sup> Therefore <span data-ref="0125.g1:/ M O /">$MO$</span> is a first bimedial straight line.
Q. E. D.

39. **Therefore <span data-ref="0125.g1:/ A B /">$BA$</span>, <span data-ref="0125.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span> and <span data-ref="0125.g1:/ A B /">$BA$</span>, <span data-ref="0125.g1:/ G E /">$GE$</span> are pairs of rational straight lines commensurable in square only.** The text has "Therefore <span data-ref="0125.g1:/ A B /">$BA$</span>, <span data-ref="0125.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0125.g1:/ G E /">$GE$</span> are rational straight lines commensurable in square only," which I have altered because it would naturally convey the impression that *any two* of the three straight lines are commensurable in square only, whereas <span data-ref="0125.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0125.g1:/ G E /">$GE$</span> are commensurable in length (l. 18), and it is only the other two pairs which are commensurable in square only.

A *second binomial* straight line being [x. 49] of the form
$$\frac{k\rho}{\sqrt{1 - \lambda^2}} + k\rho,$$
the present proposition is equivalent to finding the square root of the expression
$$\rho \left(\frac{k\rho}{\sqrt{1 - \lambda^2}} + k\rho\right).$$
