<p class="qed">Therefore <span data-ref="0134.g1:/ M O /">$MO$</span> is the side of the sum of two medial areas<br />

Euclid here finds the square root of the expression [cf. X. 53]
$$\rho(\sqrt{k}\cdot\rho + \sqrt{\lambda}\cdot\rho).$$
As usual, we solve the equations
$$\left.\begin{aligned} u+v &= \sqrt{k}\cdot\rho \\ uv &= \frac{1}{4}\lambda\rho^2 \end{aligned}\right\}\quad\quad\quad\quad\dots\dots(1);$$
then, $u$, $v$ being found, we put
$$\left.\begin{aligned} x^2 &= \rho u \\ y^2 &= \rho v \end{aligned}\right\}\quad\quad\quad\quad\dots\dots(2),$$
and $(x+y)$ is the square root required.

Euclid proves that $(x+y)$ is the *side of (the sum of) two medial areas*, as<br />
follows.

As in the last two propositions, $x$, $y$ are proved to be incommensurable<br />
in square.

Now $\sqrt{k}\cdot\rho$, $\rho$ are commensurable in square only;
therefore $\rho(u+v)$, or $(x^2+y^2)$, is a medial area $\dots\dots(3).$

Next, $xy = \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\lambda}\cdot\rho^2$, which is again a medial area $\dots\dots(4).$

Lastly,
$$\sqrt{k}\cdot\rho \smile \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\lambda}\cdot\rho,$$
$$\sqrt{k}\cdot\rho^2 \smile \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\lambda}\cdot\rho^2;$$
that is,
$$(x^2+y^2) \smile xy \dots\dots(5).$$

Hence [(3), (4), (5)] $(x+y)$ is the *side of the sum of two medial areas*.

Solving the equations algebraically, we have
$$\begin{aligned} u &= \frac{\rho}{2}(\sqrt{k} + \sqrt{k-\lambda}), \\ v &= \frac{\rho}{2}(\sqrt{k} - \sqrt{k-\lambda}), \end{aligned}$$
and
$$x+y = \rho\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}(\sqrt{k}+\sqrt{k-\lambda})} + \rho\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}(\sqrt{k}-\sqrt{k-\lambda})}.$$

The corresponding square root found in X. 96 is $x-y$, where $x$, $y$ are the<br />
same as here.

The two square roots are (cf. note on X. 53) the roots of the biquadratic<br />
equation
$$x^4 - 2\sqrt{k}\cdot\rho^2 x^2 + (k-\lambda)\rho^4 = 0.$$

### [LEMMA.

<p class="enunciation">If a straight line be cut into unequal parts, the squares<br />
on the unequal parts are greater<br />
than twice the rectangle con-<br />
tained by the unequal parts.</p>

![Lemma after Proposition 59](0135.g1.svg)

Let <span data-ref="0135.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> be a straight line, and let it be cut into unequal<br />
parts at <span data-ref="0135.g1:C">$C$</span>, and let <span data-ref="0135.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> be the greater ;<br />
I say that the squares on <span data-ref="0135.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0135.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span> are greater than twice the<br />
rectangle <span data-ref="0135.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0135.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span>.
