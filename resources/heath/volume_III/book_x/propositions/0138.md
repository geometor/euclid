In the hexad of propositions beginning with this we have the solution of<br />
the converse problem to that of X. 54—59. We find the *squares* of the<br />
irrational straight lines of X. 36—41 and prove that they are respectively equal<br />
to the rectangles contained by a rational straight line and the *first*, *second*,<br />
*third*, *fourth*, *fifth* and *sixth binomials*.

In X. 60 we prove that, $\rho + \sqrt{k} \,.\, \rho$ being a *binomial* straight line [X. 36],
$$\frac{(\rho + \sqrt{k} \,.\, \rho)^2}{\sigma}$$
is a *first binomial* straight line, and we find it geometrically.

The procedure may be represented thus.<br />
Take $x, y, z$ such that
$$\begin{aligned}
\sigma x &= \rho^2,\\
\sigma y &= k\rho^2,\\
\sigma \cdot 2z &= 2\sqrt{k} \cdot \rho^2,
\end{aligned}$$
$\rho^2, k\rho^2$ being of course the squares on the terms of the original binomial,<br />
and $2\sqrt{k} \cdot \rho^2$ twice the rectangle contained by them.

Then
$$(x + y) + 2z = \frac{(\rho + \sqrt{k} \cdot \rho)^2}{\sigma},$$
and we have to prove that $(x + y) + 2z$ is a *first binomial* straight line of which<br />
$(x + y), 2z$ are the terms and $(x + y)$ the greater.

Euclid divides the proof into two parts, showing first that $(x + y) + 2z$ is<br />
*some* binomial, and secondly that it is the *first binomial*.
