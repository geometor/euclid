----

In the hexad of propositions beginning with this we have the solution of the converse problem to that of X. 54—59. We find the *squares* of the irrational straight lines of X. 36—41 and prove that they are respectively equal to the rectangles contained by a rational straight line and the *first*, *second*, *third*, *fourth*, *fifth* and *sixth binomials*.

In X. 60 we prove that, $\rho + \sqrt{k} \,.\, \rho$ being a *binomial* straight line [X. 36],

$$\frac{(\rho + \sqrt{k} \,.\, \rho)^2}{\sigma}$$

is a :term:`*first binomial* <first-binomial>` straight line, and we find it geometrically.

The procedure may be represented thus. Take $x, y, z$ such that

$$\begin{aligned}
\sigma x &= \rho^2,\\
\sigma y &= k\rho^2,\\
\sigma \cdot 2z &= 2\sqrt{k} \cdot \rho^2,
\end{aligned}$$

$\rho^2, k\rho^2$ being of course the squares on the terms of the original binomial, and $2\sqrt{k} \cdot \rho^2$ twice the rectangle contained by them.

Then

$$(x + y) + 2z = \frac{(\rho + \sqrt{k} \cdot \rho)^2}{\sigma},$$

and we have to prove that $(x + y) + 2z$ is a :term:`*first binomial* <first-binomial>` straight line of which $(x + y), 2z$ are the terms and $(x + y)$ the greater.

Euclid divides the proof into two parts, showing first that $(x + y) + 2z$ is *some* binomial, and secondly that it is the :term:`*first binomial* <first-binomial>`.
