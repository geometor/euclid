($\alpha$) $\rho \sim \sqrt{k} \cdot \rho$, so that $\rho^2, k\rho^2$ are rational and commensurable; therefore $\rho^2 + k\rho^2$, or $\sigma (x + y)$, is a rational area,
whence
$$(x + y)\text{ is rational and } \sim \sigma \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(1).$$
Next, $2\rho \cdot \sqrt{k} \cdot \rho$ is a medial area, so that $\sigma \cdot 2z$ is a medial area,
whence
$$2z\text{ is rational but } \smile \sigma \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(2).$$
Hence [(1), (2)] $(x + y), 2z$ are rational and commensurable in square only
$$\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(3);$$
thus $(x + y) + 2z$ is a *binomial* straight line. [x. 36]

($\beta$) $\rho^2 : \sqrt{k} \cdot \rho^2 = \sqrt{k} \cdot \rho^2 : k\rho^2,$
so that $\sigma x : \sigma z = \sigma z : \sigma y,$
and $x : z = z : y,$
or
$$xy = z^2 = \frac{1}{4}(2z)^2 \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(4).$$
Now $\rho^2, k\rho^2$ are commensurable, so that $\sigma x, \sigma y$ are commensurable, and therefore
$$x \sim y \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(5).$$
And, since [Lemma] $\rho^2 + k\rho^2 > 2\sqrt{k} \cdot \rho^2,$
$$x + y > 2z.$$
But $(x + y)$ is given, being equal to $\frac{\rho^2 + k\rho^2}{\sigma} \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(6).$
Therefore [(4), (5), (6), and x. 17] $\sqrt{(x + y)^2 - (2z)^2} \sim (x + y).$
And $(x + y), 2z$ are rational and $\smile$ [(3)],
while $(x + y) \sim \sigma$ [(1)].
Hence $(x + y) + 2z$ is a *first binomial*.
The actual value of $(x + y) + 2z$ is, of course,
$$\frac{\rho^2}{\sigma}(1 + k + 2\sqrt{k}).$$

### PROPOSITION 61.

*The square on the first bimedial straight line applied to a rational straight line produces as breadth the second binomial.*

Let <span data-ref="0139.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> be a first bimedial straight line divided into its medials at <span data-ref="0139.g1:C">$C$</span>, of which medials <span data-ref="0139.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is the greater; let a rational straight line <span data-ref="0139.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> be set out, and let there be applied to <span data-ref="0139.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> the parallelogram <span data-ref="0139.g1:< D F >">$DF$</span> equal to the square on <span data-ref="0139.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, producing <span data-ref="0139.g1:/ D G /">$DG$</span> as its breadth;
I say that <span data-ref="0139.g1:/ D G /">$DG$</span> is a second binomial straight line.
