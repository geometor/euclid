The form of the proposition and the figure being similar to those of X. 60,<br />
I can somewhat abbreviate the reproduction of the proof.<br />
Take $x, y, z$ such that
$$\begin{aligned}
\sigma x &= k^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho^2,\\
\sigma y &= k^{\frac{3}{2}}\rho^2,\\
\sigma \cdot 2z &= 2k\rho^2.
\end{aligned}$$
Then shall $(x + y) + 2z$ be a second binomial.

($\alpha$) $k^{\frac{1}{4}}\rho, k^{\frac{3}{4}}\rho$ are medial straight lines commensurable in square only and<br />
containing a rational rectangle. <span class="justification">[X. 37]</span><br />
The squares $k^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho^2, k^{\frac{3}{2}}\rho^2$ are medial;<br />
thus the sum, or $\sigma (x + y)$, is medial. <span class="justification">[X. 23, Por.]</span><br />
Therefore $(x + y)$ is rational and $\smile \sigma.$<br />
And $\sigma \cdot 2z$ is rational;<br />
therefore
$$2z\text{ is rational and } \sim \sigma \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(1).$$
Therefore $(x + y), 2z$ are rational and $\smile \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(2),$<br />
so that
$$(x + y) + 2z\text{ is a binomial.}$$

($\beta$) As before,
$$(x + y) > 2z.$$
Now, $k^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho^2, k^{\frac{3}{2}}\rho^2$ being commensurable,
$$x \sim y.$$
And
$$xy = z^2,$$
while
$$x + y = \frac{k^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho^2 + k^{\frac{3}{2}}\rho^2}{\sigma}.$$
Hence <span class="justification">[X. 17]</span>
$$\sqrt{(x + y)^2 - (2z)^2} \sim (x + y) \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(3).$$
But $2z \sim \sigma,$ by (1).<br />
Therefore [(1), (2), (3)] $(x + y) + 2z$ is a *second binomial* straight line.

Of course
$$(x + y) + 2z = \frac{\rho^2}{\sigma}\{\sqrt{k}(1 + k) + 2k\}.$$

### PROPOSITION 62.

<p class="enunciation">The square on the second bimedial straight line applied to<br />
a rational straight line produces as breadth the third binomial.</p>

Let <span data-ref="0141.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> be a second bimedial straight line divided into<br />
its medials at <span data-ref="0141.g1:C">$C$</span>, so that <span data-ref="0141.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is the<br />
greater segment;<br />
let <span data-ref="0141.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> be any rational straight line,<br />
and to <span data-ref="0141.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> let there be applied the<br />
parallelogram <span data-ref="0141.g1:< D F >">$DF$</span> equal to the square<br />
on <span data-ref="0141.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> and producing <span data-ref="0141.g1:/ D G /">$DG$</span> as its<br />
breadth;<br />
I say that <span data-ref="0141.g1:/ D G /">$DG$</span> is a third binomial<br />
straight line.
