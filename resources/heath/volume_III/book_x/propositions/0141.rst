:title: Proposition 61 (commentary concluded); Proposition 62

The form of the proposition and the figure being similar to those of X. 60, I can somewhat abbreviate the reproduction of the proof. Take $x, y, z$ such that

$$\begin{aligned}
\sigma x &= k^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho^2,\\
\sigma y &= k^{\frac{3}{2}}\rho^2,\\
\sigma \cdot 2z &= 2k\rho^2.
\end{aligned}$$

Then shall $(x + y) + 2z$ be a second binomial.

($\alpha$) $k^{\frac{1}{4}}\rho, k^{\frac{3}{4}}\rho$ are medial straight lines commensurable in square only and containing a rational rectangle. :just:`[X. 37] <X.37>` The squares $k^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho^2, k^{\frac{3}{2}}\rho^2$ are medial; thus the sum, or $\sigma (x + y)$, is medial. :just:`[X. 23, Por.]` Therefore $(x + y)$ is rational and $\smile \sigma.$ And $\sigma \cdot 2z$ is rational; therefore

$$2z\text{ is rational and } \sim \sigma \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(1).$$

Therefore $(x + y), 2z$ are rational and $\smile \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(2),$ so that

$$(x + y) + 2z\text{ is a binomial.}$$

($\beta$) As before,

$$(x + y) > 2z.$$

Now, $k^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho^2, k^{\frac{3}{2}}\rho^2$ being commensurable,

$$x \sim y.$$

And

$$xy = z^2,$$

while

$$x + y = \frac{k^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho^2 + k^{\frac{3}{2}}\rho^2}{\sigma}.$$

Hence :just:`[X. 17] <X.17>`

$$\sqrt{(x + y)^2 - (2z)^2} \sim (x + y) \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(3).$$

But $2z \sim \sigma,$ by (1). Therefore [(1), (2), (3)] $(x + y) + 2z$ is a :term:`*second binomial* <second-binomial>` straight line.

Of course

$$(x + y) + 2z = \frac{\rho^2}{\sigma}\{\sqrt{k}(1 + k) + 2k\}.$$

PROPOSITION 62.
---------------

.. enunciation::

   The square on the second bimedial straight line applied to a rational straight line produces as breadth the third binomial.

Let :g:`AB <0141.g1:/ A B />` be a second bimedial straight line divided into its medials at :g:`C <0141.g1:C>`, so that :g:`AC <0141.g1:/ A C />` is the greater segment; let :g:`DE <0141.g1:/ D E />` be any rational straight line, and to :g:`DE <0141.g1:/ D E />` let there be applied the parallelogram :g:`DF <0141.g1:< D F >>` equal to the square on :g:`AB <0141.g1:/ A B />` and producing :g:`DG <0141.g1:/ D G />` as its breadth; I say that :g:`DG <0141.g1:/ D G />` is a third binomial straight line.
