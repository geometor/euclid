Then, since <span data-ref="0141.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is a second bimedial divided at <span data-ref="0141.g1:C">$C$</span>, therefore <span data-ref="0141.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0141.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span> are medial straight lines commensurable in square only and containing a medial rectangle, [x. 38]
so that the sum of the squares on <span data-ref="0141.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0141.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span> is also medial.
<p align="right">[x. 15 and 23 Por.]</p>
And it is equal to <span data-ref="0141.g1:< D L >">$DL$</span>;
therefore <span data-ref="0141.g1:< D L >">$DL$</span> is also medial.
And it is applied to the rational straight line <span data-ref="0141.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>;
therefore <span data-ref="0141.g1:/ M D /">$MD$</span> is also rational and incommensurable in length with <span data-ref="0141.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>. [x. 22]
For the same reason,
<span data-ref="0141.g1:/ M G /">$MG$</span> is also rational and incommensurable in length with <span data-ref="0141.g1:/ M L /">$ML$</span>, that is, with <span data-ref="0141.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>;
therefore each of the straight lines <span data-ref="0141.g1:/ D M /">$DM$</span>, <span data-ref="0141.g1:/ M G /">$MG$</span> is rational and incommensurable in length with <span data-ref="0141.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>.
And, since <span data-ref="0141.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is incommensurable in length with <span data-ref="0141.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span>, and, as <span data-ref="0141.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is to <span data-ref="0141.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span>, so is the square on <span data-ref="0141.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> to the rectangle <span data-ref="0141.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0141.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span>, therefore the square on <span data-ref="0141.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is also incommensurable with the rectangle <span data-ref="0141.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0141.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span>. [x. 11]
Hence the sum of the squares on <span data-ref="0141.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0141.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span> is incommensurable with twice the rectangle <span data-ref="0141.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0141.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span>, [x. 12, 13] that is, <span data-ref="0141.g1:< D L >">$DL$</span> is incommensurable with <span data-ref="0141.g1:< M F >">$MF$</span>, so that <span data-ref="0141.g1:/ D M /">$DM$</span> is also incommensurable with <span data-ref="0141.g1:/ M G /">$MG$</span>. [VI. 1, x. 11]
And they are rational;
therefore <span data-ref="0141.g1:/ D G /">$DG$</span> is binomial. [x. 36]

It is to be proved that it is also a third binomial straight line.
In manner similar to the foregoing we may conclude that <span data-ref="0141.g1:/ D M /">$DM$</span> is greater than <span data-ref="0141.g1:/ M G /">$MG$</span>, and that <span data-ref="0141.g1:/ D K /">$DK$</span> is commensurable with <span data-ref="0141.g1:/ K M /">$KM$</span>.
And the rectangle <span data-ref="0141.g1:/ D K /">$DK$</span>, <span data-ref="0141.g1:/ K M /">$KM$</span> is equal to the square on <span data-ref="0141.g1:/ M N /">$MN$</span>;
therefore the square on <span data-ref="0141.g1:/ D M /">$DM$</span> is greater than the square on <span data-ref="0141.g1:/ M G /">$MG$</span> by the square on a straight line commensurable with <span data-ref="0141.g1:/ D M /">$DM$</span>.
And neither of the straight lines <span data-ref="0141.g1:/ D M /">$DM$</span>, <span data-ref="0141.g1:/ M G /">$MG$</span> is commensurable in length with <span data-ref="0141.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>.
Therefore <span data-ref="0141.g1:/ D G /">$DG$</span> is a third binomial straight line. [x. Deff. II. 3]

<p align="right">Q. E. D.</p>
