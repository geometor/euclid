----

To prove that [cf. x. 40]

$$
\frac{1}{\sigma} \left\{ \frac{\rho}{\sqrt{2(1 + k^2)}} \sqrt{\sqrt{1 + k^2} + k} + \frac{\rho}{\sqrt{2(1 + k^2)}} \sqrt{\sqrt{1 + k^2} - k} \right\}^2
$$

is a :term:`*fifth binomial* <fifth-binomial>` straight line.
For brevity denote it by $\frac{1}{\sigma} (u + v)^2$, and put

$$
\left.
\begin{aligned}
\sigma x &= u^2, \\
\sigma y &= v^2, \\
\sigma \cdot 2z &= 2uv.
\end{aligned}
\right\}
$$

Remembering that [x. 40] $u^2 \smile v^2$, $(u^2 + v^2)$ is medial, and $2uv$ is rational,
we proceed thus.
($\alpha$) $\sigma(x + y)$ is medial ;
therefore $(x + y)$ is rational and $\smile \sigma$ .................................(1).
Next, $\sigma \cdot 2z$ is rational ;
therefore $2z$ is rational and $\sim \sigma$ .......................................(2).
Thus $(x + y)$, $2z$ are rational and $\sim$ ................................(3),
so that $(x + y) + 2z$ is a binomial straight line.
($\beta$) As before,

$$
\begin{aligned}
x + y &> 2z, \\
xy &= z^2,
\end{aligned}
$$

and

$$
x \smile y.
$$

Therefore [x. 18] $\sqrt{(x + y)^2 - (2z)^2} \smile (x + y)$ .......................(4).
Hence [(2), (3), (4)] $(x + y) + 2z$ is a :term:`*fifth binomial* <fifth-binomial>` straight line.
It is of course

$$
\frac{\rho^2}{\sigma} \left\{ \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + k^2}} + \frac{1}{1 + k^2} \right\}.
$$

PROPOSITION 65.
---------------

.. enunciation::

   The square on the side of the sum of two medial areas applied to a rational straight line produces as breadth the sixth binomial.

| Let :g:`$AB$ <0148.g1:/ A B />` be the side of the sum of two medial areas,
| divided at :g:`$C$ <0148.g1:C>`,
| let :g:`$DE$ <0148.g1:/ D E />` be a rational straight line,
| and let there be applied to :g:`$DE$ <0148.g1:/ D E />` the parallelogram :g:`$DF$ <0148.g1:< D F >>` equal
| to the square on :g:`$AB$ <0148.g1:/ A B />`, producing :g:`$DG$ <0148.g1:/ D G />` as its breadth ;
