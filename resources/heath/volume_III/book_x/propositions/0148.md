I say that <span data-ref="0148.g1:/ D G /">$DG$</span> is a sixth binomial straight line.
   For let the same construction be made as before.
   Then, since <span data-ref="0148.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is the side of the sum of two medial areas, divided at <span data-ref="0148.g1:C">$C$</span>,
therefore <span data-ref="0148.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0148.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span> are straight lines incommensurable in square which make the sum of the squares on them medial, the rectangle contained by them medial, and moreover the sum of the squares on them incommensurable with the rectangle contained by them, [x. 41]
so that, in accordance with what was before proved, each of the rectangles <span data-ref="0148.g1:< D L >">$DL$</span>, <span data-ref="0148.g1:< M F >">$MF$</span> is medial.

![Proposition 65 Diagram](0148.g1.svg)

   And they are applied to the rational straight line <span data-ref="0148.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> ;
therefore each of the straight lines <span data-ref="0148.g1:/ D M /">$DM$</span>, <span data-ref="0148.g1:/ M G /">$MG$</span> is rational and incommensurable in length with <span data-ref="0148.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>. [x. 22]
   And, since the sum of the squares on <span data-ref="0148.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0148.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span> is incommensurable with twice the rectangle <span data-ref="0148.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0148.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span>,
therefore <span data-ref="0148.g1:< D L >">$DL$</span> is incommensurable with <span data-ref="0148.g1:< M F >">$MF$</span>.
   Therefore <span data-ref="0148.g1:/ D M /">$DM$</span> is also incommensurable with <span data-ref="0148.g1:/ M G /">$MG$</span> ; [vI. 1, x. 11]
therefore <span data-ref="0148.g1:/ D M /">$DM$</span>, <span data-ref="0148.g1:/ M G /">$MG$</span> are rational straight lines commensurable in square only ;
therefore <span data-ref="0148.g1:/ D G /">$DG$</span> is binomial. [x. 36]
   I say next that it is also a sixth binomial straight line.
   Similarly again we can prove that the rectangle <span data-ref="0148.g1:/ D K /">$DK$</span>, <span data-ref="0148.g1:/ K M /">$KM$</span> is equal to the square on <span data-ref="0148.g1:/ M N /">$MN$</span>,
and that <span data-ref="0148.g1:/ D K /">$DK$</span> is incommensurable in length with <span data-ref="0148.g1:/ K M /">$KM$</span> ;
and, for the same reason, the square on <span data-ref="0148.g1:/ D M /">$DM$</span> is greater than the square on <span data-ref="0148.g1:/ M G /">$MG$</span> by the square on a straight line incommensurable in length with <span data-ref="0148.g1:/ D M /">$DM$</span>.
   And neither of the straight lines <span data-ref="0148.g1:/ D M /">$DM$</span>, <span data-ref="0148.g1:/ M G /">$MG$</span> is commensurable in length with the rational straight line <span data-ref="0148.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> set out.
   Therefore <span data-ref="0148.g1:/ D G /">$DG$</span> is a sixth binomial straight line.
                                                                 Q. E. D.
