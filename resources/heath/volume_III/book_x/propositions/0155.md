### PROPOSITION 70.

*A straight line commensurable with the side of the sum of two medial areas is the side of the sum of two medial areas.*

Let <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> be the side of the sum of two medial areas, and <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> commensurable with <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>;
it is to be proved that <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> is also the side of the sum of two medial areas.

For, since <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is the side of the sum of two medial areas,
let it be divided into its straight lines at <span data-ref="0155.g1:E">$E$</span>;
therefore <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span>, <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span> are straight lines incommensurable in square which make the sum of the squares on them medial, the rectangle contained by them medial, and furthermore the sum of the squares on <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span>, <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span> incommensurable with the rectangle <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span>, <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span>. [x. 41]

![Proposition 70](0155.g1.svg)

Let the same construction be made as before.

We can then prove similarly that
<span data-ref="0155.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span>, <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ F D /">$FD$</span> are also incommensurable in square,
the sum of the squares on <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span>, <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span> is commensurable with the sum of the squares on <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span>, <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ F D /">$FD$</span>,
and the rectangle <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span>, <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span> with the rectangle <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span>, <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ F D /">$FD$</span>;
so that the sum of the squares on <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span>, <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ F D /">$FD$</span> is also medial,
the rectangle <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span>, <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ F D /">$FD$</span> is medial,
and moreover the sum of the squares on <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span>, <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ F D /">$FD$</span> is incommensurable with the rectangle <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span>, <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ F D /">$FD$</span>.

Therefore <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> is the side of the sum of two medial areas.
<p align="right">Q. E. D.</p>

### PROPOSITION 71.

*If a rational and a medial area be added together, four irrational straight lines arise, namely a binomial or a first bimedial or a major or a side of a rational plus a medial area.*

Let <span data-ref="0156.g1:< A B >">$AB$</span> be rational, and <span data-ref="0156.g1:< C D >">$CD$</span> medial;
I say that the "side" of the area <span data-ref="0156.g1:< A D >">$AD$</span> is a binomial or a first bimedial or a major or a side of a rational plus a medial area.
