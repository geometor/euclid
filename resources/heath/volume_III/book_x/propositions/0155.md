### PROPOSITION 70.

<p class="enunciation">A straight line commensurable with the side of the sum<br />
of two medial areas is the side of the sum of two medial areas.</p>

Let <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> be the side of the sum of two medial areas, and<br />
<span data-ref="0155.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> commensurable with <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>;<br />
it is to be proved that <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> is also the side of the<br />
sum of two medial areas.

<p class="sub-clause">For, since <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is the side of the sum of two<br />
medial areas,</p>

let it be divided into its straight lines at <span data-ref="0155.g1:E">$E$</span>;

<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span>, <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span> are straight lines incommensur-<br />
able in square which make the sum of the squares<br />
on them medial, the rectangle contained by them<br />
medial, and furthermore the sum of the squares on <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span>, <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span><br />
incommensurable with the rectangle <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span>, <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span>. <span class="justification">[x. 41]</span></p>

![Proposition 70](0155.g1.svg)

Let the same construction be made as before.

<p class="assertion">We can then prove similarly that<br />
<span data-ref="0155.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span>, <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ F D /">$FD$</span> are also incommensurable in square,<br />
the sum of the squares on <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span>, <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span> is commensurable with<br />
the sum of the squares on <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span>, <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ F D /">$FD$</span>,<br />
and the rectangle <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span>, <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span> with the rectangle <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span>, <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ F D /">$FD$</span>;<br />
so that the sum of the squares on <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span>, <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ F D /">$FD$</span> is also medial,<br />
the rectangle <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span>, <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ F D /">$FD$</span> is medial,<br />
and moreover the sum of the squares on <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span>, <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ F D /">$FD$</span> is incom-<br />
mensurable with the rectangle <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span>, <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ F D /">$FD$</span>.</p>

<p class="qed">Therefore <span data-ref="0155.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> is the side of the sum of two medial areas.<span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

### PROPOSITION 71.

<p class="enunciation">If a rational and a medial area be added together, four<br />
irrational straight lines arise, namely a binomial or a first<br />
bimedial or a major or a side of a rational plus a medial<br />
area.</p>

Let <span data-ref="0156.g1:< A B >">$AB$</span> be rational, and <span data-ref="0156.g1:< C D >">$CD$</span> medial;<br />
I say that the "side" of the area <span data-ref="0156.g1:< A D >">$AD$</span> is a binomial or a first<br />
bimedial or a major or a side of a rational plus a medial<br />
area.
