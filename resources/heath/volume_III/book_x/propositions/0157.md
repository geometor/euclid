And, since <span data-ref="0156.g1:< A B >">$AB$</span> is greater than <span data-ref="0156.g1:< C D >">$CD$</span>,
while <span data-ref="0156.g1:< A B >">$AB$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0156.g1:< E G >">$EG$</span> and <span data-ref="0156.g1:< C D >">$CD$</span> to <span data-ref="0156.g1:< H I >">$HI$</span>,
therefore <span data-ref="0156.g1:< E G >">$EG$</span> is also greater than <span data-ref="0156.g1:< H I >">$HI$</span>;
therefore <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span> is also greater than <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ H K /">$HK$</span>.

The square, then, on <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span> is greater than the square on <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ H K /">$HK$</span> either by the square on a straight line commensurable in length with <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span> or by the square on a straight line incommensurable with it.

First, let the square on it be greater by the square on a straight line commensurable with itself.
Now the greater straight line <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ H E /">$HE$</span> is commensurable in length with the rational straight line <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> set out;
therefore <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ E K /">$EK$</span> is a first binomial. [x. Deff. II. 1]

But <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> is rational;
and, if an area be contained by a rational straight line and the first binomial, the side of the square equal to the area is binomial. [x. 54]
Therefore the "side" of <span data-ref="0156.g1:< E I >">$EI$</span> is binomial;
so that the "side" of <span data-ref="0156.g1:< A D >">$AD$</span> is also binomial.

Next, let the square on <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span> be greater than the square on <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ H K /">$HK$</span> by the square on a straight line incommensurable with <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span>.
Now the greater straight line <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span> is commensurable in length with the rational straight line <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> set out;
therefore <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ E K /">$EK$</span> is a fourth binomial. [x. Deff. II. 4]

But <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> is rational;
and, if an area be contained by a rational straight line and the fourth binomial, the "side" of the area is the irrational straight line called major. [x. 57]
Therefore the "side" of the area <span data-ref="0156.g1:< E I >">$EI$</span> is major;
so that the "side" of the area <span data-ref="0156.g1:< A D >">$AD$</span> is also major.

Next, let <span data-ref="0156.g1:< A B >">$AB$</span> be less than <span data-ref="0156.g1:< C D >">$CD$</span>;
therefore <span data-ref="0156.g1:< E G >">$EG$</span> is also less than <span data-ref="0156.g1:< H I >">$HI$</span>,
so that <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span> is also less than <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ H K /">$HK$</span>.

Now the square on <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ H K /">$HK$</span> is greater than the square on <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span> either by the square on a straight line commensurable with <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ H K /">$HK$</span> or by the square on a straight line incommensurable with it.
