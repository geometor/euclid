<p class="sub-clause">And, since <span data-ref="0156.g1:< A B >">$AB$</span> is greater than <span data-ref="0156.g1:< C D >">$CD$</span>,<br />
while <span data-ref="0156.g1:< A B >">$AB$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0156.g1:< E G >">$EG$</span> and <span data-ref="0156.g1:< C D >">$CD$</span> to <span data-ref="0156.g1:< H I >">$HI$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0156.g1:< E G >">$EG$</span> is also greater than <span data-ref="0156.g1:< H I >">$HI$</span> ;<br />
therefore <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span> is also greater than <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ H K /">$HK$</span>.</p>

The square, then, on <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span> is greater than the square on<br />
<span data-ref="0156.g1:/ H K /">$HK$</span> either by the square on a straight line commensurable<br />
in length with <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span> or by the square on a straight line in-<br />
commensurable with it.

First, let the square on it be greater by the square on a<br />
straight line commensurable with itself.

<p class="sub-clause">Now the greater straight line <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ H E /">$HE$</span> is commensurable in<br />
length with the rational straight line <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> set out ;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ E K /">$EK$</span> is a first binomial. <span class="justification">[x. Deff. II. 1]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> is rational ;<br />
and, if an area be contained by a rational straight line and the<br />
first binomial, the side of the square equal to the area is<br />
binomial. <span class="justification">[x. 54]</span></p>
<p class="assertion">Therefore the "side" of <span data-ref="0156.g1:< E I >">$EI$</span> is binomial ;<br />
so that the "side" of <span data-ref="0156.g1:< A D >">$AD$</span> is also binomial.</p>

Next, let the square on <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span> be greater than the square<br />
on <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ H K /">$HK$</span> by the square on a straight line incommensurable<br />
with <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span>.

<p class="sub-clause">Now the greater straight line <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span> is commensurable in<br />
length with the rational straight line <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> set out ;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ E K /">$EK$</span> is a fourth binomial. <span class="justification">[x. Deff. II. 4]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> is rational ;<br />
and, if an area be contained by a rational straight line and the<br />
fourth binomial, the "side" of the area is the irrational straight<br />
line called major. <span class="justification">[x. 57]</span></p>
<p class="assertion">Therefore the "side" of the area <span data-ref="0156.g1:< E I >">$EI$</span> is major ;<br />
so that the "side" of the area <span data-ref="0156.g1:< A D >">$AD$</span> is also major.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">Next, let <span data-ref="0156.g1:< A B >">$AB$</span> be less than <span data-ref="0156.g1:< C D >">$CD$</span> ;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0156.g1:< E G >">$EG$</span> is also less than <span data-ref="0156.g1:< H I >">$HI$</span>,<br />
so that <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span> is also less than <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ H K /">$HK$</span>.</p>

Now the square on <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ H K /">$HK$</span> is greater than the square on <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span><br />
either by the square on a straight line commensurable with<br />
<span data-ref="0156.g1:/ H K /">$HK$</span> or by the square on a straight line incommensurable<br />
with it.
