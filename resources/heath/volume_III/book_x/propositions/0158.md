First, let the square on it be greater by the square on a<br />
straight line commensurable in length with itself.

<p class="sub-clause">Now the lesser straight line <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span> is commensurable in<br />
length with the rational straight line <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> set out;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ E K /">$EK$</span> is a second binomial. <span class="justification">[X. Deff. II. 2]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> is rational,<br />
and, if an area be contained by a rational straight line and<br />
the second binomial, the side of the square equal to it is a<br />
first bimedial; <span class="justification">[X. 55]</span></p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the "side" of the area <span data-ref="0156.g1:< E I >">$EI$</span> is a first bimedial,<br />
so that the "side" of <span data-ref="0156.g1:< A D >">$AD$</span> is also a first bimedial.</p>

Next, let the square on <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ H K /">$HK$</span> be greater than the square<br />
on <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ H E /">$HE$</span> by the square on a straight line incommensurable<br />
with <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ H K /">$HK$</span>.

<p class="sub-clause">Now the lesser straight line <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span> is commensurable with<br />
the rational straight line <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> set out;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ E K /">$EK$</span> is a fifth binomial. <span class="justification">[X. Deff. II. 5]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> is rational;<br />
and, if an area be contained by a rational straight line and the<br />
fifth binomial, the side of the square equal to the area is a<br />
side of a rational plus a medial area. <span class="justification">[X. 58]</span></p>
<p class="assertion">Therefore the "side" of the area <span data-ref="0156.g1:< E I >">$EI$</span> is a side of a rational<br />
plus a medial area,<br />
so that the "side" of the area <span data-ref="0156.g1:< A D >">$AD$</span> is also a side of a rational<br />
plus a medial area.</p>

<p class="qed">Therefore etc.<span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

A rational area being of the form $k\rho^2$, and a medial area of the form<br />
$\sqrt\lambda . \rho^2$, the problem is to classify
$$\sqrt{k\rho^2 + \sqrt\lambda . \rho^2}$$
according to the different possible relations between $k, \lambda$.

Put
$$\begin{aligned}
\sigma u &= k\rho^2, \\
\sigma v &= \sqrt\lambda . \rho^2.
\end{aligned}$$

Then, since the former rectangle is rational, the latter medial,<br />
$u$ is rational and $\frown \sigma$,<br />
$v$ is rational and $\smile \sigma$.

Also the rectangles are incommensurable;<br />
so that
$$u \smile v.$$

Hence $u, v$ are rational and $\sim$;<br />
whence $(u + v)$ is a binomial straight line.
