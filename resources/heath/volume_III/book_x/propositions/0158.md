First, let the square on it be greater by the square on a straight line commensurable in length with itself.
Now the lesser straight line <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span> is commensurable in length with the rational straight line <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> set out;
therefore <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ E K /">$EK$</span> is a second binomial. [X. Deff. II. 2]

But <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> is rational,
and, if an area be contained by a rational straight line and the second binomial, the side of the square equal to it is a first bimedial; [X. 55]
therefore the "side" of the area <span data-ref="0156.g1:< E I >">$EI$</span> is a first bimedial,
so that the "side" of <span data-ref="0156.g1:< A D >">$AD$</span> is also a first bimedial.

Next, let the square on <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ H K /">$HK$</span> be greater than the square on <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ H E /">$HE$</span> by the square on a straight line incommensurable with <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ H K /">$HK$</span>.

Now the lesser straight line <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span> is commensurable with the rational straight line <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> set out;
therefore <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ E K /">$EK$</span> is a fifth binomial. [X. Deff. II. 5]

But <span data-ref="0156.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> is rational;
and, if an area be contained by a rational straight line and the fifth binomial, the side of the square equal to the area is a side of a rational plus a medial area. [X. 58]

Therefore the "side" of the area <span data-ref="0156.g1:< E I >">$EI$</span> is a side of a rational plus a medial area,
so that the "side" of the area <span data-ref="0156.g1:< A D >">$AD$</span> is also a side of a rational plus a medial area.

Therefore etc.

Q. E. D.

A rational area being of the form $k\rho^2$, and a medial area of the form $\sqrt\lambda . \rho^2$, the problem is to classify
$$\sqrt{k\rho^2 + \sqrt\lambda . \rho^2}$$
according to the different possible relations between $k, \lambda$.

Put
$$\begin{aligned}
\sigma u &= k\rho^2, \\
\sigma v &= \sqrt\lambda . \rho^2.
\end{aligned}$$

Then, since the former rectangle is rational, the latter medial,
$$u \text{ is rational and } \frown \sigma,$$
$$v \text{ is rational and } \smile \sigma.$$

Also the rectangles are incommensurable;
so that
$$u \smile v.$$

Hence $u, v$ are rational and $\sim$;
whence $(u + v)$ is a binomial straight line.
