We have to classify, according to the different possible relations between<br />
$k, \lambda$, the straight line
$$\sqrt{\sqrt k.\rho^2 + \sqrt\lambda.\rho^2},$$
where $\sqrt k.\rho^2$ and $\sqrt\lambda.\rho^2$ are incommensurable.

Suppose that
$$\begin{aligned}
\sigma u &= \sqrt k.\rho^2, \\
\sigma v &= \sqrt\lambda.\rho^2.
\end{aligned}$$

It is immaterial whether $\sqrt k.\rho^2$ or $\sqrt\lambda.\rho^2$ is the greater. Suppose, e.g.,<br />
that the former is.

Now, $\sqrt k.\rho^2, \sqrt\lambda.\rho^2$ being both *medial* areas, and $\sigma$ rational,<br />
$u, v$ are both rational and $\smile \sigma \dotfill (1).$

Again, by hypothesis,
$$\sigma u \smile \sigma v,$$
$$\text{or } u \smile v \dotfill (2).$$

Hence [(1), (2)] $(u + v)$ is a binomial straight line.

Next, $\sqrt{u^2 - v^2}$ is either commensurable or incommensurable in length<br />
with $u$.

($\alpha$) Suppose $\sqrt{u^2 - v^2} \frown u$.<br />
In this case $(u + v)$ is a *third binomial* straight line,<br />
and therefore <span class="justification">[X. 56]</span>
$$\sqrt{\sigma(u + v)} \text{ is a second bimedial straight line.}$$

($\beta$) If $\sqrt{u^2 - v^2} \smile u$,<br />
$(u + v)$ is a *sixth binomial* straight line,<br />
and therefore <span class="justification">[X. 59]</span>
$$\sqrt{\sigma(u + v)} \text{ is a side of the sum of two medial areas.}$$

<p class="enunciation">The binomial straight line and the irrational straight lines<br />
after it are neither the same with the medial nor with one<br />
another.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">For the square on a medial, if applied to a rational straight<br />
line, produces as breadth a straight line rational and incom-<br />
mensurable in length with that to which it is applied. <span class="justification">[X. 22]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But the square on the binomial, if applied to a rational<br />
straight line, produces as breadth the first binomial. <span class="justification">[X. 60]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">The square on the first bimedial, if applied to a rational<br />
straight line, produces as breadth the second binomial. <span class="justification">[X. 61]</span></p>
