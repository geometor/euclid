We have to classify, according to the different possible relations between $k, \lambda$, the straight line
$$\sqrt{\sqrt k.\rho^2 + \sqrt\lambda.\rho^2},$$
where $\sqrt k.\rho^2$ and $\sqrt\lambda.\rho^2$ are incommensurable.

Suppose that
$$\begin{aligned}
\sigma u &= \sqrt k.\rho^2, \\
\sigma v &= \sqrt\lambda.\rho^2.
\end{aligned}$$

It is immaterial whether $\sqrt k.\rho^2$ or $\sqrt\lambda.\rho^2$ is the greater. Suppose, e.g., that the former is.

Now, $\sqrt k.\rho^2, \sqrt\lambda.\rho^2$ being both *medial* areas, and $\sigma$ rational,
$$u, v \text{ are both rational and } \smile \sigma \dotfill (1).$$

Again, by hypothesis,
$$\sigma u \smile \sigma v,$$
$$\text{or } u \smile v \dotfill (2).$$

Hence [(1), (2)] $(u + v)$ is a binomial straight line.

Next, $\sqrt{u^2 - v^2}$ is either commensurable or incommensurable in length with $u$.

($\alpha$) Suppose $\sqrt{u^2 - v^2} \frown u$.

In this case $(u + v)$ is a *third binomial* straight line,
and therefore [X. 56]
$$\sqrt{\sigma(u + v)} \text{ is a \emph{second bimedial} straight line.}$$

($\beta$) If $\sqrt{u^2 - v^2} \smile u$,
$(u + v)$ is a *sixth binomial* straight line,
and therefore [X. 59]
$$\sqrt{\sigma(u + v)} \text{ is a \emph{side of the sum of two medial areas.}}$$

---

The binomial straight line and the irrational straight lines after it are neither the same with the medial nor with one another.

For the square on a medial, if applied to a rational straight line, produces as breadth a straight line rational and incommensurable in length with that to which it is applied. [X. 22]

But the square on the binomial, if applied to a rational straight line, produces as breadth the first binomial. [X. 60]

The square on the first bimedial, if applied to a rational straight line, produces as breadth the second binomial. [X. 61]
