:title: Proposition 72 (conclusion) / Classification of Irrationals

.. subclause::

   | the sixth binomial, the "side" of the area is the side of the
   | sum of two medial areas; :just:`[X. 59] <X.59>`

.. assertion::

   | so that the "side" of the area :g:`$AD$ <0159.g1:< A D >>` is also the side of the
   | sum of two medial areas.

.. qed::

   Therefore etc. :just:`Q. E. D.`

----

| We have to classify, according to the different possible relations between
| $k, \lambda$, the straight line

$$\sqrt{\sqrt k.\rho^2 + \sqrt\lambda.\rho^2},$$

where $\sqrt k.\rho^2$ and $\sqrt\lambda.\rho^2$ are incommensurable.

Suppose that

$$\begin{aligned}
\sigma u &= \sqrt k.\rho^2, \\
\sigma v &= \sqrt\lambda.\rho^2.
\end{aligned}$$

| It is immaterial whether $\sqrt k.\rho^2$ or $\sqrt\lambda.\rho^2$ is the greater. Suppose, e.g.,
| that the former is.

| Now, $\sqrt k.\rho^2, \sqrt\lambda.\rho^2$ being both *medial* areas, and $\sigma$ rational,
| $u, v$ are both rational and $\smile \sigma \dotfill (1).$

Again, by hypothesis,

$$\sigma u \smile \sigma v,$$

$$\text{or } u \smile v \dotfill (2).$$

Hence [(1), (2)] $(u + v)$ is a binomial straight line.

| Next, $\sqrt{u^2 - v^2}$ is either commensurable or incommensurable in length
| with $u$.

| ($\alpha$) Suppose $\sqrt{u^2 - v^2} \frown u$.
| In this case $(u + v)$ is a :term:`*third binomial* <third-binomial>` straight line,
| and therefore :just:`[X. 56] <X.56>`

$$\sqrt{\sigma(u + v)} \text{ is a second bimedial straight line.}$$

| ($\beta$) If $\sqrt{u^2 - v^2} \smile u$,
| $(u + v)$ is a :term:`*sixth binomial* <sixth-binomial>` straight line,
| and therefore :just:`[X. 59] <X.59>`

$$\sqrt{\sigma(u + v)} \text{ is a side of the sum of two medial areas.}$$

----

.. enunciation::

   The binomial straight line and the irrational straight lines after it are neither the same with the medial nor with one another.

.. subclause::

   | For the square on a medial, if applied to a rational straight
   | line, produces as breadth a straight line rational and incom-
   | mensurable in length with that to which it is applied. :just:`[X. 22] <X.22>`

.. subclause::

   | But the square on the binomial, if applied to a rational
   | straight line, produces as breadth the first binomial. :just:`[X. 60] <X.60>`

.. subclause::

   | The square on the first bimedial, if applied to a rational
   | straight line, produces as breadth the second binomial. :just:`[X. 61] <X.61>`
