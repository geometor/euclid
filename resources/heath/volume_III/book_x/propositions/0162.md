The square on the second bimedial, if applied to a rational straight line, produces as breadth the third binomial. [X. 62]

The square on the major, if applied to a rational straight line, produces as breadth the fourth binomial. [X. 63]

The square on the side of a rational plus a medial area, if applied to a rational straight line, produces as breadth the fifth binomial. [X. 64]

The square on the side of the sum of two medial areas, if applied to a rational straight line, produces as breadth the sixth binomial. [X. 65]

And the said breadths differ both from the first and from one another: from the first because it is rational, and from one another because they are not the same in order;
so that the irrational straight lines themselves also differ from one another.

---

The explanation after X. 72 is for the purpose of showing that all the irrational straight lines treated hitherto are different from one another, viz. the medial, the six irrational straight lines beginning with the binomial, and the six consisting of the first, second, third, fourth, fifth and sixth binomials.

### PROPOSITION 73.

*If from a rational straight line there be subtracted a rational straight line commensurable with the whole in square only, the remainder is irrational; and let it be called an* **apotome.**

For from the rational straight line <span data-ref="0162.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> let the rational straight line <span data-ref="0162.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, commensurable with the whole in square only, be subtracted;

![Proposition 73](0162.g1.svg)

I say that the remainder <span data-ref="0162.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is the irrational straight line called **apotome**.

For, since <span data-ref="0162.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is incommensurable in length with <span data-ref="0162.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>,
and, as <span data-ref="0162.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0162.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, so is the square on <span data-ref="0162.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> to the rectangle <span data-ref="0162.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0162.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>,
therefore the square on <span data-ref="0162.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is incommensurable with the rectangle <span data-ref="0162.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0162.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>. [X. 11]

But the squares on <span data-ref="0162.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0162.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> are commensurable with the square on <span data-ref="0162.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, [X. 15]
and twice the rectangle <span data-ref="0162.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0162.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is commensurable with the rectangle <span data-ref="0162.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0162.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>. [X. 6]
