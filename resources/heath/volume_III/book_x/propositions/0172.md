This proposition proves the equivalent of the well-known theorem of surds<br />
that,<br />
if $a - \sqrt b = x - \sqrt y$, then $a = x, b = y$;<br />
and, if $\sqrt a - \sqrt b = \sqrt x - \sqrt y$, then $a = x, b = y$.

The method of proof corresponds to that of x. 42 for positive signs.

Suppose, if possible, that an *apotome* can be expressed as $(x - y)$ and also<br />
as $(x' - y')$, where $x, y$ are rational straight lines commensurable in square only,<br />
and $x', y'$ are so also.

Of $x, x'$, let $x$ be the greater.

Now, since
$$x - y = x' - y',$$
$$x^2 + y^2 - (x'^2 + y'^2) = 2xy - 2x'y'.$$

But $(x^2 + y^2), (x'^2 + y'^2)$ are both rational, so that their difference is a<br />
rational area.

On the other hand, $2xy, 2x'y'$ are both *medial* areas, being of the form<br />
$\sqrt k \cdot \rho^2$;<br />
therefore the difference between two medial areas is rational:<br />
which is impossible [x. 26].

Therefore etc.

### PROPOSITION 80.

<p class="enunciation">To a first apotome of a medial straight line only one<br />
medial straight line can be annexed which is commensurable<br />
with the whole in square only and which contains with the<br />
whole a rational rectangle.</p>
