As the irrationality of the *second apotome of a medial* straight line was<br />
deduced [x. 75] from the irrationality of an apotome, so the present theorem<br />
is reduced to x. 79.

Suppose, if possible, that $(x - y)$, $(x' - y')$ are the *same* second apotome of<br />
a medial straight line;<br />
and let (say) $x$ be greater than $x'$.

Apply $(x^2 + y^2)$, $2xy$ and also $(x'^2 + y'^2)$, $2x'y'$ to a rational straight line $\sigma$,<br />
i.e. put
$$\left. \begin{aligned} x^2 + y^2 &= \sigma u \\ 2xy &= \sigma v \end{aligned} \right\} \quad\text{and}\quad \left. \begin{aligned} x'^2 + y'^2 &= \sigma u' \\ 2x'y' &= \sigma v' \end{aligned} \right\}.$$

Dealing with $(x - y)$ first, we have:<br />
$(x^2 + y^2)$ is a medial area, and $2xy$ is also a medial area.<br />
Therefore $u$, $v$ are both rational and $\smile \sigma$ ............................................(1).

$$\begin{aligned}
&\text{Also, since } x \underset{-}{\sim} y, & x &\smile y, \\
&\text{so that } & x^2 &\smile xy, \\
&\text{whence, as usual, } & x^2 + y^2 &\smile 2xy, \\
&\text{that is, } & \sigma u &\smile \sigma v, \\
&\text{and therefore } & u &\smile v \text{ .....................................................(2).}
\end{aligned}$$

Thus [(1) and (2)] $u$, $v$ are rational and $\underset{-}{\sim}$,<br />
so that $(u - v)$ is an *apotome*.<br />
Similarly $(u' - v')$ is proved to be the same apotome.<br />
Hence this apotome is formed in two ways:<br />
which contradicts x. 79.<br />
Therefore the original hypothesis is false, and *a second apotome of a*<br />
*medial straight line is uniquely formed*.

### PROPOSITION 82.

<p class="enunciation">To a minor straight line only one straight line can be<br />
annexed which is incommensurable in square with the whole<br />
and which makes, with the whole, the sum of the squares on<br />
them rational but twice the rectangle contained by them medial.</p>
