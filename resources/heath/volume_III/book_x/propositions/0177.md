Suppose, if possible, that, with the usual notation,
$$x - y = x' - y' ;$$
and let $x$ (say) be greater than $x'$.

In this case $(x^2 + y^2)$, $(x'^2 + y'^2)$ are both *rational* areas,<br />
and $2xy$, $2x'y'$ are both *medial* areas.

But, as before,
$$(x^2 + y^2) - (x'^2 + y'^2) = 2xy - 2x'y',$$
so that the difference between two medial areas is rational :<br />
which is impossible [x. 26].

Therefore etc.

### PROPOSITION 83.

<p class="enunciation">To a straight line which produces with a rational area a<br />
medial whole only one straight line can be annexed which is<br />
incommensurable in square with the whole straight line and<br />
which with the whole straight line makes the sum of the squares<br />
on them medial, but twice the rectangle contained by them<br />
rational.</p>
