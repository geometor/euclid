*on them medial and twice the rectangle contained by them both
medial and also incommensurable with the sum of the squares
on them.*

Let <span data-ref="0179.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> be the straight line which produces with a medial
area a medial whole,
and <span data-ref="0179.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> an annex to it;
therefore <span data-ref="0179.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0179.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span> are straight lines incommensurable in square
which fulfil the aforesaid conditions. \[[x. 78](x.78.md)\]

![Proposition 84](0179.g1.svg)

I say that no other straight line can be annexed to <span data-ref="0179.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>
which fulfils the aforesaid conditions.

For, if possible, let <span data-ref="0179.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> be so annexed,
so that <span data-ref="0179.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0179.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> are also straight lines incommensurable in
square which make the squares on <span data-ref="0179.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0179.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> added together
medial, twice the rectangle <span data-ref="0179.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0179.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> medial, and also the
squares on <span data-ref="0179.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0179.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> incommensurable with twice the rectangle
<span data-ref="0179.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0179.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span>. \[[x. 78](x.78.md)\]

Let a rational straight line <span data-ref="0179.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> be set out,
let <span data-ref="0179.g1:< E G >">$EG$</span> equal to the squares on <span data-ref="0179.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0179.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span> be applied to <span data-ref="0179.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>,
producing <span data-ref="0179.g1:/ E M /">$EM$</span> as breadth,
and let <span data-ref="0179.g1:< H G >">$HG$</span> equal to twice the rectangle <span data-ref="0179.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0179.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span> be applied
to <span data-ref="0179.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, producing <span data-ref="0179.g1:/ H M /">$HM$</span> as breadth;
therefore the remainder, the square on <span data-ref="0179.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> \[[II. 7](II.7.md)\], is equal
to <span data-ref="0179.g1:< E L >">$EL$</span>;
therefore <span data-ref="0179.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is the "side" of <span data-ref="0179.g1:< E L >">$EL$</span>.

Again, let <span data-ref="0179.g1:< E I >">$EI$</span> equal to the squares on <span data-ref="0179.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0179.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> be applied
to <span data-ref="0179.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, producing <span data-ref="0179.g1:/ E N /">$EN$</span> as breadth.
But the square on <span data-ref="0179.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is also equal to <span data-ref="0179.g1:< E L >">$EL$</span>;
therefore the remainder, twice the rectangle <span data-ref="0179.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0179.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> \[[II. 7](II.7.md)\],
is equal to <span data-ref="0179.g1:< H I >">$HI$</span>.
