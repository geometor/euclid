With the usual notation, suppose that
$$x - y = x' - y'. \qquad (x > x')$$

Let
$$\left. \begin{array}{r} x^2 + y^2 = \sigma u \\ 2xy = \sigma v \end{array} \right\} \quad \text{and} \quad \left. \begin{array}{r} x'^2 + y'^2 = \sigma u' \\ 2x'y' = \sigma v' \end{array} \right\}.$$

Consider $(x - y)$ first ;<br />
it follows, since $(x^2 + y^2)$, $2xy$ are both medial areas, that<br />
$u, v$ are both rational and $\smile \sigma$ .................................................(1).

But
$$x^2 + y^2 \smile 2xy,$$
that is,
$$\sigma u \smile \sigma v,$$
and therefore
$$u \smile v \text{ .................................................(2).}$$

Therefore [(1) and (2)] $u, v$ are rational and $\sim$ ;<br />
hence $(u - v)$ is an apotome.

Similarly $(u' - v')$ is proved to be the same apotome.

Thus the same apotome is formed as such in two ways :<br />
which is impossible <span class="justification">[X. 79]</span>.

Therefore, etc.

### DEFINITIONS III.

**1.** Given a rational straight line and an apotome, if the<br />
square on the whole be greater than the square on the annex<br />
by the square on a straight line commensurable in length with<br />
the whole, and the whole be commensurable in length with<br />
the rational straight line set out, let the apotome be called a<br />
**first apotome**.

**2.** But if the annex be commensurable in length with<br />
the rational straight line set out, and the square on the whole<br />
be greater than that on the annex by the square on a straight<br />
line commensurable with the whole, let the apotome be called<br />
a **second apotome**.

**3.** But if neither be commensurable in length with the<br />
rational straight line set out, and the square on the whole be<br />
greater than the square on the annex by the square on a<br />
straight line commensurable with the whole, let the apotome<br />
be called a **third apotome**.

**4.** Again, if the square on the whole be greater than<br />
the square on the annex by the square on a straight line<br />
incommensurable with the whole, then, if the whole be com-<br />
mensurable in length with the rational straight line set out,<br />
let the apotome be called a **fourth apotome** ;

**5.** if the annex be so commensurable, a **fifth** ;

**6.** and, if neither, a **sixth**.
