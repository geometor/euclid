But $E$ has not to $CD$ the ratio which a square number has to a square number;
therefore neither has the square on $A$ to the square on $GH$ the ratio which a square number has to a square number;
therefore $A$ is incommensurable in length with $GH$. \[[x. 9](x.9.md)\]

Therefore neither of the straight lines $FG, GH$ is commensurable in length with the rational straight line $A$ set out.

Now let the square on $K$ be that by which the square on $FG$ is greater than the square on $GH$.

Since then, as $BC$ is to $CD$, so is the square on $FG$ to the square on $GH$,
therefore, *convertendo*, as $BC$ is to $BD$, so is the square on $FG$ to the square on $K$. \[[v. 19, Por.](v.19.md)\]

But $BC$ has to $BD$ the ratio which a square number has to a square number;
therefore the square on $FG$ also has to the square on $K$ the ratio which a square number has to a square number.

Therefore $FG$ is commensurable in length with $K$, \[[x. 9](x.9.md)\]
and the square on $FG$ is greater than the square on $GH$ by the square on a straight line commensurable with $FG$.

And neither of the straight lines $FG, GH$ is commensurable in length with the rational straight line $A$ set out;
therefore $FH$ is a third apotome. \[[x. Deff. III. 3](deff.x.3.md)\]

Therefore the third apotome $FH$ has been found.

$$\text{Q. E. D.}$$

Let $\rho$ be a rational straight line.
Take numbers $p, qm^2, q(m^2 - n^2)$ which have not to one another the ratio of square to square.

Now let $x, y$ be such that
$$p : qm^2 = \rho^2 : x^2 \tag{1}$$
and
$$qm^2 : q(m^2 - n^2) = x^2 : y^2 \tag{2}.$$

Then shall $(x - y)$ be a *third apotome*.

For ($\alpha$), from (1),
$x$ is rational but $\smile \rho$ .................................................(3).

And, from (2), $y$ is rational but $\smile x$.
Therefore $x, y$ are rational and $\sim$,
so that $(x - y)$ is an apotome.
