.. subclause::

   | But :g:`$E$ <0185.g1:E>` has not to :g:`$CD$ <0185.g1:/ C D />` the ratio which a square number
   | has to a square number ;

   | the ratio which a square number has to a square number ;
   | therefore :g:`$A$ <0185.g1:A>` is incommensurable in length with :g:`$GH$ <0185.g1:/ G H />`. :just:`[X. 9] <X.9>`

   | Therefore neither of the straight lines :g:`$FG$ <0185.g1:/ F G />`, :g:`$GH$ <0185.g1:/ G H />` is

   | Since then, as :g:`$BC$ <0185.g1:/ B C />` is to :g:`$CD$ <0185.g1:/ C D />`, so is the square on :g:`$FG$ <0185.g1:/ F G />` to

   | :g:`$FG$ <0185.g1:/ F G />` to the square on :g:`$K$ <0185.g1:K>`. :just:`[V. 19, Por.]`

   Therefore the third apotome :g:`$FH$ <0185.g1:/ F H />` has been found. :just:`Q. E. D.`

----

| Let $\rho$ be a rational straight line.
| Take numbers $p$, $qm^2$, $q(m^2 - n^2)$ which have not to one another the ratio
| of square to square.

Now let $x$, $y$ be such that

$$p : qm^2 = \rho^2 : x^2 \text{ .................................................(1)}$$

and

$$qm^2 : q(m^2 - n^2) = x^2 : y^2 \text{ ...................................(2).}$$

Then shall $(x - y)$ be a :term:`*third apotome* <third-apotome>`.

| For ($\alpha$), from (1),
| $x$ is rational but $\smile \rho$ ................................................................(3).

| And, from (2), $y$ is rational but $\smile x$.
| Therefore $x, y$ are rational and $\sim$,
| so that $(x - y)$ is an apotome.
