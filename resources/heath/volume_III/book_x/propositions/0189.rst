.. subclause::

   | Now, since :g:`$DE$ <0188.g1:/ D E />` has not to :g:`$EF$ <0188.g1:/ E F />` the ratio which a square
   | number has to a square number,

.. assertion::

   | therefore neither has the square on :g:`$BG$ <0188.g1:/ B G />` to the square on :g:`$GC$ <0188.g1:/ G C />`
   | the ratio which a square number has to a square number;
   | therefore :g:`$BG$ <0188.g1:/ B G />` is incommensurable in length with :g:`$GC$ <0188.g1:/ G C />`. :just:`[X. 9] <X.9>`

.. subclause::

   And both are rational;

.. assertion::

   | therefore :g:`$BG$ <0188.g1:/ B G />`, :g:`$GC$ <0188.g1:/ G C />` are rational straight lines commensurable
   | in square only;
   | therefore :g:`$BC$ <0188.g1:/ B C />` is an apotome. :just:`[X. 73] <X.73>`

| Now let the square on :g:`$H$ <0188.g1:H>` be that by which the square on
| :g:`$BG$ <0188.g1:/ B G />` is greater than the square on :g:`$GC$ <0188.g1:/ G C />`.

.. subclause::

   | Since then, as :g:`$DE$ <0188.g1:/ D E />` is to :g:`$EF$ <0188.g1:/ E F />`, so is the square on :g:`$BG$ <0188.g1:/ B G />` to
   | the square on :g:`$GC$ <0188.g1:/ G C />`,

.. assertion::

   | therefore also, *convertendo*, as :g:`$ED$ <0188.g1:/ E D />` is to :g:`$DF$ <0188.g1:/ D F />`, so is the square
   | on :g:`$GB$ <0188.g1:/ G B />` to the square on :g:`$H$ <0188.g1:H>`. :just:`[V. 19, Por.]`

.. subclause::

   | But :g:`$ED$ <0188.g1:/ E D />` has not to :g:`$DF$ <0188.g1:/ D F />` the ratio which a square number
   | has to a square number;

.. assertion::

   | therefore neither has the square on :g:`$GB$ <0188.g1:/ G B />` to the square on :g:`$H$ <0188.g1:H>`
   | the ratio which a square number has to a square number;
   | therefore :g:`$BG$ <0188.g1:/ B G />` is incommensurable in length with :g:`$H$ <0188.g1:H>`. :just:`[X. 9] <X.9>`

.. subclause::

   | And the square on :g:`$BG$ <0188.g1:/ B G />` is greater than the square on :g:`$GC$ <0188.g1:/ G C />`
   | by the square on :g:`$H$ <0188.g1:H>`;

.. assertion::

   | therefore the square on :g:`$BG$ <0188.g1:/ B G />` is greater than the square on :g:`$GC$ <0188.g1:/ G C />`
   | by the square on a straight line incommensurable with :g:`$BG$ <0188.g1:/ B G />`.

.. subclause::

   | And the whole :g:`$BG$ <0188.g1:/ B G />` is commensurable in length with the
   | rational straight line :g:`$A$ <0188.g1:A>` set out.

.. assertion:: [X. Deff. III. 4]

   Therefore :g:`$BC$ <0188.g1:/ B C />` is a fourth apotome.

.. qed::

   Therefore the fourth apotome has been found. :just:`Q. E. D.`

----

| Beginning with $\rho$, $k\rho$, as in x. 85, 86, we take numbers $m$, $n$ such that
| $(m+n)$ has not to either of the numbers $m$, $n$ the ratio of a square number to
| a square number.

Take $x$ such that

$$(m+n) : n = k^2\rho^2 : x^2 \text{ ..............................(1),}$$

whence

$$x = k\rho \sqrt{\frac{n}{m+n}}$$

$$= \frac{k\rho}{\sqrt{1+\lambda}}\text{, say.}$$

Then shall $(k\rho - x)$, or $\left(k\rho - \frac{k\rho}{\sqrt{1+\lambda}}\right)$, be a *fourth apotome*.
