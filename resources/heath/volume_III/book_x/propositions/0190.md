For, by (1), $x$ is rational and $\sim k\rho$.
Also $\sqrt{k^2\rho^2 - x^2}$ is incommensurable with $k\rho$, since
$$(m+n) : m = k^2\rho^2 : (k^2\rho^2 - x^2)\text{,}$$
and the ratio $(m+n) : m$ is not that of a square number to a square number.
And $k\rho \sim \rho$.
As explained in the note on x. 51, the *fourth apotome*
$$k\rho - \frac{k\rho}{\sqrt{1+\lambda}}$$
is the lesser root of the quadratic equation
$$x^2 - 2k\rho \cdot x + \frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda} k^2\rho^2 = 0.$$

### PROPOSITION 89.

*To find the fifth apotome.*

![Proposition 89 Diagram](0190.g1.svg)

Let a rational straight line <span data-ref="0190.g1:A">$A$</span> be set out,
and let <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ C G /">$CG$</span> be commensurable in length
with <span data-ref="0190.g1:A">$A$</span>;
therefore <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ C G /">$CG$</span> is rational.
Let two numbers <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span>, <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ F E /">$FE$</span> be set out
such that <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> again has not to either of the
numbers <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span>, <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ F E /">$FE$</span> the ratio which a square
number has to a square number;
and let it be contrived that, as <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ F E /">$FE$</span> is to <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ E D /">$ED$</span>,
so is the square on <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ C G /">$CG$</span> to the square on <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ G B /">$GB$</span>.
Therefore the square on <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ G B /">$GB$</span> is also
rational; [x. 6]
therefore <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span> is also rational.

Now since, as <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> is to <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, so is the square on <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span> to
the square on <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ G C /">$GC$</span>,
while <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> has not to <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> the ratio which a square number
has to a square number,
therefore neither has the square on <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span> to the square on <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ G C /">$GC$</span>
the ratio which a square number has to a square number;
therefore <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span> is incommensurable in length with <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ G C /">$GC$</span>. [x. 9]
And both are rational;
therefore <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span>, <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ G C /">$GC$</span> are rational straight lines commensurable
in square only;
therefore <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is an apotome. [x. 73]
