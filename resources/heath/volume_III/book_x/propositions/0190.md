For, by (1), $x$ is rational and $\sim k\rho$.
Also $\sqrt{k^2\rho^2 - x^2}$ is incommensurable with $k\rho$, since
$$(m+n) : m = k^2\rho^2 : (k^2\rho^2 - x^2)\text{,}$$
and the ratio $(m+n) : m$ is not that of a square number to a square number.
And $k\rho \sim \rho$.
As explained in the note on x. 51, the *fourth apotome*
$$k\rho - \frac{k\rho}{\sqrt{1+\lambda}}$$
is the lesser root of the quadratic equation
$$x^2 - 2k\rho \cdot x + \frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda} k^2\rho^2 = 0.$$

### PROPOSITION 89.

<p class="enunciation">To find the fifth apotome.</p>

![Proposition 89 Diagram](0190.g1.svg)

Let a rational straight line <span data-ref="0190.g1:A">$A$</span> be set out,<br />
and let <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ C G /">$CG$</span> be commensurable in length<br />
with <span data-ref="0190.g1:A">$A$</span>;
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ C G /">$CG$</span> is rational.</p>

Let two numbers <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span>, <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ F E /">$FE$</span> be set out<br />
such that <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> again has not to either of the<br />
numbers <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span>, <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ F E /">$FE$</span> the ratio which a square<br />
number has to a square number;<br />
and let it be contrived that, as <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ F E /">$FE$</span> is to <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ E D /">$ED$</span>,<br />
so is the square on <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ C G /">$CG$</span> to the square on <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ G B /">$GB$</span>.
<p class="assertion">Therefore the square on <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ G B /">$GB$</span> is also<br />
rational; <span class="justification">[X. 6]</span><br />
therefore <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span> is also rational.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">Now since, as <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> is to <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, so is the square on <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span> to<br />
the square on <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ G C /">$GC$</span>,<br />
while <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> has not to <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> the ratio which a square number<br />
has to a square number,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore neither has the square on <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span> to the square on <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ G C /">$GC$</span><br />
the ratio which a square number has to a square number;<br />
therefore <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span> is incommensurable in length with <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ G C /">$GC$</span>. <span class="justification">[X. 9]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">And both are rational ;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span>, <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ G C /">$GC$</span> are rational straight lines commensurable<br />
in square only;<br />
therefore <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is an apotome. <span class="justification">[X. 73]</span></p>
