I say next that it is also a fifth apotome.
For let the square on <span data-ref="0190.g1:H">$H$</span> be that by which the square on
<span data-ref="0190.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span> is greater than the square on <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ G C /">$GC$</span>.
Since then, as the square on <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span> is to the square on <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ G C /">$GC$</span>,
so is <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> to <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>,
therefore, *convertendo*, as <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ E D /">$ED$</span> is to <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span>, so is the square on
<span data-ref="0190.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span> to the square on <span data-ref="0190.g1:H">$H$</span>. [v. 19, Por.]
But <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ E D /">$ED$</span> has not to <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span> the ratio which a square number
has to a square number;
therefore neither has the square on <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span> to the square on <span data-ref="0190.g1:H">$H$</span>
the ratio which a square number has to a square number;
therefore <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span> is incommensurable in length with <span data-ref="0190.g1:H">$H$</span>. [x. 9]
And the square on <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span> is greater than the square on <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ G C /">$GC$</span>
by the square on <span data-ref="0190.g1:H">$H$</span>;
therefore the square on <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ G B /">$GB$</span> is greater than the square on <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ G C /">$GC$</span>
by the square on a straight line incommensurable in length
with <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ G B /">$GB$</span>.
And the annex <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ C G /">$CG$</span> is commensurable in length with the
rational straight line <span data-ref="0190.g1:A">$A$</span> set out;
therefore <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is a fifth apotome. [x. Deff. III. 5]
Therefore the fifth apotome <span data-ref="0190.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> has been found.

Q. E. D.

Let $\rho$, $k\rho$ and the numbers $m$, $n$ of the last proposition be taken.
Take $x$ such that
$$n : (m+n) = k^2\rho^2 : x^2 \text{ ..............................(1).}$$
In this case $x > k\rho$, and
$$x = k\rho \sqrt{\frac{m+n}{n}}$$
$$= k\rho \sqrt{1+\lambda}\text{, say.}$$
Then shall $(x - k\rho)$, or $(k\rho\sqrt{1+\lambda} - k\rho)$, be a *fifth apotome*.
For, by (1), $x$ is rational and $\sim k\rho$.
And since, by (1), $(m+n) : m = x^2 : (x^2 - k^2\rho^2)$,
$\sqrt{x^2 - k^2\rho^2}$ is incommensurable with $x$.
Also $k\rho \sim \rho$.
As explained in the note on x. 52, the *fifth apotome*
$$k\rho\sqrt{1+\lambda} - k\rho$$
is the lesser root of the quadratic
$$x^2 - 2k\rho\sqrt{1+\lambda}\cdot x + \lambda k^2\rho^2 = 0.$$
