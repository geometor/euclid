I say next that <span data-ref="0195.g1:/ L N /">$LN$</span> is an apotome.

For, since each of the rectangles <span data-ref="0195.g1:< A I >">$AI$</span>, <span data-ref="0195.g1:< F K >">$FK$</span> is rational,
and they are equal to <span data-ref="0195.g1:< L M >">$LM$</span>, <span data-ref="0195.g1:< N O >">$NO$</span>,
therefore each of the squares <span data-ref="0195.g1:< L M >">$LM$</span>, <span data-ref="0195.g1:< N O >">$NO$</span>, that is, the squares on
<span data-ref="0195.g1:/ L P /">$LP$</span>, <span data-ref="0195.g1:/ P N /">$PN$</span> respectively, is also rational;
therefore each of the straight lines <span data-ref="0195.g1:/ L P /">$LP$</span>, <span data-ref="0195.g1:/ P N /">$PN$</span> is also rational.

Again, since <span data-ref="0195.g1:< D H >">$DH$</span> is medial and is equal to <span data-ref="0195.g1:< L O >">$LO$</span>,
therefore <span data-ref="0195.g1:< L O >">$LO$</span> is also medial.

Since then <span data-ref="0195.g1:< L O >">$LO$</span> is medial,
while <span data-ref="0195.g1:< N O >">$NO$</span> is rational,
therefore <span data-ref="0195.g1:< L O >">$LO$</span> is incommensurable with <span data-ref="0195.g1:< N O >">$NO$</span>.

But, as <span data-ref="0195.g1:< L O >">$LO$</span> is to <span data-ref="0195.g1:< N O >">$NO$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0195.g1:/ L P /">$LP$</span> to <span data-ref="0195.g1:/ P N /">$PN$</span>; [VI. 1]
therefore <span data-ref="0195.g1:/ L P /">$LP$</span> is incommensurable in length with <span data-ref="0195.g1:/ P N /">$PN$</span>. [x. 11]

And both are rational;
therefore <span data-ref="0195.g1:/ L P /">$LP$</span>, <span data-ref="0195.g1:/ P N /">$PN$</span> are rational straight lines commensurable
in square only;
therefore <span data-ref="0195.g1:/ L N /">$LN$</span> is an apotome. [x. 73]

And it is the "side" of the area <span data-ref="0195.g1:< A B >">$AB$</span>;
therefore the "side" of the area <span data-ref="0195.g1:< A B >">$AB$</span> is an apotome.

Therefore etc.

This proposition corresponds to x. 54, and the problem solved in it is to
find and to classify the side of a square equal to the rectangle contained by a
*first apotome* and $\rho$, or (algebraically) to find
$$\sqrt{\rho(k\rho - k\rho\sqrt{1-\lambda^2})}.$$

First find $u, v$ from the equations
$$\left.\begin{aligned}
u + v &= k\rho \\
uv &= \frac{1}{4}k^2\rho^2(1 - \lambda^2)
\end{aligned}\right\} \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(1).$$

If $u, v$ represent the values so found, put
$$\left.\begin{aligned}
x^2 &= \rho u \\
y^2 &= \rho v
\end{aligned}\right\} \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(2),$$
and $(x - y)$ shall be the square root required.

To prove this Euclid argues thus.

By (1),
$$u : \frac{1}{2}k\rho\sqrt{1-\lambda^2} = \frac{1}{2}k\rho\sqrt{1-\lambda^2} : v,$$
whence
$$\rho u : \frac{1}{2}k\rho^2\sqrt{1-\lambda^2} = \frac{1}{2}k\rho^2\sqrt{1-\lambda^2} : \rho v,$$
or
$$x^2 : \frac{1}{2}k\rho^2\sqrt{1-\lambda^2} = \frac{1}{2}k\rho^2\sqrt{1-\lambda^2} : y^2.$$

But [Lemma after x. 53]
$$x^2 : xy = xy : y^2,$$
so that
$$xy = \frac{1}{2}k\rho^2\sqrt{1-\lambda^2} \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(3).$$
