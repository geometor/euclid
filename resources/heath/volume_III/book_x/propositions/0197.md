I say next that <span data-ref="0195.g1:/ L N /">$LN$</span> is an apotome.

<p class="sub-clause">For, since each of the rectangles <span data-ref="0195.g1:< A I >">$AI$</span>, <span data-ref="0195.g1:< F K >">$FK$</span> is rational,<br />
and they are equal to <span data-ref="0195.g1:< L M >">$LM$</span>, <span data-ref="0195.g1:< N O >">$NO$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore each of the squares <span data-ref="0195.g1:< L M >">$LM$</span>, <span data-ref="0195.g1:< N O >">$NO$</span>, that is, the squares on<br />
<span data-ref="0195.g1:/ L P /">$LP$</span>, <span data-ref="0195.g1:/ P N /">$PN$</span> respectively, is also rational;<br />
therefore each of the straight lines <span data-ref="0195.g1:/ L P /">$LP$</span>, <span data-ref="0195.g1:/ P N /">$PN$</span> is also rational.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">Again, since <span data-ref="0195.g1:< D H >">$DH$</span> is medial and is equal to <span data-ref="0195.g1:< L O >">$LO$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0195.g1:< L O >">$LO$</span> is also medial.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">Since then <span data-ref="0195.g1:< L O >">$LO$</span> is medial,<br />
while <span data-ref="0195.g1:< N O >">$NO$</span> is rational,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0195.g1:< L O >">$LO$</span> is incommensurable with <span data-ref="0195.g1:< N O >">$NO$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">But, as <span data-ref="0195.g1:< L O >">$LO$</span> is to <span data-ref="0195.g1:< N O >">$NO$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0195.g1:/ L P /">$LP$</span> to <span data-ref="0195.g1:/ P N /">$PN$</span>; <span class="justification">[VI. 1]</span></p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0195.g1:/ L P /">$LP$</span> is incommensurable in length with <span data-ref="0195.g1:/ P N /">$PN$</span>. <span class="justification">[x. 11]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">And both are rational;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0195.g1:/ L P /">$LP$</span>, <span data-ref="0195.g1:/ P N /">$PN$</span> are rational straight lines commensurable<br />
in square only;<br />
therefore <span data-ref="0195.g1:/ L N /">$LN$</span> is an apotome. <span class="justification">[x. 73]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">And it is the "side" of the area <span data-ref="0195.g1:< A B >">$AB$</span>;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the "side" of the area <span data-ref="0195.g1:< A B >">$AB$</span> is an apotome.</p>

<p class="qed">Therefore etc.</p>

This proposition corresponds to x. 54, and the problem solved in it is to<br />
find and to classify the side of a square equal to the rectangle contained by a<br />
*first apotome* and $\rho$, or (algebraically) to find
$$\sqrt{\rho(k\rho - k\rho\sqrt{1-\lambda^2})}.$$

First find $u, v$ from the equations
$$\left.\begin{aligned}
u + v &= k\rho \\
uv &= \frac{1}{4}k^2\rho^2(1 - \lambda^2)
\end{aligned}\right\} \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(1).$$

If $u, v$ represent the values so found, put
$$\left.\begin{aligned}
x^2 &= \rho u \\
y^2 &= \rho v
\end{aligned}\right\} \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(2),$$
and $(x - y)$ shall be the square root required.

To prove this Euclid argues thus.

By (1),
$$u : \frac{1}{2}k\rho\sqrt{1-\lambda^2} = \frac{1}{2}k\rho\sqrt{1-\lambda^2} : v,$$
whence
$$\rho u : \frac{1}{2}k\rho^2\sqrt{1-\lambda^2} = \frac{1}{2}k\rho^2\sqrt{1-\lambda^2} : \rho v,$$
or
$$x^2 : \frac{1}{2}k\rho^2\sqrt{1-\lambda^2} = \frac{1}{2}k\rho^2\sqrt{1-\lambda^2} : y^2.$$

But [Lemma after x. 53]
$$x^2 : xy = xy : y^2,$$
so that
$$xy = \frac{1}{2}k\rho^2\sqrt{1-\lambda^2} \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(3).$$
