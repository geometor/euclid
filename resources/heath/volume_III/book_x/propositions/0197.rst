:title: Proposition 91 (Conclusion) and Commentary

I say next that :g:`$LN$ <0195.g1:/ L N />` is an apotome.

.. subclause::

   For, since each of the rectangles :g:`$AI$ <0195.g1:< A I >>`, :g:`$FK$ <0195.g1:< F K >>` is rational, and they are equal to :g:`$LM$ <0195.g1:< L M >>`, :g:`$NO$ <0195.g1:< N O >>`,

.. assertion::

   therefore each of the squares :g:`$LM$ <0195.g1:< L M >>`, :g:`$NO$ <0195.g1:< N O >>`, that is, the squares on :g:`$LP$ <0195.g1:/ L P />`, :g:`$PN$ <0195.g1:/ P N />` respectively, is also rational; therefore each of the straight lines :g:`$LP$ <0195.g1:/ L P />`, :g:`$PN$ <0195.g1:/ P N />` is also rational.

.. subclause::

   Again, since :g:`$DH$ <0195.g1:< D H >>` is medial and is equal to :g:`$LO$ <0195.g1:< L O >>`,

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$LO$ <0195.g1:< L O >>` is also medial.

.. subclause::

   Since then :g:`$LO$ <0195.g1:< L O >>` is medial, while :g:`$NO$ <0195.g1:< N O >>` is rational,

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$LO$ <0195.g1:< L O >>` is incommensurable with :g:`$NO$ <0195.g1:< N O >>`.

.. subclause:: [VI. 1] <VI.1>

   But, as :g:`$LO$ <0195.g1:< L O >>` is to :g:`$NO$ <0195.g1:< N O >>`, so is :g:`$LP$ <0195.g1:/ L P />` to :g:`$PN$ <0195.g1:/ P N />`;

.. assertion:: [x. 11]

   therefore :g:`$LP$ <0195.g1:/ L P />` is incommensurable in length with :g:`$PN$ <0195.g1:/ P N />`.

.. subclause::

   And both are rational;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$LP$ <0195.g1:/ L P />`, :g:`$PN$ <0195.g1:/ P N />` are rational straight lines commensurable in square only; therefore :g:`$LN$ <0195.g1:/ L N />` is an apotome. :just:`[x. 73]`

.. subclause::

   And it is the "side" of the area :g:`$AB$ <0195.g1:< A B >>`;

.. assertion::

   therefore the "side" of the area :g:`$AB$ <0195.g1:< A B >>` is an apotome.

.. qed::

   Therefore etc.

----

This proposition corresponds to x. 54, and the problem solved in it is to find and to classify the side of a square equal to the rectangle contained by a :term:`*first apotome* <first-apotome>` and $\rho$, or (algebraically) to find

$$\sqrt{\rho(k\rho - k\rho\sqrt{1-\lambda^2})}.$$

First find $u, v$ from the equations

$$\left.\begin{aligned}
u + v &= k\rho \\
uv &= \frac{1}{4}k^2\rho^2(1 - \lambda^2)
\end{aligned}\right\} \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(1).$$

If $u, v$ represent the values so found, put

$$\left.\begin{aligned}
x^2 &= \rho u \\
y^2 &= \rho v
\end{aligned}\right\} \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(2),$$

and $(x - y)$ shall be the square root required.

To prove this Euclid argues thus.

By (1),

$$u : \frac{1}{2}k\rho\sqrt{1-\lambda^2} = \frac{1}{2}k\rho\sqrt{1-\lambda^2} : v,$$

whence

$$\rho u : \frac{1}{2}k\rho^2\sqrt{1-\lambda^2} = \frac{1}{2}k\rho^2\sqrt{1-\lambda^2} : \rho v,$$

or

$$x^2 : \frac{1}{2}k\rho^2\sqrt{1-\lambda^2} = \frac{1}{2}k\rho^2\sqrt{1-\lambda^2} : y^2.$$

But [Lemma after x. 53]

$$x^2 : xy = xy : y^2,$$

so that

$$xy = \frac{1}{2}k\rho^2\sqrt{1-\lambda^2} \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(3).$$
