I say that <span data-ref="0207.g1:/ L N /">$LN$</span> is the irrational straight line called minor.
For, since <span data-ref="0207.g1:< A K >">$AK$</span> is rational and is equal to the squares on <span data-ref="0207.g1:/ L P /">$LP$</span>, <span data-ref="0207.g1:/ P N /">$PN$</span>,
therefore the sum of the squares on <span data-ref="0207.g1:/ L P /">$LP$</span>, <span data-ref="0207.g1:/ P N /">$PN$</span> is rational.
Again, since <span data-ref="0207.g1:< D K >">$DK$</span> is medial,
and <span data-ref="0207.g1:< D K >">$DK$</span> is equal to twice the rectangle <span data-ref="0207.g1:/ L P /">$LP$</span>, <span data-ref="0207.g1:/ P N /">$PN$</span>,
therefore twice the rectangle <span data-ref="0207.g1:/ L P /">$LP$</span>, <span data-ref="0207.g1:/ P N /">$PN$</span> is medial.
And, since <span data-ref="0207.g1:< A I >">$AI$</span> was proved incommensurable with <span data-ref="0207.g1:< F K >">$FK$</span>,
therefore the square on <span data-ref="0207.g1:/ L P /">$LP$</span> is also incommensurable with the square on <span data-ref="0207.g1:/ P N /">$PN$</span>.
Therefore <span data-ref="0207.g1:/ L P /">$LP$</span>, <span data-ref="0207.g1:/ P N /">$PN$</span> are straight lines incommensurable in square which make the sum of the squares on them rational, but twice the rectangle contained by them medial.
Therefore <span data-ref="0207.g1:/ L N /">$LN$</span> is the irrational straight line called minor; [x. 76]
and it is the “side” of the area <span data-ref="0207.g1:< A B >">$AB$</span>.
Therefore the “side” of the area <span data-ref="0207.g1:< A B >">$AB$</span> is minor.

Q. E. D.

We have here to find and classify the straight line
$$\sqrt{\rho\left(k\rho - \frac{k\rho}{\sqrt{1+\lambda}}\right)}.$$
As usual, we find $u$, $v$ from the equations
$$\left.\begin{aligned}
u + v &= k\rho \\
uv &= \frac{1}{4}\frac{k^2\rho^2}{1+\lambda}
\end{aligned}\right\} \tag{1},$$
and then, giving $u$, $v$ their values, we put
$$\left.\begin{aligned}
x^2 &= \rho u \\
y^2 &= \rho v
\end{aligned}\right\} \tag{2}.$$
Then $(x - y)$ is the required square root.
This is proved in the same way as before, and, as before, it is proved that
$$xy = \frac{1}{2}\frac{k\rho^2}{\sqrt{1+\lambda}}.$$
Now, from (1), by x. 18,
$$u \mathbin{\sim\kern-0.6em\raisebox{0.1ex}{/}} v;$$
therefore
$$\rho u \mathbin{\sim\kern-0.6em\raisebox{0.1ex}{/}} \rho v,$$
or
$$x^2 \mathbin{\sim\kern-0.6em\raisebox{0.1ex}{/}} y^2,$$
so that $x$, $y$ are incommensurable in square.
And $x^2 + y^2$, or $\rho(u + v)$, is a rational area ($k\rho^2$).
But $2xy = \frac{k\rho^2}{\sqrt{1+\lambda}}$, which is a medial area.
Hence [x. 76] $(x - y)$ is the irrational straight line called *minor*.
