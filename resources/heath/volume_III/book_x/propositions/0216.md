### PROPOSITION 97.

*The square on an apotome applied to a rational straight line produces as breadth a first apotome.*

Let <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> be an apotome, and <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> rational,
and to <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> let there be applied <span data-ref="0216.g1:< C F E D >">$CE$</span> equal to the square on <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> and producing <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span> as breadth;
I say that <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span> is a first apotome.

![Proposition 97 Diagram](0216.g1.svg)

For let <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span> be the annex to <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>;
therefore <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ B G /">$GB$</span> are rational straight lines commensurable in square only. \hfill [x. 73]

To <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> let there be applied <span data-ref="0216.g1:< C K H D >">$CH$</span> equal to the square on <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, and <span data-ref="0216.g1:< K M L H >">$KL$</span> equal to the square on <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span>.
Therefore the whole <span data-ref="0216.g1:< C M L D >">$CL$</span> is equal to the squares on <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ B G /">$GB$</span>,
and, in these, <span data-ref="0216.g1:< C F E D >">$CE$</span> is equal to the square on <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>;
therefore the remainder <span data-ref="0216.g1:< F M L E >">$FL$</span> is equal to twice the rectangle <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ B G /">$GB$</span>. \hfill [II. 7]

Let <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ F M /">$FM$</span> be bisected at the point <span data-ref="0216.g1:N">$N$</span>,
and let <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ N O /">$NO$</span> be drawn through <span data-ref="0216.g1:N">$N$</span> parallel to <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>;
therefore each of the rectangles <span data-ref="0216.g1:< F N O E >">$FO$</span>, <span data-ref="0216.g1:< N M L O >">$LN$</span> is equal to the rectangle <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ B G /">$GB$</span>.

Now, since the squares on <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ B G /">$GB$</span> are rational,
and <span data-ref="0216.g1:< C M L D >">$DM$</span> is equal to the squares on <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ B G /">$GB$</span>,
therefore <span data-ref="0216.g1:< C M L D >">$DM$</span> is rational.

And it has been applied to the rational straight line <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, producing <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ C M /">$CM$</span> as breadth;
therefore <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ C M /">$CM$</span> is rational and commensurable in length with <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>. \hfill [x. 20]

Again, since twice the rectangle <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ B G /">$GB$</span> is medial, and <span data-ref="0216.g1:< F M L E >">$FL$</span> is equal to twice the rectangle <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ B G /">$GB$</span>,
therefore <span data-ref="0216.g1:< F M L E >">$FL$</span> is medial.
