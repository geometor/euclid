And, since the squares on <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ B G /">$GB$</span> are rational,

And <span data-ref="0216.g1:< C M L D >">$CL$</span> is equal to the squares on <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ B G /">$GB$</span>,
and <span data-ref="0216.g1:< F M L E >">$FL$</span> to twice the rectangle <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ B G /">$GB$</span>;
therefore <span data-ref="0216.g1:< C M L D >">$DM$</span> is incommensurable with <span data-ref="0216.g1:< F M L E >">$FL$</span>.

But, as <span data-ref="0216.g1:< C M L D >">$DM$</span> is to <span data-ref="0216.g1:< F M L E >">$FL$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ C M /">$CM$</span> to <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ F M /">$FM$</span>; \hfill [VI. 1]
therefore <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ C M /">$CM$</span> is incommensurable in length with <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ F M /">$FM$</span>. \hfill [x. 11]

And both are rational;
therefore <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ C M /">$CM$</span>, <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ F M /">$MF$</span> are rational straight lines commensurable in square only;
therefore <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span> is an apotome. \hfill [x. 73]

I say next that it is also a first apotome.

For, since the rectangle <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ B G /">$GB$</span> is a mean proportional between the squares on <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ B G /">$GB$</span>,
and <span data-ref="0216.g1:< C K H D >">$CH$</span> is equal to the square on <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>,
<span data-ref="0216.g1:< K M L H >">$KL$</span> equal to the square on <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span>,
and <span data-ref="0216.g1:< N M L O >">$NL$</span> equal to the rectangle <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ B G /">$GB$</span>,
therefore <span data-ref="0216.g1:< N M L O >">$NL$</span> is also a mean proportional between <span data-ref="0216.g1:< C K H D >">$CH$</span>, <span data-ref="0216.g1:< K M L H >">$KL$</span>;
therefore, as <span data-ref="0216.g1:< C K H D >">$CH$</span> is to <span data-ref="0216.g1:< N M L O >">$NL$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0216.g1:< N M L O >">$NL$</span> to <span data-ref="0216.g1:< K M L H >">$KL$</span>.

But, as <span data-ref="0216.g1:< C K H D >">$CH$</span> is to <span data-ref="0216.g1:< N M L O >">$NL$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ C K /">$CK$</span> to <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ N M /">$NM$</span>,
and, as <span data-ref="0216.g1:< N M L O >">$NL$</span> is to <span data-ref="0216.g1:< K M L H >">$KL$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ N M /">$NM$</span> to <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ K M /">$KM$</span>; \hfill [VI. 1]
therefore the rectangle <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ C K /">$CK$</span>, <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ K M /">$KM$</span> is equal to the square on <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ N M /">$NM$</span> [VI. 17], that is, to the fourth part of the square on <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ F M /">$FM$</span>.

And, since the square on <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span> is commensurable with the square on <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ B G /">$GB$</span>,
<span data-ref="0216.g1:< C K H D >">$CH$</span> is also commensurable with <span data-ref="0216.g1:< K M L H >">$KL$</span>.

But, as <span data-ref="0216.g1:< C K H D >">$CH$</span> is to <span data-ref="0216.g1:< K M L H >">$KL$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ C K /">$CK$</span> to <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ K M /">$KM$</span>; \hfill [VI. 1]
therefore <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ C K /">$CK$</span> is commensurable with <span data-ref="0216.g1:/ K M /">$KM$</span>. \hfill [x. 11]
