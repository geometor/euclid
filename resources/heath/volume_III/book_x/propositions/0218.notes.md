# Transcription Notes: Folio 0218 (Page 214)

## Summary & Folio Continuity
- **Folio Range**: Folio 0218 corresponds to page 214 of Heath's *Euclid's Elements*, Volume III.
- **Preceding Context**: Folio 0217 contains the major construction and proof of Proposition 97 (the square on an apotome applied to a rational line produces as breadth a first apotome).
- **Folio Contents**:
  - Conclusion of the proof of Proposition 97 and Q.E.D.
  - Horizontal transition rule to Heath's commentary.
  - Introductory scholarly commentary on the hexad of converse propositions (Propositions 97–102, which correspond to Propositions 60–65 for binomials).
  - Modern algebraic representation of Proposition 97, formulating $(\rho - \sqrt{k}\rho)^2 / \sigma$ as $(x+y) - 2z$, with steps $(\alpha)$ establishing the apotome property and $(\beta)$ establishing the mean proportional relation.
- **Succeeding Continuity**: Continues on folio 0219 (page 215) with step (6) and the final conclusion of the commentary on Proposition 97, followed by Proposition 98.

## Textual / Lexical / Mathematical Observations
- **Notation for Commensurability and Incommensurability**:
  - Commensurable in length: denoted by $\sim$.
  - Incommensurable in length: denoted by $\smile$.
  - In line (3), $\sigma \cdot 2z$ is medial, hence $2z$ is rational and incommensurable in length with $\sigma$ ($2z$ is rational and $\smile \sigma$, by X. 22).
  - In line (4), $(x+y)$ and $2z$ are both rational and incommensurable in length, hence commensurable in square only (printed as "rational and $\sim$").
- **Propositions Cited**:
  - [x. 17] for commensurability of segments resulting from deficient application.
  - [x. Deff. III. 1] for definition of first apotome.
