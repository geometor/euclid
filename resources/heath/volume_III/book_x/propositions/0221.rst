:title: Proposition 98 (concluded)

.. subclause::

   | Since then :g:`$CM$ <0219.g1:/ C M />`, :g:`$MF$ <0219.g1:/ F M />` are two unequal straight lines, and
   | the rectangle :g:`$CK$ <0219.g1:/ C K />`, :g:`$KM$ <0219.g1:/ K M />` equal to the fourth part of the square
   | on :g:`$MF$ <0219.g1:/ F M />` and deficient by a square figure has been applied to
   | the greater, :g:`$CM$ <0219.g1:/ C M />`, and divides it into commensurable parts,

.. assertion::

   | therefore the square on :g:`$CM$ <0219.g1:/ C M />` is greater than the square on :g:`$MF$ <0219.g1:/ F M />`
   | by the square on a straight line commensurable in length with
   | :g:`$CM$ <0219.g1:/ C M />`. :just:`[x. 17]`

.. subclause::

   | And the annex :g:`$FM$ <0219.g1:/ F M />` is commensurable in length with the
   | rational straight line :g:`$CD$ <0219.g1:/ C D />` set out;

.. assertion:: [x. Deff. III. 2]

   therefore :g:`$CF$ <0219.g1:/ C F />` is a second apotome.

.. qed::

   Therefore etc. :just:`Q. E. D.`

----

In this case we have to find and classify

$$\frac{(k^{\frac{1}{4}}\rho \sim k^{\frac{3}{4}}\rho)^2}{\sigma}.$$

Take $x$, $y$, $z$ such that

$$\left.\begin{aligned}
\sigma x &= k^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho^2 \\
\sigma y &= k^{\frac{3}{2}}\rho^2 \\
\sigma \cdot 2z &= 2k\rho^2
\end{aligned}\right\} \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(1).$$

$(\alpha)$ Now $k^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho^2$, $k^{\frac{3}{2}}\rho^2$ are medial areas;
therefore $\sigma (x + y)$ is medial,
whence $(x + y)$ is rational and $\smile \sigma\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(2).$

But $2k\rho^2$, and therefore $\sigma \cdot 2z$, is rational,
whence $2z$ is rational and $\sim \sigma\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(3).$

And, $\sigma (x + y)$ being medial, and $\sigma \cdot 2z$ rational,

$$\sigma (x + y) \smile \sigma \cdot 2z,$$

or

$$(x + y) \smile 2z.$$

| Hence $(x + y)$, $2z$ are rational straight lines commensurable in square only,
| and therefore $(x + y) - 2z$ is an *apotome*.

$(\beta)$ We prove, as before, that

$$xy = \frac{1}{4}(2z)^2\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(4).$$

Also $k^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho^2 \sim k^{\frac{3}{2}}\rho^2$, or $\sigma x \sim \sigma y$,
so that

$$x \sim y\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(5).$$

| [This step is omitted in P, and Heiberg accordingly brackets it. The
| result is, however, assumed.]

Therefore [(4), (5)], by x. 17,

$$\sqrt{(x + y)^2 - (2z)^2} \sim (x + y).$$

And $2z \sim \sigma$.
Therefore $(x + y) - 2z$ is a *second apotome*.

Obviously

$$(x + y) - 2z = \frac{\rho^2}{\sigma}\{\sqrt{k}(1 + k) - 2k\}.$$
