### PROPOSITION 99.

*The square on a second apotome of a medial straight line applied to a rational straight line produces as breadth a third apotome.*

Let <span data-ref="0222.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> be a second apotome of a medial straight line, and <span data-ref="0222.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> rational,
and to <span data-ref="0222.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> let there be applied <span data-ref="0222.g1:< C F E D >">$CE$</span> equal to the square on <span data-ref="0222.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, producing <span data-ref="0222.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span> as breadth;
I say that <span data-ref="0222.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span> is a third apotome.

![Proposition 99](0222.g1.svg)

For let <span data-ref="0222.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span> be the annex to <span data-ref="0222.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>;
therefore <span data-ref="0222.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0222.g1:/ B G /">$GB$</span> are medial straight lines commensurable in square only which contain a medial rectangle. [x. 75]

Let <span data-ref="0222.g1:< C K H D >">$CH$</span> equal to the square on <span data-ref="0222.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span> be applied to <span data-ref="0222.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, producing <span data-ref="0222.g1:/ C K /">$CK$</span> as breadth,
and let <span data-ref="0222.g1:< K M L H >">$KL$</span> equal to the square on <span data-ref="0222.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span> be applied to <span data-ref="0222.g1:/ K H /">$KH$</span>, producing <span data-ref="0222.g1:/ K M /">$KM$</span> as breadth;
therefore the whole <span data-ref="0222.g1:< C M L D >">$CL$</span> is equal to the squares on <span data-ref="0222.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0222.g1:/ B G /">$GB$</span>;
therefore <span data-ref="0222.g1:< C M L D >">$CL$</span> is also medial. [x. 15 and 23, Por.]

And it is applied to the rational straight line <span data-ref="0222.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, producing <span data-ref="0222.g1:/ C M /">$CM$</span> as breadth;
therefore <span data-ref="0222.g1:/ C M /">$CM$</span> is rational and incommensurable in length with <span data-ref="0222.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>. [x. 22]

Now, since the whole <span data-ref="0222.g1:< C M L D >">$CL$</span> is equal to the squares on <span data-ref="0222.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0222.g1:/ B G /">$GB$</span>, and, in these, <span data-ref="0222.g1:< C F E D >">$CE$</span> is equal to the square on <span data-ref="0222.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>,
therefore the remainder <span data-ref="0222.g1:< F M L E >">$LF$</span> is equal to twice the rectangle <span data-ref="0222.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0222.g1:/ B G /">$GB$</span>. [II. 7]

Let then <span data-ref="0222.g1:/ F M /">$FM$</span> be bisected at the point <span data-ref="0222.g1:N">$N$</span>,
and let <span data-ref="0222.g1:/ N O /">$NO$</span> be drawn parallel to <span data-ref="0222.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>;
therefore each of the rectangles <span data-ref="0222.g1:< F N O E >">$FO$</span>, <span data-ref="0222.g1:< N M L O >">$NL$</span> is equal to the rectangle <span data-ref="0222.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0222.g1:/ B G /">$GB$</span>.
