Thus [(1), (2), (3)] $(x + y)$, $2z$ are rational and $\sim$,
so that $(x + y) - 2z$ is an *apotome*.

$(\beta)$ $\sigma x \sim \sigma y$, so that $x \sim y$.

And, as before,
$$xy = \frac{1}{4}(2z)^2.$$

Therefore [x. 17]
$$\sqrt{(x + y)^2 - (2z)^2} \sim (x + y).$$

And neither $(x + y)$ nor $2z$ is $\sim \sigma$.

Therefore $(x + y) - 2z$ is a *third apotome*.

It is of course equal to
$$\frac{\rho^2}{\sigma}\left\{\frac{k + \lambda}{\sqrt{k}} - 2\sqrt{\lambda}\right\}.$$

### PROPOSITION 100.

*The square on a minor straight line applied to a rational straight line produces as breadth a fourth apotome.*

Let <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> be a minor and <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> a rational straight line, and to the rational straight line <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> let <span data-ref="0225.g1:< C F E D >">$CE$</span> be applied equal to the square on <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> and producing <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span> as breadth;
I say that <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span> is a fourth apotome.

![Proposition 100](0225.g1.svg)

For let <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span> be the annex to <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>;
therefore <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ B G /">$GB$</span> are straight lines incommensurable in square which make the sum of the squares on <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ B G /">$GB$</span> rational, but twice the rectangle <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ B G /">$GB$</span> medial. [x. 76]

To <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> let there be applied <span data-ref="0225.g1:< C K H D >">$CH$</span> equal to the square on <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span> and producing <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ C K /">$CK$</span> as breadth,
and <span data-ref="0225.g1:< K M L H >">$KL$</span> equal to the square on <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span>, producing <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ K M /">$KM$</span> as breadth;
therefore the whole <span data-ref="0225.g1:< C M L D >">$CL$</span> is equal to the squares on <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ B G /">$GB$</span>.

And the sum of the squares on <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ B G /">$GB$</span> is rational;
therefore <span data-ref="0225.g1:< C M L D >">$CL$</span> is also rational.

And it is applied to the rational straight line <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, producing <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ C M /">$CM$</span> as breadth;
therefore <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ C M /">$CM$</span> is also rational and commensurable in length with <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>. [x. 20]
