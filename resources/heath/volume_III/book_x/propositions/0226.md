And, since the whole <span data-ref="0225.g1:< C M L D >">$CL$</span> is equal to the squares on <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ B G /">$GB$</span>, and, in these, <span data-ref="0225.g1:< C F E D >">$CE$</span> is equal to the square on <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, therefore the remainder <span data-ref="0225.g1:< F M L E >">$FL$</span> is equal to twice the rectangle <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ B G /">$GB$</span>. [II. 7]

Let then <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ F M /">$FM$</span> be bisected at the point <span data-ref="0225.g1:N">$N$</span>, and let <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ N O /">$NO$</span> be drawn through <span data-ref="0225.g1:N">$N$</span> parallel to either of the straight lines <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ M L /">$ML$</span>; therefore each of the rectangles <span data-ref="0225.g1:< F N O E >">$FO$</span>, <span data-ref="0225.g1:< N M L O >">$NL$</span> is equal to the rectangle <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ B G /">$GB$</span>.

And, since twice the rectangle <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ B G /">$GB$</span> is medial and is equal to <span data-ref="0225.g1:< F M L E >">$FL$</span>, therefore <span data-ref="0225.g1:< F M L E >">$FL$</span> is also medial.

And it is applied to the rational straight line <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ F E /">$FE$</span>, producing <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ F M /">$FM$</span> as breadth; therefore <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ F M /">$FM$</span> is rational and incommensurable in length with <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>. [X. 22]

And, since the sum of the squares on <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ B G /">$GB$</span> is rational, while twice the rectangle <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ B G /">$GB$</span> is medial, the squares on <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ B G /">$GB$</span> are incommensurable with twice the rectangle <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ B G /">$GB$</span>.

But <span data-ref="0225.g1:< C M L D >">$CL$</span> is equal to the squares on <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ B G /">$GB$</span>, and <span data-ref="0225.g1:< F M L E >">$FL$</span> equal to twice the rectangle <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ B G /">$GB$</span>; therefore <span data-ref="0225.g1:< C M L D >">$CL$</span> is incommensurable with <span data-ref="0225.g1:< F M L E >">$FL$</span>.

But, as <span data-ref="0225.g1:< C M L D >">$CL$</span> is to <span data-ref="0225.g1:< F M L E >">$FL$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ C M /">$CM$</span> to <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ F M /">$MF$</span>; [VI. 1] therefore <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ C M /">$CM$</span> is incommensurable in length with <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ F M /">$MF$</span>. [X. 11]

And both are rational; therefore <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ C M /">$CM$</span>, <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ F M /">$MF$</span> are rational straight lines commensurable in square only; therefore <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span> is an apotome. [X. 73]

I say that it is also a fourth apotome.

For, since <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ B G /">$GB$</span> are incommensurable in square, therefore the square on <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span> is also incommensurable with the square on <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ B G /">$GB$</span>.

And <span data-ref="0225.g1:< C K H D >">$CH$</span> is equal to the square on <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, and <span data-ref="0225.g1:< K M L H >">$KL$</span> equal to the square on <span data-ref="0225.g1:/ B G /">$GB$</span>; therefore <span data-ref="0225.g1:< C K H D >">$CH$</span> is incommensurable with <span data-ref="0225.g1:< K M L H >">$KL$</span>.
