It follows that $\sigma(x+y)$ is rational and $\sigma \cdot 2z$ medial,<br />
so that $(x+y)$ is rational and $\sim \sigma$ ....................................(1),<br />
while $2z$ is rational and $\smile \sigma$ ........................................(2),<br />
and $\sigma(x+y) \smile \sigma \cdot 2z$,<br />
so that $(x+y) \smile 2z$ ................................................(3).

Thus [(1), (2), (3)] $(x+y)$, $2z$ are rational and $\sim$,<br />
so that $(x+y) - 2z$ is an apotome.

Next, since
$$u^2 \smile v^2,$$
$$\sigma x \smile \sigma y,$$
or
$$x \smile y.$$

And it is proved, as usual, that
$$xy = z^2 = \frac{1}{4}(2z)^2.$$

Therefore [X. 18] $\sqrt{(x+y)^2 - (2z)^2} \smile (x+y)$.

But $(x+y) \sim \sigma$,<br />
therefore $x+y-2z$ is a _fourth apotome_.

Its value is of course $\frac{\rho^2}{\sigma}\left(1 - \frac{1}{\sqrt{1+k^2}}\right)$.

### PROPOSITION 101.

<p class="enunciation">The square on the straight line which produces with a<br />
rational area a medial whole, if applied to a rational straight<br />
line, produces as breadth a fifth apotome.</p>

Let <span data-ref="0228.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> be the straight line which produces with a<br />
rational area a medial whole, and <span data-ref="0228.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> a rational straight line,<br />
and to <span data-ref="0228.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> let <span data-ref="0228.g1:< C F E D >">$CE$</span> be applied equal to the square on <span data-ref="0228.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> and<br />
producing <span data-ref="0228.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span> as breadth;<br />
I say that <span data-ref="0228.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span> is a fifth apotome.

<p class="sub-clause">For let <span data-ref="0228.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span> be the annex to <span data-ref="0228.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0228.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0228.g1:/ B G /">$GB$</span> are straight lines incommensurable in<br />
square which make the sum of the squares on them medial<br />
but twice the rectangle contained by them rational. <span class="justification">[X. 77]</span></p>

To <span data-ref="0228.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> let there be applied <span data-ref="0228.g1:< C K H D >">$CH$</span> equal to the square on<br />
<span data-ref="0228.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, and <span data-ref="0228.g1:< K M L H >">$KL$</span> equal to the square on <span data-ref="0228.g1:/ B G /">$GB$</span>;
<p class="assertion">therefore the whole <span data-ref="0228.g1:< C M L D >">$CL$</span> is equal to the squares on <span data-ref="0228.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0228.g1:/ B G /">$GB$</span>.</p>
