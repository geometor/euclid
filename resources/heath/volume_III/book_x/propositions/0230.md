We have to find and classify
$$\frac{1}{\sigma}\left\{\frac{\rho}{\sqrt{2(1+k^2)}}\sqrt{\sqrt{1+k^2}+k} - \frac{\rho}{\sqrt{2(1+k^2)}}\sqrt{\sqrt{1+k^2}-k}\right\}^2.$$

Call this $\frac{1}{\sigma}(u-v)^2$, and take $x, y, z$ such that
$$\left.\begin{aligned}
\sigma x &= u^2 \\
\sigma y &= v^2 \\
\sigma \cdot 2z &= 2uv
\end{aligned}\right\}.$$

In this case $u^2, v^2$ are incommensurable, $(u^2+v^2)$ is a medial area and $2uv$<br />
a rational area.

Since $\sigma(x+y)$ is medial and $\sigma \cdot 2z$ rational,<br />
$(x+y)$ is rational and $\smile \sigma$,<br />
$2z$ is rational and $\frown \sigma$,<br />
while
$$(x+y) \smile 2z.$$

It follows that $(x+y), 2z$ are rational and $\sim$,<br />
so that $(x+y) - 2z$ is an apotome.

Again, as before,
$$xy = z^2 = \frac{1}{4}(2z)^2,$$
and, since $u^2 \smile v^2$,
$$\sigma x \smile \sigma y,$$
or
$$x \smile y.$$

Hence [X. 18]
$$\sqrt{(x+y)^2 - (2z)^2} \smile (x+y).$$

And $2z \frown \sigma$.

Therefore $(x+y) - 2z$ is a _fifth apotome_.

It is of course equal to
$$\frac{\rho^2}{\sigma}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+k^2}} - \frac{1}{1+k^2}\right).$$
