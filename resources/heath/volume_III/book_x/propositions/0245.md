<p class="assertion">the square on <span data-ref="0244.g1:/ D G /">$DG$</span> is greater than the square on <span data-ref="0244.g1:/ G E /">$GE$</span> by the<br />
square on a straight line commensurable with <span data-ref="0244.g1:/ D G /">$DG$</span>, and the<br />
greater term <span data-ref="0244.g1:/ D G /">$DG$</span> is commensurable in length with the rational<br />
straight line <span data-ref="0244.g1:/ D C /">$DC$</span> set out. <span class="justification">[x. Deff. II. 1]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore <span data-ref="0244.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span> is also commensurable in length with <span data-ref="0244.g1:/ D G /">$DG$</span>; <span class="justification">[x. 12]</span><br />
therefore the remainder <span data-ref="0244.g1:/ G F /">$GF$</span> is also commensurable in length<br />
with <span data-ref="0244.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span>. <span class="justification">[x. 15]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But <span data-ref="0244.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span> is incommensurable in length with <span data-ref="0244.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0244.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span> is also incommensurable in length with <span data-ref="0244.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>. <span class="justification">[x. 13]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore <span data-ref="0244.g1:/ G F /">$GF$</span>, <span data-ref="0244.g1:/ F E /">$FE$</span> are rational straight lines commensur-<br />
able in square only;<br />
therefore <span data-ref="0244.g1:/ E G /">$EG$</span> is an apotome. <span class="justification">[x. 73]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But it is also rational:</p>
<p class="assertion">which is impossible.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore the apotome is not the same with the binomial<br />
straight line.</p>

<p class="qed">Q. E. D.</p>

This proposition proves the equivalent of the fact that<br />
$\sqrt{x} + \sqrt{y}$ cannot be equal to $\sqrt{x'} - \sqrt{y'}$, and<br />
$x + \sqrt{y}$ cannot be equal to $x' - \sqrt{y'}$.

We should prove these results by squaring the respective expressions; and<br />
Euclid's procedure corresponds to this exactly.

He has to prove that
$$\rho + \sqrt{k} \cdot \rho \text{ cannot be equal to } \rho' - \sqrt{\lambda} \cdot \rho'.$$

For, if possible, let this be so.

Take the straight lines $\frac{(\rho + \sqrt{k} \cdot \rho)^2}{\sigma}$, $\frac{(\rho' - \sqrt{\lambda} \cdot \rho')^2}{\sigma}$;<br />
these must be equal, and therefore
$$\frac{\rho^2}{\sigma} (1 + k + 2\sqrt{k}) = \frac{\rho'^2}{\sigma} (1 + \lambda - 2\sqrt{\lambda}) \dots\dots\dots\dots\dots(1).$$

Now $\frac{\rho^2}{\sigma} (1 + k)$, $\frac{\rho'^2}{\sigma} (1 + \lambda)$ are rational and $\mathbin{\Cap}$;<br />
therefore
$$\left\{ \frac{\rho'^2}{\sigma} (1 + \lambda) - \frac{\rho^2}{\sigma} (1 + k) \right\} \mathbin{\Cap} \frac{\rho'^2}{\sigma} (1 + \lambda)$$
$$\smile \frac{\rho'^2}{\sigma} \cdot 2\sqrt{\lambda}.$$

And, since both sides are rational, it follows that
$$\left\{ \frac{\rho'^2}{\sigma} (1 + \lambda) - \frac{\rho^2}{\sigma} (1 + k) \right\} - \frac{\rho'^2}{\sigma} \cdot 2\sqrt{\lambda} \text{ is an \textit{apotome}}.$$
