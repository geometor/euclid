This proposition is the connecting link which enables Euclid to prove that<br />

<p class="enunciation">The apotome and the irrational straight lines following it<br />
are neither the same with the medial straight line nor with one<br />
another.</p>

For the square on a medial straight line, if applied to a<br />
rational straight line, produces as breadth a straight line<br />
rational and incommensurable in length with that to which it<br />
is applied, <span class="justification">[x. 22]</span><br />
while the square on an apotome, if applied to a rational<br />
straight line, produces as breadth a first apotome, <span class="justification">[x. 97]</span><br />
the square on a first apotome of a medial straight line, if<br />
applied to a rational straight line, produces as breadth a<br />
second apotome, <span class="justification">[x. 98]</span><br />
the square on a second apotome of a medial straight line, if<br />
applied to a rational straight line, produces as breadth a third<br />
apotome, <span class="justification">[x. 99]</span><br />
the square on a minor straight line, if applied to a rational<br />
straight line, produces as breadth a fourth apotome, <span class="justification">[x. 100]</span><br />
the square on the straight line which produces with a rational<br />
area a medial whole, if applied to a rational straight line,<br />
produces as breadth a fifth apotome, <span class="justification">[x. 101]</span><br />
and the square on the straight line which produces with a<br />
medial area a medial whole, if applied to a rational straight<br />
line, produces as breadth a sixth apotome. <span class="justification">[x. 102]</span>

one another, from the first because it is rational, and from one<br />
another since they are not the same in order,</p>

differ from one another.</p>
