We find $(u + w)$, $w$ algebraically thus.

By (1),
$$u = \frac{\sigma^2}{\rho + \sqrt k \cdot \rho};$$
and, by (2), (3),
$$\frac{u + w}{w} = \frac{\rho}{\sqrt k \cdot \rho},$$
whence
$$\begin{aligned}
w &= \frac{u \cdot \sqrt k \cdot \rho}{\rho - \sqrt k \cdot \rho} \\
&= \frac{\sigma^2 \cdot \sqrt k \cdot \rho}{\rho^2 - k\rho^2}.
\end{aligned}$$

Thus
$$u + w = w \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt k} = \frac{\sigma^2 \cdot \rho}{\rho^2 - k\rho^2}.$$

Therefore
$$(u + w) - w = \sigma^2 \cdot \frac{\rho - \sqrt k \cdot \rho}{\rho^2 - k\rho^2}.$$

### PROPOSITION 113.

<p class="enunciation">The square on a rational straight line, if applied to an<br />
apotome, produces as breadth the binomial straight line the<br />
terms of which are commensurable with the terms of the<br />
apotome and in the same ratio; and further the binomial<br />
so arising has the same order as the apotome.</p>

![Proposition 113](0252.g1.svg)

Let <span data-ref="0252.g1:A">$A$</span> be a rational straight line and <span data-ref="0252.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> an apotome,<br />
and let the rectangle <span data-ref="0252.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>, <span data-ref="0252.g1:/ K H /">$KH$</span> be equal to<br />
the square on <span data-ref="0252.g1:A">$A$</span>, so that the square on the<br />
rational straight line <span data-ref="0252.g1:A">$A$</span> when applied to the<br />
apotome <span data-ref="0252.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> produces <span data-ref="0252.g1:/ K H /">$KH$</span> as breadth;<br />
I say that <span data-ref="0252.g1:/ K H /">$KH$</span> is a binomial straight line the<br />
terms of which are commensurable with the<br />
terms of <span data-ref="0252.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> and in the same ratio; and<br />
further <span data-ref="0252.g1:/ K H /">$KH$</span> has the same order as <span data-ref="0252.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>.

<p class="sub-clause">For let <span data-ref="0252.g1:/ D C /">$DC$</span> be the annex to <span data-ref="0252.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0252.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, <span data-ref="0252.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> are rational straight lines commensurable<br />
in square only. <span class="justification">[X. 73]</span></p>

Let the rectangle <span data-ref="0252.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, <span data-ref="0252.g1:G">$G$</span> be also equal to the square on <span data-ref="0252.g1:A">$A$</span>.

<p class="sub-clause">But the square on <span data-ref="0252.g1:A">$A$</span> is rational;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the rectangle <span data-ref="0252.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, <span data-ref="0252.g1:G">$G$</span> is also rational.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">And it has been applied to the rational straight line <span data-ref="0252.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0252.g1:G">$G$</span> is rational and commensurable in length with <span data-ref="0252.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>.<br />
<span class="justification">[X. 20]</span></p>
