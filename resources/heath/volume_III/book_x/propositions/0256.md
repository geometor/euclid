To find $(u - w)$, $w$ algebraically we have
$$u = \frac{\sigma^2}{\rho - \sqrt{k} \cdot \rho},$$
$$\frac{u - w}{w} = \frac{\rho}{\sqrt{k} \cdot \rho}.$$
From the latter
$$\begin{aligned}
w &= \frac{u \cdot \sqrt{k} \cdot \rho}{\rho + \sqrt{k} \cdot \rho} \\
&= \frac{\sigma^2 \cdot \sqrt{k} \cdot \rho}{\rho^2 - k\rho^2}.
\end{aligned}$$
Thus
$$u - w = w \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{k}} = \frac{\sigma^2\rho}{\rho^2 - k\rho^2}.$$
Therefore
$$(u - w) + w = \sigma^2 \cdot \frac{\rho + \sqrt{k} \cdot \rho}{\rho^2 - k\rho^2}.$$

### PROPOSITION 114.

<p class="enunciation">If an area be contained by an apotome and the binomial<br />
straight line the terms of which are commensurable with the<br />
terms of the apotome and in the same ratio, the “side” of the<br />
area is rational.</p>

For let an area, the rectangle <span data-ref="0256.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0256.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, be contained by<br />
the apotome <span data-ref="0256.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> and the binomial<br />
straight line <span data-ref="0256.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>,<br />
and let <span data-ref="0256.g1:/ C E /">$CE$</span> be the greater term of<br />
the latter;<br />
let the terms <span data-ref="0256.g1:/ C E /">$CE$</span>, <span data-ref="0256.g1:/ E D /">$ED$</span> of the<br />
binomial straight line be commen-<br />
surable with the terms <span data-ref="0256.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span>, <span data-ref="0256.g1:/ F B /">$FB$</span> of<br />
the apotome and in the same ratio;<br />
and let the “side” of the rectangle<br />
<span data-ref="0256.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0256.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> be <span data-ref="0256.g1:G">$G$</span>;

I say that <span data-ref="0256.g1:G">$G$</span> is rational.

For let a rational straight line <span data-ref="0256.g1:H">$H$</span> be set out,<br />
