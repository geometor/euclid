Heiberg is clearly right in holding that this proposition, at all events, is<br />
alien to the general scope of Book X, and is therefore probably an interpola-<br />
tion, made however before Theon's time. It is of the same character as a<br />
scholium at the end of the Book, which is (along with the interpolated proposi-<br />
tion proving, in two ways, the incommensurability of the diagonal of a square<br />
with its side) relegated by August as well as Heiberg to an Appendix.

The proposition amounts to this.

The straight line $k^{\frac{1}{4}}\rho$ being medial, if $\sigma$ be a rational straight line, $\sqrt{k^{\frac{1}{4}}\rho\sigma}$<br />
is a new irrational straight line. So is the mean proportional between this<br />
and another rational straight line $\sigma'$, and so on indefinitely.

### ANCIENT EXTENSIONS OF THE THEORY OF BOOK X.

From the hints given by the author of the commentary found in Arabic<br />
by Woepcke (cf. pp. 3—4 above) it would seem probable that Apollonius'<br />
extensions of the theory of irrationals took two directions : (1) generalising<br />
the *medial* straight line of Euclid, and (2) forming compound irrationals by the<br />
addition and subtraction of more than two terms of the sort composing the<br />
binomials, apotomes, etc. The commentator writes (Woepcke's article, pp. 694<br />
sqq.) :

“It is also necessary that we should know that, not only when we join<br />
together two straight lines rational and commensurable in square do we obtain<br />
the binomial straight line, but three or four lines produce in an analogous<br />
manner the same thing. In the first case, we obtain the trinomial straight<br />
line, since the whole line is irrational; and in the second case we obtain the<br />
quadrinomial, and so on *ad infinitum*. The proof of the (irrationality of the)<br />
line composed of three lines rational and commensurable in square is exactly<br />
the same as the proof relating to the combination of two lines.

“But we must start afresh and remark that not only can we take one sole<br />
medial line between two lines commensurable in square, but we can take three<br />
or four of them and so on *ad infinitum*, since we can take, between any two<br />
given straight lines, as many lines as we wish in continued proportion.

“Likewise, in the lines formed by addition not only can we construct the<br />
binomial straight line, but we can also construct the trinomial, as well as the<br />
first and second trimedial; and, further, the line composed of three straight<br />
lines incommensurable in square and such that the one of them gives with<br />
each of the two others a sum of squares (which is) rational, while the rectangle<br />
contained by the two lines is medial, so that there results a *major* (irrational)<br />
composed of three lines.

“And, in an analogous manner, we obtain the straight line which is the<br />
‘side’ of a rational plus a medial area, composed of three straight lines, and,<br />
likewise, that which is the ‘side’ of (the sum of) two medials.”
