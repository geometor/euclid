(2) *The first trimedial straight line.*

The commentator here says : “Suppose we have three medial lines com-<br />
mensurable in square [only], one of which contains with each of the two others<br />
a rational rectangle; then the straight line composed of the two lines is<br />
irrational and is called the first bimedial; the remaining line is medial, and<br />
the area contained by these two lines is irrational. Consequently the square<br />
on the whole line is irrational.”

To begin with, the conditions here given are incompatible. If $x$, $y$, $z$ be<br />
medial straight lines such that $xy$, $xz$ are both rational,
$$y : z = xy : xz = m : n,$$
and $y$, $z$ are commensurable in length and not in square only.

Hence it seems that we must, with Woepcke, understand “*three medial<br />
straight lines such that one is commensurable with each of the other two in<br />
square only and makes with it a rational rectangle.*”

If $x$, $y$, $z$ be the three medial straight lines,
$$(x^2 + y^2 + z^2) \mathbin{\cap} x^2,$$
so that $(x^2 + y^2 + z^2)$ is medial.<br />
Also we have $2xy$, $2xz$ both rational and $2yz$ medial.<br />
Now $(x^2 + y^2 + z^2) + 2yz + 2xy + 2xz$ cannot be rational, for, if it were, the<br />
sum of two medial areas, $(x^2 + y^2 + z^2)$, $2yz$, would be rational : which is im-<br />
possible. <span class="justification">[Cf. X. 72]</span><br />
Hence
$$(x + y + z) \text{ is irrational.}$$

(3) *The second trimedial straight line.*

Suppose $x$, $y$, $z$ to be medial straight lines commensurable in square only<br />
and containing with each other medial rectangles.<br />
Then
$$(x^2 + y^2 + z^2) \mathbin{\cap} x^2\text{, and is medial.}$$
Also
$$2yz\text{, } 2zx\text{, } 2xy \text{ are all medial areas.}$$

To prove the irrationality in this case I presume that the metnod would<br />
be like that of X. 38 about the *second bimedial*.<br />
Suppose $\sigma$ to be a rational straight line and let
$$\left. \begin{aligned}
(x^2 + y^2 + z^2) &= \sigma t \\
2yz &= \sigma u \\
2zx &= \sigma v \\
2xy &= \sigma w
\end{aligned} \right\}$$
Here, since, e.g.,
$$xz : xy = v : w,$$
or
$$z : y = v : w,$$
and similarly
$$x : z = w : u,$$
$u$, $v$, $w$ are commensurable in square only.

Also, since
$$\begin{aligned}
(x^2 + y^2 + z^2) &\mathbin{\cap} x^2 \\
&\mathbin{\cup} xy,
\end{aligned}$$
$t$ is incommensurable with $w$.
