Textual & Lexical Observations
------------------------------

- **Def. 9 & 10 (Similar / Equal & Similar solids)**: Euclid defines similarity of solids by similarity and number of faces; equality by equality of multitude and magnitude of faces (a classical locus of controversy addressed by Simson and Legendre in later commentary).
- **Def. 11 (Solid Angle)**: Euclid presents two alternative definitions: first as an inclination of lines towards all lines, and second ("Otherwise :") as contained by plane angles constructed to one point.
- **Def. 12 & 13 (Pyramid & Prism)**: Classical polyhedral definitions.
- **Def. 14–17 (Sphere)**: Defined kinematically by revolution of a semicircle about its fixed diameter; axis, centre, and diameter follow.
- **Def. 18–19 (Cone)**: Defined kinematically by revolution of a right-angled triangle about a fixed leg. The second paragraph classifies cones as **right-angled**, **obtuse-angled**, and **acute-angled** depending on the ratio of the fixed leg to the revolving leg (the pre-Apollonian classification of conics).
