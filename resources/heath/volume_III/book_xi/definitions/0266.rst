20. And the :term:`**base** <base-of-cone>` is the circle described by the straight line which is carried round.

21. When, one side of those about the right angle in a rectangular parallelogram remaining fixed, the parallelogram is carried round and restored again to the same position from which it began to be moved, the figure so comprehended is a :term:`**cylinder** <cylinder>`.

22. The :term:`**axis of the cylinder** <axis-of-cylinder>` is the straight line which remains fixed and about which the parallelogram is turned.

23. And the :term:`**bases** <bases-of-cylinder>` are the circles described by the two sides opposite to one another which are carried round.

24. :term:`**Similar cones and cylinders** <similar-cones-and-cylinders>` are those in which the axes and the diameters of the bases are proportional.

25. A :term:`**cube** <cube>` is a solid figure contained by six equal squares.

26. An :term:`**octahedron** <octahedron>` is a solid figure contained by eight equal and equilateral triangles.

27. An :term:`**icosahedron** <icosahedron>` is a solid figure contained by twenty equal and equilateral triangles.

28. A :term:`**dodecahedron** <dodecahedron>` is a solid figure contained by twelve equal, equilateral, and equiangular pentagons.

DEFINITION 1.
-------------

Στερεόν ἐστι τὸ :term:`μῆκος <length>` καὶ :term:`πλάτος <breadth>` καὶ :term:`βάθος <depth>` ἔχον.

This definition was evidently traditional, as may be inferred from a number of passages in :person:`Plato <plato>` and :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>`. Thus :person:`Plato <plato>` speaks (:cite:`*Sophist*, 235 D <plato:sophist:235.d>`) of making an imitation of a model (παράδειγμα) "in length and breadth and depth" and (:cite:`*Laws*, 817 E <plato:laws:817.e>`) of "the art of measuring length, surface and depth" as one of three :term:`μαθήματα <mathemata>`. *Depth*, the third dimension, is used alone as a description of "body" by :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>`, the term being regarded as connoting the other two dimensions; thus (:cite:`*Metaph.* 1020 a 13, 11 <aristotle:met:1020.a.13.11>`) "length is a line, breadth a surface, and depth body"; "that which is continuous in one direction is length, in two directions breadth, and in three depth." Similarly :person:`Plato <plato>` (:cite:`*Rep.* 528 B, D <plato:rep:528.b.d>`), when reconsidering his classification of astronomy as next to (plane) geometry: "although the science dealing with the additional dimension of depth is next in order, yet, owing to the fact that it is studied absurdly, I passed it over and put next to geometry astronomy, the motion of (bodies having) depth." In :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>` (:cite:`*Topics* VI. 5, 142 b 24 <aristotle:topics:142.b.24>`) we find "the definition of body, that which has three dimensions (:term:`διαστάσεις <dimensions>`)"; elsewhere he speaks of it as "that which has all the dimensions" (:cite:`*De caelo* I. 1, 268 b 6 <aristotle:de-caelo:268.b.6>`), "that which has dimension every way" (τὸ πάντῃ διάστασιν ἔχον, :cite:`*Metaph.* 1066 b 32 <aristotle:met:1066.b.32>`) etc. In the :cite:`*Physics* <aristotle:phys>`
