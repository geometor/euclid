Successive applications of this result show that, if the angles <span data-ref="0268.g1:< N A D )">$NAD$</span>, <span data-ref="0268.g1:< D A P )">$DAP$</span> each contain a certain angle a certain number of times, the dihedral angles <span data-ref="0268.g1:< N A M D )">$NAMD$</span>, <span data-ref="0268.g1:< D A M P )">$DAMP$</span> will contain the corresponding dihedral angle the same number of times respectively.

Hence, where the angles <span data-ref="0268.g1:< N A D )">$NAD$</span>, <span data-ref="0268.g1:< D A P )">$DAP$</span> are commensurable, the dihedral angles corresponding to them are in the same ratio.

Legendre then extends the proof to the case where the plane angles are incommensurable by reference to an exactly similar extension in his proposition corresponding to Euclid VI. 1, for which see the note on that proposition.

Modern text-books make the extension by an appeal to *limits*.

### DEFINITION 7.

Ἐπίπεδον πρὸς ἐπίπεδον ὁμοίως κεκλίσθαι λέγεται καὶ ἕτερον πρὸς ἕτερον, ὅταν αἱ εἰρημέναι τῶν κλίσεων γωνίαι ἴσαι ἀλλήλαις ὦσιν.

### DEFINITION 8.

Παράλληλα ἐπίπεδά ἐστι τὰ ἀσύμπτωτα.

Heron has the same definition of parallel planes (Def. 115). The Greek word which is translated "which do not meet" is ἀσύμπτωτα, the term which has been adopted for the asymptotes of a curve.

### DEFINITION 9.

Ὅμοια στερεὰ σχήματά ἐστι τὰ ὑπὸ ὁμοίων ἐπιπέδων περιεχόμενα ἴσων τὸ πλῆθος.

### DEFINITION 10.

Ἴσα δὲ καὶ ὅμοια στερεὰ σχήματά ἐστι τὰ ὑπὸ ὁμοίων ἐπιπέδων περιεχόμενα ἴσων τῷ πλήθει καὶ τῷ μεγέθει.

These definitions, the second of which practically only substitutes the words "equal and similar" for the word "similar" in the first, have been the mark of much criticism.

Simson holds that the equality of solid figures is a thing which ought to be *proved*, by the method of superposition, or otherwise, and hence that Def. 10 is not a definition but a *theorem* which ought not to have been placed among the definitions. Secondly, he gives an example to show that the definition or theorem is not universally true.
