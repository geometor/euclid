# Transcription Notes: Folio 0271 (Page 267)

## Summary & Folio Continuity
- **Page Context**: Page 267 of Volume III, Book XI Definitions commentary. Continues Heath's discussion of Definition 10 from folio 0270 (Cauchy's and Legendre's theorems on convex polyhedra, Heron's Def. 118 on similar solids).
- **Transitions**: Introduces Definition 11 (solid angle, στερεὰ γωνία) with its two ancient alternative formulations preserved in the Greek text. Heath begins commentary analyzing Heiberg's conjecture regarding pre-Euclidean sources, Plato's formulation in the *Timaeus*, and definitions by Apollonius and Heron. The sentence at the bottom breaks across to folio 0272 ("and from a scholium to / this definition...").

## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Dual Definition of Solid Angle**: Heath provides both traditional Greek definitions:
  1. The inclination of more than two lines meeting one another and not in the same surface (line-based).
  2. The inclination / containment under more than two plane angles not in the same plane meeting at one point (face-based).
- **Platonic Terminology**: Plato's term ξυνισταμένα (*Timaeus*, p. 55) is highlighted in connection with regular polyhedra.
- **Heron & Apollonius**: Apollonius's definition of an angle as a "bringing together" (συναγωγή) and Heron's generalized definition (Def. 22) allowing curved surfaces (κοινῶς).
