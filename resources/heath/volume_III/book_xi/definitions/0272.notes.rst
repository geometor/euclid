Transcription Notes: Folio 0272 (Page 268)
==========================================

Summary & Folio Continuity
--------------------------

- **Page Context**: Page 268 of Volume III, Book XI Definitions commentary. Continues Heath's commentary on Definition 11 from folio 0271, then introduces Definition 12 (Pyramid, Πυραμίς) and Definition 13 (Prism, Πρῖσμα) with their Greek texts and commentaries.
- **Transitions**: The text flows seamlessly from folio 0271 regarding ancient controversies on solid angles formed by curved surfaces (cones, sphere quadrants). Definition 13's discussion of Heron's wide definition breaks at the bottom into folio 0273 ("By this Heron must / apparently mean...").

Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
--------------------------------------------

- **Curved Solid Angles**: Scholium discussion on the vertex of a cone and the corner of an octant of a sphere ("fourth part of a sphere" in scholiast's phrasing).
- **Pyramid (Def. 12)**: Defined in Greek as a solid figure contained by planes set up from one plane to one point. Heath compares definitions across Legendre, Taylor, Smith & Bryant, Mehler, and Rausenberger.
- **Typographical Note**: The scan has a clear printer's flaw: "cal! it" instead of "call it" (an exclamation mark standing in for double-l or broken type). Transcribed verbatim as in original print.
- **Prism (Def. 13)**: Euclid's definition specifies opposite faces equal, similar, and parallel, with remaining faces parallelograms.
