# BOOK XI. PROPOSITIONS.

### PROPOSITION 1.

<p class="enunciation">A part of a straight line cannot be in the plane of reference<br />
and a part in a plane more elevated.</p>

For, if possible, let a part <span data-ref="0276.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> of the straight line <span data-ref="0276.g1:/ A B C /">$ABC$</span><br />
be in the plane of reference, and a part<br />
<span data-ref="0276.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> in a plane more elevated.

![Book XI, Proposition 1](0276.g1.svg)

There will then be in the plane of<br />
reference some straight line continuous<br />
with <span data-ref="0276.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> in a straight line.

Let it be <span data-ref="0276.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>;

<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0276.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is a common segment of the<br />
two straight lines <span data-ref="0276.g1:/ A B C /">$ABC$</span>, <span data-ref="0276.g1:/ A B D /">$ABD$</span>:</p>

<p class="assertion">which is impossible, inasmuch as, if we<br />
describe a circle with centre <span data-ref="0276.g1:B">$B$</span> and distance<br />
<span data-ref="0276.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, the diameters will cut off unequal circumferences of the<br />
circle.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore a part of a straight line cannot be in the plane<br />
of reference, and a part in a plane more elevated.</p>

<p class="qed"><span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

---

**1. the plane of reference,** τὸ ὑποκείμενον ἐπίπεδον, the plane laid down or assumed.  
**2. more elevated,** μετεωροτέρῳ.

unsatisfactory owing to the fact that Euclid is not able to make any use of his<br />
definition of a plane for the purpose of these proofs, and they really depend<br />
