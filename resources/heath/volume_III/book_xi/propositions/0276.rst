BOOK XI. PROPOSITIONS.
======================

PROPOSITION 1.
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

.. enunciation::

   A part of a straight line cannot be in the plane of reference and a part in a plane more elevated.

| For, if possible, let a part :g:`$AB$ <0276.g1:/ A B />` of the straight line :g:`$ABC$ <0276.g1:/ A B C />`
| be in the plane of reference, and a part
| :g:`$BC$ <0276.g1:/ B C />` in a plane more elevated.

.. picture:: 0276.g1.svg
   :alt: Book XI, Proposition 1

| There will then be in the plane of
| reference some straight line continuous
| with :g:`$AB$ <0276.g1:/ A B />` in a straight line.

Let it be :g:`$BD$ <0276.g1:/ B D />`;

.. assertion::

   | therefore :g:`$AB$ <0276.g1:/ A B />` is a common segment of the
   | two straight lines :g:`$ABC$ <0276.g1:/ A B C />`, :g:`$ABD$ <0276.g1:/ A B D />`:

.. assertion::

   | which is impossible, inasmuch as, if we
   | describe a circle with centre :g:`$B$ <0276.g1:B>` and distance
   | :g:`$AB$ <0276.g1:/ A B />`, the diameters will cut off unequal circumferences of the
   | circle.

.. assertion::

   | Therefore a part of a straight line cannot be in the plane
   | of reference, and a part in a plane more elevated.

.. qed::

   :just:`Q. E. D.`

----

| **1. the plane of reference,** τὸ ὑποκείμενον ἐπίπεδον, the plane laid down or assumed.
| **2. more elevated,** μετεωροτέρῳ.

| unsatisfactory owing to the fact that Euclid is not able to make any use of his
| definition of a plane for the purpose of these proofs, and they really depend
