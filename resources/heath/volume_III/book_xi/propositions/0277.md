A curious point in Euclid's proof is the reason given why two straight lines<br />
cannot have a common segment. The argument is precisely that of the<br />
"proof" of the same thing given by Proclus on I. 1 (see note on Book I.<br />
Post. 2, Vol. I. p. 197) and is of course inconclusive. The fact that two<br />
straight lines cannot have a common segment must be taken to be involved<br />
in the definition of, and the postulates relating to, the straight line; and the<br />
"proof" given here can hardly, I should say, be Euclid's, though the interpo-<br />
lation, if it be such, must have been made very early.
