| "And, because the points :g:`$B$ <0276.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0276.g1:C>` are in this plane, the straight line :g:`$BC$ <0276.g1:/ B C />` is
| in it. :just:`[Simson's def.]`

.. assertion::

   | Therefore there are two straight lines :g:`$ABC$ <0276.g1:/ A B C />`, :g:`$ABD$ <0276.g1:/ A B D />` in the same plane that
   | have a common segment :g:`$AB$ <0276.g1:/ A B />`:

.. assertion::

   which is impossible."

| :person:`Simson <simson>`, of course, justifies the last inference by reference to his Corollary
| to I. 11, which, however, as we have seen, is not a valid proof of the assump-
| tion, which is really implied in I. Post. 2.
| An alternative reading, perhaps due to :person:`Theon <theon-of-alexandria>`, says, after the words
| "which is impossible" in the Greek text, "for a straight line does not meet a
| straight line in more points than one; otherwise the straight lines will
| coincide." :person:`Simson <simson>` (who however does not seem to have had the second
| clause beginning "otherwise" in the text which he used) attacks this alterna-
| tive reading in a rather confused note chiefly directed against a criticism by
| :person:`Thomas Simpson <thomas-simpson>`, without (as it seems to me) sufficient reason. It contains
| surely a legitimate argument. The supposed straight lines :g:`$ABC$ <0276.g1:/ A B C />`, :g:`$ABD$ <0276.g1:/ A B D />` meet
| in more than two points, namely in all the points between :g:`$A$ <0276.g1:A>` and :g:`$B$ <0276.g1:B>`. But two
| straight lines cannot have two points common without coinciding altogether;
| therefore :g:`$ABC$ <0276.g1:/ A B C />` must coincide with :g:`$ABD$ <0276.g1:/ A B D />`.

PROPOSITION 2.
--------------

.. enunciation::

   If two straight lines cut one another, they are in one plane, and every triangle is in one plane.

| For let the two straight lines :g:`$AB$ <0278.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$CD$ <0278.g1:/ C D />` cut one another at
| the point :g:`$E$ <0278.g1:E>`;
| I say that :g:`$AB$ <0278.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$CD$ <0278.g1:/ C D />` are in one plane,
| and every triangle is in one plane.

.. picture:: 0278.g1.svg
   :alt: Book XI, Proposition 2

| For let points :g:`$F$ <0278.g1:F>`, :g:`$G$ <0278.g1:G>` be taken at
| random on :g:`$EC$ <0278.g1:/ E C />`, :g:`$EB$ <0278.g1:/ E B />`,
| let :g:`$CB$ <0278.g1:/ C B />`, :g:`$FG$ <0278.g1:/ F G />` be joined,
| and let :g:`$FH$ <0278.g1:/ F H />`, :g:`$GK$ <0278.g1:/ G K />` be drawn across;
| I say first that the triangle :g:`$ECB$ <0278.g1:< E C B >>` is
| in one plane.

.. subclause::

   | For, if part of the triangle :g:`$ECB$ <0278.g1:< E C B >>`,
   | either :g:`$FHC$ <0278.g1:< F H C >>` or :g:`$GBK$ <0278.g1:< G B K >>`, is in the plane of reference, and the rest
   | in another,

.. assertion::

   | a part also of one of the straight lines :g:`$EC$ <0278.g1:/ E C />`, :g:`$EB$ <0278.g1:/ E B />` will be in the
   | plane of reference, and a part in another.

.. subclause::

   | But, if the part :g:`$FCBG$ <0278.g1:< F C B G >>` of the triangle :g:`$ECB$ <0278.g1:< E C B >>` be in the
   | plane of reference, and the rest in another,

.. assertion::

   | a part also of both the straight lines :g:`$EC$ <0278.g1:/ E C />`, :g:`$EB$ <0278.g1:/ E B />` will be in the
   | plane of reference and a part in another:

.. assertion:: [XI. 1] <XI.1>

   which was proved absurd.
