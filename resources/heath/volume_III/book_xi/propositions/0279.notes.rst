Summary & Folio Continuity
--------------------------

- **Folio Range**: Folio 0279 represents page 275 of Volume III, Book XI.
- **Preceding Context**: Folio 0278 contained the enunciation, setting-out, construction, and initial demonstration of Proposition 2.
- **Current Content**:

  - Concluding four lines of Euclid's proof of Proposition 2, establishing that intersecting straight lines $AB, CD$ are in one plane and every triangle is in one plane, terminated by Q. E. D.
  - Heath's critical commentary examining the insufficiency of Euclid's proof. Euclid only considers straight line boundaries and fails to preclude non-planar surfaces or curved boundaries.
  - Robert Simson's revised enunciation: "Two straight lines which intersect are in one plane, and three straight lines which intersect two and two are in one plane."
  - Smith and Bryant's alternative figure with lines $PQ, RS, XY$ meeting at points $A, B, C$.
  - Legendre's rotation-of-plane demonstration.
  - H. M. Taylor's uniqueness demonstration establishing that two distinct planes through three non-collinear points cannot exist.

Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
--------------------------------------------

- **Critical Commentary**: Heath pulls no punches regarding Euclid's proof: "It must be admitted that the 'proof' of this proposition is not of any value." This is one of the rare propositions where modern rigor exposes a severe structural gap in Euclid's foundation of solid geometry.
- **Plane Rotation**: Simson and Legendre use mechanical motion (revolving a plane about an axis) to establish existence.
- **Uniqueness Proof**: Taylor's proof uses the property that any straight line in a plane containing a triangle must intersect at least two sides (or sides produced).
