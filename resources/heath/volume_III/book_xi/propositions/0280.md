It follows from the above that

*A plane is determined (i.e. uniquely determined) by any of the following data:*
(1) *by three straight lines meeting one another two and two,*
(2) *by three points not in a straight line,*
(3) *by two straight lines meeting one another,*
(4) *by a straight line and a point without it.*

### PROPOSITION 3.

*If two planes cut one another, their common section is a straight line.*

For let the two planes <span data-ref="0280.g1:< A B >">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0280.g1:< B C >">$BC$</span> cut one another, and let the line <span data-ref="0280.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> be their common section;
I say that the line <span data-ref="0280.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> is a straight line.

![Book XI, Proposition 3](0280.g1.svg)

For, if not, from <span data-ref="0280.g1:D">$D$</span> to <span data-ref="0280.g1:B">$B$</span> let the straight line <span data-ref="0280.g1:/ D E B /">$DEB$</span> be joined in the plane <span data-ref="0280.g1:< A B >">$AB$</span>, and in the plane <span data-ref="0280.g1:< B C >">$BC$</span> the straight line <span data-ref="0280.g1:/ D F B /">$DFB$</span>.

Then the two straight lines <span data-ref="0280.g1:/ D E B /">$DEB$</span>, <span data-ref="0280.g1:/ D F B /">$DFB$</span> will have the same extremities, and will clearly enclose an area: which is absurd.

Therefore <span data-ref="0280.g1:/ D E B /">$DEB$</span>, <span data-ref="0280.g1:/ D F B /">$DFB$</span> are not straight lines.

Similarly we can prove that neither will there be any other straight line joined from <span data-ref="0280.g1:D">$D$</span> to <span data-ref="0280.g1:B">$B$</span> except <span data-ref="0280.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> the common section of the planes <span data-ref="0280.g1:< A B >">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0280.g1:< B C >">$BC$</span>.

Therefore etc.

                                                                        Q. E. D.

I think Simson is right in objecting to the words after "which is absurd," to the effect that <span data-ref="0280.g1:/ D E B /">$DEB$</span>, <span data-ref="0280.g1:/ D F B /">$DFB$</span> are not straight lines, and that neither can there be any other straight line joined from <span data-ref="0280.g1:D">$D$</span> to <span data-ref="0280.g1:B">$B$</span> except <span data-ref="0280.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span>, as being unnecessary.  It is right to conclude at once from the absurdity that <span data-ref="0280.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> *cannot but be a straight line*.

Legendre makes his proof depend on Prop. 2.  "For, if, among the points common to the two planes, three should be found which are not in a straight line, the two planes in question, each passing through three points, would only amount to one and the same plane."  [This of course assumes that three points determine one and *only* one plane, which, strictly speaking, involves more than Prop. 2 itself, as shown in the last note.]
