It follows from the above that

*A plane is determined (i.e. uniquely determined) by any of the following data:*<br />
(1) *by three straight lines meeting one another two and two,*<br />
(2) *by three points not in a straight line,*<br />
(3) *by two straight lines meeting one another,*<br />
(4) *by a straight line and a point without it.*

### PROPOSITION 3.

<p class="enunciation">If two planes cut one another, their common section is a<br />
straight line.</p>

For let the two planes <span data-ref="0280.g1:< A B >">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0280.g1:< B C >">$BC$</span> cut one another,<br />
and let the line <span data-ref="0280.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> be their common<br />
section;<br />
I say that the line <span data-ref="0280.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> is a straight line.

![Book XI, Proposition 3](0280.g1.svg)

For, if not, from <span data-ref="0280.g1:D">$D$</span> to <span data-ref="0280.g1:B">$B$</span> let the straight<br />
line <span data-ref="0280.g1:/ D E B /">$DEB$</span> be joined in the plane <span data-ref="0280.g1:< A B >">$AB$</span>, and<br />
in the plane <span data-ref="0280.g1:< B C >">$BC$</span> the straight line <span data-ref="0280.g1:/ D F B /">$DFB$</span>.

<p class="assertion">Then the two straight lines <span data-ref="0280.g1:/ D E B /">$DEB$</span>, <span data-ref="0280.g1:/ D F B /">$DFB$</span><br />
will have the same extremities, and will<br />
clearly enclose an area:</p>
<p class="assertion">which is absurd.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore <span data-ref="0280.g1:/ D E B /">$DEB$</span>, <span data-ref="0280.g1:/ D F B /">$DFB$</span> are not straight lines.</p>

<p class="assertion">Similarly we can prove that neither will there be any<br />
other straight line joined from <span data-ref="0280.g1:D">$D$</span> to <span data-ref="0280.g1:B">$B$</span> except <span data-ref="0280.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> the common<br />
section of the planes <span data-ref="0280.g1:< A B >">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0280.g1:< B C >">$BC$</span>.</p>

<p class="qed">Therefore etc. <span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

I think Simson is right in objecting to the words after "which is absurd,"<br />
to the effect that <span data-ref="0280.g1:/ D E B /">$DEB$</span>, <span data-ref="0280.g1:/ D F B /">$DFB$</span> are not straight lines, and that neither can there<br />
be any other straight line joined from <span data-ref="0280.g1:D">$D$</span> to <span data-ref="0280.g1:B">$B$</span> except <span data-ref="0280.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span>, as being unneces-<br />
sary. It is right to conclude at once from the absurdity that <span data-ref="0280.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> *cannot but*<br />
*be a straight line*.

Legendre makes his proof depend on Prop. 2. "For, if, among the points<br />
common to the two planes, three should be found which are not in a straight<br />
line, the two planes in question, each passing through three points, would only<br />
amount to one and the same plane." [This of course assumes that three<br />
points determine one and *only* one plane, which, strictly speaking, involves<br />
more than Prop. 2 itself, as shown in the last note.]
