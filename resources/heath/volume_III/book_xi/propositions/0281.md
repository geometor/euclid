*If two planes meet in a point, they meet in a straight line.*

Let <span data-ref="0281.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span>, <span data-ref="0281.g1:< A D E >">$ADE$</span> be two given planes meeting<br />
at <span data-ref="0281.g1:A">$A$</span>.

Take any points <span data-ref="0281.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0281.g1:C">$C$</span> lying on the plane <span data-ref="0281.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span>,<br />
and not on the plane <span data-ref="0281.g1:< A D E >">$ADE$</span> but on the same side<br />
of it.

Join <span data-ref="0281.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0281.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, and produce <span data-ref="0281.g1:[ B A ]">$BA$</span> to <span data-ref="0281.g1:F">$F$</span>.

Join <span data-ref="0281.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span>.

<p class="sub-clause">Then, since <span data-ref="0281.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0281.g1:F">$F$</span> are on opposite sides of the<br />
plane <span data-ref="0281.g1:< A D E >">$ADE$</span>,<br />
<span data-ref="0281.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0281.g1:F">$F$</span> are also on opposite sides of it.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore <span data-ref="0281.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span> must meet the plane <span data-ref="0281.g1:< A D E >">$ADE$</span> in<br />
some point, say <span data-ref="0281.g1:G">$G$</span>.</p>

<p class="assertion">Then, since <span data-ref="0281.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0281.g1:G">$G$</span> are both in each of the planes <span data-ref="0281.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span>, <span data-ref="0281.g1:< A D E >">$ADE$</span>, the straight<br />
line <span data-ref="0281.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span> is in both planes. <span class="justification">[Simson's def.]</span></p>

![Book XI, Proposition 3, Commentary Diagram 1](0281.g1.svg)

This is also the place to insert the proposition that, *If three planes intersect<br />
two and two, their lines of intersection either meet in a point or are parallel two<br />
and two.*

Let there be three planes intersecting in the straight lines <span data-ref="0281.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0281.g2:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, <span data-ref="0281.g2:/ E F /">$EF$</span>.

![Book XI, Proposition 3, Commentary Diagram 2](0281.g2.svg)

Now <span data-ref="0281.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0281.g2:/ E F /">$EF$</span> are in a plane; therefore they either meet in a point or are<br />
parallel.

<p class="sub-clause">(1) Let them meet in <span data-ref="0281.g2:O">$O$</span>.</p>

Then <span data-ref="0281.g2:O">$O$</span>, being a point in <span data-ref="0281.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, lies in the plane <span data-ref="0281.g2:< A D >">$AD$</span>, and, being also a<br />
point in <span data-ref="0281.g2:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, lies also in the plane <span data-ref="0281.g2:< E D >">$ED$</span>.

<p class="assertion">Therefore <span data-ref="0281.g2:O">$O$</span>, being common to the planes <span data-ref="0281.g2:< A D >">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0281.g2:< D E >">$DE$</span>, must lie on <span data-ref="0281.g2:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, the<br />
line of their intersection;<br />
i.e. <span data-ref="0281.g2:[ C D ]">$CD$</span>, if produced, passes through <span data-ref="0281.g2:O">$O$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">(2) Let <span data-ref="0281.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0281.g2:/ E F /">$EF$</span> not meet, but let them be parallel.</p>

Then <span data-ref="0281.g2:/ C D /">$CD$</span> cannot meet <span data-ref="0281.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span>; for, if it did, it must necessarily meet <span data-ref="0281.g2:/ E F /">$EF$</span>,<br />
by the first case.

<p class="assertion">Therefore <span data-ref="0281.g2:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, <span data-ref="0281.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, being in one plane, are parallel.</p>

<p class="assertion">Similarly <span data-ref="0281.g2:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, <span data-ref="0281.g2:/ E F /">$EF$</span> are parallel.</p>

### PROPOSITION 4.

<p class="enunciation">If a straight line be set up at right angles to two straight<br />
lines which cut one another, at their common point of section,<br />
it will also be at right angles to the plane through them.</p>
