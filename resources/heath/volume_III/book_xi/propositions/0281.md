*If two planes meet in a point, they meet in a straight line.*

Let <span data-ref="0281.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span>, <span data-ref="0281.g1:< A D E >">$ADE$</span> be two given planes meeting at <span data-ref="0281.g1:A">$A$</span>.

Take any points <span data-ref="0281.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0281.g1:C">$C$</span> lying on the plane <span data-ref="0281.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span>, and not on the plane <span data-ref="0281.g1:< A D E >">$ADE$</span> but on the same side of it.

Join <span data-ref="0281.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0281.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, and produce <span data-ref="0281.g1:[ B A ]">$BA$</span> to <span data-ref="0281.g1:F">$F$</span>.

Join <span data-ref="0281.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span>.

Then, since <span data-ref="0281.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0281.g1:F">$F$</span> are on opposite sides of the plane <span data-ref="0281.g1:< A D E >">$ADE$</span>, <span data-ref="0281.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0281.g1:F">$F$</span> are also on opposite sides of it.

Therefore <span data-ref="0281.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span> must meet the plane <span data-ref="0281.g1:< A D E >">$ADE$</span> in some point, say <span data-ref="0281.g1:G">$G$</span>.

Then, since <span data-ref="0281.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0281.g1:G">$G$</span> are both in each of the planes <span data-ref="0281.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span>, <span data-ref="0281.g1:< A D E >">$ADE$</span>, the straight line <span data-ref="0281.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span> is in both planes. This is also the place to insert the proposition that, *If three planes intersect two and two, their lines of intersection either meet in a point or are parallel two and two.*

Let there be three planes intersecting in the straight lines <span data-ref="0281.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0281.g2:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, <span data-ref="0281.g2:/ E F /">$EF$</span>.

![Book XI, Proposition 3, Commentary Diagram 2](0281.g2.svg)

Now <span data-ref="0281.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0281.g2:/ E F /">$EF$</span> are in a plane; therefore they either meet in a point or are parallel.

(1) Let them meet in <span data-ref="0281.g2:O">$O$</span>.

Then <span data-ref="0281.g2:O">$O$</span>, being a point in <span data-ref="0281.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, lies in the plane <span data-ref="0281.g2:< A D >">$AD$</span>, and, being also a point in <span data-ref="0281.g2:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, lies also in the plane <span data-ref="0281.g2:< E D >">$ED$</span>.

Therefore <span data-ref="0281.g2:O">$O$</span>, being common to the planes <span data-ref="0281.g2:< A D >">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0281.g2:< D E >">$DE$</span>, must lie on <span data-ref="0281.g2:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, the line of their intersection; i.e. <span data-ref="0281.g2:[ C D ]">$CD$</span>, if produced, passes through <span data-ref="0281.g2:O">$O$</span>.

(2) Let <span data-ref="0281.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0281.g2:/ E F /">$EF$</span> not meet, but let them be parallel.

Then <span data-ref="0281.g2:/ C D /">$CD$</span> cannot meet <span data-ref="0281.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span>; for, if it did, it must necessarily meet <span data-ref="0281.g2:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, by the first case.

Therefore <span data-ref="0281.g2:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, <span data-ref="0281.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, being in one plane, are parallel.

Similarly <span data-ref="0281.g2:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, <span data-ref="0281.g2:/ E F /">$EF$</span> are parallel.

### PROPOSITION 4.

*If a straight line be set up at right angles to two straight lines which cut one another, at their common point of section, it will also be at right angles to the plane through them.*
