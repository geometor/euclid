(7) triangles <span data-ref="0282.g1:< F E G >">$FEG$</span>, <span data-ref="0282.g1:< F E H >">$FEH$</span> equal in all respects, <span class="justification">[by (2), (6) and I. 8]</span><br />
so that the angles <span data-ref="0282.g1:< F E G )">$FEG$</span>, <span data-ref="0282.g1:< F E H )">$FEH$</span> are equal,<br />
and therefore <span data-ref="0282.g1:/ F E /">$FE$</span> is at right angles to <span data-ref="0282.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span>.

In consequence of the length of the above proof others have been<br />
suggested, and the proof which now finds most general acceptance is that of<br />
Cauchy, which is as follows.

Let <span data-ref="0284.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> be perpendicular to two straight lines <span data-ref="0284.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, <span data-ref="0284.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> in the plane <span data-ref="0284.g1:< M N >">$MN$</span><br />
at their point of intersection <span data-ref="0284.g1:B">$B$</span>.<br />
In the plane <span data-ref="0284.g1:< M N >">$MN$</span> draw <span data-ref="0284.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span>, any straight line<br />
through <span data-ref="0284.g1:B">$B$</span>.<br />
Join <span data-ref="0284.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, and let <span data-ref="0284.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> meet <span data-ref="0284.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span> in <span data-ref="0284.g1:E">$E$</span>.<br />
Produce <span data-ref="0284.g1:[ A B ]">$AB$</span> to <span data-ref="0284.g1:F">$F$</span> so that <span data-ref="0284.g1:/ B F /">$BF$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0284.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>.<br />
Join <span data-ref="0284.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0284.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span>, <span data-ref="0284.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0284.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span>, <span data-ref="0284.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, <span data-ref="0284.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span>.

![Book XI, Proposition 4, Cauchy's Proof](0284.g1.svg)

Since <span data-ref="0284.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is perpendicular to <span data-ref="0284.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span> at its<br />
middle point <span data-ref="0284.g1:B">$B$</span>,
<p class="assertion"><span data-ref="0284.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0284.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span>.</p>

<p class="assertion">Similarly <span data-ref="0284.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0284.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">Since in the triangles <span data-ref="0284.g1:< A C D >">$ACD$</span>, <span data-ref="0284.g1:< F C D >">$FCD$</span> the two<br />
sides <span data-ref="0284.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0284.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> are respectively equal to the two<br />
sides <span data-ref="0284.g1:/ F C /">$FC$</span>, <span data-ref="0284.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, and the third sides <span data-ref="0284.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0284.g1:/ F D /">$FD$</span> are<br />
also equal,</p>
<p class="assertion">the angles <span data-ref="0284.g1:< A C D )">$ACD$</span>, <span data-ref="0284.g1:< F C D )">$FCD$</span> are equal. <span class="justification">[I. 8]</span></p>

The triangles <span data-ref="0284.g1:< A C E >">$ACE$</span>, <span data-ref="0284.g1:< F C E >">$FCE$</span> thus have two sides and the included angle<br />
equal, whence
<p class="assertion"><span data-ref="0284.g1:/ E A /">$EA$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0284.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>. <span class="justification">[I. 4]</span></p>

The triangles <span data-ref="0284.g1:< A B E >">$ABE$</span>, <span data-ref="0284.g1:< F B E >">$FBE$</span> have now all their sides equal respectively;<br />
<p class="assertion">therefore the angles <span data-ref="0284.g1:< A B E )">$ABE$</span>, <span data-ref="0284.g1:< F B E )">$FBE$</span> are equal, <span class="justification">[I. 8]</span><br />
and <span data-ref="0284.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is perpendicular to <span data-ref="0284.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span>.</p>

And <span data-ref="0284.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span> is in any straight line through <span data-ref="0284.g1:B">$B$</span> in the plane <span data-ref="0284.g1:< M N >">$MN$</span>.

Legendre's proof is not so easy, but it is interesting.  We are first required<br />
to draw through any point <span data-ref="0284.g2:E">$E$</span> within the angle<br />
<span data-ref="0284.g2:< C B D )">$CBD$</span> a straight line <span data-ref="0284.g2:/ C D /">$CD$</span> bisected at <span data-ref="0284.g2:E">$E$</span>.<br />
To do this we draw <span data-ref="0284.g2:/ E K /">$EK$</span> parallel to <span data-ref="0284.g2:/ D B /">$DB$</span><br />
meeting <span data-ref="0284.g2:/ B C /">$BC$</span> in <span data-ref="0284.g2:K">$K$</span>, and then mark off <span data-ref="0284.g2:/ K C /">$KC$</span> equal<br />
to <span data-ref="0284.g2:/ B K /">$BK$</span>.<br />
<span data-ref="0284.g2:/ C E /">$CE$</span> is then joined and produced to <span data-ref="0284.g2:D">$D$</span>; and<br />
<span data-ref="0284.g2:/ C D /">$CD$</span> is the straight line required.
