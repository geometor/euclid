And it can readily be proved that,
*If from a point without a plane oblique straight lines be drawn to the plane,*
*(1) those meeting the plane at equal distances from the foot of the perpendicular are equal, and*
*(2) of two straight lines meeting the plane at unequal distances from the foot of the perpendicular, the more remote is the greater.*

Lastly, it is easily seen that
*From a point outside a plane only one perpendicular can be drawn to that plane.*

For, if possible, let there be two perpendiculars.  Then a plane can be drawn through them, and this will cut the original plane in a straight line.

This straight line and the two perpendiculars will form a plane triangle which has two right angles: which is impossible.

### PROPOSITION 5.

*If a straight line be set up at right angles to three straight lines which meet one another, at their common point of section, the three straight lines are in one plane.*

For let a straight line <span data-ref="0285.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> be set up at right angles to the three straight lines <span data-ref="0285.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, <span data-ref="0285.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>, <span data-ref="0285.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span>, at their point of meeting at <span data-ref="0285.g1:B">$B$</span>;
I say that <span data-ref="0285.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, <span data-ref="0285.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>, <span data-ref="0285.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span> are in one plane.

![Book XI, Proposition 5](0285.g1.svg)

For suppose they are not, but, if possible, let <span data-ref="0285.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>, <span data-ref="0285.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span> be in the plane of reference and <span data-ref="0285.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> in one more elevated;
let the plane through <span data-ref="0285.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0285.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> be produced;
it will thus make, as common section in the plane of reference, a straight line.        [XI. 3]

Let it make <span data-ref="0285.g1:/ B F /">$BF$</span>.

Therefore the three straight lines <span data-ref="0285.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0285.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, <span data-ref="0285.g1:/ B F /">$BF$</span> are in one plane, namely that drawn through <span data-ref="0285.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0285.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>.

Now, since <span data-ref="0285.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is at right angles to each of the straight lines <span data-ref="0285.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>, <span data-ref="0285.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span>,
therefore <span data-ref="0285.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is also at right angles to the plane through <span data-ref="0285.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>, <span data-ref="0285.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span>.        [XI. 4]
