to <span data-ref="0284.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span>.</p>

It follows of course from this proposition that the perpendicular <span data-ref="0284.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is the<br />
*shortest distance from <span data-ref="0284.g1:A">$A$</span> to the plane <span data-ref="0284.g1:< M N >">$MN$</span>*.

And it can readily be proved that,<br />
*If from a point without a plane oblique straight lines be drawn to the plane,*<br />
*(1) those meeting the plane at equal distances from the foot of the perpendicular<br />
are equal, and*<br />
*(2) of two straight lines meeting the plane at unequal distances from the foot of<br />
the perpendicular, the more remote is the greater.*

Lastly, it is easily seen that<br />
*From a point outside a plane only one perpendicular can be drawn to that<br />
plane.*

For, if possible, let there be two perpendiculars.  Then a plane can be<br />
drawn through them, and this will cut the original plane in a straight line.

This straight line and the two perpendiculars will form a plane triangle<br />
which has two right angles: which is impossible.

### PROPOSITION 5.

<p class="enunciation">If a straight line be set up at right angles to three straight<br />
lines which meet one another, at their common point of section,<br />
the three straight lines are in one plane.</p>

For let a straight line <span data-ref="0285.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> be set up at right angles to the<br />
three straight lines <span data-ref="0285.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, <span data-ref="0285.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>, <span data-ref="0285.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span>, at<br />
their point of meeting at <span data-ref="0285.g1:B">$B$</span>;<br />
I say that <span data-ref="0285.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, <span data-ref="0285.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>, <span data-ref="0285.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span> are in one plane.

![Book XI, Proposition 5](0285.g1.svg)

<p class="sub-clause">For suppose they are not, but, if<br />
possible, let <span data-ref="0285.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>, <span data-ref="0285.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span> be in the plane of<br />
reference and <span data-ref="0285.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> in one more elevated;<br />
let the plane through <span data-ref="0285.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0285.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> be<br />
produced;<br />
it will thus make, as common section in the plane of reference,<br />
a straight line. <span class="justification">[XI. 3]</span></p>

Let it make <span data-ref="0285.g1:/ B F /">$BF$</span>.

<p class="assertion">Therefore the three straight lines <span data-ref="0285.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0285.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, <span data-ref="0285.g1:/ B F /">$BF$</span> are in one<br />
plane, namely that drawn through <span data-ref="0285.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0285.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">Now, since <span data-ref="0285.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is at right angles to each of the straight<br />
lines <span data-ref="0285.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>, <span data-ref="0285.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0285.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is also at right angles to the plane through<br />
<span data-ref="0285.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>, <span data-ref="0285.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span>. <span class="justification">[XI. 4]</span></p>
