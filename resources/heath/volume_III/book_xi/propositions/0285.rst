.. assertion::

   | whence :just:`[I. 48] <I.48>` the angle :g:`$ABE$ <0284.g1:< A B E )>` is a right angle, and :g:`$AB$ <0284.g1:/ A B />` is perpendicular
   | to :g:`$BE$ <0284.g1:/ B E />`.

| It follows of course from this proposition that the perpendicular :g:`$AB$ <0284.g1:/ A B />` is the
| *shortest distance from* :g:`$A$ <0284.g1:A>` *to the plane* :g:`$MN$ <0284.g1:< M N >>`.

| And it can readily be proved that,
| *If from a point without a plane oblique straight lines be drawn to the plane,*
| *(1) those meeting the plane at equal distances from the foot of the perpendicular
| are equal, and*
| *(2) of two straight lines meeting the plane at unequal distances from the foot of
| the perpendicular, the more remote is the greater.*

| Lastly, it is easily seen that
| *From a point outside a plane only one perpendicular can be drawn to that
| plane.*

| For, if possible, let there be two perpendiculars.  Then a plane can be
| drawn through them, and this will cut the original plane in a straight line.

| This straight line and the two perpendiculars will form a plane triangle
| which has two right angles: which is impossible.

PROPOSITION 5.
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

.. enunciation::

   If a straight line be set up at right angles to three straight lines which meet one another, at their common point of section, the three straight lines are in one plane.

| For let a straight line :g:`$AB$ <0285.g1:/ A B />` be set up at right angles to the
| three straight lines :g:`$BC$ <0285.g1:/ B C />`, :g:`$BD$ <0285.g1:/ B D />`, :g:`$BE$ <0285.g1:/ B E />`, at
| their point of meeting at :g:`$B$ <0285.g1:B>`;
| I say that :g:`$BC$ <0285.g1:/ B C />`, :g:`$BD$ <0285.g1:/ B D />`, :g:`$BE$ <0285.g1:/ B E />` are in one plane.

.. picture:: 0285.g1.svg
   :alt: Book XI, Proposition 5

.. subclause::

   | For suppose they are not, but, if
   | possible, let :g:`$BD$ <0285.g1:/ B D />`, :g:`$BE$ <0285.g1:/ B E />` be in the plane of
   | reference and :g:`$BC$ <0285.g1:/ B C />` in one more elevated;
   | let the plane through :g:`$AB$ <0285.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$BC$ <0285.g1:/ B C />` be
   | produced;
   | it will thus make, as common section in the plane of reference,
   | a straight line. :just:`[XI. 3] <XI.3>`

Let it make :g:`$BF$ <0285.g1:/ B F />`.

.. assertion::

   | Therefore the three straight lines :g:`$AB$ <0285.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$BC$ <0285.g1:/ B C />`, :g:`$BF$ <0285.g1:/ B F />` are in one
   | plane, namely that drawn through :g:`$AB$ <0285.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$BC$ <0285.g1:/ B C />`.

.. subclause::

   | Now, since :g:`$AB$ <0285.g1:/ A B />` is at right angles to each of the straight
   | lines :g:`$BD$ <0285.g1:/ B D />`, :g:`$BE$ <0285.g1:/ B E />`,

.. assertion::

   | therefore :g:`$AB$ <0285.g1:/ A B />` is also at right angles to the plane through
   | :g:`$BD$ <0285.g1:/ B D />`, :g:`$BE$ <0285.g1:/ B E />`. :just:`[XI. 4] <XI.4>`
