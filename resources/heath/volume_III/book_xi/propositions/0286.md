<p class="sub-clause">But the plane through <span data-ref="0285.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>, <span data-ref="0285.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span> is the plane of reference;</p>

<p class="assertion">Thus <span data-ref="0285.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> will also make right angles with all the straight<br />

<p class="sub-clause">But <span data-ref="0285.g1:/ B F /">$BF$</span> which is in the plane of reference meets it;</p>

<p class="sub-clause">But, by hypothesis, the angle <span data-ref="0285.g1:< A B C )">$ABC$</span> is also right;</p>

<p class="sub-clause">And they are in one plane:</p>
<p class="assertion">which is impossible.</p>
<p class="assertion">Therefore the straight line <span data-ref="0285.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is not in a more elevated<br />

It follows that, *if a right angle be turned about one of the straight lines<br />
containing it the other will describe a plane.*

At any point in a straight line it is possible to draw *only one plane which<br />
is at right angles to the straight line.*

One such plane can be found by taking any two planes through the given<br />
straight line, drawing perpendiculars to the straight<br />
line in the respective planes, e.g. <span data-ref="0286.g1:/ B O /">$BO$</span>, <span data-ref="0286.g1:/ C O /">$CO$</span> in the<br />
planes <span data-ref="0286.g1:< A O B >">$AOB$</span>, <span data-ref="0286.g1:< A O C >">$AOC$</span>, each perpendicular to <span data-ref="0286.g1:/ A O /">$AO$</span>,<br />
and then drawing a plane (<span data-ref="0286.g1:< B O C >">$BOC$</span>) through the<br />
perpendiculars.
