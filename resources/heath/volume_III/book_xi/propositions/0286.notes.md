# Transcription Notes: Folio 0286 (Page 282)

## Summary & Folio Continuity
- **Page & Book**: Volume III, Book XI, Page 282.
- **Top of Page**: Continuation and conclusion of Euclid's proof of Proposition 5 (that if a straight line is set up at right angles to three straight lines at their meeting point, the three straight lines are in one plane). Concludes with Q. E. D.
- **Middle & Bottom of Page**: Heath's mathematical commentary on Proposition 5.
  - Deduces two immediate corollaries:
    1. If a right angle is turned about one of its containing lines, the other describes a plane.
    2. At any point in a straight line, *only one plane* can be drawn perpendicular to that line (demonstrated with supporting diagram).
    3. Through an external point, *one plane and only one* can be drawn perpendicular to a straight line (construction with supporting diagram).
- **Continuity**: The text at the bottom breaks off mid-sentence at "either", which continues onto folio 0287 (page 283) with cases "(1) it would intersect $AB$ at $O$ but not pass through $OQ$, or / (2) it would intersect $AB$ at a point different from $O$."

## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Euclidean Text**: Relies on [XI. Def. 3] to establish that $AB$ makes right angles with all lines meeting it in the plane of reference, leading to the contradiction of two distinct right angles $\angle ABF = \angle ABC$ in one plane.
- **Heath Commentary**: Provides synthetic uniqueness proofs for planes perpendicular to a given straight line at a point on the line and from an external point.
