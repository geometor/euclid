.. subclause::

   But the plane through :g:`$BD$ <0285.g1:/ B D />`, :g:`$BE$ <0285.g1:/ B E />` is the plane of reference;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$AB$ <0285.g1:/ A B />` is at right angles to the plane of reference.

.. assertion::

   | Thus :g:`$AB$ <0285.g1:/ A B />` will also make right angles with all the straight
   | lines which meet it and are in the plane of reference. :just:`[XI. Def. 3] <XI.def.3>`

.. subclause::

   But :g:`$BF$ <0285.g1:/ B F />` which is in the plane of reference meets it;

.. assertion::

   therefore the angle :g:`$ABF$ <0285.g1:< A B F )>` is right.

.. subclause::

   But, by hypothesis, the angle :g:`$ABC$ <0285.g1:< A B C )>` is also right;

.. assertion::

   therefore the angle :g:`$ABF$ <0285.g1:< A B F )>` is equal to the angle :g:`$ABC$ <0285.g1:< A B C )>`.

.. subclause::

   And they are in one plane:

.. assertion::

   which is impossible.

.. assertion::

   | Therefore the straight line :g:`$BC$ <0285.g1:/ B C />` is not in a more elevated
   | plane;
   | therefore the three straight lines :g:`$BC$ <0285.g1:/ B C />`, :g:`$BD$ <0285.g1:/ B D />`, :g:`$BE$ <0285.g1:/ B E />` are in one
   | plane.

.. qed::

   | Therefore, if a straight line be set up at right angles to
   | three straight lines, at their point of meeting, the three straight
   | lines are in one plane. :just:`Q. E. D.`

----

| It follows that, *if a right angle be turned about one of the straight lines
| containing it the other will describe a plane.*

| At any point in a straight line it is possible to draw *only one plane which
| is at right angles to the straight line.*

| One such plane can be found by taking any two planes through the given
| straight line, drawing perpendiculars to the straight
| line in the respective planes, e.g. :g:`$BO$ <0286.g1:/ B O />`, :g:`$CO$ <0286.g1:/ C O />` in the
| planes :g:`$AOB$ <0286.g1:< A O B >>`, :g:`$AOC$ <0286.g1:< A O C >>`, each perpendicular to :g:`$AO$ <0286.g1:/ A O />`,
| and then drawing a plane (:g:`$BOC$ <0286.g1:< B O C >>`) through the
| perpendiculars.

.. picture:: 0286.g1.svg
   :alt: Book XI - Proposition 5, uniqueness of perpendicular plane at a point

| If there were another plane through :g:`$O$ <0286.g1:O>` per-
| pendicular to :g:`$AO$ <0286.g1:/ A O />`, it must meet the plane through
| :g:`$AO$ <0286.g1:/ A O />` and some perpendicular to it as :g:`$OC$ <0286.g1:/ O C />` in a
| straight line :g:`$OC'$ <0286.g1:/ O C' />` different from :g:`$OC$ <0286.g1:/ O C />`.

| Then, by XI. 4, :g:`$AOC'$ <0286.g1:< A O C' )>` is a right angle, and in
| the same plane with the right angle :g:`$AOC$ <0286.g1:< A O C )>`: which is impossible.

| Next, *one plane and only one can be drawn through a point outside a straight
| line at right angles to that line.*

| Let :g:`$P$ <0286.g2:P>` be the given point, :g:`$AB$ <0286.g2:/ A B />` the given straight
| line.

| In the plane through :g:`$P$ <0286.g2:P>` and :g:`$AB$ <0286.g2:/ A B />`, draw :g:`$PO$ <0286.g2:/ P O />` per-
| pendicular to :g:`$AB$ <0286.g2:/ A B />`, and through :g:`$O$ <0286.g2:O>` draw another straight
| line :g:`$OQ$ <0286.g2:/ O Q />` at right angles to :g:`$AB$ <0286.g2:/ A B />`.

   :alt: Book XI - Proposition 5, perpendicular plane from an external point
