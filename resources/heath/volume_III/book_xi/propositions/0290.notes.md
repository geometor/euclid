# Transcription Notes: Folio 0290 (Page 286)

## Summary & Folio Continuity
- **Page & Book**: Volume III, Book XI, Page 286.
- **Top of Page**: Conclusion of Euclid's proof of Proposition 7: the section $EF$ and hypothetical elevated path $EGF$ would enclose an area, which is impossible; hence $EF$ lies in the plane of the parallels. Concludes with Q. E. D.
- **Middle & Bottom of Page**: Heath's extended critical commentary on Proposition 7.
  - Discusses whether Proposition 7 is redundant under modern surface definitions of a plane, noting Euclid's need to prove that *two parallel straight lines determine a plane uniquely*.
  - Mounts a vigorous critique of Robert Simson's version of the proposition, exposing Simson's question-begging assumption ("draw the straight line $EHF$ in the plane $ABCD$").
  - Rebuts Simson's first objection that Prop. 7 was interpolated by an unskilful editor, clarifying the distinction between planar geometry (Book I) and solid geometry (Book XI).
- **Continuity**: Heath's refutation of Simson continues onto folio 0291 (page 287) with "But in Book XI. it becomes a question whether there can be *more than* one plane through parallel straight lines."

## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Foundational Concepts**: Heath highlights the essential role of XI. 7 in establishing plane uniqueness for parallel lines.
- **Heath vs. Simson**: Direct engagement with Simson's famous 18th-century edition of Euclid, highlighting how modern critical scholarship restored Euclid's authentic logic against well-meaning but flawed "improvements".
