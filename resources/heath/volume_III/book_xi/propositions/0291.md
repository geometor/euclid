But in Book XI. it becomes a question whether there can be *more than* one plane through parallel straight lines.

Simson goes on to say (2) “Besides, this 7th Proposition is demonstrated by the preceding 3rd; in which the very same thing which is proposed to be demonstrated in the 7th is twice assumed, viz., that the straight line drawn from one point to another in a plane is in that plane.” But there is nothing in Prop. 3 about a plane in which two parallel straight lines are; therefore there is no assumption of the result of Prop. 7. What is assumed is that, given two points in *a* plane, they can be joined by a straight line in the plane: a legitimate assumption.

Lastly, says Simson, “And the same thing is assumed in the preceding 6th Prop. in which the straight line which joins the points <span data-ref="0287.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0287.g1:D">$D$</span> that are in the plane to which <span data-ref="0287.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> and <span data-ref="0287.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> are at right angles is supposed to be in that plane.” Here again there is no question of *a plane in which two parallels are*; so that the criticism here, as with reference to Prop. 3, appears to rest on a misapprehension.

### PROPOSITION 8.

<p class="enunciation">If two straight lines be parallel, and one of them be at<br />
right angles to any plane, the remaining one will also be at<br />
right angles to the same plane.</p>

Let <span data-ref="0291.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0291.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> be two parallel straight lines,<br />
and let one of them, <span data-ref="0291.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, be at right<br />
angles to the plane of reference;<br />
I say that the remaining one, <span data-ref="0291.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, will<br />
also be at right angles to the same<br />
plane.

![Book XI - Proposition 8](0291.g1.svg)

For let <span data-ref="0291.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0291.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> meet the plane of<br />
reference at the points <span data-ref="0291.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0291.g1:D">$D$</span>,<br />
and let <span data-ref="0291.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> be joined;
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0291.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0291.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, <span data-ref="0291.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> are in one plane. <span class="justification">[XI. 7]</span></p>

Let <span data-ref="0291.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> be drawn, in the plane of reference, at right angles<br />
to <span data-ref="0291.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>,<br />
let <span data-ref="0291.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> be made equal to <span data-ref="0291.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>,<br />
and let <span data-ref="0291.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span>, <span data-ref="0291.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span>, <span data-ref="0291.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> be joined.

<p class="sub-clause">Now, since <span data-ref="0291.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is at right angles to the plane of reference,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0291.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is also at right angles to all the straight lines<br />
which meet it and are in the plane of reference; <span class="justification">[XI. Def. 3]</span><br />
