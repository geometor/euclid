# Transcription Notes: Folio 0291 (Page 287)

## Summary & Folio Continuity
- **Page & Book**: Volume III, Book XI, Page 287.
- **Top of Page**: Conclusion of Heath's commentary on Proposition 7, completing his refutation of Simson's second and third objections regarding alleged circularity in XI. 3 and XI. 6.
- **Middle to Bottom of Page**: Proposition 8 begins with enunciation: "If two straight lines be parallel, and one of them be at right angles to any plane, the remaining one will also be at right angles to the same plane."
- **Euclidean Proof**: Setting out, construction, and initial deductions.
  - $AB \parallel CD$, $AB \perp$ plane of reference.
  - $BD$ joined; $AB, CD, BD$ are coplanar by XI. 7.
  - $DE \perp BD$ in the plane, $DE = AB$, and $BE, AE, AD$ joined.
  - $AB \perp$ plane implies $\angle ABD = \angle ABE = 90^\circ$ (XI. Def. 3).
- **Continuity**: Ends with "And, since the straight line $BD$ has fallen on the parallels $AB$, $CD$,"; continues directly on folio 0292 (page 288) with "therefore the angles $ABD$, $CDB$ are equal to two right angles. [I. 29]".

## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Direct Application of Proposition 7**: Proposition 8 explicitly cites [XI. 7] to prove that $AB, CD, BD$ are in one plane, demonstrating why Proposition 7 was necessary.
- **Converse Relationship**: Proposition 8 is the direct converse of Proposition 6.
