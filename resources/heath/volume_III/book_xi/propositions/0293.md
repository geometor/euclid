Simson objects to the words which explain why <span data-ref="0291.g1:/ D C /">$DC$</span> is in the plane through <span data-ref="0291.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>, <span data-ref="0291.g1:/ D A /">$DA$</span>, viz. "inasmuch as <span data-ref="0291.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0291.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> are in the plane through <span data-ref="0291.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>, <span data-ref="0291.g1:/ D A /">$DA$</span>, and <span data-ref="0291.g1:/ D C /">$DC$</span> is also in the plane in which <span data-ref="0291.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0291.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> are," as being too roundabout. He concludes that they are corrupt or interpolated, and that we ought only to have the words "because all three are in the plane in which are the parallels <span data-ref="0291.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0291.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>" (by Prop. 7 preceding). But I think Euclid's words can be defended. Prop. 7 says nothing of a plane determined by *two* transversals as <span data-ref="0291.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>, <span data-ref="0291.g1:/ D A /">$DA$</span> are. Hence it is natural to say that <span data-ref="0291.g1:/ D C /">$DC$</span> is in the same plane in which <span data-ref="0291.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0291.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> are [Prop. 7], and <span data-ref="0291.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0291.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> are in the same plane as <span data-ref="0291.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>, <span data-ref="0291.g1:/ D A /">$DA$</span> [Prop. 2], so that <span data-ref="0291.g1:/ D C /">$DC$</span> is in the plane through <span data-ref="0291.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>, <span data-ref="0291.g1:/ D A /">$DA$</span>.

Legendre's alternative proof is split by him into two propositions.
(1) *Let <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> be a perpendicular to the plane <span data-ref="0293.g1:< M N >">$MN$</span> and <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> a line situated in that plane; if from <span data-ref="0293.g1:B">$B$</span>, the foot of the perpendicular, <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> be drawn perpendicular to <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, and <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> be joined, I say that <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> will be perpendicular to <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>.*
(2) *If <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is perpendicular to the plane <span data-ref="0293.g1:< M N >">$MN$</span>, every straight line <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> parallel to <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> will be perpendicular to the same plane.*

To prove both propositions together we suppose <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> given, join <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>, and draw <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> perpendicular to <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> in the plane <span data-ref="0293.g1:< M N >">$MN$</span>.

(1) As before, we make <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> equal to <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span> and join <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span>, <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ B F /">$BF$</span>, <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span>, <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span>.
    Then, since the angles <span data-ref="0293.g1:< B D E )">$BDE$</span>, <span data-ref="0293.g1:< B D F )">$BDF$</span> are right, and <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>, <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span> equal,
        <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ B F /">$BF$</span>. [I. 4]
    And, since <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is perpendicular to the plane,
        the angles <span data-ref="0293.g1:< A B E )">$ABE$</span>, <span data-ref="0293.g1:< A B F )">$ABF$</span> are both right.
    Therefore, in the triangles <span data-ref="0293.g1:< A B E >">$ABE$</span>, <span data-ref="0293.g1:< A B F >">$ABF$</span>,
        <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span>. [I. 4]
    Lastly, in the triangles <span data-ref="0293.g1:< A D E >">$ADE$</span>, <span data-ref="0293.g1:< A D F >">$ADF$</span>, since <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span>, and <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> to <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span>, while <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> is common,
        the angle <span data-ref="0293.g1:< A D E )">$ADE$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0293.g1:< A D F )">$ADF$</span>, [I. 8]
so that <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> is perpendicular to <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>.
(2) <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ E D /">$ED$</span> being thus perpendicular to <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ D A /">$DA$</span>, and also (by construction) perpendicular to <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span>,
        <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ E D /">$ED$</span> is perpendicular to the plane <span data-ref="0293.g1:< A D B >">$ADB$</span>. [XI. 4]
    But <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, being parallel to <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, is in the plane <span data-ref="0293.g1:< A D B >">$ADB$</span>;
therefore <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ E D /">$ED$</span> is perpendicular to <span data-ref="0293.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>. [XI. Def. 3]
