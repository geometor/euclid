The result of this proposition does not appear to be quoted in Euclid until XII. 3; but Euclid no doubt inserted it here advisedly, because it has the effect of incidentally proving that the "inclination of two planes to one another," as defined in XI. Def. 6, is one and the same angle at whatever point of the common section the plane angle measuring it is drawn.

---

### PROPOSITION 11.

<p class="enunciation">From a given elevated point to draw a straight line perpen-<br />
dicular to a given plane.</p>

Let <span data-ref="0296.g1:A">$A$</span> be the given elevated point, and the plane of<br />
reference the given plane;<br />
thus it is required to draw from the<br />
point <span data-ref="0296.g1:A">$A$</span> a straight line perpendicular to<br />
the plane of reference.

![Proposition 11](0296.g1.svg)

Let any straight line <span data-ref="0296.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> be drawn,<br />
at random, in the plane of reference,<br />
and let <span data-ref="0296.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> be drawn from the point <span data-ref="0296.g1:A">$A$</span><br />
perpendicular to <span data-ref="0296.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>. <span class="justification">[I. 12]</span>

If then <span data-ref="0296.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> is also perpendicular to<br />
the plane of reference, that which was<br />
enjoined will have been done.

But, if not, let <span data-ref="0296.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> be drawn from the point <span data-ref="0296.g1:D">$D$</span> at right<br />
angles to <span data-ref="0296.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> and in the plane of reference, <span class="justification">[I. 11]</span><br />
let <span data-ref="0296.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span> be drawn from <span data-ref="0296.g1:A">$A$</span> perpendicular to <span data-ref="0296.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>, <span class="justification">[I. 12]</span><br />
and let <span data-ref="0296.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span> be drawn through the point <span data-ref="0296.g1:F">$F$</span> parallel to <span data-ref="0296.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>.<br />
<span class="justification">[I. 31]</span>

Now, since <span data-ref="0296.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is at right angles to each of the straight<br />
lines <span data-ref="0296.g1:/ D A /">$DA$</span>, <span data-ref="0296.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>,
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0296.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is also at right angles to the plane through<br />
<span data-ref="0296.g1:/ E D /">$ED$</span>, <span data-ref="0296.g1:/ D A /">$DA$</span>. <span class="justification">[XI. 4]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">And <span data-ref="0296.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span> is parallel to it;</p>
but, if two straight lines be parallel, and one of them be at<br />
right angles to any plane, the remaining one will also be at<br />
right angles to the same plane; <span class="justification">[XI. 8]</span>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0296.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span> is also at right angles to the plane through<br />
<span data-ref="0296.g1:/ E D /">$ED$</span>, <span data-ref="0296.g1:/ D A /">$DA$</span>.</p>
