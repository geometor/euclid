Therefore <span data-ref="0296.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span> is also at right angles to all the straight lines which meet it and are in the plane through <span data-ref="0296.g1:/ E D /">$ED$</span>, <span data-ref="0296.g1:/ D A /">$DA$</span>. [XI. Def. 3]
    But <span data-ref="0296.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span> meets it and is in the plane through <span data-ref="0296.g1:/ E D /">$ED$</span>, <span data-ref="0296.g1:/ D A /">$DA$</span>;
therefore <span data-ref="0296.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span> is at right angles to <span data-ref="0296.g1:/ F A /">$FA$</span>,
so that <span data-ref="0296.g1:/ F A /">$FA$</span> is also at right angles to <span data-ref="0296.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span>.
    But <span data-ref="0296.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span> is also at right angles to <span data-ref="0296.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>;
therefore <span data-ref="0296.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span> is at right angles to each of the straight lines <span data-ref="0296.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span>, <span data-ref="0296.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>.
    But, if a straight line be set up at right angles to two straight lines which cut one another, at the point of section, it will also be at right angles to the plane through them; [XI. 4]
therefore <span data-ref="0296.g1:/ F A /">$FA$</span> is at right angles to the plane through <span data-ref="0296.g1:/ E D /">$ED$</span>, <span data-ref="0296.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span>.
    But the plane through <span data-ref="0296.g1:/ E D /">$ED$</span>, <span data-ref="0296.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span> is the plane of reference;
therefore <span data-ref="0296.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span> is at right angles to the plane of reference.
    Therefore from the given elevated point <span data-ref="0296.g1:A">$A$</span> the straight line <span data-ref="0296.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span> has been drawn perpendicular to the plane of reference.

<p align="right">Q. E. F.</p>

The text-books differ in the *form* which they give to this proposition rather than in substance. They commonly assume the construction of a *plane* through the point <span data-ref="0297.g1:A">$A$</span> at right angles to any straight line <span data-ref="0297.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> in the given plane (the construction being effected in the manner shown at the end of the note on XI. 5 above). The advantage of this method is that it enables a perpendicular to be drawn from a point *in* the plane also, by the same construction. (Where the letters for the two figures differ, those referring to the second figure are put in brackets.)

![Proposition 11, Alternative Method](0297.g1.svg)

We can include the construction of the plane through <span data-ref="0297.g1:A">$A$</span> perpendicular to <span data-ref="0297.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, and make the whole into one proposition, thus.
    <span data-ref="0297.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> being any straight line in the given plane <span data-ref="0297.g1:< M N >">$MN$</span>, draw <span data-ref="0297.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> perpendicular to <span data-ref="0297.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>.
    In any plane passing through <span data-ref="0297.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> but not through <span data-ref="0297.g1:A">$A$</span> draw <span data-ref="0297.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> at right angles to <span data-ref="0297.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>.
    Through <span data-ref="0297.g1:/ D A /">$DA$</span>, <span data-ref="0297.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> draw a plane; this will intersect the given plane <span data-ref="0297.g1:< M N >">$MN$</span> in a straight line, as <span data-ref="0297.g1:/ F D /">$FD$</span> (<span data-ref="0297.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>).
    In the plane <span data-ref="0297.g1:< A G >">$AG$</span> draw <span data-ref="0297.g1:/ A H /">$AH$</span> perpendicular to <span data-ref="0297.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span> (<span data-ref="0297.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>).
    Then <span data-ref="0297.g1:/ A H /">$AH$</span> is the perpendicular required.
