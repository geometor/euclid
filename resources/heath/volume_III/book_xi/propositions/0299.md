### PROPOSITION 13.

<p class="enunciation">From the same point two straight lines cannot be set up at<br />
right angles to the same plane on the same side.</p>

For, if possible, from the same point <span data-ref="0299.g1:A">$A$</span> let the two straight<br />
lines <span data-ref="0299.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0299.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> be set up at right<br />
angles to the plane of reference and on<br />
the same side,<br />
and let a plane be drawn through <span data-ref="0299.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span>,<br />
<span data-ref="0299.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>;<br />
it will then make, as section through <span data-ref="0299.g1:A">$A$</span><br />
in the plane of reference, a straight line.<br />
<span class="justification">[XI. 3]</span>

![Proposition 13](0299.g1.svg)

Let it make <span data-ref="0299.g1:/ D A E /">$DAE$</span>;
<p class="assertion">therefore the straight lines <span data-ref="0299.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0299.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0299.g1:/ D A E /">$DAE$</span> are in one plane.</p>

And, since <span data-ref="0299.g1:/ C A /">$CA$</span> is at right angles to the plane of reference,<br />
it will also make right angles with all the straight lines which<br />
meet it and are in the plane of reference. <span class="justification">[XI. Def. 3]</span>

<p class="sub-clause">But <span data-ref="0299.g1:/ D A E /">$DAE$</span> meets it and is in the plane of reference;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the angle <span data-ref="0299.g1:< C A E )">$CAE$</span> is right.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">For the same reason<br />
the angle <span data-ref="0299.g1:< B A E )">$BAE$</span> is also right;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the angle <span data-ref="0299.g1:< C A E )">$CAE$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0299.g1:< B A E )">$BAE$</span>.</p>

<p class="assertion">And they are in one plane:<br />
which is impossible.</p>

<p class="qed">Therefore etc. <span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

---

Simson added words to this as follows:

"Also, from a point above a plane there can be but one perpendicular to that plane; for, if there could be two, they would be parallel to one another <span class="justification">[XI. 6]</span>, which is absurd."

Euclid does not give this result, but we have already had it in the note above to XI. 4 (*ad fin.*).
