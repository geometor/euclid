## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Enunciation**: *Planes to which the same straight line is at right angles will be parallel.*
- **Method of Proof**: *Reductio ad absurdum*. If planes $CD$ and $EF$ meet, their common section is a straight line $GH$ by XI. 3. Choosing an arbitrary point $K$ on $GH$ and connecting $AK$ and $BK$, the line $AB$ must be perpendicular to both $BK$ (in plane $EF$) and $AK$ (in plane $CD$) by XI. Def. 3. This produces triangle $\triangle ABK$ containing two right angles ($\angle ABK + \angle BAK = 2\text{ right angles}$), violating Euclid I. 17.
- **Cross-references cited in margins**:
  - `[XI. 3]`: Intersection of two planes is a straight line.
  - `[XI. Def. 3]`: Definition of a straight line perpendicular to a plane.
  - `[I. 17]`: In any triangle, two angles taken together in any manner are less than two right angles.
  - `[XI. Def. 8]`: Definition of parallel planes.
