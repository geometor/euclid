But planes to which the same straight line is at right angles are parallel; [XI. 14]
therefore the plane through <span data-ref="0301.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0301.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is parallel to the plane through <span data-ref="0301.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>, <span data-ref="0301.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>.

Therefore, if two straight lines meeting one another be parallel to two straight lines meeting one another, not being in the same plane, the planes through them are parallel.

Q. E. D.

---

This result is arrived at in the American text-books already quoted by starting from the relation between a plane and a straight line parallel to it. The series of propositions is worth giving. A straight line and a plane being parallel if they do not meet however far they may be produced, we have the following propositions.

1. *Any plane containing one, and only one, of two parallel straight lines is parallel to the other.*

For suppose <span data-ref="0302.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0302.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> to be parallel and <span data-ref="0302.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> to lie in the plane <span data-ref="0302.g1:< M N >">$MN$</span>.
Then <span data-ref="0302.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0302.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> determine a plane intersecting <span data-ref="0302.g1:< M N >">$MN$</span> in the straight line <span data-ref="0302.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>
Thus, if <span data-ref="0302.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> meets <span data-ref="0302.g1:< M N >">$MN$</span>, it must meet it at some point in <span data-ref="0302.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>.
But this is impossible, since <span data-ref="0302.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is parallel to <span data-ref="0302.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>.
Therefore <span data-ref="0302.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> will not meet the plane <span data-ref="0302.g1:< M N >">$MN$</span>, and is therefore parallel to it.
[This proposition and the proof are in Legendre.]

![Book XI, Proposition 15 Commentary 1](0302.g1.svg)

The following theorems follow as corollaries.

2. *Through a given straight line a plane can be drawn parallel to any other given straight line; and, if the lines are not parallel, only one such plane can be drawn.*

We have simply to draw through any point on the first line a straight line parallel to the second line and then pass a plane through these two intersecting lines. This plane is then, by the above proposition, parallel to the second given straight line.

3. *Through a given point a plane can be drawn parallel to any two straight lines in space; and, if the latter are not parallel, only one such plane can be drawn.*

Here we draw through the point straight lines parallel respectively to the given straight lines and then draw a plane through the lines so drawn.

Next we have the partial converse of the first proposition above.

4. *If a straight line is parallel to a plane, it is also parallel to the intersection of any plane through it with the given plane.*

Let <span data-ref="0302.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span> be parallel to the plane <span data-ref="0302.g2:< M N >">$MN$</span>, and let any plane through <span data-ref="0302.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span> intersect <span data-ref="0302.g2:< M N >">$MN$</span> in <span data-ref="0302.g2:/ C D /">$CD$</span>.
Now <span data-ref="0302.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span> and <span data-ref="0302.g2:/ C D /">$CD$</span> cannot meet, because, if they did, <span data-ref="0302.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span> would meet the plane <span data-ref="0302.g2:< M N >">$MN$</span>.
And <span data-ref="0302.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0302.g2:/ C D /">$CD$</span> are in one plane.
Therefore <span data-ref="0302.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0302.g2:/ C D /">$CD$</span> are parallel.
