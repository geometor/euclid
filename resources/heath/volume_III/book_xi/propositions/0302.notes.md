## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Completion of Prop. 15**: Invokes XI. 14 ($GB \perp$ both planes $\implies$ planes are parallel).
- **Heath Commentary Structure**:
  - Compares Euclid's direct perpendicular construction ($BG$) with the alternative axiomatic sequence common in 19th/early 20th-century American textbooks (e.g. Wentworth, Wells, etc.) and Legendre's *Éléments de géométrie*.
  - Item 1: Plane containing one of two parallel lines is parallel to the other.
  - Item 2 (Corollary): Plane through a line parallel to another line.
  - Item 3 (Corollary): Plane through a point parallel to two skew or intersecting lines.
  - Item 4: Partial converse: line parallel to plane is parallel to the trace of any intersecting plane through it.
- **Punctuation Note**: In item 1, "in the straight line $CD$" lacks an end period in the printed text, preserved verbatim.
