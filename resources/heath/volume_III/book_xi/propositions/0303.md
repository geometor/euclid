5. *If each of two intersecting straight lines is parallel to a given plane, the plane containing them is parallel to the given plane.*

Let <span data-ref="0303.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0303.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> be parallel to the plane <span data-ref="0303.g1:< M N >">$MN$</span>.
Then, if the plane <span data-ref="0303.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> were to meet the plane <span data-ref="0303.g1:< M N >">$MN$</span>, the intersection would be parallel both to <span data-ref="0303.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> and to <span data-ref="0303.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>: which is impossible.

![Book XI, Proposition 15 Commentary 3](0303.g1.svg)

Lastly, we have Euclid's proposition.

6. *If two straight lines forming an angle are respectively parallel to two other straight lines forming an angle, the plane of the first angle is parallel to the plane of the second.*

Let <span data-ref="0303.g2:< A B C )">$ABC$</span>, <span data-ref="0303.g2:< D E F )">$DEF$</span> be the angles formed by straight lines parallel to one another respectively.
Then, since <span data-ref="0303.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is parallel to <span data-ref="0303.g2:/ D E /">$DE$</span>, the plane of <span data-ref="0303.g2:< D E F >">$DEF$</span> is parallel to <span data-ref="0303.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span> [(1) above].
Similarly the plane of <span data-ref="0303.g2:< D E F >">$DEF$</span> is parallel to <span data-ref="0303.g2:/ B C /">$BC$</span>.
Hence the plane of <span data-ref="0303.g2:< D E F >">$DEF$</span> is parallel to the plane of <span data-ref="0303.g2:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> [(5)].

![Book XI, Proposition 15 Commentary 4](0303.g2.svg)

Legendre arrives at the result by yet another method. He first proves Eucl. XI. 16 to the effect that, *if two parallel planes are cut by a third, the lines of intersection are parallel*, and then deduces from this that, *if two parallel straight lines are terminated by two parallel planes, the straight lines are equal in length*.
(The latter inference is obvious because the plane through the parallels cuts the parallel planes in parallel lines; which therefore, with the given parallel lines, form a parallelogram.)

Legendre is now in a position to prove Euclid's proposition XI. 15.
If <span data-ref="0303.g3:< A B C )">$ABC$</span>, <span data-ref="0303.g3:< D E F )">$DEF$</span> be the angles, make <span data-ref="0303.g3:/ A B /">$AB$</span> equal to <span data-ref="0303.g3:/ D E /">$DE$</span>, and <span data-ref="0303.g3:/ B C /">$BC$</span> equal to <span data-ref="0303.g3:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, and join <span data-ref="0303.g3:/ C A /">$CA$</span>, <span data-ref="0303.g3:/ F D /">$FD$</span>, <span data-ref="0303.g3:/ B E /">$BE$</span>, <span data-ref="0303.g3:/ C F /">$CF$</span>, <span data-ref="0303.g3:/ A D /">$AD$</span>.
Then, as in Eucl. XI. 10, the triangles <span data-ref="0303.g3:< A B C >">$ABC$</span>, <span data-ref="0303.g3:< D E F >">$DEF$</span> are equal in all respects;
and <span data-ref="0303.g3:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0303.g3:/ B E /">$BE$</span>, <span data-ref="0303.g3:/ C F /">$CF$</span> are all equal.
It is now proved that the planes are parallel by *reductio ad absurdum* from the last preceding result. For, if the plane <span data-ref="0303.g3:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> is not parallel to the plane <span data-ref="0303.g3:< D E F >">$DEF$</span>, let the plane drawn through <span data-ref="0303.g3:B">$B$</span> parallel to the plane <span data-ref="0303.g3:< D E F >">$DEF$</span> meet <span data-ref="0303.g3:/ C F /">$CF$</span>, <span data-ref="0303.g3:/ A D /">$AD$</span> in <span data-ref="0303.g3:H">$H$</span>, <span data-ref="0303.g3:G">$G$</span> respectively.
Then, by the last result <span data-ref="0303.g3:/ B E /">$BE$</span>, <span data-ref="0303.g3:/ H F /">$HF$</span>, <span data-ref="0303.g3:/ G D /">$GD$</span> will all be equal.
But <span data-ref="0303.g3:/ B E /">$BE$</span>, <span data-ref="0303.g3:/ C F /">$CF$</span>, <span data-ref="0303.g3:/ A D /">$AD$</span> are all equal:
which is impossible.
Therefore etc.
