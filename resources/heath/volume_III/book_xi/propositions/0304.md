### PROPOSITION 16.

*If two parallel planes be cut by any plane, their common sections are parallel.*

For let the two parallel planes <span data-ref="0304.g1:< A B >">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0304.g1:< C D >">$CD$</span> be cut by the plane <span data-ref="0304.g1:< E F G H >">$EFGH$</span>,
and let <span data-ref="0304.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, <span data-ref="0304.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span> be their common sections;
I say that <span data-ref="0304.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> is parallel to <span data-ref="0304.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span>.

![Book XI, Proposition 16](0304.g1.svg)

For, if not, <span data-ref="0304.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, <span data-ref="0304.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span> will, when produced, meet either in the direction of <span data-ref="0304.g1:F">$F$</span>, <span data-ref="0304.g1:H">$H$</span> or of <span data-ref="0304.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0304.g1:G">$G$</span>.
Let them be produced, as in the direction of <span data-ref="0304.g1:F">$F$</span>, <span data-ref="0304.g1:H">$H$</span>, and let them, first, meet at <span data-ref="0304.g1:K">$K$</span>.

Now, since <span data-ref="0304.g1:/ E F K /">$EFK$</span> is in the plane <span data-ref="0304.g1:< A B >">$AB$</span>,
therefore all the points on <span data-ref="0304.g1:/ E F K /">$EFK$</span> are also in the plane <span data-ref="0304.g1:< A B >">$AB$</span>. [XI. 1]

But <span data-ref="0304.g1:K">$K$</span> is one of the points on the straight line <span data-ref="0304.g1:/ E F K /">$EFK$</span>;
therefore <span data-ref="0304.g1:K">$K$</span> is in the plane <span data-ref="0304.g1:< A B >">$AB$</span>.

For the same reason
<span data-ref="0304.g1:K">$K$</span> is also in the plane <span data-ref="0304.g1:< C D >">$CD$</span>;
therefore the planes <span data-ref="0304.g1:< A B >">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0304.g1:< C D >">$CD$</span> will meet when produced.

But they do not meet, because they are, by hypothesis, parallel;
therefore the straight lines <span data-ref="0304.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, <span data-ref="0304.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span> will not meet when produced in the direction of <span data-ref="0304.g1:F">$F$</span>, <span data-ref="0304.g1:H">$H$</span>.

Similarly we can prove that neither will the straight lines <span data-ref="0304.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, <span data-ref="0304.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span> meet when produced in the direction of <span data-ref="0304.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0304.g1:G">$G$</span>.

But straight lines which do not meet in either direction are parallel. [I. Def. 23]

Therefore <span data-ref="0304.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> is parallel to <span data-ref="0304.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span>.

Therefore etc.

Q. E. D.
