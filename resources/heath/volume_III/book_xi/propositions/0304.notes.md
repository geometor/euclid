## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Enunciation**: *If two parallel planes be cut by any plane, their common sections are parallel.*
- **Method of Proof**: Direct *reductio ad absurdum*.
  - Assumes $EF, GH$ meet when produced (say at $K$ in direction $F, H$).
  - Since $EFK$ is a line in plane $AB$, point $K$ lies in plane $AB$ (XI. 1).
  - Similarly $K$ lies in plane $CD$.
  - Hence planes $AB, CD$ meet at $K$, which contradicts the hypothesis that they are parallel planes.
  - Symmetrical reasoning applies to direction $E, G$.
  - Concludes $EF \parallel GH$ by I. Def. 23.
- **Marginal Citations**: `[XI. 1]`, `[I. Def. 23]`.
