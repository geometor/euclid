Simson points out that, in here quoting I. Def. 23, Euclid should have said "But straight lines *in one plane* which do not meet in either direction are parallel."

From this proposition is deduced the converse of XI. 14.

*If a straight line is perpendicular to one of two parallel planes, it is perpendicular to the other also.*

For suppose that <span data-ref="0305.g1:< M N >">$MN$</span>, <span data-ref="0305.g1:< P Q >">$PQ$</span> are two parallel planes, and that <span data-ref="0305.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is perpendicular to <span data-ref="0305.g1:< M N >">$MN$</span>.

Through <span data-ref="0305.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> draw any plane, and let it intersect the planes <span data-ref="0305.g1:< M N >">$MN$</span>, <span data-ref="0305.g1:< P Q >">$PQ$</span> in <span data-ref="0305.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0305.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> respectively.
Therefore <span data-ref="0305.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0305.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> are parallel. [XI. 16]
But <span data-ref="0305.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is perpendicular to <span data-ref="0305.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>;
therefore <span data-ref="0305.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is also perpendicular to <span data-ref="0305.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>.

That is, <span data-ref="0305.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is perpendicular to any line in <span data-ref="0305.g1:< P Q >">$PQ$</span> passing through <span data-ref="0305.g1:B">$B$</span>;
therefore <span data-ref="0305.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is perpendicular to <span data-ref="0305.g1:< P Q >">$PQ$</span>.

![Book XI, Proposition 16 Commentary](0305.g1.svg)

It follows as a corollary that

*Through a given point one plane, and only one, can be drawn parallel to a given plane.*

In the above figure let <span data-ref="0305.g1:A">$A$</span> be the given point and <span data-ref="0305.g1:< P Q >">$PQ$</span> the given plane.
Draw <span data-ref="0305.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> perpendicular to <span data-ref="0305.g1:< P Q >">$PQ$</span>.
Through <span data-ref="0305.g1:A">$A$</span> draw a plane <span data-ref="0305.g1:< M N >">$MN$</span> at right angles to <span data-ref="0305.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> (see note on XI. 5 above).
Then <span data-ref="0305.g1:< M N >">$MN$</span> is parallel to <span data-ref="0305.g1:< P Q >">$PQ$</span>. [XI. 14]
If there could pass through <span data-ref="0305.g1:A">$A$</span> a second plane parallel to <span data-ref="0305.g1:< P Q >">$PQ$</span>, <span data-ref="0305.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> would also be perpendicular to it.
That is, <span data-ref="0305.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> would be perpendicular to two different planes through <span data-ref="0305.g1:A">$A$</span>: which is impossible (see the same note).

Also it is readily proved that,

*If two planes are parallel to a third plane, they are parallel to one another.*

### PROPOSITION 17.

*If two straight lines be cut by parallel planes, they will be cut in the same ratios.*

For let the two straight lines <span data-ref="0305.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0305.g2:/ C D /">$CD$</span> be cut by the parallel planes <span data-ref="0305.g2:< G H >">$GH$</span>, <span data-ref="0305.g2:< K L >">$KL$</span>, <span data-ref="0305.g2:< M N >">$MN$</span> at the points <span data-ref="0305.g2:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0305.g2:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0305.g2:B">$B$</span> and <span data-ref="0305.g2:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0305.g2:F">$F$</span>, <span data-ref="0305.g2:D">$D$</span>;
I say that, as the straight line <span data-ref="0305.g2:/ A E /">$AE$</span> is to <span data-ref="0305.g2:/ E B /">$EB$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0305.g2:/ C F /">$CF$</span> to <span data-ref="0305.g2:/ F D /">$FD$</span>.
