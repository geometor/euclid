Simson points out that, in here quoting I. Def. 23, Euclid should have<br />
said “But straight lines *in one plane* which do not meet in either direction are<br />
parallel.”

From this proposition is deduced the converse of XI. 14.

*If a straight line is perpendicular to one of two parallel planes, it is*<br />
*perpendicular to the other also.*

For suppose that <span data-ref="0305.g1:< M N >">$MN$</span>, <span data-ref="0305.g1:< P Q >">$PQ$</span> are two parallel planes, and that <span data-ref="0305.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is perpen-<br />
dicular to <span data-ref="0305.g1:< M N >">$MN$</span>.<br />
Through <span data-ref="0305.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> draw any plane, and let it intersect<br />
the planes <span data-ref="0305.g1:< M N >">$MN$</span>, <span data-ref="0305.g1:< P Q >">$PQ$</span> in <span data-ref="0305.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0305.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> respectively.<br />
Therefore <span data-ref="0305.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0305.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> are parallel. [XI. 16]<br />
But <span data-ref="0305.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is perpendicular to <span data-ref="0305.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> ;<br />
therefore <span data-ref="0305.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is also perpendicular to <span data-ref="0305.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>.<br />
That is, <span data-ref="0305.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is perpendicular to any line in <span data-ref="0305.g1:< P Q >">$PQ$</span><br />
passing through <span data-ref="0305.g1:B">$B$</span> ;<br />
therefore <span data-ref="0305.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is perpendicular to <span data-ref="0305.g1:< P Q >">$PQ$</span>.

![Book XI, Proposition 16 Commentary](0305.g1.svg)

It follows as a corollary that

*Through a given point one plane, and only one, can be drawn parallel to a*<br />
*given plane.*

In the above figure let <span data-ref="0305.g1:A">$A$</span> be the given point and <span data-ref="0305.g1:< P Q >">$PQ$</span> the given plane.<br />
Draw <span data-ref="0305.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> perpendicular to <span data-ref="0305.g1:< P Q >">$PQ$</span>.<br />
Through <span data-ref="0305.g1:A">$A$</span> draw a plane <span data-ref="0305.g1:< M N >">$MN$</span> at right angles to <span data-ref="0305.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> (see note on XI. 5<br />
above).<br />
Then <span data-ref="0305.g1:< M N >">$MN$</span> is parallel to <span data-ref="0305.g1:< P Q >">$PQ$</span>. [XI. 14]<br />
If there could pass through <span data-ref="0305.g1:A">$A$</span> a second plane parallel to <span data-ref="0305.g1:< P Q >">$PQ$</span>, <span data-ref="0305.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> would<br />
also be perpendicular to it.<br />
That is, <span data-ref="0305.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> would be perpendicular to two different planes through <span data-ref="0305.g1:A">$A$</span> :<br />
which is impossible (see the same note).

Also it is readily proved that,

*If two planes are parallel to a third plane, they are parallel to one another.*

<p class="enunciation">If two straight lines be cut by parallel planes, they will be<br />
cut in the same ratios.</p>

lines <span data-ref="0305.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0305.g2:/ C D /">$CD$</span> be cut by the<br />
