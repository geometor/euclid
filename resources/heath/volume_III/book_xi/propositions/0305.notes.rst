Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
--------------------------------------------

- **Simson's Critique**: Robert Simson observed that Euclid's quotation of I. Def. 23 omitted the crucial condition "in one plane", which is essential in solid geometry since skew lines do not meet but are not parallel.
- **Converse of XI. 14**: If line $\perp$ one of two parallel planes, it is $\perp$ the other. Proved via plane through the line cutting the parallel planes in parallel lines (XI. 16).
- **Corollaries**:

  - Uniqueness of parallel plane through a given point (appealing to note on XI. 5).
  - Transitivity of parallel planes.

- **Proposition 17 Enunciation**: *If two straight lines be cut by parallel planes, they will be cut in the same ratios.*

  - Straight lines $AB, CD$ cut by three parallel planes $GH, KL, MN$ at $A, E, B$ and $C, F, D$.
  - Auxiliary line: transversal $AD$ cutting middle plane $KL$ at $O$; connections $AC, BD, EO, OF$.
