:title: Proposition 16 (commentary) and Proposition 17

| :person:`Simson <simson>` points out that, in here quoting I. Def. 23, Euclid should have
| said “But straight lines *in one plane* which do not meet in either direction are
| parallel.”

From this proposition is deduced the converse of XI. 14.

| *If a straight line is perpendicular to one of two parallel planes, it is*
| *perpendicular to the other also.*

| For suppose that :g:`$MN$ <0305.g1:< M N >>`, :g:`$PQ$ <0305.g1:< P Q >>` are two parallel planes, and that :g:`$AB$ <0305.g1:/ A B />` is perpen-
| dicular to :g:`$MN$ <0305.g1:< M N >>`.
| Through :g:`$AB$ <0305.g1:/ A B />` draw any plane, and let it intersect
| the planes :g:`$MN$ <0305.g1:< M N >>`, :g:`$PQ$ <0305.g1:< P Q >>` in :g:`$AC$ <0305.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$BD$ <0305.g1:/ B D />` respectively.
| Therefore :g:`$AC$ <0305.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$BD$ <0305.g1:/ B D />` are parallel. [XI. 16]
| But :g:`$AC$ <0305.g1:/ A C />` is perpendicular to :g:`$AB$ <0305.g1:/ A B />` ;
| therefore :g:`$AB$ <0305.g1:/ A B />` is also perpendicular to :g:`$BD$ <0305.g1:/ B D />`.
| That is, :g:`$AB$ <0305.g1:/ A B />` is perpendicular to any line in :g:`$PQ$ <0305.g1:< P Q >>`
| passing through :g:`$B$ <0305.g1:B>` ;
| therefore :g:`$AB$ <0305.g1:/ A B />` is perpendicular to :g:`$PQ$ <0305.g1:< P Q >>`.

.. picture:: 0305.g1.svg
   :alt: Book XI, Proposition 16 Commentary

It follows as a corollary that

| *Through a given point one plane, and only one, can be drawn parallel to a*
| *given plane.*

| In the above figure let :g:`$A$ <0305.g1:A>` be the given point and :g:`$PQ$ <0305.g1:< P Q >>` the given plane.
| Draw :g:`$AB$ <0305.g1:/ A B />` perpendicular to :g:`$PQ$ <0305.g1:< P Q >>`.
| Through :g:`$A$ <0305.g1:A>` draw a plane :g:`$MN$ <0305.g1:< M N >>` at right angles to :g:`$AB$ <0305.g1:/ A B />` (see note on XI. 5
| above).
| Then :g:`$MN$ <0305.g1:< M N >>` is parallel to :g:`$PQ$ <0305.g1:< P Q >>`. [XI. 14]
| If there could pass through :g:`$A$ <0305.g1:A>` a second plane parallel to :g:`$PQ$ <0305.g1:< P Q >>`, :g:`$AB$ <0305.g1:/ A B />` would
| also be perpendicular to it.
| That is, :g:`$AB$ <0305.g1:/ A B />` would be perpendicular to two different planes through :g:`$A$ <0305.g1:A>` :
| which is impossible (see the same note).

Also it is readily proved that,

*If two planes are parallel to a third plane, they are parallel to one another.*

PROPOSITION 17.
---------------

.. enunciation::

   If two straight lines be cut by parallel planes, they will be cut in the same ratios.

| For let the two straight
| lines :g:`$AB$ <0305.g2:/ A B />`, :g:`$CD$ <0305.g2:/ C D />` be cut by the
| parallel planes :g:`$GH$ <0305.g2:< G H >>`, :g:`$KL$ <0305.g2:< K L >>`, :g:`$MN$ <0305.g2:< M N >>`
| at the points :g:`$A$ <0305.g2:A>`, :g:`$E$ <0305.g2:E>`, :g:`$B$ <0305.g2:B>` and :g:`$C$ <0305.g2:C>`,
| :g:`$F$ <0305.g2:F>`, :g:`$D$ <0305.g2:D>`;
| I say that, as the straight line
| :g:`$AE$ <0305.g2:/ A E />` is to :g:`$EB$ <0305.g2:/ E B />`, so is :g:`$CF$ <0305.g2:/ C F />` to :g:`$FD$ <0305.g2:/ F D />`.
