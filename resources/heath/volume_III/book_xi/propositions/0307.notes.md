## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Proposition 18 Completion**:
  - $AB \perp$ plane of reference $\implies AB \perp CE \implies \angle ABF = 90^\circ$ (XI. Def. 3).
  - Construction made $\angle GFB = 90^\circ$, so $AB \parallel FG$ (I. 28).
  - Parallels to perpendiculars: since $AB \perp$ plane of reference, $FG \parallel AB \implies FG \perp$ plane of reference (XI. 8).
  - By XI. Def. 4, plane $DE \perp$ plane of reference.
- **Heath Commentary on Definition of Perpendicular Planes**:
  - Euclid's definition (XI. Def. 4) requires *all* straight lines in one plane drawn perpendicular to the common section to be perpendicular to the other plane. This is why Euclid had to take an arbitrary point $F$ and prove $FG \perp$ plane.
  - Modern approach (Legendre): defines perpendicular planes via the *inclination of planes* (dihedral angle is a right angle). Because XI. 10 proves dihedral angle is invariant along the common section, proving *one* perpendicular line suffices.
  - Literature cited: Legendre, H. M. Taylor, Smith and Bryant, Rausenberger, Schultze and Sevenoak, Holgate.
