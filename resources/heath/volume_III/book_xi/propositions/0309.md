Legendre, followed by other writers already quoted, uses a preliminary<br />
proposition equivalent to Euclid's definition of planes at right angles to one<br />
another.

*If two planes are perpendicular to one another, a straight line drawn in one<br />
of them perpendicular to their common section will be perpendicular to the other.*

Let the perpendicular planes <span data-ref="0308.g1:< C E >">$CE$</span>, <span data-ref="0308.g1:< M N >">$MN$</span> (figure of last note) intersect in<br />
<span data-ref="0308.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, and let <span data-ref="0308.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> be drawn in <span data-ref="0308.g1:< C E >">$CE$</span> perpendicular to <span data-ref="0308.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>.

In the plane <span data-ref="0308.g1:< M N >">$MN$</span> draw <span data-ref="0308.g1:/ B F /">$BF$</span> at right angles to <span data-ref="0308.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>.

Then, since the planes are perpendicular, the angle <span data-ref="0308.g1:< A B F )">$ABF$</span> (their *inclination*)<br />
is a right angle.

Therefore <span data-ref="0308.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is perpendicular to both <span data-ref="0308.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> and <span data-ref="0308.g1:/ B F /">$BF$</span>, and therefore to the<br />
plane <span data-ref="0308.g1:< M N >">$MN$</span>.

We are now in a position to prove XI. 19, viz. *If two planes be perpendicular<br />
to a third, their intersection is also perpen-<br />
dicular to that third plane.*

Let each of the two planes <span data-ref="0309.g1:< A C >">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0309.g1:< A D >">$AD$</span><br />
intersecting in <span data-ref="0309.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> be perpendicular to the<br />
plane <span data-ref="0309.g1:< M N >">$MN$</span>.<br />
Let <span data-ref="0309.g1:< A C >">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0309.g1:< A D >">$AD$</span> intersect <span data-ref="0309.g1:< M N >">$MN$</span> in <span data-ref="0309.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, <span data-ref="0309.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span><br />
respectively.<br />
In the plane <span data-ref="0309.g1:< M N >">$MN$</span> draw <span data-ref="0309.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span> at right<br />
angles to <span data-ref="0309.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> and <span data-ref="0309.g1:/ B F /">$BF$</span> at right angles to<br />
<span data-ref="0309.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>.

![Book XI, Proposition 19, Heath Commentary Diagram](0309.g1.svg)

Now, since the planes <span data-ref="0309.g1:< A C >">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0309.g1:< M N >">$MN$</span> are at<br />
right angles, and <span data-ref="0309.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span> is drawn in the latter perpendicular to <span data-ref="0309.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, <span data-ref="0309.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span> is<br />
perpendicular to the plane <span data-ref="0309.g1:< A C >">$AC$</span>.<br />
Hence <span data-ref="0309.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is perpendicular to <span data-ref="0309.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span>. <span class="justification">[XI. 4]</span><br />
Similarly <span data-ref="0309.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is perpendicular to <span data-ref="0309.g1:/ B F /">$BF$</span>.<br />
Therefore <span data-ref="0309.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is perpendicular to the plane through <span data-ref="0309.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span>, <span data-ref="0309.g1:/ B F /">$BF$</span>, i.e. to the<br />
plane <span data-ref="0309.g1:< M N >">$MN$</span>.
