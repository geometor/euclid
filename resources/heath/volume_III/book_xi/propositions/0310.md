*Given a plane and a straight line not perpendicular to it, one plane, and only one, can be drawn through the straight line perpendicular to the plane.*

Let <span data-ref="0310.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> be the given straight line, <span data-ref="0310.g1:< M N >">$MN$</span> the given plane.

From any point <span data-ref="0310.g1:C">$C$</span> in <span data-ref="0310.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> draw <span data-ref="0310.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> perpendicular to the plane <span data-ref="0310.g1:< M N >">$MN$</span>.

Through <span data-ref="0310.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> and <span data-ref="0310.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> draw a plane <span data-ref="0310.g1:< A E >">$AE$</span>.

Then the plane <span data-ref="0310.g1:< A E >">$AE$</span> is perpendicular to the plane <span data-ref="0310.g1:< M N >">$MN$</span>.
[XI. 18]

![Book XI, Proposition 19, Lemma 1 Diagram](0310.g1.svg)

If any other plane could be drawn through <span data-ref="0310.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> perpendicular to <span data-ref="0310.g1:< M N >">$MN$</span>, the intersection <span data-ref="0310.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> of the two planes perpendicular to <span data-ref="0310.g1:< M N >">$MN$</span> would itself be perpendicular to <span data-ref="0310.g1:< M N >">$MN$</span>:
[XI. 19]
which contradicts the hypothesis.

*To draw a common perpendicular to two straight lines not in the same plane.*

Let <span data-ref="0310.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0310.g2:/ C D /">$CD$</span> be the given straight lines.

Through <span data-ref="0310.g2:/ C D /">$CD$</span> draw the plane <span data-ref="0310.g2:< M N >">$MN$</span> parallel to <span data-ref="0310.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span> (Prop. 2 in note to XI. 15).

Through <span data-ref="0310.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span> draw the plane <span data-ref="0310.g2:< A F >">$AF$</span> perpendicular to the plane <span data-ref="0310.g2:< M N >">$MN$</span> (see the last preceding proposition).

![Book XI, Proposition 19, Lemma 2 Diagram](0310.g2.svg)

Let the planes <span data-ref="0310.g2:< A F >">$AF$</span>, <span data-ref="0310.g2:< M N >">$MN$</span> intersect in <span data-ref="0310.g2:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, and let <span data-ref="0310.g2:/ E F /">$EF$</span> meet <span data-ref="0310.g2:/ C D /">$CD$</span> in <span data-ref="0310.g2:G">$G$</span>.

From <span data-ref="0310.g2:G">$G$</span>, in the plane <span data-ref="0310.g2:< A F >">$AF$</span>, draw <span data-ref="0310.g2:/ G H /">$GH$</span> at right angles to <span data-ref="0310.g2:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, meeting <span data-ref="0310.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span> in <span data-ref="0310.g2:H">$H$</span>.

<span data-ref="0310.g2:/ G H /">$GH$</span> is then the required perpendicular.

For <span data-ref="0310.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is parallel to <span data-ref="0310.g2:/ E F /">$EF$</span> (Prop. 4 in note to XI. 15); therefore <span data-ref="0310.g2:/ G H /">$GH$</span>, being perpendicular to <span data-ref="0310.g2:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, is also perpendicular to <span data-ref="0310.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span>.

But, the plane <span data-ref="0310.g2:< A F >">$AF$</span> being perpendicular to the plane <span data-ref="0310.g2:< M N >">$MN$</span>, and <span data-ref="0310.g2:/ G H /">$GH$</span> being perpendicular to <span data-ref="0310.g2:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, their intersection,
<span data-ref="0310.g2:/ G H /">$GH$</span> is perpendicular to the plane <span data-ref="0310.g2:< M N >">$MN$</span>, and therefore to <span data-ref="0310.g2:/ C D /">$CD$</span>.

Therefore <span data-ref="0310.g2:/ G H /">$GH$</span> is perpendicular to both <span data-ref="0310.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span> and <span data-ref="0310.g2:/ C D /">$CD$</span>.

*Only one common perpendicular can be drawn to two straight lines not in one plane.*

For, if possible, let <span data-ref="0310.g2:/ K L /">$KL$</span> also be perpendicular to both <span data-ref="0310.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span> and <span data-ref="0310.g2:/ C D /">$CD$</span>.

Let the plane through <span data-ref="0310.g2:/ K L /">$KL$</span>, <span data-ref="0310.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span> meet the plane <span data-ref="0310.g2:< M N >">$MN$</span> in <span data-ref="0310.g2:/ L Q /">$LQ$</span>.

Then <span data-ref="0310.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is parallel to <span data-ref="0310.g2:/ L Q /">$LQ$</span> (Prop. 4 in note to XI. 15), so that <span data-ref="0310.g2:/ K L /">$KL$</span>, being perpendicular to <span data-ref="0310.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, is also perpendicular to <span data-ref="0310.g2:/ L Q /">$LQ$</span>.

Therefore <span data-ref="0310.g2:/ K L /">$KL$</span> is perpendicular to both <span data-ref="0310.g2:/ C L /">$CL$</span> and <span data-ref="0310.g2:/ L Q /">$LQ$</span>, and consequently to the plane <span data-ref="0310.g2:< M N >">$MN$</span>.

But, if <span data-ref="0310.g2:/ K P /">$KP$</span> be drawn in the plane <span data-ref="0310.g2:< A F >">$AF$</span> perpendicular to <span data-ref="0310.g2:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, <span data-ref="0310.g2:/ K P /">$KP$</span> is also perpendicular to the plane <span data-ref="0310.g2:< M N >">$MN$</span>.
