angle <span data-ref="0311.g3:< B A E )">$BAE$</span> be constructed, in the plane through <span data-ref="0311.g3:/ B A /">$BA$</span>, <span data-ref="0311.g3:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, equal to the angle <span data-ref="0311.g3:< D A B )">$DAB$</span>;
let <span data-ref="0311.g3:/ A E /">$AE$</span> be made equal to <span data-ref="0311.g3:/ A D /">$AD$</span>,
and let <span data-ref="0311.g3:/ B E C /">$BEC$</span>, drawn across through the point <span data-ref="0311.g3:E">$E$</span>, cut the straight lines <span data-ref="0311.g3:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0311.g3:/ A C /">$AC$</span> at the points <span data-ref="0311.g3:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0311.g3:C">$C$</span>;
let <span data-ref="0311.g3:/ D B /">$DB$</span>, <span data-ref="0311.g3:/ D C /">$DC$</span> be joined.

Now, since <span data-ref="0311.g3:/ D A /">$DA$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0311.g3:/ A E /">$AE$</span>,
and <span data-ref="0311.g3:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is common,
two sides are equal to two sides;
and the angle <span data-ref="0311.g3:< D A B )">$DAB$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0311.g3:< B A E )">$BAE$</span>;
therefore the base <span data-ref="0311.g3:/ D B /">$DB$</span> is equal to the base <span data-ref="0311.g3:/ B E /">$BE$</span>.
[I. 4]

And, since the two sides <span data-ref="0311.g3:/ B D /">$BD$</span>, <span data-ref="0311.g3:/ D C /">$DC$</span> are greater than <span data-ref="0311.g3:/ B C /">$BC$</span>,
[I. 20]
and of these <span data-ref="0311.g3:/ D B /">$DB$</span> was proved equal to <span data-ref="0311.g3:/ B E /">$BE$</span>,
therefore the remainder <span data-ref="0311.g3:/ D C /">$DC$</span> is greater than the remainder <span data-ref="0311.g3:/ E C /">$EC$</span>.

Now, since <span data-ref="0311.g3:/ D A /">$DA$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0311.g3:/ A E /">$AE$</span>,
and <span data-ref="0311.g3:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is common,
and the base <span data-ref="0311.g3:/ D C /">$DC$</span> is greater than the base <span data-ref="0311.g3:/ E C /">$EC$</span>,
therefore the angle <span data-ref="0311.g3:< D A C )">$DAC$</span> is greater than the angle <span data-ref="0311.g3:< E A C )">$EAC$</span>.
[I. 25]

But the angle <span data-ref="0311.g3:< D A B )">$DAB$</span> was also proved equal to the angle <span data-ref="0311.g3:< B A E )">$BAE$</span>;
therefore the angles <span data-ref="0311.g3:< D A B )">$DAB$</span>, <span data-ref="0311.g3:< D A C )">$DAC$</span> are greater than the angle <span data-ref="0311.g3:< B A C )">$BAC$</span>.

Similarly we can prove that the remaining angles also, taken together two and two, are greater than the remaining one.

Therefore etc.

Q. E. D.

---

After excluding the obvious case in which all three angles are equal, Euclid goes on to say "If not, let the angle <span data-ref="0311.g3:< B A C )">$BAC$</span> be greater," without adding greater than *what*. Heiberg is clearly right in saying that he means greater than <span data-ref="0311.g3:< B A D )">$BAD$</span>, i.e. greater than *one* of the adjacent angles. This is proved by the words at the end "Similarly we can prove," etc. Euclid thus excludes as obvious the case where one of the three angles is not greater than either of the other two, but proves the remaining cases. This is scientific, but he might further have excluded as obvious the case in which one angle is greater than one of the others but equal to or less than the remaining one.
