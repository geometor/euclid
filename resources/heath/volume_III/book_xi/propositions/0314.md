It will be observed that, although Euclid enunciates this proposition for *any* solid angle, he only proves it for the particular case of a *trihedral* angle. This is in accordance with his manner of proving one case and leaving the others to the reader. The omission of the convex polyhedral angle here corresponds to the omission, after I. 32, of the proposition about the interior angles of a convex polygon given by Proclus and in most books. The proof of the present proposition for any convex polyhedral angle can of course be arranged so as not to assume the proposition that the interior angles of a convex polygon together with four right angles are equal to twice as many right angles as the figure has sides.

Let there be any convex polyhedral angle with <span data-ref="0314.g1:V">$V$</span> as vertex, and let it be cut by any plane meeting its faces in, say, the polygon <span data-ref="0314.g1:< A B C D E >">$ABCDE$</span>.

Take <span data-ref="0314.g1:O">$O$</span> any point within the polygon, and in its plane, and join <span data-ref="0314.g1:/ O A /">$OA$</span>, <span data-ref="0314.g1:/ O B /">$OB$</span>, <span data-ref="0314.g1:/ O C /">$OC$</span>, <span data-ref="0314.g1:/ O D /">$OD$</span>, <span data-ref="0314.g1:/ O E /">$OE$</span>.

Then all the angles of the triangles with vertex <span data-ref="0314.g1:O">$O$</span> are equal to twice as many right angles as the polygon has sides;
[I. 32]
therefore the interior angles of the polygon together with all the angles round <span data-ref="0314.g1:O">$O$</span> are equal to twice as many right angles as the polygon has sides.

Also the sum of the angles of the triangles <span data-ref="0314.g1:< V A B >">$VAB$</span>, <span data-ref="0314.g1:< V B C >">$VBC$</span>, etc., with vertex <span data-ref="0314.g1:V">$V$</span> are equal to twice as many right angles as the polygon has sides;
and all the said angles are equal to the sum of (1) the plane angles at <span data-ref="0314.g1:V">$V$</span> forming the polyhedral angle and (2) the base angles of the triangles with vertex <span data-ref="0314.g1:V">$V$</span>.

This latter sum is therefore equal to the sum of (3) all the angles round <span data-ref="0314.g1:O">$O$</span> and (4) all the interior angles of the polygon.

And so on.
