<p class="sub-clause">And, since the three angles of each of the triangles <span data-ref="0313.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span>,<br />
<span data-ref="0313.g1:< A C D >">$ACD$</span>, <span data-ref="0313.g1:< A D B >">$ADB$</span> are equal to two right angles,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the nine angles of the three triangles, the angles<br />
<span data-ref="0313.g1:< C B A )">$CBA$</span>, <span data-ref="0313.g1:< A C B )">$ACB$</span>, <span data-ref="0313.g1:< B A C )">$BAC$</span>, <span data-ref="0313.g1:< A C D )">$ACD$</span>, <span data-ref="0313.g1:< C D A )">$CDA$</span>, <span data-ref="0313.g1:< C A D )">$CAD$</span>, <span data-ref="0313.g1:< A D B )">$ADB$</span>, <span data-ref="0313.g1:< D B A )">$DBA$</span>, <span data-ref="0313.g1:< B A D )">$BAD$</span><br />

<p class="sub-clause">and of them the six angles <span data-ref="0313.g1:< A B C )">$ABC$</span>, <span data-ref="0313.g1:< B C A )">$BCA$</span>, <span data-ref="0313.g1:< A C D )">$ACD$</span>, <span data-ref="0313.g1:< C D A )">$CDA$</span>, <span data-ref="0313.g1:< A D B )">$ADB$</span>,<br />
<span data-ref="0313.g1:< D B A )">$DBA$</span> are greater than two right angles ;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the remaining three angles <span data-ref="0313.g1:< B A C )">$BAC$</span>, <span data-ref="0313.g1:< C A D )">$CAD$</span>, <span data-ref="0313.g1:< D A B )">$DAB$</span><br />

It will be observed that, although Euclid enunciates this proposition for<br />
*any* solid angle, he only proves it for the particular case of a *trihedral* angle.<br />
This is in accordance with his manner of proving one case and leaving the<br />
others to the reader. The omission of the convex polyhedral angle here<br />
corresponds to the omission, after I. 32, of the proposition about the interior<br />
angles of a convex polygon given by Proclus and in most books. The proof<br />
of the present proposition for any convex polyhedral angle can of course be<br />
arranged so as not to assume the proposition that the interior angles of a<br />
convex polygon together with four right angles are equal to twice as many<br />
right angles as the figure has sides.

Let there be any convex polyhedral angle with <span data-ref="0314.g1:V">$V$</span> as vertex, and let it be<br />
cut by any plane meeting its faces in, say, the<br />
polygon <span data-ref="0314.g1:< A B C D E >">$ABCDE$</span>.

Take <span data-ref="0314.g1:O">$O$</span> any point within the polygon, and<br />
in its plane, and join <span data-ref="0314.g1:/ O A /">$OA$</span>, <span data-ref="0314.g1:/ O B /">$OB$</span>, <span data-ref="0314.g1:/ O C /">$OC$</span>, <span data-ref="0314.g1:/ O D /">$OD$</span>, <span data-ref="0314.g1:/ O E /">$OE$</span>.

Then all the angles of the triangles with<br />
vertex <span data-ref="0314.g1:O">$O$</span> are equal to twice as many right angles<br />
as the polygon has sides; <span class="justification">[I. 32]</span><br />
therefore the interior angles of the polygon to-<br />
gether with all the angles round <span data-ref="0314.g1:O">$O$</span> are equal to<br />
twice as many right angles as the polygon has<br />
sides.

Also the sum of the angles of the triangles<br />
<span data-ref="0314.g1:< V A B >">$VAB$</span>, <span data-ref="0314.g1:< V B C >">$VBC$</span>, etc., with vertex <span data-ref="0314.g1:V">$V$</span> are equal to twice as many right angles as the<br />
polygon has sides;<br />
and all the said angles are equal to the sum of (1) the plane angles at <span data-ref="0314.g1:V">$V$</span><br />
forming the polyhedral angle and (2) the base angles of the triangles with<br />
vertex <span data-ref="0314.g1:V">$V$</span>.

This latter sum is therefore equal to the sum of (3) all the angles<br />
round <span data-ref="0314.g1:O">$O$</span> and (4) all the interior angles of the polygon.

Now, by Euclid's proposition, of the three angles forming the solid angle at<br />
<span data-ref="0314.g1:A">$A$</span>, the angles <span data-ref="0314.g1:< V A E )">$VAE$</span>, <span data-ref="0314.g1:< V A B )">$VAB$</span> are together greater than the angle <span data-ref="0314.g1:< E A B )">$EAB$</span>.

Similarly, at <span data-ref="0314.g1:B">$B$</span>, the angles <span data-ref="0314.g1:< V B A )">$VBA$</span>, <span data-ref="0314.g1:< V B C )">$VBC$</span> are together greater than the angle<br />
<span data-ref="0314.g1:< A B C )">$ABC$</span>.

And so on.
