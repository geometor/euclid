So are the triangles <span data-ref="0315.g1:< V A C >">$VAC$</span>, <span data-ref="0315.g1:< V' A' C' >">$V'A'C'$</span>, and the triangles <span data-ref="0315.g1:< V B C >">$VBC$</span>, <span data-ref="0315.g1:< V' B' C' >">$V'B'C'$</span>.<br />
Thus <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ C A /">$CA$</span>, <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> are respectively equal to <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ B' C' /">$B'C'$</span>, <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ C' A' /">$C'A'$</span>, <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ A' B' /">$A'B'$</span>, and the<br />
triangles <span data-ref="0315.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span>, <span data-ref="0315.g1:< A' B' C' >">$A'B'C'$</span> are equal in all respects.

the angles <span data-ref="0315.g1:< A D E )">$ADE$</span>, <span data-ref="0315.g1:< D A E )">$DAE$</span> are equal to the angles <span data-ref="0315.g1:< A' D' E' )">$A'D'E'$</span>, <span data-ref="0315.g1:< D' A' E' )">$D'A'E'$</span> respectively,<br />
and <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0315.g1:/ A' D' /">$A'D'$</span>.<br />
Therefore the triangles <span data-ref="0315.g1:< A D E >">$ADE$</span>, <span data-ref="0315.g1:< A' D' E' >">$A'D'E'$</span> are equal in all respects.<br />

and the angle <span data-ref="0315.g1:< E A F )">$EAF$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0315.g1:< E' A' F' )">$E'A'F'$</span> (from above);<br />
therefore the triangles <span data-ref="0315.g1:< A E F >">$AEF$</span>, <span data-ref="0315.g1:< A' E' F' >">$A'E'F'$</span> are equal in all respects.

Lastly, in the triangles <span data-ref="0315.g1:< D E F >">$DEF$</span>, <span data-ref="0315.g1:< D' E' F' >">$D'E'F'$</span>, the three sides are respectively<br />
equal to the three sides;<br />
therefore the triangles are equal in all respects.<br />
Therefore the angles <span data-ref="0315.g1:< E D F )">$EDF$</span>, <span data-ref="0315.g1:< E' D' F' )">$E'D'F'$</span> are equal.

But these angles are the measures of the dihedral angles formed by the<br />
planes <span data-ref="0315.g1:< C V A >">$CVA$</span>, <span data-ref="0315.g1:< A V B >">$AVB$</span> and by the planes <span data-ref="0315.g1:< C' V' A' >">$C'V'A'$</span>, <span data-ref="0315.g1:< A' V' B' >">$A'V'B'$</span> respectively.<br />
Therefore these dihedral angles are equal.<br />
Similarly for the other two dihedral angles.<br />
Hence the trihedral angles coincide if one is applied to the other;<br />
that is, they are equal.

To understand what is implied by “taken in the same order” we may<br />
suppose ourselves to be placed at the vertices, and to take the faces in clock-<br />
wise direction, or the reverse, for *both* angles.

If the face angles and dihedral angles are *taken in reverse directions*, i.e.<br />
in clockwise direction in one and in counterclockwise direction in the other,<br />
then, if the other conditions in the above three propositions are fulfilled, the<br />
trihedral angles are not equal but *symmetrical*.

If the faces of a trihedral angle be produced beyond the vertex, they form<br />
another trihedral angle. It is easily seen that these *vertical trihedral angles*<br />
are *symmetrical*.
