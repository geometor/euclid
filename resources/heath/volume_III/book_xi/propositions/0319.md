Let the angles <span data-ref="0319.g1:< A B C )">$ABC$</span>, <span data-ref="0319.g1:< D E F )">$DEF$</span>, <span data-ref="0319.g1:< G H K )">$GHK$</span> be the three given plane angles, and let two of these, taken together in any manner, be greater than the remaining one, while, further, the three are less than four right angles;
thus it is required to construct a solid angle out of angles equal to the angles <span data-ref="0319.g1:< A B C )">$ABC$</span>, <span data-ref="0319.g1:< D E F )">$DEF$</span>, <span data-ref="0319.g1:< G H K )">$GHK$</span>.

![Proposition 23, Figure 1](0319.g1.svg)

Let <span data-ref="0319.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0319.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, <span data-ref="0319.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>, <span data-ref="0319.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, <span data-ref="0319.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span>, <span data-ref="0319.g1:/ H K /">$HK$</span> be cut off equal to one another,
and let <span data-ref="0319.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0319.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span>, <span data-ref="0319.g1:/ G K /">$GK$</span> be joined;
it is therefore possible to construct a triangle out of straight lines equal to <span data-ref="0319.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0319.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span>, <span data-ref="0319.g1:/ G K /">$GK$</span>. [XI. 22]
Let <span data-ref="0319.g2:< L M N >">$LMN$</span> be so constructed that <span data-ref="0319.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0319.g2:/ L M /">$LM$</span>, <span data-ref="0319.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span> to <span data-ref="0319.g2:/ M N /">$MN$</span>, and further <span data-ref="0319.g1:/ G K /">$GK$</span> to <span data-ref="0319.g2:/ N L /">$NL$</span>,
let the circle <span data-ref="0319.g2:( L M N )">$LMN$</span> be described about the triangle <span data-ref="0319.g2:< L M N >">$LMN$</span>,
let its centre be taken, and let it be <span data-ref="0319.g2:O">$O$</span>;
let <span data-ref="0319.g2:/ L O /">$LO$</span>, <span data-ref="0319.g2:/ M O /">$MO$</span>, <span data-ref="0319.g2:/ N O /">$NO$</span> be joined;
I say that <span data-ref="0319.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is greater than <span data-ref="0319.g2:/ L O /">$LO$</span>.

![Proposition 23, Figure 2](0319.g2.svg)

For, if not, <span data-ref="0319.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is either equal to <span data-ref="0319.g2:/ L O /">$LO$</span>, or less.
First, let it be equal.
Then, since <span data-ref="0319.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0319.g2:/ L O /">$LO$</span>,
while <span data-ref="0319.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0319.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, and <span data-ref="0319.g2:/ O L /">$OL$</span> to <span data-ref="0319.g2:/ O M /">$OM$</span>,
the two sides <span data-ref="0319.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0319.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> are equal to the two sides <span data-ref="0319.g2:/ L O /">$LO$</span>, <span data-ref="0319.g2:/ O M /">$OM$</span> respectively;
and, by hypothesis, the base <span data-ref="0319.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is equal to the base <span data-ref="0319.g2:/ L M /">$LM$</span>;
therefore the angle <span data-ref="0319.g1:< A B C )">$ABC$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0319.g2:< L O M )">$LOM$</span>. [I. 8]
For the same reason the angle <span data-ref="0319.g1:< D E F )">$DEF$</span> is also equal to the angle <span data-ref="0319.g2:< M O N )">$MON$</span>,
and further the angle <span data-ref="0319.g1:< G H K )">$GHK$</span> to the angle <span data-ref="0319.g2:< N O L )">$NOL$</span>;
