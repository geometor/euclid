Hence their vertical angles are equal to the three given angles respectively; and the required solid angle is constructed.
We return now to the proposition to be proved as a preliminary to the construction, viz. that, in the figures, <span data-ref="0319.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is greater than <span data-ref="0319.g2:/ L O /">$LO$</span>.
It will be observed that Euclid, as his manner is, proves it for one case only, that, namely, in which <span data-ref="0319.g2:O">$O$</span>, the centre of the circle circumscribing the triangle <span data-ref="0319.g2:< L M N >">$LMN$</span>, falls *within* the triangle, leaving the other cases for the reader to prove. As usual, however, the two other cases are found in the Greek text, after the formal conclusion of the proposition, as above, ending with the words ὅπερ ἔδει ποιῆσαι. This position for the proofs itself suggests that they are not Euclid’s but are interpolated; and this is rendered certain by the fact that words distinguishing three cases at the point where the centre <span data-ref="0319.g2:O">$O$</span> of the circumscribing circle is found, “It [the centre] will then be either within the triangle <span data-ref="0319.g2:< L M N >">$LMN$</span> or on one of its sides or without. First let it be within,” are found in the MSS. B and V only and are manifestly interpolated. Nevertheless the additional two cases must have been inserted very early, as they are found in all the best MSS.
In order to give a clear view of the proof of all three cases as given in the text, we will reproduce all three (Euclid’s as well as the others) with abbreviations to make them catch the eye better.
In all three cases the proof is by *reductio ad absurdum*, and it is proved first that <span data-ref="0319.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> cannot be *equal* to <span data-ref="0319.g2:/ L O /">$LO$</span>, and secondly that <span data-ref="0319.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> cannot be *less* than <span data-ref="0319.g2:/ L O /">$LO$</span>.

#### Case I.
(1) Suppose, if possible, that <span data-ref="0319.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> $=$ <span data-ref="0319.g2:/ L O /">$LO$</span>.
Then <span data-ref="0319.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0319.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> are respectively equal to <span data-ref="0319.g2:/ L O /">$LO$</span>, <span data-ref="0319.g2:/ O M /">$OM$</span>;
and <span data-ref="0319.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> $=$ <span data-ref="0319.g2:/ L M /">$LM$</span> (by construction).
Therefore <span data-ref="0319.g1:< A B C )">$\angle ABC$</span> $=$ <span data-ref="0319.g2:< L O M )">$\angle LOM$</span>.
Similarly <span data-ref="0319.g1:< D E F )">$\angle DEF$</span> $=$ <span data-ref="0319.g2:< M O N )">$\angle MON$</span>,
<span data-ref="0319.g1:< G H K )">$\angle GHK$</span> $=$ <span data-ref="0319.g2:< N O L )">$\angle NOL$</span>,
Adding, we have
<span data-ref="0319.g1:< A B C )">$\angle ABC$</span> $+$ <span data-ref="0319.g1:< D E F )">$\angle DEF$</span> $+$ <span data-ref="0319.g1:< G H K )">$\angle GHK$</span> $=$ <span data-ref="0319.g2:< L O M )">$\angle LOM$</span> $+$ <span data-ref="0319.g2:< M O N )">$\angle MON$</span> $+$ <span data-ref="0319.g2:< N O L )">$\angle NOL$</span>
$=$ four right angles:
which contradicts the hypothesis.
Therefore <span data-ref="0319.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> $\ne$ <span data-ref="0319.g2:/ L O /">$LO$</span>.
