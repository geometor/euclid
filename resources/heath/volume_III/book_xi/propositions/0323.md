We return now to the proposition to be proved as a preliminary to the<br />
construction, viz. that, in the figures, <span data-ref="0319.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is greater than <span data-ref="0319.g2:/ L O /">$LO$</span>.

It will be observed that Euclid, as his manner is, proves it for one case<br />
only, that, namely, in which <span data-ref="0319.g2:O">$O$</span>, the centre of the circle circumscribing the<br />
triangle <span data-ref="0319.g2:< L M N >">$LMN$</span>, falls *within* the triangle, leaving the other cases for the reader<br />
to prove. As usual, however, the two other cases are found in the Greek text,<br />
after the formal conclusion of the proposition, as above, ending with the words<br />
ὅπερ ἔδει ποιῆσαι. This position for the proofs itself suggests that they are not<br />
Euclid’s but are interpolated; and this is rendered certain by the fact that<br />
words distinguishing three cases at the point where the centre <span data-ref="0319.g2:O">$O$</span> of the<br />
circumscribing circle is found, “It [the centre] will then be either within the<br />
triangle <span data-ref="0319.g2:< L M N >">$LMN$</span> or on one of its sides or without. First let it be within,” are<br />
found in the MSS. B and V only and are manifestly interpolated. Nevertheless<br />
the additional two cases must have been inserted very early, as they are found<br />
in all the best MSS.

In order to give a clear view of the proof of all three cases as given in the<br />
text, we will reproduce all three (Euclid’s as well as the others) with abbrevia-<br />
tions to make them catch the eye better.

In all three cases the proof is by *reductio ad absurdum*, and it is proved<br />
first that <span data-ref="0319.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> cannot be *equal* to <span data-ref="0319.g2:/ L O /">$LO$</span>, and secondly that <span data-ref="0319.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> cannot be *less*<br />
than <span data-ref="0319.g2:/ L O /">$LO$</span>.

### Case I.

(1) Suppose, if possible, that <span data-ref="0319.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> $=$ <span data-ref="0319.g2:/ L O /">$LO$</span>.<br />
Then <span data-ref="0319.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0319.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> are respectively equal to <span data-ref="0319.g2:/ L O /">$LO$</span>, <span data-ref="0319.g2:/ O M /">$OM$</span>;<br />
and <span data-ref="0319.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> $=$ <span data-ref="0319.g2:/ L M /">$LM$</span> (by construction).<br />
Therefore <span data-ref="0319.g1:< A B C )">$\angle ABC$</span> $=$ <span data-ref="0319.g2:< L O M )">$\angle LOM$</span>.<br />
Similarly <span data-ref="0319.g1:< D E F )">$\angle DEF$</span> $=$ <span data-ref="0319.g2:< M O N )">$\angle MON$</span>,<br />
<span data-ref="0319.g1:< G H K )">$\angle GHK$</span> $=$ <span data-ref="0319.g2:< N O L )">$\angle NOL$</span>,<br />
Adding, we have<br />
<span data-ref="0319.g1:< A B C )">$\angle ABC$</span> $+$ <span data-ref="0319.g1:< D E F )">$\angle DEF$</span> $+$ <span data-ref="0319.g1:< G H K )">$\angle GHK$</span> $=$ <span data-ref="0319.g2:< L O M )">$\angle LOM$</span> $+$ <span data-ref="0319.g2:< M O N )">$\angle MON$</span> $+$ <span data-ref="0319.g2:< N O L )">$\angle NOL$</span><br />
$=$ four right angles:<br />
which contradicts the hypothesis.<br />
Therefore <span data-ref="0319.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> $\ne$ <span data-ref="0319.g2:/ L O /">$LO$</span>.
