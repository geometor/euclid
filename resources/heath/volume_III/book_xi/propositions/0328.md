<p class="assertion">Therefore <span data-ref="0327.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is parallel to <span data-ref="0327.g1:/ D C /">$DC$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">Again, since the two parallel planes <span data-ref="0327.g1:< B F >">$BF$</span>, <span data-ref="0327.g1:< A E >">$AE$</span> are cut by<br />
the plane <span data-ref="0327.g1:< A C >">$AC$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">their common sections are parallel. <span class="justification">[XI. 16]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore <span data-ref="0327.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is parallel to <span data-ref="0327.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">But <span data-ref="0327.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> was also proved parallel to <span data-ref="0327.g1:/ D C /">$DC$</span>;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0327.g1:< A C >">$AC$</span> is a parallelogram.</p>

<p class="assertion">Similarly we can prove that each of the planes <span data-ref="0327.g1:< D F >">$DF$</span>, <span data-ref="0327.g1:< F G >">$FG$</span>,<br />
<span data-ref="0327.g1:< G B >">$GB$</span>, <span data-ref="0327.g1:< B F >">$BF$</span>, <span data-ref="0327.g1:< A E >">$AE$</span> is a parallelogram.</p>

Let <span data-ref="0327.g1:/ A H /">$AH$</span>, <span data-ref="0327.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span> be joined.

<p class="sub-clause">Then, since <span data-ref="0327.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is parallel to <span data-ref="0327.g1:/ D C /">$DC$</span>, and <span data-ref="0327.g1:/ B H /">$BH$</span> to <span data-ref="0327.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span>,<br />
the two straight lines <span data-ref="0327.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0327.g1:/ B H /">$BH$</span> which meet one another are<br />
parallel to the two straight lines <span data-ref="0327.g1:/ D C /">$DC$</span>, <span data-ref="0327.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span> which meet one<br />
another, not in the same plane;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore they will contain equal angles; <span class="justification">[XI. 10]</span><br />
therefore the angle <span data-ref="0327.g1:< A B H )">$ABH$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0327.g1:< D C F )">$DCF$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">And, since the two sides <span data-ref="0327.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0327.g1:/ B H /">$BH$</span> are equal to the two<br />
sides <span data-ref="0327.g1:/ D C /">$DC$</span>, <span data-ref="0327.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span>, <span class="justification">[I. 34]</span><br />

<p class="assertion">therefore the base <span data-ref="0327.g1:/ A H /">$AH$</span> is equal to the base <span data-ref="0327.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span>,<br />

<p class="sub-clause">And the parallelogram <span data-ref="0327.g1:< B G >">$BG$</span> is double of the triangle <span data-ref="0327.g1:< A B H >">$ABH$</span>,<br />

<p class="assertion">therefore the parallelogram <span data-ref="0327.g1:< B G >">$BG$</span> is equal to the parallelo-<br />
gram <span data-ref="0327.g1:< C E >">$CE$</span>.</p>

<p class="assertion">Similarly we can prove that<br />
<span data-ref="0327.g1:< A C >">$AC$</span> is also equal to <span data-ref="0327.g1:< G F >">$GF$</span>,<br />

As Heiberg says, this proposition is carelessly enunciated. Euclid means<br />
a solid contained by *six* planes and not more, the planes are *parallel two and<br />
two*, and the opposite faces are equal in the sense of identically equal, or, as<br />
Simson puts it, *equal and similar*. The similarity is necessary in order to<br />
enable the equality of the parallelepipeds in the next proposition to be inferred<br />
from the 10th definition of Book XI. Hence a better enunciation would be:

<p class="enunciation">If a solid be contained by six planes parallel two and two, the opposite faces<br />
respectively are equal and similar parallelograms.</p>

The proof is simple and requires no elucidation.
