----

As :person:`Heiberg <heiberg>` says, this proposition is carelessly enunciated. Euclid means a solid contained by *six* planes and not more, the planes are *parallel two and two*, and the opposite faces are equal in the sense of identically equal, or, as :person:`Simson <simson>` puts it, *equal and similar*. The similarity is necessary in order to enable the equality of the parallelepipeds in the next proposition to be inferred from the 10th definition of Book XI. Hence a better enunciation would be:

.. enunciation::

   If a solid be contained by six planes parallel two and two, the opposite faces respectively are equal and similar parallelograms.

The proof is simple and requires no elucidation.
