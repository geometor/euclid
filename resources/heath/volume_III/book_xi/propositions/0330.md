<p class="sub-clause">But the three planes are equal to the three opposite;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the three solids <span data-ref="0329.g1:< L Q >">$LQ$</span>, <span data-ref="0329.g1:< K R >">$KR$</span>, <span data-ref="0329.g1:< A U >">$AU$</span> are equal to one<br />
another.</p>

<p class="assertion">For the same reason<br />
the three solids <span data-ref="0329.g1:< E D >">$ED$</span>, <span data-ref="0329.g1:< D M >">$DM$</span>, <span data-ref="0329.g1:< M T >">$MT$</span> are also equal to one another.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore, whatever multiple the base <span data-ref="0329.g1:< L F >">$LF$</span> is of the base<br />
<span data-ref="0329.g1:< A F >">$AF$</span>, the same multiple also is the solid <span data-ref="0329.g1:< L U >">$LU$</span> of the solid <span data-ref="0329.g1:< A U >">$AU$</span>.</p>

<p class="assertion">For the same reason,<br />
whatever multiple the base <span data-ref="0329.g1:< N F >">$NF$</span> is of the base <span data-ref="0329.g1:< F H >">$FH$</span>, the same<br />
multiple also is the solid <span data-ref="0329.g1:< N U >">$NU$</span> of the solid <span data-ref="0329.g1:< H U >">$HU$</span>.</p>

<p class="assertion">And, if the base <span data-ref="0329.g1:< L F >">$LF$</span> is equal to the base <span data-ref="0329.g1:< N F >">$NF$</span>, the solid <span data-ref="0329.g1:< L U >">$LU$</span><br />
is also equal to the solid <span data-ref="0329.g1:< N U >">$NU$</span>;<br />
if the base <span data-ref="0329.g1:< L F >">$LF$</span> exceeds the base <span data-ref="0329.g1:< N F >">$NF$</span>, the solid <span data-ref="0329.g1:< L U >">$LU$</span> also<br />
exceeds the solid <span data-ref="0329.g1:< N U >">$NU$</span>;<br />
and, if one falls short, the other falls short.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore, there being four magnitudes, the two bases<br />
<span data-ref="0329.g1:< A F >">$AF$</span>, <span data-ref="0329.g1:< F H >">$FH$</span>, and the two solids <span data-ref="0329.g1:< A U >">$AU$</span>, <span data-ref="0329.g1:< U H >">$UH$</span>,<br />
equimultiples have been taken of the base <span data-ref="0329.g1:< A F >">$AF$</span> and the solid<br />
<span data-ref="0329.g1:< A U >">$AU$</span>, namely the base <span data-ref="0329.g1:< L F >">$LF$</span> and the solid <span data-ref="0329.g1:< L U >">$LU$</span>,<br />
and equimultiples of the base <span data-ref="0329.g1:< H F >">$HF$</span> and the solid <span data-ref="0329.g1:< H U >">$HU$</span>, namely<br />
the base <span data-ref="0329.g1:< N F >">$NF$</span> and the solid <span data-ref="0329.g1:< N U >">$NU$</span>,<br />
and it has been proved that, if the base <span data-ref="0329.g1:< L F >">$LF$</span> exceeds the base<br />
<span data-ref="0329.g1:< F N >">$FN$</span>, the solid <span data-ref="0329.g1:< L U >">$LU$</span> also exceeds the solid <span data-ref="0329.g1:< N U >">$NU$</span>,<br />
if the bases are equal, the solids are equal,<br />
and if the base falls short, the solid falls short.</p>

<p class="qed">Therefore, as the base <span data-ref="0329.g1:< A F >">$AF$</span> is to the base <span data-ref="0329.g1:< F H >">$FH$</span>, so is the<br />

<span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

It is to be observed that, as the word *parallelogrammic* was used in Book I.<br />
without any definition of its meaning, so παραλληλεπίπεδος, *parallelepipedal*, is<br />
here used without explanation. While it means simply "with parallel planes,"<br />
i.e. "faces," the term is appropriated to the particular solid which has *six*<br />
plane faces parallel two and two. The proper translation of στερεὸν<br />
παραλληλεπίπεδον is *parallelepipedal solid*, not *solid parallelepiped*, as it is<br />
usually translated. Still less is the solid a *parallelopiped*, as the word is not<br />
uncommonly written.
