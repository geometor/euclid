.. subclause::

   But the three planes are equal to the three opposite;

.. assertion::

   therefore the three solids :g:`LQ <0329.g1:< L Q >>`, :g:`KR <0329.g1:< K R >>`, :g:`AU <0329.g1:< A U >>` are :eq:`equal <0329.g1:< L Q > = 0329.g1:< K R > = 0329.g1:< A U >>` to one another.

   Therefore, whatever multiple the base :g:`LF <0329.g1:< L F >>` is of the base :g:`AF <0329.g1:< A F >>`, the same multiple also is the solid :g:`LU <0329.g1:< L U >>` of the solid :g:`AU <0329.g1:< A U >>`.

It is to be observed that, as the word *parallelogrammic* was used in Book I. without any definition of its meaning, so :term:`παραλληλεπίπεδος <parallelepiped>`, *parallelepipedal*, is here used without explanation. While it means simply "with parallel planes," i.e. "faces," the term is appropriated to the particular solid which has *six* plane faces parallel two and two. The proper translation of στερεὸν παραλληλεπίπεδον is *parallelepipedal solid*, not *solid parallelepiped*, as it is usually translated. Still less is the solid a *parallelopiped*, as the word is not uncommonly written.
