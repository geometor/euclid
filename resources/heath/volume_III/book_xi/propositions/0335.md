Simson properly observes that it ought to be proved that the diagonals of<br />
two opposite faces are in one plane, before we speak of drawing a plane<br />
through them. Clavius supplied the proof, which is of course simple enough.

Since <span data-ref="0334.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, <span data-ref="0334.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> are both parallel to <span data-ref="0334.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span> or <span data-ref="0334.g1:/ B H /">$BH$</span>, they are parallel to one<br />
another.

Consequently a plane can be drawn through <span data-ref="0334.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, <span data-ref="0334.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> and the diagonals<br />
<span data-ref="0334.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>, <span data-ref="0334.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span> are in that plane <span class="justification">[XI. 7]</span>. Moreover <span data-ref="0334.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, <span data-ref="0334.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> are equal as well as<br />
parallel ; so that <span data-ref="0334.g1:/ C F /">$CF$</span>, <span data-ref="0334.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> are also equal and parallel.

Simson does not, however, seem to have noticed a more serious difficulty.<br />
The two prisms are shown by Euclid to be contained by equal faces—the faces<br />
are in fact equal and similar—and Euclid then infers at once that the prisms<br />
are *equal*. But they are not equal in the only sense in which we have, at<br />
present, a right to speak of solids being equal, namely in the sense that they<br />
can be *applied*, the one to the other. They cannot be so applied because the<br />
faces, though equal respectively, are not *similarly arranged* ; consequently the<br />
prisms are *symmetrical*, and it ought to be proved that they are, though not<br />
equal and similar, equal in content, or *equivalent*, as Legendre has it.

Legendre addressed himself to proving that the two prisms are equivalent,<br />
and his method has been adopted, though his<br />
name is not mentioned, by Schultze and Seven-<br />
oak and by Holgate. Certain preliminary pro-<br />
positions are necessary.

**1.** *The sections of a prism made by parallel<br />
planes cutting all the lateral edges are equal<br />
polygons.*

Suppose a prism <span data-ref="0335.g1:< M N >">$MN$</span> cut by parallel planes<br />
which make sections <span data-ref="0335.g1:< A B C D E >">$ABCDE$</span>, <span data-ref="0335.g1:< A' B' C' D' E' >">$A'B'C'D'E'$</span>.

Now <span data-ref="0335.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0335.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, <span data-ref="0335.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, ... are respectively parallel<br />
to <span data-ref="0335.g1:/ A' B' /">$A'B'$</span>, <span data-ref="0335.g1:/ B' C' /">$B'C'$</span>, <span data-ref="0335.g1:/ C' D' /">$C'D'$</span>, ... <span class="justification">[XI. 16]</span>

Therefore the angles <span data-ref="0335.g1:< A B C )">$ABC$</span>, <span data-ref="0335.g1:< B C D )">$BCD$</span>, ... are<br />
equal to the angles <span data-ref="0335.g1:< A' B' C' )">$A'B'C'$</span>, <span data-ref="0335.g1:< B' C' D' )">$B'C'D'$</span>, ... respec-<br />
tively. <span class="justification">[XI. 10]</span>

Also <span data-ref="0335.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0335.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, <span data-ref="0335.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, ... are respectively equal<br />
to <span data-ref="0335.g1:/ A' B' /">$A'B'$</span>, <span data-ref="0335.g1:/ B' C' /">$B'C'$</span>, <span data-ref="0335.g1:/ C' D' /">$C'D'$</span>, ... <span class="justification">[I. 34]</span>

Thus the polygons <span data-ref="0335.g1:< A B C D E >">$ABCDE$</span>, <span data-ref="0335.g1:< A' B' C' D' E' >">$A'B'C'D'E'$</span> are equilateral and equiangular<br />
to one another.

**2.** *Two prisms are equal when they have a solid angle in each contained by<br />
three faces equal each to each and similarly arranged.*

Let the faces <span data-ref="0336.g1:< A B C D E >">$ABCDE$</span>, <span data-ref="0336.g1:< A G >">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0336.g1:< A L >">$AL$</span> be equal and similarly placed to the<br />
faces <span data-ref="0336.g1:< A' B' C' D' E' >">$A'B'C'D'E'$</span>, <span data-ref="0336.g1:< A' G' >">$A'G'$</span>, <span data-ref="0336.g1:< A' L' >">$A'L'$</span>.

Since the three plane angles at <span data-ref="0336.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0336.g1:A'">$A'$</span> are equal respectively and are<br />
similarly placed, the trihedral angle at <span data-ref="0336.g1:A">$A$</span> is equal to the trihedral angle at <span data-ref="0336.g1:A'">$A'$</span>.<br />
<span class="justification">[(3) in note to XI. 21]</span>
