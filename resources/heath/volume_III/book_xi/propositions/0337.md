Now suppose the parallelepiped of Euclid's proposition to be cut by the plane through <span data-ref="0337.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0337.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span>.

Let <span data-ref="0337.g1:< K L M N >">$KLMN$</span> be a right section of the parallelepiped cutting the edges <span data-ref="0337.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0337.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, <span data-ref="0337.g1:/ G F /">$GF$</span>, <span data-ref="0337.g1:/ H E /">$HE$</span>.

Then <span data-ref="0337.g1:< K L M N >">$KLMN$</span> is a parallelogram; and, if the diagonal <span data-ref="0337.g1:/ K M /">$KM$</span> be drawn,
<p align="center"><span data-ref="0337.g1:< K L M >">$\triangle KLM$</span> = <span data-ref="0337.g1:< M N K >">$\triangle MNK$</span>.</p>

![Parallelepiped cut by plane](0337.g1.svg)

Now the prism of which the $\Delta\text{s}$ <span data-ref="0337.g1:< A B G >">$ABG$</span>, <span data-ref="0337.g1:< D C F >">$DCF$</span> are the bases is equal to the right prism on <span data-ref="0337.g1:< K L M >">$\triangle KLM$</span> as base and of height <span data-ref="0337.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>.

Similarly the prism of which the $\Delta\text{s}$ <span data-ref="0337.g1:< A G H >">$AGH$</span>, <span data-ref="0337.g1:< D F E >">$DFE$</span> are the bases is equal to the right prism on <span data-ref="0337.g1:< M N K >">$\triangle MNK$</span> as base and with height <span data-ref="0337.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>. [(3) above]

And the right prisms on $\Delta\text{s}$ <span data-ref="0337.g1:< K L M >">$KLM$</span>, <span data-ref="0337.g1:< M N K >">$MNK$</span> as bases and of equal height <span data-ref="0337.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> are equal. [(2), Cor. above]

Consequently the two prisms into which the parallelepiped is divided are equivalent.

### PROPOSITION 29.

*Parallelepipedal solids which are on the same base and of the same height, and in which the extremities of the sides which stand up are on the same straight lines, are equal to one another.*

Let <span data-ref="0337.g2:< C M >">$CM$</span>, <span data-ref="0337.g2:< C N >">$CN$</span> be parallelepipedal solids on the same base <span data-ref="0337.g2:< A B >">$AB$</span> and of the same height, and let the extremities of their sides which stand up, namely <span data-ref="0337.g2:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0337.g2:/ A F /">$AF$</span>, <span data-ref="0337.g2:/ L M /">$LM$</span>, <span data-ref="0337.g2:/ L N /">$LN$</span>, <span data-ref="0337.g2:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, <span data-ref="0337.g2:/ C E /">$CE$</span>, <span data-ref="0337.g2:/ B H /">$BH$</span>, <span data-ref="0337.g2:/ B K /">$BK$</span>, be on the same straight lines <span data-ref="0337.g2:/ F N /">$FN$</span>, <span data-ref="0337.g2:/ D K /">$DK$</span>;  
I say that the solid <span data-ref="0337.g2:< C M >">$CM$</span> is equal to the solid <span data-ref="0337.g2:< C N >">$CN$</span>.

![Proposition 29](0337.g2.svg)

For, since each of the figures <span data-ref="0337.g2:< C H >">$CH$</span>, <span data-ref="0337.g2:< C K >">$CK$</span> is a parallelogram, <span data-ref="0337.g2:/ C B /">$CB$</span> is equal to each of the straight lines <span data-ref="0337.g2:/ D H /">$DH$</span>, <span data-ref="0337.g2:/ E K /">$EK$</span>, [I. 34]  
hence <span data-ref="0337.g2:/ D H /">$DH$</span> is also equal to <span data-ref="0337.g2:/ E K /">$EK$</span>.

Let <span data-ref="0337.g2:/ E H /">$EH$</span> be subtracted from each;  
therefore the remainder <span data-ref="0337.g2:/ D E /">$DE$</span> is equal to the remainder <span data-ref="0337.g2:/ H K /">$HK$</span>.

Hence the triangle <span data-ref="0337.g2:< D C E >">$DCE$</span> is also equal to the triangle <span data-ref="0337.g2:< H B K >">$HBK$</span>, [I. 8, 4]  
and the parallelogram <span data-ref="0337.g2:< D G >">$DG$</span> to the parallelogram <span data-ref="0337.g2:< H N >">$HN$</span>. [I. 36]
