Joining <span data-ref="0340.g1:/ O P /">$OP$</span>, <span data-ref="0340.g1:/ N Q /">$NQ$</span>, we have a rectangular parallelepiped on <span data-ref="0340.g1:< A O N D >">$AOND$</span> as base<br />

base <span data-ref="0340.g1:< A D M I >">$ADMI$</span> and of the same height (<span data-ref="0340.g1:/ A O /">$AO$</span>).

That is, a rectangular parallelepiped has been constructed which is<br />
equivalent to the given parallelepiped and has (1) the same height, (2) an<br />
equivalent base.

The American text-books which I have quoted adopt a somewhat different<br />
construction shown in the subjoined figure.
