:title: Proposition 33 (concluded)

.. assertion::

   | hence the solid :g:`$AB$ <0346.g1:< A B >>` has also to :g:`$KP$ <0346.g1:< K P >>` the ratio triplicate of that
   | which :g:`$AE$ <0346.g1:/ A E />` has to :g:`$EK$ <0346.g1:/ E K />`.

.. subclause::

   | But the solid :g:`$KP$ <0346.g1:< K P >>` is equal to the solid :g:`$CD$ <0346.g1:< C D >>`,
   | and the straight line :g:`$EK$ <0346.g1:/ E K />` to :g:`$CF$ <0346.g1:/ C F />`;

.. assertion::

   | therefore the solid :g:`$AB$ <0346.g1:< A B >>` has also to the solid :g:`$CD$ <0346.g1:< C D >>` the ratio
   | triplicate of that which the corresponding side of it, :g:`$AE$ <0346.g1:/ A E />`, has
   | to the corresponding side :g:`$CF$ <0346.g1:/ C F />`.

.. qed::

   Therefore etc. :just:`Q. E. D.`

.. assertion::

   | PORISM. From this it is manifest that, if four straight
   | lines be &lt;continuously&gt; proportional, as the first is to the
   | fourth, so will a parallelepipedal solid on the first be to the
   | similar and similarly described parallelepipedal solid on the
   | second, inasmuch as the first has to the fourth the ratio
   | triplicate of that which it has to the second.

----

The proof may be summarised as follows.

| The three edges :g:`$AE$ <0346.g1:/ A E />`, :g:`$GE$ <0346.g1:/ G E />`, :g:`$HE$ <0346.g1:/ H E />` of the parallelepiped :g:`$AB$ <0346.g1:< A B >>` which meet at
| :g:`$E$ <0346.g1:E>`, the vertex corresponding to :g:`$R$ <0346.g1:R>` in the other parallelepiped, are produced,
| and lengths :g:`$EK$ <0346.g1:/ E K />`, :g:`$EL$ <0346.g1:/ E L />`, :g:`$EM$ <0346.g1:/ E M />` are marked off equal respectively to the edges :g:`$CF$ <0346.g1:/ C F />`,
| :g:`$FN$ <0346.g1:/ F N />`, :g:`$FR$ <0346.g1:/ F R />` of :g:`$CD$ <0346.g1:< C D >>`.

The parallelograms and solids are then completed as shown in the figure.

| Euclid first shows that the solid :g:`$CD$ <0346.g1:< C D >>` and the new solid :g:`$PK$ <0346.g1:< P K >>` are equal and
| similar according to the criterion in XI. Def. 10, viz. that they are contained
| by the same number of equal and similar planes. (They are arranged in the
| same order, and it would be easy to prove equality by proving the equality of
| a pair of solid angles and then applying one solid to the other.)

We have now, by hypothesis,

$$AE : CF = EG : FN = EH : FR\text{;}$$

that is,

$$AE : EK = EG : EL = EH : EM\text{.}$$

But

$$\begin{align*}
AE : EK &= \square AG : \square GK, & \text{[VI. 1]} \\
EG : EL &= \square GK : \square KL, & \\
EH : EM &= \square HK : \square KM\text{.} &
\end{align*}$$

Again, by XI. 25 or 32,

$$\begin{align*}
\square AG : \square GK &= (\text{solid } AB) : (\text{solid } EO)\text{,} \\
\square GK : \square KL &= (\text{solid } EO) : (\text{solid } QL)\text{,} \\
\square HK : \square KM &= (\text{solid } QL) : (\text{solid } KP)\text{.}
\end{align*}$$

Therefore

$$(\text{solid } AB) : (\text{solid } EO) = (\text{solid } EO) : (\text{solid } QL) = (\text{solid } QL) : (\text{solid } KP)\text{,}$$

| or the solid :g:`$AB$ <0346.g1:< A B >>` is to the solid :g:`$KP$ <0346.g1:< K P >>` (that is, :g:`$CD$ <0346.g1:< C D >>`) in the ratio triplicate of that
| which the solid :g:`$AB$ <0346.g1:< A B >>` has to the solid :g:`$EO$ <0346.g1:< E O >>`, i.e. the ratio triplicate of that which
| :g:`$AE$ <0346.g1:/ A E />` has to :g:`$EK$ <0346.g1:/ E K />` (or :g:`$CF$ <0346.g1:/ C F />`).

| genuine.
