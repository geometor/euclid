## Textual & Lexical Observations
- Simson's Proposition D fills the gap analogous to Book VI. 23 following VI. 19-20 in plane geometry.
- Proposition 34 begins the critical treatment of reciprocal proportionality of bases and heights in parallelepipeds, analogous to Book VI. 14, 15 for parallelograms and triangles.
- The phrase "the sides which stand up" (*αἱ ἐφεστηκυῖαι εὐθεῖαι*) refers to the lateral edges perpendicular to the base planes in Case 1.

## Mathematical & Editorial Analysis
- Simson's proof of Prop. D uses auxiliary lines $a, b, c, d$ to represent the compounding of ratios by Book V. 22 (*ex aequali*).
- Case 1 of Proposition 34 considers right parallelepipeds ("sides which stand up... at right angles to their bases"). The initial trivial subcase posits equal bases, concluding that heights must also be equal.
