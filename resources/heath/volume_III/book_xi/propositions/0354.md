were equal to the height <span data-ref="0350.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, the solid <span data-ref="0350.g1:< A B >">$AB$</span> would be to the solid <span data-ref="0350.g1:< C D >">$CD$</span> as the<br />
base <span data-ref="0350.g1:< E H >">$EH$</span> is to the base <span data-ref="0350.g1:< N Q >">$NQ$</span>, i.e. as a greater to a less, so that the solids would<br />
not be equal, as they are by hypothesis. Again, if the height <span data-ref="0350.g1:/ C M /">$CM$</span> were less<br />

solid and *a fortiori* greater than <span data-ref="0350.g1:< C D >">$CD$</span>: which contradicts the hypothesis.

Clavius rather ingeniously puts the first assumption the other way, saying<br />
that, if the heights are equal in the equal parallelepipeds, the bases must be<br />
equal. This follows *directly* from XI. 32, which proves that the parallelepipeds<br />
are to one another as their bases; though Clavius deduces it indirectly from<br />
XI. 31. The advantage of Clavius’ alternative is that it makes the second<br />
assumption unnecessary. He merely says, if the heights be not equal, let <span data-ref="0350.g1:/ C M /">$CM$</span><br />
be the greater, and then proceeds with Euclid’s construction.

It is also to be observed that, when Euclid comes to the corresponding<br />
proposition for cones and cylinders [XII. 15], he begins by supposing the<br />
*heights* equal, inferring by XII. 11 (corresponding to XI. 32) that, the solids<br />
being equal, the bases are also equal, and then proceeds to the case where the<br />
heights are unequal without making any preliminary inference about the<br />
bases. The analogy then of XII. 15, and the fact that he quotes XI. 32 here<br />
(which directly proves that, if the solids are equal, and also their heights, their<br />
bases are also equal), make Clavius’ form the more convenient to adopt.

The two assumptions being proved as above, the proposition can be put<br />
shortly as follows.

I. Suppose the edges terminating at the corners of the base to be *per-<br />
pendicular to it*.

Then (*a*), if the base <span data-ref="0350.g1:< E H >">$EH$</span> be equal to the base <span data-ref="0350.g1:< N Q >">$NQ$</span>, the parallelepipeds<br />
being also equal, the heights must be equal (converse of XI. 31), so that the<br />
bases are reciprocally proportional to the heights, the ratio of the bases and<br />
the ratio of the heights being both ratios of equality.

(*b*) If the base <span data-ref="0350.g1:< E H >">$EH$</span> be greater than the base <span data-ref="0350.g1:< N Q >">$NQ$</span>, and consequently (by<br />
deduction from XI. 32) the height <span data-ref="0350.g1:/ C M /">$CM$</span> greater than the height <span data-ref="0350.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, cut off<br />
<span data-ref="0350.g1:/ C T /">$CT$</span> from <span data-ref="0350.g1:/ C M /">$CM$</span> equal to <span data-ref="0350.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, and draw the plane <span data-ref="0350.g1:< T V >">$TV$</span> through <span data-ref="0350.g1:T">$T$</span> parallel to the<br />
base <span data-ref="0350.g1:< N Q >">$NQ$</span>, making the parallelepiped <span data-ref="0350.g1:< C V >">$CV$</span>, with <span data-ref="0350.g1:/ C T /">$CT$</span> ($=$ <span data-ref="0350.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>) for its height.
