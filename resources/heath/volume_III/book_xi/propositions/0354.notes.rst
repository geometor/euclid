Textual & Lexical Observations

- Key terms: *reductio ad absurdum*, *a fortiori*, *perpendicular to it*.
- Textual criticism: Heath details how Heiberg identified an early interpolation in Simson's text ("For if, the bases *EH*, *NQ* being equal...") and shows how the genuine text was erased from inferior MSS.

Mathematical & Editorial Analysis
---------------------------------

- Heath shows that the first assumption (equal bases and equal volumes $\implies$ equal heights) is more easily deduced from XI. 31 than XI. 32: if heights were unequal, cutting off a slice of height equal to the other would give a part equal to the whole, a contradiction.
- Clavius's formulation: If heights are equal, bases are equal (direct from XI. 32); if heights are unequal, let $CM$ be greater and proceed. This avoids the second assumption entirely and matches the structure Euclid later adopts in Book XII, Proposition 15.
