# Transcription Notes: Folio 0355 (Page 351)

## Summary & Folio Continuity
- **Page Number**: 351 (Volume III, Book XI)
- **Content**: Completion of Book XI, Proposition 34 (Case 1 converse parts (*a*) and (*b*); Case 2 for oblique parallelepipeds) followed by Heath's critical commentary on interpolated text in the second part of Proposition 34.
- **Preceding Context**: Folio 0354 (Page 350) established Case 1 (parallelepipeds with lateral edges perpendicular to bases) and proved the direct proportion $(\text{solid } AB) : (\text{solid } CV) = \text{▱ } HE : \text{▱ } NQ$.
- **Succeeding Context**: Folio 0356 (Page 352) commences Book XI, Proposition 35.

## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Structure of Case 1 Converse**: Heath splits the converse into:
  - (*a*) equal bases implying equal heights by XI. 31;
  - (*b*) unequal bases leading to equality of solids via proportions and Book V, Proposition 9.
- **Structure of Case 2**: Parallelepipeds whose edges are not perpendicular to their bases are reduced via XI. 29 and 30 to right parallelepipeds on the same bases ($FK, RO$) and of the same height.
- **Heath's Textual Criticism**: Heath highlights redundant and inept phrases interpolated into the Greek text:
  1. The phrase "in which the sides which stand up are not on the same straight lines" (repeated four times), noting Simson's observation that the extremities of the edges may or may not be in straight lines;
  2. Spurious phrases purporting to quote the result of the first part: "(solids) in which the heights are at right angles to their bases", pointing out the mathematical absurdity of implying heights could be anything other than perpendicular to bases.
