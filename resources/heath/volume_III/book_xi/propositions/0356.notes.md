## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Mathematical Significance**: Proves that if two equal plane (trihedral) angles have elevated edges making equal angles with the corresponding planar arms, their projections make equal inclinations with the base planes ($\angle GAL = \angle MDN$).
- **Terminology**:
  - "elevated straight lines" (μετέωροι εὐθεῖαι): straight lines standing elevated out of the plane of the angle.
  - "rectilineal angles": Heath retains Euclid's classical terminology.
- **Construction Details**:
  - Arbitrary point $M$ chosen on $DM$; cut off $AH = DM$.
  - Perpendicular $MN$ dropped to plane $EDF$; parallel $HK \parallel GL$ drawn from $H$, establishing $HK \perp \text{plane } BAC$ via XI. 8.
  - Perpendiculars $KC \perp AC$, $NF \perp DF$, $KB \perp AB$, $NE \perp DE$ are constructed in the base planes, and lines $HC, CB, MF, FE$ joined.
