This proposition is required for the next, where it is necessary to know that, if in two equiangular parallelepipeds equal angles, one in each, be contained by three plane angles respectively, one of which is an angle of the parallelogram forming the base in one parallelepiped, while its equal is likewise in the base of the other, and the edges in which the two remaining angles forming the solid angles meet are *equal*, the parallelepipeds are *of the same height*.

Bearing in mind the definition of the *inclination of a straight line to a plane*, we might enunciate the proposition more shortly thus.

*If there be two trihedral angles identically equal to one another, corresponding edges in each are equally inclined to the planes through the other two edges respectively.*

The proof, which is necessarily somewhat long, may be summarised thus.

It is required to prove that the angles *GAL*, *MDN* in the figure are equal, *G*, *M* being any points on *AG*, *DM*, and *GL*, *MN* perpendicular to the planes *BAC*, *EDF* respectively.

If *AH* is made equal to *DM*, and *HK* is drawn in the plane *GAL* parallel to *GL*,
$$\text{\it HK\rm{} is also perpendicular to the plane \it BAC\rm{}.} \qquad \text{[XI. 8]}$$

Draw *KB*, *KC* perpendicular to *AB*, *AC* respectively and *NE*, *NF* perpendicular to *DE*, *DF* respectively, and complete the figures.

Now (1)
$$\left.\begin{aligned}
HA^2 &= HK^2 + KA^2 \\
&= HK^2 + KC^2 + CA^2 \\
&= HC^2 + CA^2
\end{aligned}\right\} \qquad \text{[I. 47]}$$
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Therefore}\qquad \angle HCA &= \text{a right angle.} \\
\text{Similarly}\qquad \angle MFD &= \text{a right angle.}
\end{aligned}$$

(2) $\Delta$s *HAC*, *MDF* have therefore two angles equal and one side.
$$\text{Therefore}\qquad \Delta HAC \equiv \Delta MDF\text{, and } AC = DF. \qquad \text{[I. 26]}$$

(3) Similarly $\Delta HAB \equiv \Delta MDE$, and $AB = DE$.

(4) Hence $\Delta$s *ABC*, *DEF* are equal in all respects, so that $BC = EF$,
$$\text{and}\qquad \begin{aligned}
\angle ABC &= \angle DEF, \\
\angle ACB &= \angle DFE.
\end{aligned}$$

(5) Therefore the complements of these angles are equal,
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{i.e.}\qquad \angle KBC &= \angle NEF, \\
\text{and}\qquad \angle KCB &= \angle NFE.
\end{aligned}$$

(6) The $\Delta$s *KBC*, *NEF* have two angles equal and one side, and are therefore equal in all respects, so that
$$\begin{aligned}
KB &= NE, \\
KC &= NF.
\end{aligned}$$

(7) The right-angled triangles *KAC*, *NDF* are equal in all respects, since $AC = DF$ [(2) above], $KC = NF$.
$$\text{Consequently}\qquad AK = DN.$$

(8) In $\Delta$s *HAK*, *MDN*,
$$\begin{aligned}
HK^2 + KA^2 &= HA^2 \\
&= MD^2\text{, by hypothesis,} \\
&= MN^2 + ND^2.
\end{aligned}$$
