This proposition is required for the next, where it is necessary to know<br />
that, if in two equiangular parallelepipeds equal angles, one in each, be<br />
contained by three plane angles respectively, one of which is an angle of the<br />
parallelogram forming the base in one parallelepiped, while its equal is likewise<br />
in the base of the other, and the edges in which the two remaining angles<br />
forming the solid angles meet are *equal*, the parallelepipeds are *of the same<br />
height*.

Bearing in mind the definition of the *inclination of a straight line to a<br />
plane*, we might enunciate the proposition more shortly thus.

*If there be two trihedral angles identically equal to one another, corresponding<br />
edges in each are equally inclined to the planes through the other two edges<br />
respectively.*

The proof, which is necessarily somewhat long, may be summarised thus.<br />
It is required to prove that the angles <span data-ref="0356.g1:< G A L )">$GAL$</span>, <span data-ref="0356.g1:< M D N )">$MDN$</span> in the figure are equal,<br />
<span data-ref="0356.g1:G">$G$</span>, <span data-ref="0356.g1:M">$M$</span> being any points on <span data-ref="0356.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0356.g1:/ D M /">$DM$</span>, and <span data-ref="0356.g1:/ G L /">$GL$</span>, <span data-ref="0356.g1:/ M N /">$MN$</span> perpendicular to the<br />
planes <span data-ref="0356.g1:< B A C >">$BAC$</span>, <span data-ref="0356.g1:< E D F >">$EDF$</span> respectively.

If <span data-ref="0356.g1:/ A H /">$AH$</span> is made equal to <span data-ref="0356.g1:/ D M /">$DM$</span>, and <span data-ref="0356.g1:/ H K /">$HK$</span> is drawn in the plane <span data-ref="0356.g1:< G A L >">$GAL$</span> parallel<br />
to <span data-ref="0356.g1:/ G L /">$GL$</span>,
$$\text{\it HK\rm{} is also perpendicular to the plane \it BAC\rm{}.} \qquad \text{[XI. 8]}$$

Draw <span data-ref="0356.g1:/ K B /">$KB$</span>, <span data-ref="0356.g1:/ K C /">$KC$</span> perpendicular to <span data-ref="0356.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0356.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> respectively and <span data-ref="0356.g1:/ N E /">$NE$</span>, <span data-ref="0356.g1:/ N F /">$NF$</span><br />
perpendicular to <span data-ref="0356.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>, <span data-ref="0356.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span> respectively, and complete the figures.

Now (1)
$$\left.\begin{aligned}
HA^2 &= HK^2 + KA^2 \\
&= HK^2 + KC^2 + CA^2 \\
&= HC^2 + CA^2
\end{aligned}\right\} \qquad \text{[I. 47]}$$
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Therefore}\qquad \angle HCA &= \text{a right angle.} \\
\text{Similarly}\qquad \angle MFD &= \text{a right angle.}
\end{aligned}$$

(2) $\Delta$s <span data-ref="0356.g1:< H A C >">$HAC$</span>, <span data-ref="0356.g1:< M D F >">$MDF$</span> have therefore two angles equal and one side.
$$\text{Therefore}\qquad \Delta HAC \equiv \Delta MDF\text{, and } AC = DF. \qquad \text{[I. 26]}$$

(3) Similarly $\Delta$ <span data-ref="0356.g1:< H A B >">$HAB$</span> $\equiv \Delta$ <span data-ref="0356.g1:< M D E >">$MDE$</span>, and <span data-ref="0356.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> $=$ <span data-ref="0356.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>.

(4) Hence $\Delta$s <span data-ref="0356.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span>, <span data-ref="0356.g1:< D E F >">$DEF$</span> are equal in all respects, so that <span data-ref="0356.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> $=$ <span data-ref="0356.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>,<br />
and
$$\begin{aligned}
\angle ABC &= \angle DEF, \\
\angle ACB &= \angle DFE.
\end{aligned}$$

(5) Therefore the complements of these angles are equal,<br />
i.e.
$$\begin{aligned}
\angle KBC &= \angle NEF, \\
\text{and}\qquad \angle KCB &= \angle NFE.
\end{aligned}$$

(6) The $\Delta$s <span data-ref="0356.g1:< K B C >">$KBC$</span>, <span data-ref="0356.g1:< N E F >">$NEF$</span> have two angles equal and one side, and are<br />
therefore equal in all respects, so that
$$\begin{aligned}
KB &= NE, \\
KC &= NF.
\end{aligned}$$

(7) The right-angled triangles <span data-ref="0356.g1:< K A C >">$KAC$</span>, <span data-ref="0356.g1:< N D F >">$NDF$</span> are equal in all respects, since<br />
<span data-ref="0356.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> $=$ <span data-ref="0356.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span> [(2) above], <span data-ref="0356.g1:/ K C /">$KC$</span> $=$ <span data-ref="0356.g1:/ N F /">$NF$</span>.
$$\text{Consequently}\qquad AK = DN.$$

(8) In $\Delta$s <span data-ref="0356.g1:< H A K >">$HAK$</span>, <span data-ref="0356.g1:< M D N >">$MDN$</span>,
$$\begin{aligned}
HK^2 + KA^2 &= HA^2 \\
&= MD^2\text{, by hypothesis,} \\
&= MN^2 + ND^2.
\end{aligned}$$
