:title: Proposition 37 (conclusion and alternative proof)

.. assertion::

   For the same reason :g:`$ME$ <0363.g1:< M E >>` also has to :g:`$NG$ <0363.g1:< N G >>` the ratio triplicate of that which :g:`$EF$ <0363.g1:/ E F />` has to :g:`$GH$ <0363.g1:/ G H />`. :just:`[*id.*]`

.. subclause::

   And, as :g:`$AB$ <0363.g1:/ A B />` is to :g:`$CD$ <0363.g1:/ C D />`, so is :g:`$EF$ <0363.g1:/ E F />` to :g:`$GH$ <0363.g1:/ G H />`.

.. assertion::

   Therefore also, as :g:`$AK$ <0363.g1:< A K >>` is to :g:`$LC$ <0363.g1:< L C >>`, so is :g:`$ME$ <0363.g1:< M E >>` to :g:`$NG$ <0363.g1:< N G >>`.

Next, as the solid :g:`$AK$ <0363.g1:< A K >>` is to the solid :g:`$LC$ <0363.g1:< L C >>`, so let the solid :g:`$ME$ <0363.g1:< M E >>` be to the solid :g:`$NG$ <0363.g1:< N G >>`; I say that, as the straight line :g:`$AB$ <0363.g1:/ A B />` is to :g:`$CD$ <0363.g1:/ C D />`, so is :g:`$EF$ <0363.g1:/ E F />` to :g:`$GH$ <0363.g1:/ G H />`.

.. subclause::

   For since, again, :g:`$KA$ <0363.g1:< K A >>` has to :g:`$LC$ <0363.g1:< L C >>` the ratio triplicate of that which :g:`$AB$ <0363.g1:/ A B />` has to :g:`$CD$ <0363.g1:/ C D />`, :just:`[XI. 33] <XI.33>` and :g:`$ME$ <0363.g1:< M E >>` also has to :g:`$NG$ <0363.g1:< N G >>` the ratio triplicate of that which :g:`$EF$ <0363.g1:/ E F />` has to :g:`$GH$ <0363.g1:/ G H />`, :just:`[*id.*]` and, as :g:`$KA$ <0363.g1:< K A >>` is to :g:`$LC$ <0363.g1:< L C >>`, so is :g:`$ME$ <0363.g1:< M E >>` to :g:`$NG$ <0363.g1:< N G >>`,

.. assertion::

   therefore also, as :g:`$AB$ <0363.g1:/ A B />` is to :g:`$CD$ <0363.g1:/ C D />`, so is :g:`$EF$ <0363.g1:/ E F />` to :g:`$GH$ <0363.g1:/ G H />`.

.. qed::

   Therefore etc.

.. qed::

   Q. E. D.

----

In this proposition it is assumed that, if two ratios be equal, the ratio triplicate of one is equal to the ratio triplicate of the other and, conversely, that, if ratios which are the triplicate of two other ratios are equal, those other ratios are themselves equal.

To avoid the necessity for these assumptions :person:`Simson <simson>` adopts the alternative proof found in the MS. which :person:`Heiberg <heiberg>` calls **b**, and also adopted by :person:`Clavius <clavius>`, who, however, gives Euclid's proof as well, attributing it to :person:`Theon <theon-of-alexandria>`. The alternative proof proceeds after the manner of VI. 22, thus.

Make :g:`$AB$ <0363.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$CD$ <0363.g1:/ C D />`, :g:`$O$ <0363.g1:/ O />`, :g:`$P$ <0363.g1:/ P />` continuous proportionals, and also :g:`$EF$ <0363.g1:/ E F />`, :g:`$GH$ <0363.g1:/ G H />`, :g:`$Q$ <0363.g1:/ Q />`, :g:`$R$ <0363.g1:/ R />`.

.. picture:: 0363.g1.svg
   :alt: Proposition 37 alternative proof diagram

I. Then, since

$$AB : CD = EF : GH,$$

we have, :term:`*ex aequali* <ex-aequali>`,

$$AB : P = EF : R. \qquad \text{[V. 22]}$$

But

$$\text{(solid } AK\text{)} : \text{(solid } CL\text{)} = AB : P, \qquad \text{[XI. 33 and Por.]}$$

and

$$\text{(solid } EM\text{)} : \text{(solid } GN\text{)} = EF : R.$$

Therefore

$$\text{(solid } AK\text{)} : \text{(solid } CL\text{)} = \text{(solid } EM\text{)} : \text{(solid } GN\text{)}$$
