<p class="sub-clause">And the straight lines <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>, <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span> join their extremities ;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> is parallel to <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span>. <span class="justification">[I. 33]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore the angle <span data-ref="0365.g1:< E D T )">$EDT$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0365.g1:< B G T )">$BGT$</span>,<br />
for they are alternate ; <span class="justification">[I. 29]</span><br />
and the angle <span data-ref="0365.g1:< D T U )">$DTU$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0365.g1:< G T S )">$GTS$</span>. <span class="justification">[I. 15]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore <span data-ref="0365.g1:< D T U >">$DTU$</span>, <span data-ref="0365.g1:< G T S >">$GTS$</span> are two triangles which have two<br />
angles equal to two angles, and one side equal to one side,<br />
namely that subtending one of the equal angles, that is, <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ D U /">$DU$</span><br />
equal to <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ G S /">$GS$</span>,<br />
for they are the halves of <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>, <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span> ;<br />
therefore they will also have the remaining sides equal to the<br />
remaining sides. <span class="justification">[I. 26]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ D T /">$DT$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ T G /">$TG$</span>, and <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ U T /">$UT$</span> to <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ T S /">$TS$</span>.</p>

<p class="qed">Therefore etc.<br />
<span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

Euclid enunciates this proposition of a cube only, though it is true of any<br />
parallelepiped, no doubt because its truth for a cube is all that was wanted for<br />
the only proposition where it is needed, viz. XIII. 17.

Simson remarks that it should be proved that the straight lines bisecting<br />
the corresponding opposite sides of opposite planes are in one plane. This is,<br />
however, clear because e.g. since <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ D K /">$DK$</span>, <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ C L /">$CL$</span> are equal and parallel, <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ K L /">$KL$</span> is equal<br />
and parallel to <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>. And, since <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ K L /">$KL$</span>, <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> are both parallel to <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ D C /">$DC$</span>, <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ K L /">$KL$</span> is<br />
parallel to <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>. And lastly, since <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ K L /">$KL$</span>, <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ M N /">$MN$</span> are both parallel to <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ K L /">$KL$</span> is<br />
parallel to <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ M N /">$MN$</span> and therefore in one plane with it.

The essential thing to be proved is that the plane passing through the<br />
opposite edges <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span>, <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ E G /">$EG$</span> passes through the straight line <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ U S /">$US$</span>, since, only if<br />
this be the case, can <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ U S /">$US$</span>, <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ D G /">$DG$</span> intersect one another.

To prove this we have only to prove that, if <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ D U /">$DU$</span>, <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ U E /">$UE$</span> and <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ B S /">$BS$</span>, <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ S G /">$SG$</span> be<br />
joined, <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ D U E /">$DUE$</span> and <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ B S G /">$BSG$</span> are both straight lines.

Now, since <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ D O /">$DO$</span> is parallel to <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ P E /">$PE$</span>,
$$\angle DOU = \angle EPU.$$
    Thus, in the $\triangle\text{s}$ <span data-ref="0365.g1:< D U O >">$DUO$</span>, <span data-ref="0365.g1:< E U P >">$EUP$</span>, two sides <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ D O /">$DO$</span>, <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ O U /">$OU$</span> are equal to two sides<br />
<span data-ref="0365.g1:/ E P /">$EP$</span>, <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ P U /">$PU$</span>, and the included angles are equal.<br />
    Therefore
$$\triangle DUO \equiv \triangle EUP,$$
$$DU = UE,$$
and
$$\angle DUO = \angle EUP,$$
so that <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ D U E /">$DUE$</span> is a straight line, bisected at <span data-ref="0365.g1:U">$U$</span>. Similarly <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ B S G /">$BSG$</span> is a straight<br />
line, bisected at <span data-ref="0365.g1:S">$S$</span>.

Thus the plane through <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span>, <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ E G /">$EG$</span> (<span data-ref="0365.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span>, <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ E G /">$EG$</span> being equal and parallel)<br />
contains the straight lines <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ D U E /">$DUE$</span>, <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ B S G /">$BSG$</span> (which are therefore equal and parallel<br />
also) and also [XI. 7] the straight lines <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ U S /">$US$</span>, <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ D G /">$DG$</span> (which accordingly intersect).

In $\triangle\text{s}$ <span data-ref="0365.g1:< D T U >">$DTU$</span>, <span data-ref="0365.g1:< G T S >">$GTS$</span>, the angles <span data-ref="0365.g1:< U D T )">$UDT$</span>, <span data-ref="0365.g1:< S G T )">$SGT$</span> are equal (being alternate),<br />
and the angles <span data-ref="0365.g1:< U T D )">$UTD$</span>, <span data-ref="0365.g1:< S T G )">$STG$</span> are also equal (being vertically opposite), while<br />
<span data-ref="0365.g1:/ D U /">$DU$</span> (half of <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>) is equal to <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ G S /">$GS$</span> (half of <span data-ref="0365.g1:/ B G /">$BG$</span>).
