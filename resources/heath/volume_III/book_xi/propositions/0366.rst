:title: Proposition 38 (conclusion and commentary)

.. subclause::

   And the straight lines :g:`$DE$ <0365.g1:/ D E />`, :g:`$BG$ <0365.g1:/ B G />` join their extremities ;

.. assertion:: [I. 33] <I.33>

   therefore :g:`$DE$ <0365.g1:/ D E />` is parallel to :g:`$BG$ <0365.g1:/ B G />`.

.. assertion::

   Therefore the angle :g:`$EDT$ <0365.g1:< E D T )>` is :eq:`equal <0365.g1:< E D T ) = 0365.g1:< B G T )>` to the angle :g:`$BGT$ <0365.g1:< B G T )>`, for they are alternate ; :just:`[I. 29] <I.29>` and the angle :g:`$DTU$ <0365.g1:< D T U )>` is :eq:`equal <0365.g1:< D T U ) = 0365.g1:< G T S )>` to the angle :g:`$GTS$ <0365.g1:< G T S )>`. :just:`[I. 15] <I.15>`

.. assertion::

   Therefore :g:`$DTU$ <0365.g1:< D T U >>`, :g:`$GTS$ <0365.g1:< G T S >>` are two triangles which have two angles equal to two angles, and one side equal to one side, namely that subtending one of the equal angles, that is, :g:`$DU$ <0365.g1:/ D U />` :eq:`equal <0365.g1:/ D U / = 0365.g1:/ G S />` to :g:`$GS$ <0365.g1:/ G S />`, for they are the halves of :g:`$DE$ <0365.g1:/ D E />`, :g:`$BG$ <0365.g1:/ B G />` ; therefore they will also have the remaining sides equal to the remaining sides. :just:`[I. 26] <I.26>`

.. assertion::

   Therefore :g:`$DT$ <0365.g1:/ D T />` is :eq:`equal <0365.g1:/ D T / = 0365.g1:/ T G /; 0365.g1:/ U T / = 0365.g1:/ T S />` to :g:`$TG$ <0365.g1:/ T G />`, and :g:`$UT$ <0365.g1:/ U T />` to :g:`$TS$ <0365.g1:/ T S />`.

.. qed::

   Therefore etc. :just:`Q. E. D.`

----

Euclid enunciates this proposition of a cube only, though it is true of any parallelepiped, no doubt because its truth for a cube is all that was wanted for the only proposition where it is needed, viz. XIII. 17.

:person:`Simson <simson>` remarks that it should be proved that the straight lines bisecting the corresponding opposite sides of opposite planes are in one plane. This is, however, clear because e.g. since :g:`$DK$ <0365.g1:/ D K />`, :g:`$CL$ <0365.g1:/ C L />` are :eq:`equal <0365.g1:/ D K / = 0365.g1:/ C L />` and parallel, :g:`$KL$ <0365.g1:/ K L />` is :eq:`equal <0365.g1:/ K L / = 0365.g1:/ C D />` and parallel to :g:`$CD$ <0365.g1:/ C D />`. And, since :g:`$KL$ <0365.g1:/ K L />`, :g:`$AB$ <0365.g1:/ A B />` are both parallel to :g:`$DC$ <0365.g1:/ D C />`, :g:`$KL$ <0365.g1:/ K L />` is parallel to :g:`$AB$ <0365.g1:/ A B />`. And lastly, since :g:`$KL$ <0365.g1:/ K L />`, :g:`$MN$ <0365.g1:/ M N />` are both parallel to :g:`$AB$ <0365.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$KL$ <0365.g1:/ K L />` is parallel to :g:`$MN$ <0365.g1:/ M N />` and therefore in one plane with it.

The essential thing to be proved is that the plane passing through the opposite edges :g:`$DB$ <0365.g1:/ D B />`, :g:`$EG$ <0365.g1:/ E G />` passes through the straight line :g:`$US$ <0365.g1:/ U S />`, since, only if this be the case, can :g:`$US$ <0365.g1:/ U S />`, :g:`$DG$ <0365.g1:/ D G />` intersect one another.

To prove this we have only to prove that, if :g:`$DU$ <0365.g1:/ D U />`, :g:`$UE$ <0365.g1:/ U E />` and :g:`$BS$ <0365.g1:/ B S />`, :g:`$SG$ <0365.g1:/ S G />` be joined, :g:`$DUE$ <0365.g1:/ D U E />` and :g:`$BSG$ <0365.g1:/ B S G />` are both straight lines.

Now, since :g:`$DO$ <0365.g1:/ D O />` is parallel to :g:`$PE$ <0365.g1:/ P E />`,

$$\angle DOU = \angle EPU.$$

Thus, in the $\triangle\text{s}$ :g:`$DUO$ <0365.g1:< D U O >>`, :g:`$EUP$ <0365.g1:< E U P >>`, two sides :g:`$DO$ <0365.g1:/ D O />`, :g:`$OU$ <0365.g1:/ O U />` are :eq:`equal <0365.g1:/ D O / = 0365.g1:/ E P /; 0365.g1:/ O U / = 0365.g1:/ P U />` to two sides :g:`$EP$ <0365.g1:/ E P />`, :g:`$PU$ <0365.g1:/ P U />`, and the included angles are equal.

Therefore

$$\triangle DUO \equiv \triangle EUP,$$

$$DU = UE,$$

and

$$\angle DUO = \angle EUP,$$

so that :g:`$DUE$ <0365.g1:/ D U E />` is a straight line, :eq:`bisected <0365.g1:/ D U / = 0365.g1:/ U E />` at :g:`$U$ <0365.g1:U>`. Similarly :g:`$BSG$ <0365.g1:/ B S G />` is a straight line, :eq:`bisected <0365.g1:/ B S / = 0365.g1:/ S G />` at :g:`$S$ <0365.g1:S>`.

Thus the plane through :g:`$DB$ <0365.g1:/ D B />`, :g:`$EG$ <0365.g1:/ E G />` (:g:`$DB$ <0365.g1:/ D B />`, :g:`$EG$ <0365.g1:/ E G />` being :eq:`equal <0365.g1:/ D B / = 0365.g1:/ E G />` and parallel) contains the straight lines :g:`$DUE$ <0365.g1:/ D U E />`, :g:`$BSG$ <0365.g1:/ B S G />` (which are therefore :eq:`equal <0365.g1:/ D U E / = 0365.g1:/ B S G />` and parallel also) and also [XI. 7] the straight lines :g:`$US$ <0365.g1:/ U S />`, :g:`$DG$ <0365.g1:/ D G />` (which accordingly intersect).

In $\triangle\text{s}$ :g:`$DTU$ <0365.g1:< D T U >>`, :g:`$GTS$ <0365.g1:< G T S >>`, the angles :g:`$UDT$ <0365.g1:< U D T )>`, :g:`$SGT$ <0365.g1:< S G T )>` are :eq:`equal <0365.g1:< U D T ) = 0365.g1:< S G T )>` (being alternate), and the angles :g:`$UTD$ <0365.g1:< U T D )>`, :g:`$STG$ <0365.g1:< S T G )>` are also :eq:`equal <0365.g1:< U T D ) = 0365.g1:< S T G )>` (being vertically opposite), while :g:`$DU$ <0365.g1:/ D U />` (half of :g:`$DE$ <0365.g1:/ D E />`) is :eq:`equal <0365.g1:/ D U / = 0365.g1:/ G S />` to :g:`$GS$ <0365.g1:/ G S />` (half of :g:`$BG$ <0365.g1:/ B G />`).
