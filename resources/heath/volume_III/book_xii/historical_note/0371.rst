:title: Historical Note on the Method of Exhaustion (cont.)

feature of :person:`Archimedes <archimedes>`’ mechanical arguments in this tract is that he regards an area as the sum of an infinite number of *straight lines* parallel to one another and terminated by the boundary or boundaries of the closed figure the area of which is to be found, and a volume as the sum of an infinite number of *plane sections* parallel to one another: which is of course the same thing as taking (as we do in the integral calculus) the sum of an infinite number of strips of breadth $dx$ (say), when $dx$ becomes indefinitely small, or the sum of an infinite number of parallel laminae of depth $dz$ (say), when $dz$ becomes indefinitely small. To give only one instance, we may take the case of the area of a segment of a parabola cut off by a chord.

Let :g:`$CBA$ <0371.g1:( C B A )>` be the parabolic segment, :g:`$CE$ <0371.g1:/ C E />` the tangent at :g:`$C$ <0371.g1:C>` meeting the

diameter :g:`$EBD$ <0371.g1:/ E B D />` through the middle point of the chord :g:`$CA$ <0371.g1:/ C A />` in :g:`$E$ <0371.g1:E>`, so that
:g:`$EB$ <0371.g1:/ E B />` $=$ :g:`$BD$ <0371.g1:/ B D />`.
Draw :g:`$AF$ <0371.g1:/ A F />` parallel to :g:`$ED$ <0371.g1:/ E D />` meeting :g:`$CE$ <0371.g1:[ C E ]>` produced in :g:`$F$ <0371.g1:F>`. Produce :g:`$CB$ <0371.g1:[ C B ]>` to :g:`$H$ <0371.g1:H>` so that :g:`$CK$ <0371.g1:/ C K />` $=$ :g:`$KH$ <0371.g1:/ K H />`, where :g:`$K$ <0371.g1:K>` is the point in which :g:`$CH$ <0371.g1:/ C H />` meets :g:`$AF$ <0371.g1:/ A F />`; and suppose :g:`$CH$ <0371.g1:/ C H />` to be a lever.
Let any diameter :g:`$MNPO$ <0371.g1:/ M N P O />` be drawn meeting the curve in :g:`$P$ <0371.g1:P>` and :g:`$CF$ <0371.g1:/ C F />`, :g:`$CK$ <0371.g1:/ C K />`, :g:`$CA$ <0371.g1:/ C A />` in :g:`$M$ <0371.g1:M>`, :g:`$N$ <0371.g1:N>`, :g:`$O$ <0371.g1:O>` respectively.
:person:`Archimedes <archimedes>` then observes that
:g:`$CA$ <0371.g1:/ C A />` $:$ :g:`$AO$ <0371.g1:/ A O />` $=$ :g:`$MO$ <0371.g1:/ M O />` $:$ :g:`$OP$ <0371.g1:/ O P />`
(“for this is proved in a lemma”),
whence
:g:`$HK$ <0371.g1:/ H K />` $:$ :g:`$KN$ <0371.g1:/ K N />` $=$ :g:`$MO$ <0371.g1:/ M O />` $:$ :g:`$OP$ <0371.g1:/ O P />`,
so that, if a straight line :g:`$TG$ <0371.g1:/ T G />` equal to :g:`$PO$ <0371.g1:/ P O />` be placed with its middle point at :g:`$H$ <0371.g1:H>`, the straight line :g:`$MO$ <0371.g1:/ M O />` with centre of gravity at :g:`$N$ <0371.g1:N>`, and the straight line :g:`$TG$ <0371.g1:/ T G />` with centre of gravity at :g:`$H$ <0371.g1:H>`, will balance about :g:`$K$ <0371.g1:K>`.
Taking all other parts of diameters like :g:`$PO$ <0371.g1:/ P O />` intercepted between the curve and :g:`$CA$ <0371.g1:/ C A />`, and placing equal straight lines with their centres of gravity at :g:`$H$ <0371.g1:H>`, these straight lines collected at :g:`$H$ <0371.g1:H>` will balance (about :g:`$K$ <0371.g1:K>`) all the lines like :g:`$MO$ <0371.g1:/ M O />` parallel to :g:`$FA$ <0371.g1:/ F A />` intercepted within the triangle :g:`$CFA$ <0371.g1:< C F A >>` in the positions in which they severally lie in the figure.
Hence :person:`Archimedes <archimedes>` infers that an area equal to that of the parabolic segment hung at :g:`$H$ <0371.g1:H>` will balance (about :g:`$K$ <0371.g1:K>`) the triangle :g:`$CFA$ <0371.g1:< C F A >>` hung at its centre of gravity, the point :g:`$X$ <0371.g1:X>` (a point on :g:`$CK$ <0371.g1:/ C K />` such that :g:`$CK$ <0371.g1:/ C K />` $= 3$ :g:`$XK$ <0371.g1:/ X K />`), and therefore that
$(\text{area of triangle }$ :g:`$CFA$ <0371.g1:< C F A >>` $) : (\text{area of segment}) =$ :g:`$HK$ <0371.g1:/ H K />` $:$ :g:`$KX$ <0371.g1:/ K X />`
$= 3 : 1,$
