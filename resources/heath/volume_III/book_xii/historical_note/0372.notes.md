# Transcription Notes: Folio 0372 (Page 368)

## Summary & Folio Continuity
- **Conclusion of the Mechanical Analogy & Plutarch on Democritus**:
  - Archimedes emphasizes that the mechanical method yields an "indication" (ἔμφασίν τινα) rather than a rigorous geometric proof.
  - Heath cites Plutarch's *De Communibus Notitiis adversus Stoicos* (xxxix, 3) on Democritus's dilemma regarding infinitesimal parallel sections of a cone (whether adjacent cross-sections are equal or unequal).
  - Heath notes that Democritus's conceptualization anticipates Cavalieri's Principle and Archimedes' method: solids conceived as infinite sums of parallel laminae (ἐξ ἴσων συγκείμενος...κύκλων).
  - This provides the historical rationale for Democritus's discovery that a pyramid is one third of a prism (Euclid XII. 7) and a cone one third of a cylinder (Euclid XII. 10).
- **Continuity**: Concludes the Historical Note to Book XII. Folio 0373 begins the formal text of Book XII Propositions (Proposition 1).
