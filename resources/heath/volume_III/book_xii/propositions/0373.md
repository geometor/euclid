# BOOK XII. PROPOSITIONS.

### PROPOSITION 1.

<p class="enunciation">Similar polygons inscribed in circles are to one another as<br />
the squares on the diameters.</p>

Let <span data-ref="0373.g1:( A B C )">$ABC$</span>, <span data-ref="0373.g1:( F G H )">$FGH$</span> be circles,<br />
let <span data-ref="0373.g1:< A B C D E >">$ABCDE$</span>, <span data-ref="0373.g1:< F G H K L >">$FGHKL$</span> be similar polygons inscribed in them,<br />
and let <span data-ref="0373.g1:/ B M /">$BM$</span>, <span data-ref="0373.g1:/ G N /">$GN$</span> be diameters of the circles;<br />
I say that, as the square on <span data-ref="0373.g1:/ B M /">$BM$</span> is to the square on <span data-ref="0373.g1:/ G N /">$GN$</span>, so<br />
is the polygon <span data-ref="0373.g1:< A B C D E >">$ABCDE$</span> to the polygon <span data-ref="0373.g1:< F G H K L >">$FGHKL$</span>.

![Similar polygons inscribed in circles](0373.g1.svg)

For let <span data-ref="0373.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span>, <span data-ref="0373.g1:/ A M /">$AM$</span>, <span data-ref="0373.g1:/ G L /">$GL$</span>, <span data-ref="0373.g1:/ F N /">$FN$</span> be joined.

<p class="sub-clause">Now, since the polygon <span data-ref="0373.g1:< A B C D E >">$ABCDE$</span> is similar to the polygon<br />
<span data-ref="0373.g1:< F G H K L >">$FGHKL$</span>,<br />
the angle <span data-ref="0373.g1:< B A E )">$BAE$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0373.g1:< G F L )">$GFL$</span>,<br />
and, as <span data-ref="0373.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span> is to <span data-ref="0373.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0373.g1:/ G F /">$GF$</span> to <span data-ref="0373.g1:/ F L /">$FL$</span>. <span class="justification">[VI. Def. 1]</span></p>

Thus <span data-ref="0373.g1:< B A E >">$BAE$</span>, <span data-ref="0373.g1:< G F L >">$GFL$</span> are two triangles which have one angle<br />
equal to one angle, namely the angle <span data-ref="0373.g1:< B A E )">$BAE$</span> to the angle<br />
<span data-ref="0373.g1:< G F L )">$GFL$</span>, and the sides about the equal angles proportional;
<p class="assertion">therefore the triangle <span data-ref="0373.g1:< A B E >">$ABE$</span> is equiangular with the triangle<br />
<span data-ref="0373.g1:< F G L >">$FGL$</span>. <span class="justification">[VI. 6]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore the angle <span data-ref="0373.g1:< A E B )">$AEB$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0373.g1:< F L G )">$FLG$</span>.</p>
