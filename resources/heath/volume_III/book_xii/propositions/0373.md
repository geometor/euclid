# BOOK XII. PROPOSITIONS.

## PROPOSITION 1.

*Similar polygons inscribed in circles are to one another as the squares on the diameters.*

Let <span data-ref="0373.g1:( A B C )">$ABC$</span>, <span data-ref="0373.g1:( F G H )">$FGH$</span> be circles,
let <span data-ref="0373.g1:< A B C D E >">$ABCDE$</span>, <span data-ref="0373.g1:< F G H K L >">$FGHKL$</span> be similar polygons inscribed in them,
and let <span data-ref="0373.g1:/ B M /">$BM$</span>, <span data-ref="0373.g1:/ G N /">$GN$</span> be diameters of the circles;
I say that, as the square on <span data-ref="0373.g1:/ B M /">$BM$</span> is to the square on <span data-ref="0373.g1:/ G N /">$GN$</span>, so is the polygon <span data-ref="0373.g1:< A B C D E >">$ABCDE$</span> to the polygon <span data-ref="0373.g1:< F G H K L >">$FGHKL$</span>.

![Similar polygons inscribed in circles](0373.g1.svg)

For let <span data-ref="0373.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span>, <span data-ref="0373.g1:/ A M /">$AM$</span>, <span data-ref="0373.g1:/ G L /">$GL$</span>, <span data-ref="0373.g1:/ F N /">$FN$</span> be joined.
Now, since the polygon <span data-ref="0373.g1:< A B C D E >">$ABCDE$</span> is similar to the polygon <span data-ref="0373.g1:< F G H K L >">$FGHKL$</span>,
the angle <span data-ref="0373.g1:< B A E )">$BAE$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0373.g1:< G F L )">$GFL$</span>,
and, as <span data-ref="0373.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span> is to <span data-ref="0373.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0373.g1:/ G F /">$GF$</span> to <span data-ref="0373.g1:/ F L /">$FL$</span>. [vi. Def. 1]
Thus <span data-ref="0373.g1:< B A E >">$BAE$</span>, <span data-ref="0373.g1:< G F L >">$GFL$</span> are two triangles which have one angle equal to one angle, namely the angle <span data-ref="0373.g1:< B A E )">$BAE$</span> to the angle <span data-ref="0373.g1:< G F L )">$GFL$</span>, and the sides about the equal angles proportional;
therefore the triangle <span data-ref="0373.g1:< A B E >">$ABE$</span> is equiangular with the triangle <span data-ref="0373.g1:< F G L >">$FGL$</span>. [vi. 6]
Therefore the angle <span data-ref="0373.g1:< A E B )">$AEB$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0373.g1:< F L G )">$FLG$</span>.
