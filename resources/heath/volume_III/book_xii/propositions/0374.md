---

As, from this point onward, the text of each proposition usually occupies<br />
considerable space, I shall generally give in the notes a summary of the<br />
argument, to enable it to be followed more easily.

Here we have to prove that a pair of corresponding sides are in the ratio<br />
of the corresponding diameters.

Since $\angle\text{s}$ <span data-ref="0373.g1:< B A E )">$BAE$</span>, <span data-ref="0373.g1:< G F L )">$GFL$</span> are equal, and the sides about those angles<br />
proportional,
<p class="assertion">$\triangle\text{s}$ <span data-ref="0373.g1:< A B E >">$ABE$</span>, <span data-ref="0373.g1:< F G L >">$FGL$</span> are equiangular,</p>
so that
<p class="assertion">$\angle$<span data-ref="0373.g1:< A E B )">$AEB$</span> $= \angle$<span data-ref="0373.g1:< F L G )">$FLG$</span></p>

Hence their equals in the same segments, $\angle\text{s}$ <span data-ref="0373.g1:< A M B )">$AMB$</span>, <span data-ref="0373.g1:< F N G )">$FNG$</span>, are equal.<br />
And the right angles <span data-ref="0373.g1:< B A M )">$BAM$</span>, <span data-ref="0373.g1:< G F N )">$GFN$</span> are equal.<br />
Therefore $\triangle\text{s}$ <span data-ref="0373.g1:< A B M >">$ABM$</span>, <span data-ref="0373.g1:< F G N >">$FGN$</span> are equiangular, so that
<p class="assertion"><span data-ref="0373.g1:/ B M /">$BM$</span> $:$ <span data-ref="0373.g1:/ G N /">$GN$</span> $=$ <span data-ref="0373.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span> $:$ <span data-ref="0373.g1:/ G F /">$GF$</span>.</p>

The duplicates of these ratios are therefore equal,<br />
whence
<p class="assertion">$(\text{polygon }$ <span data-ref="0373.g1:< A B C D E >">$ABCDE$</span>$) : (\text{polygon }$ <span data-ref="0373.g1:< F G H K L >">$FGHKL$</span>$)$<br />
$= \text{duplicate ratio of }$ <span data-ref="0373.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span> $\text{ to }$ <span data-ref="0373.g1:/ G F /">$GF$</span><br />
$= \text{duplicate ratio of }$ <span data-ref="0373.g1:/ B M /">$BM$</span> $\text{ to }$ <span data-ref="0373.g1:/ G N /">$GN$</span><br />
$=$ <span data-ref="0373.g1:/ B M /">$BM^2$</span> $:$ <span data-ref="0373.g1:/ G N /">$GN^2$</span>.</p>
