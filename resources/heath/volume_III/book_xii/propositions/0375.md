## PROPOSITION 2.

*Circles are to one another as the squares on the diameters.*

Let <span data-ref="0375.g1:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span>, <span data-ref="0375.g1:( E F G H )">$EFGH$</span> be circles, and <span data-ref="0375.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>, <span data-ref="0375.g1:/ F H /">$FH$</span> their diameters;
I say that, as the circle <span data-ref="0375.g1:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span> is to the circle <span data-ref="0375.g1:( E F G H )">$EFGH$</span>, so is the square on <span data-ref="0375.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> to the square on <span data-ref="0375.g1:/ F H /">$FH$</span>.

![Circles and inscribed polygons with areas S and T](0375.g1.svg)

For, if the square on <span data-ref="0375.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> is not to the square on <span data-ref="0375.g1:/ F H /">$FH$</span> as the circle <span data-ref="0375.g1:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span> is to the circle <span data-ref="0375.g1:( E F G H )">$EFGH$</span>,
then, as the square on <span data-ref="0375.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> is to the square on <span data-ref="0375.g1:/ F H /">$FH$</span>, so will the circle <span data-ref="0375.g1:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span> be either to some less area than the circle <span data-ref="0375.g1:( E F G H )">$EFGH$</span>, or to a greater.
First, let it be in that ratio to a less area <span data-ref="0375.g1:S">$S$</span>.
Let the square <span data-ref="0375.g1:< E F G H >">$EFGH$</span> be inscribed in the circle <span data-ref="0375.g1:( E F G H )">$EFGH$</span>;
then the inscribed square is greater than the half of the circle <span data-ref="0375.g1:( E F G H )">$EFGH$</span>, inasmuch as, if through the points <span data-ref="0375.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0375.g1:F">$F$</span>, <span data-ref="0375.g1:G">$G$</span>, <span data-ref="0375.g1:H">$H$</span> we draw tangents to the circle, the square <span data-ref="0375.g1:< E F G H >">$EFGH$</span> is half the square circumscribed about the circle, and the circle is less than the circumscribed square;
hence the inscribed square <span data-ref="0375.g1:< E F G H >">$EFGH$</span> is greater than the half of the circle <span data-ref="0375.g1:( E F G H )">$EFGH$</span>.
Let the circumferences <span data-ref="0375.g1:( E F )">$EF$</span>, <span data-ref="0375.g1:( F G )">$FG$</span>, <span data-ref="0375.g1:( G H )">$GH$</span>, <span data-ref="0375.g1:( H E )">$HE$</span> be bisected at the points <span data-ref="0375.g1:K">$K$</span>, <span data-ref="0375.g1:L">$L$</span>, <span data-ref="0375.g1:M">$M$</span>, <span data-ref="0375.g1:N">$N$</span>,
and let <span data-ref="0375.g1:/ E K /">$EK$</span>, <span data-ref="0375.g1:/ K F /">$KF$</span>, <span data-ref="0375.g1:/ F L /">$FL$</span>, <span data-ref="0375.g1:/ L G /">$LG$</span>, <span data-ref="0375.g1:/ G M /">$GM$</span>, <span data-ref="0375.g1:/ M H /">$MH$</span>, <span data-ref="0375.g1:/ H N /">$HN$</span>, <span data-ref="0375.g1:/ N E /">$NE$</span> be joined;
