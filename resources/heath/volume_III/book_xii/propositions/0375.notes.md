# Transcription Notes: Folio 0375 (Page 371)

## Summary & Folio Continuity
- **Proposition 2 Enunciation & Initial Construction**:
  - The foundational theorem of circular quadrature: *Circles are to one another as the squares on their diameters* ($\text{Area} \propto d^2$).
  - **Proof Method**: Double reductio ad absurdum via the method of exhaustion.
  - Setting up the first alternative: suppose $BD^2 : FH^2 = \text{circle } ABCD : S$, where $S < \text{circle } EFGH$.
  - Inscribing square $EFGH$, proving it is greater than half the circle (by comparison with circumscribed square).
  - Bisecting arcs at $K, L, M, N$ to form an inscribed regular octagon; proving each triangle (e.g. $\triangle EKF$) is greater than half the circular segment upon its base.
- **Continuity**: Folio ends mid-sentence ("each of the triangles EKF,") and carries over into folio 0376 (page 372).

## Textual & Lexical Observations
- **Method of Exhaustion Core Lemma**: Application of Euclid X. 1: repeatedly subtracting more than half the remaining area exhausts the remainder until it is less than the difference between circle $EFGH$ and $S$.
- **Footnotes**: None on this page.
