<span data-ref="0375.g1:< F L G >">$FLG$</span>, <span data-ref="0375.g1:< G M H >">$GMH$</span>, <span data-ref="0375.g1:< H N E >">$HNE$</span> will be half of the parallelogram about it,
while the segment about it is less than the parallelogram;
hence each of the triangles <span data-ref="0375.g1:< E K F >">$EKF$</span>, <span data-ref="0375.g1:< F L G >">$FLG$</span>, <span data-ref="0375.g1:< G M H >">$GMH$</span>, <span data-ref="0375.g1:< H N E >">$HNE$</span> is greater than the half of the segment of the circle about it.

Thus, by bisecting the remaining circumferences and joining straight lines, and by doing this continually, we shall leave some segments of the circle which will be less than the excess by which the circle <span data-ref="0375.g1:( E F G H )">$EFGH$</span> exceeds the area <span data-ref="0375.g1:S">$S$</span>.
For it was proved in the first theorem of the tenth book that, if two unequal magnitudes be set out, and if from the greater there be subtracted a magnitude greater than the half, and from that which is left a greater than the half, and if this be done continually, there will be left some magnitude which will be less than the lesser magnitude set out.

Let segments be left such as described, and let the segments of the circle <span data-ref="0375.g1:( E F G H )">$EFGH$</span> on <span data-ref="0375.g1:/ E K /">$EK$</span>, <span data-ref="0375.g1:/ K F /">$KF$</span>, <span data-ref="0375.g1:/ F L /">$FL$</span>, <span data-ref="0375.g1:/ L G /">$LG$</span>, <span data-ref="0375.g1:/ G M /">$GM$</span>, <span data-ref="0375.g1:/ M H /">$MH$</span>, <span data-ref="0375.g1:/ H N /">$HN$</span>, <span data-ref="0375.g1:/ N E /">$NE$</span> be less than the excess by which the circle <span data-ref="0375.g1:( E F G H )">$EFGH$</span> exceeds the area <span data-ref="0375.g1:S">$S$</span>.
Therefore the remainder, the polygon <span data-ref="0375.g1:< E K F L G M H N >">$EKFLGMHN$</span>, is greater than the area <span data-ref="0375.g1:S">$S$</span>.
Let there be inscribed, also, in the circle <span data-ref="0375.g1:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span> the polygon <span data-ref="0375.g1:< A O B P C Q D R >">$AOBPCQDR$</span> similar to the polygon <span data-ref="0375.g1:< E K F L G M H N >">$EKFLGMHN$</span>;
therefore, as the square on <span data-ref="0375.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> is to the square on <span data-ref="0375.g1:/ F H /">$FH$</span>, so is the polygon <span data-ref="0375.g1:< A O B P C Q D R >">$AOBPCQDR$</span> to the polygon <span data-ref="0375.g1:< E K F L G M H N >">$EKFLGMHN$</span>. [XII. 1]
But, as the square on <span data-ref="0375.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> is to the square on <span data-ref="0375.g1:/ F H /">$FH$</span>, so also is the circle <span data-ref="0375.g1:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span> to the area <span data-ref="0375.g1:S">$S$</span>;
therefore also, as the circle <span data-ref="0375.g1:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span> is to the area <span data-ref="0375.g1:S">$S$</span>, so is the polygon <span data-ref="0375.g1:< A O B P C Q D R >">$AOBPCQDR$</span> to the polygon <span data-ref="0375.g1:< E K F L G M H N >">$EKFLGMHN$</span>; [v. 11]
therefore, alternately, as the circle <span data-ref="0375.g1:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span> is to the polygon inscribed in it, so is the area <span data-ref="0375.g1:S">$S$</span> to the polygon <span data-ref="0375.g1:< E K F L G M H N >">$EKFLGMHN$</span>. [v. 16]
But the circle <span data-ref="0375.g1:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span> is greater than the polygon inscribed in it;
therefore the area <span data-ref="0375.g1:S">$S$</span> is also greater than the polygon <span data-ref="0375.g1:< E K F L G M H N >">$EKFLGMHN$</span>.
