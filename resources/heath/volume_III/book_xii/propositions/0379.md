**II.** Suppose $S > X'$.<br />
Since
$$d^2 : {d'}^2 = X : S,$$
we have, inversely,
$${d'}^2 : d^2 = S : X.$$
Suppose that
$$S : X = X' : T,$$
whence, since $S > X'$,
$$X > T.$$ <span class="justification">[v. 14]</span>
Consequently
$${d'}^2 : d^2 = X' : T,$$
where $T < X$.

This can be proved impossible in exactly the same way as shown in Part I.<br />
Hence $S$ cannot be *greater* than $X'$ as supposed.<br />
Since then $S$ is neither greater nor less than $X'$,
$$S = X',$$
and therefore
$$d^2 : {d'}^2 = X : X'.$$

With reference to the assumption that *there is some space $S$ such that*
$$d^2 : {d'}^2 = X : S,$$
i.e. that there is a fourth proportional to the areas $d^2$, ${d'}^2$, $X$, Simson observes<br />
that it is sufficient, in this and the like cases, that a thing made use of in the<br />
reasoning can possibly *exist*, though it cannot be exhibited by a geometrical<br />
construction. As regards the assumption see note on v. 18 above.

There is grave reason for suspecting the genuineness of the Lemma at the<br />
end of the proposition; though, if it be rejected, it will be necessary to delete<br />
the words "as was before proved" in corresponding places in XII. 5, 18.
