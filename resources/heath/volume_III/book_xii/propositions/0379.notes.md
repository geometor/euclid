# Transcription Notes: Folio 0379 (Page 375)
**Volume III, Book XII, Proposition 2 (Commentary: Analytical Form & Archimedes' Method)**

## Summary & Folio Continuity
- **Page Context**: Page 375 completes Heath's algebraic summary of Euclid's proof (Parts I and II), analyzes Robert Simson's defense of the fourth proportional assumption, critiques the authenticity of the Lemma, and begins Archimedes' alternative proof using circumscribed polygons (*Measurement of a Circle*).
- **Mathematical Progression**:
  - Reaches contradictions for both $S < X'$ and $S > X'$, proving $d^2 : {d'}^2 = X : X'$.
  - Discussion of the fourth proportional to geometric areas ($d^2, {d'}^2, X$).
  - Authenticity debate regarding the terminal Lemma.
  - Lemma on circumscribed polygons: begins showing that similar circumscribed polygons are in the duplicate ratio of the diameters.
- **Folio Continuity**: Follows folio 0378 (page 374) and continues directly into folio 0380 (page 376).

## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Foundational Criticism**: Heath notes that Euclid's proof assumes the existence of a fourth proportional area $S$ without explicit geometrical construction (citing Simson and Book V, Proposition 18 note).
- **Textual Interpolation**: Heath points out "grave reason for suspecting the genuineness of the Lemma at the end of the proposition," noting its impact on XII. 5 and XII. 18.
- **Archimedes' Approach**: Heath introduces the circumscribed polygon method to demonstrate an independent proof of Case II without relying on reduction to Case I.
