The radii <span data-ref="0379.g1:/ O C /">$OC$</span>, <span data-ref="0379.g1:/ O' C' /">$O'C'$</span> drawn to the points of contact are perpendicular to <span data-ref="0379.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0379.g1:/ A' B' /">$A'B'$</span>, and it follows that

Thus the polygons are to one another in the duplicate ratio of the radii, and therefore of the diameters.
Now suppose a square <span data-ref="0380.g1:< A B C D >">$ABCD$</span> described about a circle.
Make an octagon described about the circle by drawing tangents at the points <span data-ref="0380.g1:E">$E$</span> etc., where <span data-ref="0380.g1:/ O A /">$OA$</span> etc. meet the circle.
Then shall the tangent at <span data-ref="0380.g1:E">$E$</span> cut off more than half of the area between <span data-ref="0380.g1:/ A K /">$AK$</span>, <span data-ref="0380.g1:/ A H /">$AH$</span> and the arc <span data-ref="0380.g1:( H E K )">$HEK$</span>.
For the angle <span data-ref="0380.g1:< A E G )">$AEG$</span> is right, and is therefore $> \angle$<span data-ref="0380.g1:< E A G )">$EAG$</span>.

Therefore
<span data-ref="0380.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span> $>$ <span data-ref="0380.g1:/ E G /">$EG$</span>
$>$ <span data-ref="0380.g1:/ G K /">$GK$</span>.
Therefore
$\triangle$<span data-ref="0380.g1:< A G E >">$AGE$</span> $> \triangle$<span data-ref="0380.g1:< E G K >">$EGK$</span>.
Similarly
$\triangle$<span data-ref="0380.g1:< A F E >">$AFE$</span> $> \triangle$<span data-ref="0380.g1:< E F H >">$EFH$</span>.
Hence
$\triangle$<span data-ref="0380.g1:< A F G >">$AFG$</span> $> \frac{1}{2}$ (re-entrant quadrilateral <span data-ref="0380.g1:< A H E K >">$AHEK$</span>),
and _a fortiori_,
$\triangle$<span data-ref="0380.g1:< A F G >">$AFG$</span> $> \frac{1}{2}$ (area between <span data-ref="0380.g1:/ A H /">$AH$</span>, <span data-ref="0380.g1:/ A K /">$AK$</span> and the arc).
Thus the octagon takes from the square more than half the space between the square and the circle.
Similarly, if a figure of 16 equal sides be circumscribed by cutting off symmetrically the corners of the octagon, it will take away more than half of the space between the octagon and circle.
Suppose now, with the original notation, that
$$d^2 : {d'}^2 = X : S,$$
where $S$ is greater than $X'$.
Continue the construction of circumscribed polygons about $X'$ until the total area between the polygon and the circle is less than the difference between $S$ and $X'$, i.e. till
$$S > (\text{polygon about } X') > X'.$$
Circumscribe a similar polygon about $X$.
Then
$$(\text{polygon about } X) : (\text{polygon about } X') = d^2 : {d'}^2$$
$$= X : S\text{, by hypothesis,}$$
and, alternately,
$$(\text{polygon about } X) : X = (\text{polygon about } X') : S.$$
But
$$(\text{polygon about } X) > X.$$
Therefore
$$(\text{polygon about } X') > S.$$
But
$$S > (\text{polygon about } X'): \qquad \text{[above]}$$
which is impossible.
Hence $S$ cannot be _greater_ than $X'$.
