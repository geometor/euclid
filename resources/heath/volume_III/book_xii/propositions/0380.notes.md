# Transcription Notes: Folio 0380 (Page 376)
**Volume III, Book XII, Proposition 2 (Commentary: Circumscribed Polygon Exhaustion)**

## Summary & Folio Continuity
- **Page Context**: Page 376 completes the demonstration of Archimedes' circumscribed polygon method for proving the second case of Proposition 2 ($S > X'$).
- **Mathematical Progression**:
  - Establishes that similar circumscribed polygons are in the duplicate ratio of radii and diameters ($AB : A'B' = CO : C'O'$).
  - Investigates circumscription around circle $O$: square $ABCD$, followed by regular octagon formed by tangent lines at $E$ (where $OA$ meets the circle).
  - Proves $\triangle AFG > \frac{1}{2}$ re-entrant quadrilateral $AHEK$, and *a fortiori* $> \frac{1}{2}$ the area between $AH, AK$ and arc $HEK$.
  - Extends to 16-gon, demonstrating that successive circumscription exhausts the outer region by more than half at each step.
  - Derives contradiction $S > (\text{polygon about } X') > S$, proving $S$ cannot be greater than $X'$.
- **Folio Continuity**: Follows folio 0379 (page 375) and leads directly to folio 0381 (page 377), where Legendre's method is discussed.

## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Archimedean Technique**: The direct exhaustion of the external area using circumscribed polygons circumvents Euclid's reduction to Case I.
- **Geometric Inequality**: $\angle AEG$ is a right angle, so $AEG > EAG$, yielding $AG > EG > GK$, so $\triangle AGE > \triangle EGK$.
- **Typographical Quirk**: In the print on line 18, Heath has an opening quotation mark before "Therefore" (`'Therefore`), accurately retained or noted.
