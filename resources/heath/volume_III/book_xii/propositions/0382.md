Inscribe in the circle with radius <span data-ref="0381.g2:/ O B /">$OB$</span> a regular polygon such that its sides<br />
do not anywhere meet the circumference of the circle with centre <span data-ref="0381.g2:/ O D /">$OD$</span>; <span class="justification">[Lemma]</span><br />
and inscribe a similar polygon in the other circle.<br />
The areas of the polygons will then be in the duplicate ratio of <span data-ref="0381.g2:/ C A /">$CA$</span> to <span data-ref="0381.g2:/ O B /">$OB$</span>,<br />
or <span class="justification">[XII. 1]</span><br />
$(\text{polygon in circ. }$ <span data-ref="0381.g2:( C A )">$CA$</span>$) : (\text{polygon in circ. }$ <span data-ref="0381.g2:( O B )">$OB$</span>$)$<br />
$=$ <span data-ref="0381.g2:/ C A /">$CA^2$</span> $:$ <span data-ref="0381.g2:/ O B /">$OB^2$</span><br />
$= (\text{circ. }$ <span data-ref="0381.g2:( C A )">$CA$</span>$) : (\text{circ. }$ <span data-ref="0381.g2:( O D )">$OD$</span>$)$, by hypothesis.

But this is impossible, because the polygon in $(\text{circ. }$ <span data-ref="0381.g2:( C A )">$CA$</span>$)$ is less than $(\text{circ. }$<br />
<span data-ref="0381.g2:( C A )">$CA$</span>$)$, but the polygon in $(\text{circ. }$ <span data-ref="0381.g2:( O B )">$OB$</span>$)$ is *greater* than $(\text{circ. }$ <span data-ref="0381.g2:( O D )">$OD$</span>)$.<br />
'Therefore <span data-ref="0381.g2:/ C A /">$CA^2$</span> cannot be to <span data-ref="0381.g2:/ O B /">$OB^2$</span> as $(\text{circ. }$ <span data-ref="0381.g2:( C A )">$CA$</span>$)$ is to a *less* circle than<br />
$(\text{circ. }$ <span data-ref="0381.g2:( O B )">$OB$</span>$)$.

**II.** Suppose, if possible, that<br />
<span data-ref="0381.g2:/ C A /">$CA^2$</span> $:$ <span data-ref="0381.g2:/ O B /">$OB^2$</span> $= (\text{circ. }$ <span data-ref="0381.g2:( C A )">$CA$</span>$) : (\text{some circle} > \text{circ. }$ <span data-ref="0381.g2:( O B )">$OB$</span>)$.<br />
Then inversely<br />
<span data-ref="0381.g2:/ O B /">$OB^2$</span> $:$ <span data-ref="0381.g2:/ C A /">$CA^2$</span> $= (\text{circ. }$ <span data-ref="0381.g2:( O B )">$OB$</span>$) : (\text{some circle} < \text{circ. }$ <span data-ref="0381.g2:( C A )">$CA$</span>$),<br />
and this is proved impossible exactly as in Part I.<br />
Therefore<br />
<span data-ref="0381.g2:/ C A /">$CA^2$</span> $:$ <span data-ref="0381.g2:/ O B /">$OB^2$</span> $= (\text{circ. }$ <span data-ref="0381.g2:( C A )">$CA$</span>$) : (\text{circ. }$ <span data-ref="0381.g2:( O B )">$OB$</span>$)$.

### PROPOSITION 3.

<p class="enunciation">Any pyramid which has a triangular base is divided into<br />
two pyramids equal and similar to one another, similar to the<br />
whole and having triangular bases, and into two equal prisms;<br />
and the two prisms are greater than the half of the whole<br />
pyramid.</p>

Let there be a pyramid of which the triangle <span data-ref="0382.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> is the<br />
base and the point <span data-ref="0382.g1:D">$D$</span> the vertex;<br />
I say that the pyramid <span data-ref="0382.g1:< A B C D >">$ABCD$</span> is<br />
divided into two pyramids equal to<br />
one another, having triangular bases<br />
and similar to the whole pyramid,<br />
and into two equal prisms; and the<br />
two prisms are greater than the half<br />
of the whole pyramid.

![Pyramid divided into two pyramids and two prisms](0382.g1.svg)

For let <span data-ref="0382.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0382.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, <span data-ref="0382.g1:/ C A /">$CA$</span>, <span data-ref="0382.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0382.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span>,<br />
<span data-ref="0382.g1:/ D C /">$DC$</span> be bisected at the points <span data-ref="0382.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0382.g1:F">$F$</span>,<br />
<span data-ref="0382.g1:G">$G$</span>, <span data-ref="0382.g1:H">$H$</span>, <span data-ref="0382.g1:K">$K$</span>, <span data-ref="0382.g1:L">$L$</span>, and let <span data-ref="0382.g1:/ H E /">$HE$</span>, <span data-ref="0382.g1:/ E G /">$EG$</span>, <span data-ref="0382.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span>, <span data-ref="0382.g1:/ H K /">$HK$</span>, <span data-ref="0382.g1:/ K L /">$KL$</span>, <span data-ref="0382.g1:/ L H /">$LH$</span>, <span data-ref="0382.g1:/ K F /">$KF$</span>, <span data-ref="0382.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span><br />
be joined.

<p class="sub-clause">Since <span data-ref="0382.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0382.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span>, and <span data-ref="0382.g1:/ A H /">$AH$</span> to <span data-ref="0382.g1:/ D H /">$DH$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0382.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span> is parallel to <span data-ref="0382.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span>. <span class="justification">[VI. 2]</span></p>
