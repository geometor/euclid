Inscribe in the circle with radius <span data-ref="0381.g2:/ O B /">$OB$</span> a regular polygon such that its sides do not anywhere meet the circumference of the circle with radius <span data-ref="0381.g2:/ O D /">$OD$</span>; [Lemma]
and inscribe a similar polygon in the other circle.
The areas of the polygons will then be in the duplicate ratio of <span data-ref="0381.g2:/ C A /">$CA$</span> to <span data-ref="0381.g2:/ O B /">$OB$</span>, or [XII. 1]
$(\text{polygon in circ. }$ <span data-ref="0381.g2:( C A )">$CA$</span>$) : (\text{polygon in circ. }$ <span data-ref="0381.g2:( O B )">$OB$</span>$)$
$=$ <span data-ref="0381.g2:/ C A /">$CA^2$</span> $:$ <span data-ref="0381.g2:/ O B /">$OB^2$</span>
$= (\text{circ. }$ <span data-ref="0381.g2:( C A )">$CA$</span>$) : (\text{circ. }$ <span data-ref="0381.g2:( O D )">$OD$</span>$)$, by hypothesis.
But this is impossible, because the polygon in $(\text{circ. }$ <span data-ref="0381.g2:( C A )">$CA$</span>$)$ is less than $(\text{circ. }$ <span data-ref="0381.g2:( C A )">$CA$</span>$)$, but the polygon in $(\text{circ. }$ <span data-ref="0381.g2:( O B )">$OB$</span>$)$ is greater than $(\text{circ. }$ <span data-ref="0381.g2:( O D )">$OD$</span>$)$.
Therefore <span data-ref="0381.g2:/ C A /">$CA^2$</span> cannot be to <span data-ref="0381.g2:/ O B /">$OB^2$</span> as $(\text{circ. }$ <span data-ref="0381.g2:( C A )">$CA$</span>$)$ is to a less circle than $(\text{circ. }$ <span data-ref="0381.g2:( O B )">$OB$</span>$)$.
**II.** Suppose, if possible, that
<span data-ref="0381.g2:/ C A /">$CA^2$</span> $:$ <span data-ref="0381.g2:/ O B /">$OB^2$</span> $= (\text{circ. }$ <span data-ref="0381.g2:( C A )">$CA$</span>$) : (\text{some circle} > \text{circ. }$ <span data-ref="0381.g2:( O B )">$OB$</span>$)$.
Then inversely
<span data-ref="0381.g2:/ O B /">$OB^2$</span> $:$ <span data-ref="0381.g2:/ C A /">$CA^2$</span> $= (\text{circ. }$ <span data-ref="0381.g2:( O B )">$OB$</span>$) : (\text{some circle} < \text{circ. }$ <span data-ref="0381.g2:( C A )">$CA$</span>$)$,
and this is proved impossible exactly as in Part I.
Therefore
<span data-ref="0381.g2:/ C A /">$CA^2$</span> $:$ <span data-ref="0381.g2:/ O B /">$OB^2$</span> $= (\text{circ. }$ <span data-ref="0381.g2:( C A )">$CA$</span>$) : (\text{circ. }$ <span data-ref="0381.g2:( O B )">$OB$</span>$)$.

### PROPOSITION 3.

_Any pyramid which has a triangular base is divided into two pyramids equal and similar to one another, similar to the whole and having triangular bases, and into two equal prisms; and the two prisms are greater than the half of the whole pyramid._

Let there be a pyramid of which the triangle <span data-ref="0382.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> is the base and the point <span data-ref="0382.g1:D">$D$</span> the vertex;
I say that the pyramid <span data-ref="0382.g1:< A B C D >">$ABCD$</span> is divided into two pyramids equal to one another, having triangular bases and similar to the whole pyramid, and into two equal prisms; and the two prisms are greater than the half of the whole pyramid.
For let <span data-ref="0382.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0382.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, <span data-ref="0382.g1:/ C A /">$CA$</span>, <span data-ref="0382.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0382.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span>, <span data-ref="0382.g1:/ D C /">$DC$</span> be bisected at the points <span data-ref="0382.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0382.g1:F">$F$</span>, <span data-ref="0382.g1:G">$G$</span>, <span data-ref="0382.g1:H">$H$</span>, <span data-ref="0382.g1:K">$K$</span>, <span data-ref="0382.g1:L">$L$</span>, and let <span data-ref="0382.g1:/ H E /">$HE$</span>, <span data-ref="0382.g1:/ E G /">$EG$</span>, <span data-ref="0382.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span>, <span data-ref="0382.g1:/ H K /">$HK$</span>, <span data-ref="0382.g1:/ K L /">$KL$</span>, <span data-ref="0382.g1:/ L H /">$LH$</span>, <span data-ref="0382.g1:/ K F /">$KF$</span>, <span data-ref="0382.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span> be joined.
Since <span data-ref="0382.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0382.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span>, and <span data-ref="0382.g1:/ A H /">$AH$</span> to <span data-ref="0382.g1:/ D H /">$DH$</span>,
therefore <span data-ref="0382.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span> is parallel to <span data-ref="0382.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span>. [VI. 2]
