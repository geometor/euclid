For, since <span data-ref="0387.g1:/ B O /">$BO$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0387.g1:/ O C /">$OC$</span>, and <span data-ref="0387.g1:/ A L /">$AL$</span> to <span data-ref="0387.g1:/ L C /">$LC$</span>,
therefore <span data-ref="0387.g1:/ L O /">$LO$</span> is parallel to <span data-ref="0387.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>,
and the triangle <span data-ref="0387.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> is similar to the triangle <span data-ref="0387.g1:< L O C >">$LOC$</span>.

![Proposition 4](0387.g1.svg)

For the same reason
the triangle <span data-ref="0387.g1:< D E F >">$DEF$</span> is also similar to the triangle <span data-ref="0387.g1:< R V F >">$RVF$</span>.
And, since <span data-ref="0387.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is double of <span data-ref="0387.g1:/ C O /">$CO$</span>, and <span data-ref="0387.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> of <span data-ref="0387.g1:/ F V /">$FV$</span>,
therefore, as <span data-ref="0387.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is to <span data-ref="0387.g1:/ C O /">$CO$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0387.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> to <span data-ref="0387.g1:/ F V /">$FV$</span>.
And on <span data-ref="0387.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, <span data-ref="0387.g1:/ C O /">$CO$</span> are described the similar and similarly situated rectilineal figures <span data-ref="0387.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span>, <span data-ref="0387.g1:< L O C >">$LOC$</span>,
and on <span data-ref="0387.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, <span data-ref="0387.g1:/ F V /">$FV$</span> the similar and similarly situated figures <span data-ref="0387.g1:< D E F >">$DEF$</span>, <span data-ref="0387.g1:< R V F >">$RVF$</span>;
therefore, as the triangle <span data-ref="0387.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> is to the triangle <span data-ref="0387.g1:< L O C >">$LOC$</span>, so is the triangle <span data-ref="0387.g1:< D E F >">$DEF$</span> to the triangle <span data-ref="0387.g1:< R V F >">$RVF$</span>; [VI. 22]
therefore, alternately, as the triangle <span data-ref="0387.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> is to the triangle <span data-ref="0387.g1:< D E F >">$DEF$</span>, so is the triangle <span data-ref="0387.g1:< L O C >">$LOC$</span> to the triangle <span data-ref="0387.g1:< R V F >">$RVF$</span>. [V. 16]
But, as the triangle <span data-ref="0387.g1:< L O C >">$LOC$</span> is to the triangle <span data-ref="0387.g1:< R V F >">$RVF$</span>, so is the prism in which the triangle <span data-ref="0387.g1:< L O C >">$LOC$</span> is the base and <span data-ref="0387.g1:< P M N >">$PMN$</span> its opposite to the prism in which the triangle <span data-ref="0387.g1:< R V F >">$RVF$</span> is the base and <span data-ref="0387.g1:< S T U >">$STU$</span> its opposite; [Lemma following]
therefore also, as the triangle <span data-ref="0387.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> is to the triangle <span data-ref="0387.g1:< D E F >">$DEF$</span>, so is the prism in which the triangle <span data-ref="0387.g1:< L O C >">$LOC$</span> is the base and <span data-ref="0387.g1:< P M N >">$PMN$</span> its opposite to the prism in which the triangle <span data-ref="0387.g1:< R V F >">$RVF$</span> is the base and <span data-ref="0387.g1:< S T U >">$STU$</span> its opposite.
But, as the said prisms are to one another, so is the prism in which the parallelogram <span data-ref="0387.g1:< K B O L >">$KBOL$</span> is the base and the straight line <span data-ref="0387.g1:/ P M /">$PM$</span> its opposite to the prism in which the parallelogram <span data-ref="0387.g1:< Q E V R >">$QEVR$</span> is the base and the straight line <span data-ref="0387.g1:/ S T /">$ST$</span> its opposite. [XI. 39; cf. XII. 3]
