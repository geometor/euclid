Therefore also the two prisms, that in which the parallelogram <span data-ref="0387.g1:< K B O L >">$KBOL$</span> is the base and <span data-ref="0387.g1:/ P M /">$PM$</span> its opposite, and that in which the triangle <span data-ref="0387.g1:< L O C >">$LOC$</span> is the base and <span data-ref="0387.g1:< P M N >">$PMN$</span> its opposite, are to the prisms in which <span data-ref="0387.g1:< Q E V R >">$QEVR$</span> is the base and the straight line <span data-ref="0387.g1:/ S T /">$ST$</span> its opposite and in which the triangle <span data-ref="0387.g1:< R V F >">$RVF$</span> is the base and <span data-ref="0387.g1:< S T U >">$STU$</span> its opposite in the same ratio. [V. 12]
Therefore also, as the base <span data-ref="0387.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> is to the base <span data-ref="0387.g1:< D E F >">$DEF$</span>, so are the said two prisms to the said two prisms.
And similarly, if the pyramids <span data-ref="0387.g1:< P M N G >">$PMNG$</span>, <span data-ref="0387.g1:< S T U H >">$STUH$</span> be divided into two prisms and two pyramids,
as the base <span data-ref="0387.g1:< P M N >">$PMN$</span> is to the base <span data-ref="0387.g1:< S T U >">$STU$</span>, so will the two prisms in the pyramid <span data-ref="0387.g1:< P M N G >">$PMNG$</span> be to the two prisms in the pyramid <span data-ref="0387.g1:< S T U H >">$STUH$</span>.
But, as the base <span data-ref="0387.g1:< P M N >">$PMN$</span> is to the base <span data-ref="0387.g1:< S T U >">$STU$</span>, so is the base <span data-ref="0387.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> to the base <span data-ref="0387.g1:< D E F >">$DEF$</span>;
for the triangles <span data-ref="0387.g1:< P M N >">$PMN$</span>, <span data-ref="0387.g1:< S T U >">$STU$</span> are equal to the triangles <span data-ref="0387.g1:< L O C >">$LOC$</span>, <span data-ref="0387.g1:< R V F >">$RVF$</span> respectively.
Therefore also, as the base <span data-ref="0387.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> is to the base <span data-ref="0387.g1:< D E F >">$DEF$</span>, so are the four prisms to the four prisms.
And similarly also, if we divide the remaining pyramids into two pyramids and into two prisms, then, as the base <span data-ref="0387.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> is to the base <span data-ref="0387.g1:< D E F >">$DEF$</span>, so will all the prisms in the pyramid <span data-ref="0387.g1:< A B C G >">$ABCG$</span> be to all the prisms, being equal in multitude, in the pyramid <span data-ref="0387.g1:< D E F H >">$DEFH$</span>.

**Q. E. D.**

### LEMMA.

But that, as the triangle <span data-ref="0387.g1:< L O C >">$LOC$</span> is to the triangle <span data-ref="0387.g1:< R V F >">$RVF$</span>, so is the prism in which the triangle <span data-ref="0387.g1:< L O C >">$LOC$</span> is the base and <span data-ref="0387.g1:< P M N >">$PMN$</span> its opposite to the prism in which the triangle <span data-ref="0387.g1:< R V F >">$RVF$</span> is the base and <span data-ref="0387.g1:< S T U >">$STU$</span> its opposite, we must prove as follows.
For in the same figure let perpendiculars be conceived drawn from <span data-ref="0387.g1:G">$G$</span>, <span data-ref="0387.g1:H">$H$</span> to the planes <span data-ref="0387.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span>, <span data-ref="0387.g1:< D E F >">$DEF$</span>; these are of course equal because, by hypothesis, the pyramids are of equal height.
Now, since the two straight lines <span data-ref="0387.g1:/ G C /">$GC$</span> and the perpendicular from <span data-ref="0387.g1:G">$G$</span> are cut by the parallel planes <span data-ref="0387.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span>, <span data-ref="0387.g1:< P M N >">$PMN$</span>,
they will be cut in the same ratios. [XI. 17]
