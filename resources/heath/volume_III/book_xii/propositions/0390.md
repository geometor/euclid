### PROPOSITION 5.

*Pyramids which are of the same height and have triangular bases are to one another as the bases.*

Let there be pyramids of the same height, of which the triangles <span data-ref="0390.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span>, <span data-ref="0390.g1:< D E F >">$DEF$</span> are the bases and the points <span data-ref="0390.g1:G">$G$</span>, <span data-ref="0390.g1:H">$H$</span> the vertices;
I say that, as the base <span data-ref="0390.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> is to the base <span data-ref="0390.g1:< D E F >">$DEF$</span>, so is the pyramid <span data-ref="0390.g1:< A B C G >">$ABCG$</span> to the pyramid <span data-ref="0390.g1:< D E F H >">$DEFH$</span>.

![Proposition 5](0390.g1.svg)

For, if the pyramid <span data-ref="0390.g1:< A B C G >">$ABCG$</span> is not to the pyramid <span data-ref="0390.g1:< D E F H >">$DEFH$</span> as the base <span data-ref="0390.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> is to the base <span data-ref="0390.g1:< D E F >">$DEF$</span>,
then, as the base <span data-ref="0390.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> is to the base <span data-ref="0390.g1:< D E F >">$DEF$</span>, so will the pyramid <span data-ref="0390.g1:< A B C G >">$ABCG$</span> be either to some solid less than the pyramid <span data-ref="0390.g1:< D E F H >">$DEFH$</span> or to a greater.

Let it, first, be in that ratio to a less solid <span data-ref="0390.g1:W">$W$</span>, and let the pyramid <span data-ref="0390.g1:< D E F H >">$DEFH$</span> be divided into two pyramids equal to one another and similar to the whole and into two equal prisms;
then the two prisms are greater than the half of the whole pyramid. [XII. 3]
