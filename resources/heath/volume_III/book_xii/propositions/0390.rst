And since the other prisms in the pyramids are equal to these prisms respectively, $(\text{sum of prisms in }$ :g:`$GABC$ <0387.g1:< G A B C >>` $) : (\text{sum of prisms in }$ :g:`$HDEF$ <0387.g1:< H D E F >>` $) = \triangle$ :g:`$ABC$ <0387.g1:< A B C >>` $: \triangle$ :g:`$DEF$ <0387.g1:< D E F >>`.

Similarly, if the pyramids :g:`$GPMN$ <0387.g1:< G P M N >>`, :g:`$HSTU$ <0387.g1:< H S T U >>` be divided in like manner, and also the pyramids :g:`$PAKL$ <0387.g1:< P A K L >>`, :g:`$SDQR$ <0387.g1:< S D Q R >>`, we shall have e.g. $(\text{sum of prisms in }$ :g:`$GPMN$ <0387.g1:< G P M N >>` $) : (\text{sum of prisms in }$ :g:`$HSTU$ <0387.g1:< H S T U >>` $) = \triangle$ :g:`$PMN$ <0387.g1:< P M N >>` $: \triangle$ :g:`$STU$ <0387.g1:< S T U >>` $= \triangle$ :g:`$ABC$ <0387.g1:< A B C >>` $: \triangle$ :g:`$DEF$ <0387.g1:< D E F >>`, and similarly for the second pair of pyramids.

The process may be continued indefinitely, and we shall always have $(\text{sum of prisms in }$ :g:`$GABC$ <0387.g1:< G A B C >>` $) : (\text{sum of prisms in }$ :g:`$HDEF$ <0387.g1:< H D E F >>` $) = \triangle$ :g:`$ABC$ <0387.g1:< A B C >>` $: \triangle$ :g:`$DEF$ <0387.g1:< D E F >>`.

PROPOSITION 5.
--------------

.. enunciation::

   Pyramids which are of the same height and have triangular bases are to one another as the bases.

Let there be pyramids of the same height, of which the triangles :g:`$ABC$ <0390.g1:< A B C >>`, :g:`$DEF$ <0390.g1:< D E F >>` are the bases and the points :g:`$G$ <0390.g1:G>`, :g:`$H$ <0390.g1:H>` the vertices; I say that, as the base :g:`$ABC$ <0390.g1:< A B C >>` is to the base :g:`$DEF$ <0390.g1:< D E F >>`, so is the pyramid :g:`$ABCG$ <0390.g1:< A B C G >>` to the pyramid :g:`$DEFH$ <0390.g1:< D E F H >>`.

.. picture:: 0390.g1.svg
   :alt: Proposition 5

.. subclause::

   For, if the pyramid :g:`$ABCG$ <0390.g1:< A B C G >>` is not to the pyramid :g:`$DEFH$ <0390.g1:< D E F H >>` as the base :g:`$ABC$ <0390.g1:< A B C >>` is to the base :g:`$DEF$ <0390.g1:< D E F >>`,

.. assertion::

   then, as the base :g:`$ABC$ <0390.g1:< A B C >>` is to the base :g:`$DEF$ <0390.g1:< D E F >>`, so will the pyramid :g:`$ABCG$ <0390.g1:< A B C G >>` be either to some solid less than the pyramid :g:`$DEFH$ <0390.g1:< D E F H >>` or to a greater.

Let it, first, be in that ratio to a less solid :g:`$W$ <0390.g1:W>`, and let the pyramid :g:`$DEFH$ <0390.g1:< D E F H >>` be divided into two pyramids equal to one another and similar to the whole and into two equal prisms;

.. assertion::

   then the two prisms are greater than the half of the whole pyramid. :just:`[XII. 3] <XII.3>`
