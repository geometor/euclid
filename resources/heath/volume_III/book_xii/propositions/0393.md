II. Suppose, if possible, that $W > P'$.<br />
Then, inversely,
$$B' : B = W : P$$
$$= P' : V\text{,}$$
where $V$ is some solid less than $P$. <span class="justification">[Cf. XII. 2, Lemma, and note.]</span><br />
But this can be proved impossible exactly as in Part I.<br />
<p class="assertion">Therefore $W$ is neither less nor greater than $P'$,<br />
so that
$$B : B' = P : P'.$$</p>

Legendre, followed by the American editors already mentioned, and by<br />
others, approaches the subject by a different route, proving the following<br />
propositions.

<p class="enunciation">1. <em>If a pyramid be cut by a plane parallel to the base, (a) the lateral edges<br />
and the height will be cut in the same proportion, (b) the section by the plane<br />
will be a polygon similar to the base.</em></p>

![Legendre Proposition 1](0393.g1.svg)

(a) Since a lateral face <span data-ref="0393.g1:< V A B >">$VAB$</span> of the pyramid <span data-ref="0393.g1:< V A B C D E >">$V(ABCDE)$</span> is cut by two<br />
parallel planes in <span data-ref="0393.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0393.g1:/ a b /">$ab$</span>,
<p class="assertion"><span data-ref="0393.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> $\parallel$ <span data-ref="0393.g1:/ a b /">$ab$</span>;</p>
Similarly <span data-ref="0393.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> $\parallel$ <span data-ref="0393.g1:/ b c /">$bc$</span>, and so on.<br />
Therefore
<p class="assertion"><span data-ref="0393.g1:/ V A /">$VA$</span> $:$ <span data-ref="0393.g1:/ V a /">$Va$</span> $=$ <span data-ref="0393.g1:/ V B /">$VB$</span> $:$ <span data-ref="0393.g1:/ V b /">$Vb$</span> $=$ <span data-ref="0393.g1:/ V C /">$VC$</span> $:$ <span data-ref="0393.g1:/ V c /">$Vc$</span> $= \dots.$</p>
And, if <span data-ref="0393.g1:/ V O /">$VO$</span> the height be cut in <span data-ref="0393.g1:O">$O$</span>, <span data-ref="0393.g1:o">$o$</span>,<br />
<p class="assertion"><span data-ref="0393.g1:/ B O /">$BO$</span> $\parallel$ <span data-ref="0393.g1:/ b o /">$bo$</span>; and each of the above ratios is equal to <span data-ref="0393.g1:/ V O /">$VO$</span> $:$ <span data-ref="0393.g1:/ V o /">$Vo$</span>.</p>

(b) Since <span data-ref="0393.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span> $\parallel$ <span data-ref="0393.g1:/ b a /">$ba$</span>, and <span data-ref="0393.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> $\parallel$ <span data-ref="0393.g1:/ b c /">$bc$</span>,
<p class="assertion">$\angle$<span data-ref="0393.g1:< A B C )">$ABC$</span> $= \angle$<span data-ref="0393.g1:< a b c )">$abc$</span>. <span class="justification">[XI. 10]</span></p>
Similarly for all the other angles of the polygons, which are therefore<br />
equiangular.<br />
Also, by similar triangles,
<p class="assertion"><span data-ref="0393.g1:/ V A /">$VA$</span> $:$ <span data-ref="0393.g1:/ V a /">$Va$</span> $=$ <span data-ref="0393.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> $:$ <span data-ref="0393.g1:/ a b /">$ab$</span>,</p>
and so on.<br />
Therefore, by the ratios above,
<p class="assertion"><span data-ref="0393.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> $:$ <span data-ref="0393.g1:/ a b /">$ab$</span> $=$ <span data-ref="0393.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> $:$ <span data-ref="0393.g1:/ b c /">$bc$</span> $= \dots.$</p>
<p class="assertion">Therefore the polygons are similar.</p>

<p class="enunciation">2. <em>If two pyramids of the same height be cut by planes which are at the<br />
same perpendicular distance from the vertices, the sections are as the respective<br />
bases.</em></p>
