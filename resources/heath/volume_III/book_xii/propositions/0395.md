Legendre next establishes a proposition corresponding to Eucl. XII. 7, viz.

<p class="enunciation">4. <em>Any triangular pyramid is one third of the triangular prism on the same<br />
base and of the same height,</em></p>
and from this he deduces that

<p class="enunciation">COR. <em>The volume of a triangular pyramid is equal to a third of the product<br />
of its base by its height.</em></p>

He has previously proved that the volume of a triangular prism is equal to<br />
the product of its base and height, since (1) the prism is half of a parallele-<br />
piped of the same height and with a parallelogram for base which is double of<br />
the base of the prism, and (2) this parallelepiped can be transformed into an<br />
equivalent *rectangular* parallelepiped with the same height and an equivalent<br />
base.

The theorem (4) is then extended to *any pyramid* in the proposition

<p class="enunciation">5. <em>Any pyramid has for its measure the third part of the product of its base<br />
and its height, from which follow</em></p>

<p class="enunciation">COR. I. <em>Any pyramid is the third part of the prism on the same base and<br />
of the same height.</em></p>

<p class="enunciation">COR. II. <em>Two pyramids of the same height are to one another as their<br />
bases, and two pyramids on the same base are to one another as their heights.</em></p>

The first part of the second corollary corresponds to the present<br />
proposition as extended by the next, XII. 6.
