Now the sum of the exterior prisms of the pyramid :g:`$VABC$ <0394.g1:< V A B C >>` is *greater* than that pyramid; and the sum of the interior prisms in the pyramid :g:`$vabc$ <0394.g1:< v a b c >>` is *less* than that pyramid.

.. assertion::

   Consequently the difference between the sum of the first set of prisms and the sum of the second set of prisms is greater than the difference between the two pyramids.

Again, if we start from the bases :g:`$ABC$ <0394.g1:< A B C >>`, :g:`$abc$ <0394.g1:< a b c >>`, the *second* exterior prism :g:`$DEFG$ <0394.g1:< D E F G >>` is equivalent to the *first* interior prism :g:`$defa$ <0394.g1:< d e f a >>`, since their bases are equivalent and they have the same height $z$. :just:`[XI. 28 and note; XI. 32]` Similarly the third exterior prism is equivalent to the second interior prism, and so on, until we arrive at the last of each.

.. assertion::

   Therefore the prism :g:`$ABCD$ <0394.g1:< A B C D >>`, the *first* exterior prism, is the difference between the sums of the exterior and interior prisms respectively.

.. assertion::

   Therefore the difference between the two pyramids is *less* than the prism :g:`$ABCD$ <0394.g1:< A B C D >>`, which should therefore be greater than the prism with base :g:`$ABC$ <0394.g1:< A B C >>` and height :g:`$AX$ <0394.g1:/ A X />`.

.. subclause::

   But the prism :g:`$ABCD$ <0394.g1:< A B C D >>` is, by hypothesis, less than the latter prism:

.. assertion::

   which is impossible.

.. assertion::

   Consequently the pyramid :g:`$VABC$ <0394.g1:< V A B C >>` cannot be greater than the pyramid :g:`$vabc$ <0394.g1:< v a b c >>`.

.. assertion::

   Similarly it may be proved that :g:`$vabc$ <0394.g1:< v a b c >>` cannot be greater than :g:`$VABC$ <0394.g1:< V A B C >>`.

.. assertion::

   Therefore the pyramids are *equivalent*.

:person:`Legendre <legendre>` next establishes a proposition corresponding to Eucl. XII. 7, viz.

.. enunciation::

   4. *Any triangular pyramid is one third of the triangular prism on the same base and of the same height,*

and from this he deduces that

.. enunciation::

   COR. *The volume of a triangular pyramid is equal to a third of the product of its base by its height.*

He has previously proved that the volume of a triangular prism is equal to the product of its base and height, since (1) the prism is half of a parallelepiped of the same height and with a parallelogram for base which is double of the base of the prism, and (2) this parallelepiped can be transformed into an equivalent *rectangular* parallelepiped with the same height and an equivalent base.

The theorem (4) is then extended to *any pyramid* in the proposition

.. enunciation::

   5. *Any pyramid has for its measure the third part of the product of its base and its height, from which follow*

.. enunciation::

   COR. I. *Any pyramid is the third part of the prism on the same base and of the same height.*

.. enunciation::

   COR. II. *Two pyramids of the same height are to one another as their bases, and two pyramids on the same base are to one another as their heights.*

The first part of the second corollary corresponds to the present proposition as extended by the next, XII. 6.
