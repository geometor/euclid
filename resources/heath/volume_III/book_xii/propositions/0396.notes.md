## Summary & Folio Continuity

- Enunciation, setting-out, diagram, and initial proof steps.
- Decomposes polygonal bases into triangles ($ABC, ACD, ADE$ and $FGH, FHK, FKL$), turning polygonal pyramids into aggregates of triangular pyramids of equal height.

## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Triangulation of Polygonal Pyramids**: By joining diagonals $AC, AD$ from vertex $A$ and $FH, FK$ from vertex $F$, the pyramids $ABCDEM$ and $FGHKLN$ are split into triangular pyramids with shared apexes $M$ and $N$.
- **Proportion Theory Rigour**: Rather than simply adding pyramids together, Euclid strictly follows the rigorous formal steps of Book V:
  - $\triangle ABC : \triangle ACD = ABCM : ACDM$ [XII. 5]
  - By *componendo* [V. 18]: $(\triangle ABC + \triangle ACD) : \triangle ACD = (ABCM + ACDM) : ACDM$, i.e. $ABCD : ACD = ABCDM : ACDM$.
  - Combining with $ACD : ADE = ACDM : ADEM$ via *ex aequali* [V. 22] gives $ABCD : ADE = ABCDM : ADEM$.
  - A second *componendo* [V. 18] gives $ABCDE : ADE = ABCDEM : ADEM$.
