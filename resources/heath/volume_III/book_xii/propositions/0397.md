And, since <span data-ref="0396.g1:< A D E M >">$ADEM$</span>, <span data-ref="0396.g1:< F G H N >">$FGHN$</span> are two pyramids which have<br />
triangular bases and equal height,<br />
therefore, as the base <span data-ref="0396.g1:< A D E >">$ADE$</span> is to the base <span data-ref="0396.g1:< F G H >">$FGH$</span>, so is the<br />
pyramid <span data-ref="0396.g1:< A D E M >">$ADEM$</span> to the pyramid <span data-ref="0396.g1:< F G H N >">$FGHN$</span>. <span class="justification">[XII. 5]</span>

But, as the base <span data-ref="0396.g1:< A D E >">$ADE$</span> is to the base <span data-ref="0396.g1:< A B C D E >">$ABCDE$</span>, so was<br />
the pyramid <span data-ref="0396.g1:< A D E M >">$ADEM$</span> to the pyramid <span data-ref="0396.g1:< A B C D E M >">$ABCDEM$</span>.

<p class="assertion">Therefore also, *ex aequali*, as the base <span data-ref="0396.g1:< A B C D E >">$ABCDE$</span> is to the<br />
base <span data-ref="0396.g1:< F G H >">$FGH$</span>, so is the pyramid <span data-ref="0396.g1:< A B C D E M >">$ABCDEM$</span> to the pyramid<br />
<span data-ref="0396.g1:< F G H N >">$FGHN$</span>. <span class="justification">[V. 22]</span></p>

But further, as the base <span data-ref="0396.g1:< F G H >">$FGH$</span> is to the base <span data-ref="0396.g1:< F G H K L >">$FGHKL$</span>, so<br />
also was the pyramid <span data-ref="0396.g1:< F G H N >">$FGHN$</span> to the pyramid <span data-ref="0396.g1:< F G H K L N >">$FGHKLN$</span>.

<p class="assertion">Therefore also, *ex aequali*, as the base <span data-ref="0396.g1:< A B C D E >">$ABCDE$</span> is to the<br />
base <span data-ref="0396.g1:< F G H K L >">$FGHKL$</span>, so is the pyramid <span data-ref="0396.g1:< A B C D E M >">$ABCDEM$</span> to the pyramid<br />
<span data-ref="0396.g1:< F G H K L N >">$FGHKLN$</span>. <span class="justification">[V. 22]</span></p>

<p class="qed">Q. E. D.</p>

---

It will be seen that, in order to obtain the proportion<br />
(base <span data-ref="0396.g1:< A B C D E >">$ABCDE$</span>) $: \triangle$<span data-ref="0396.g1:< A D E >">$ADE$</span> $=$ (pyramid <span data-ref="0396.g1:< M A B C D E >">$MABCDE$</span>) $:$ (pyramid <span data-ref="0396.g1:< M A D E >">$MADE$</span>),<br />
Euclid employs V. 18 (*componendo*) twice over, with an *ex aequali* step <span class="justification">[V. 22]</span><br />
intervening.

We might arrive at it more concisely by using V. 24 extended to any<br />
number of antecedents.

Thus
<p class="assertion">$\triangle$<span data-ref="0396.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> $: \triangle$<span data-ref="0396.g1:< A D E >">$ADE$</span> $=$ (pyramid <span data-ref="0396.g1:< M A B C >">$MABC$</span>) $:$ (pyramid <span data-ref="0396.g1:< M A D E >">$MADE$</span>),<br />
$\triangle$<span data-ref="0396.g1:< A C D >">$ACD$</span> $: \triangle$<span data-ref="0396.g1:< A D E >">$ADE$</span> $=$ (pyramid <span data-ref="0396.g1:< M A C D >">$MACD$</span>) $:$ (pyramid <span data-ref="0396.g1:< M A D E >">$MADE$</span>),</p>
and lastly
<p class="assertion">$\triangle$<span data-ref="0396.g1:< A D E >">$ADE$</span> $: \triangle$<span data-ref="0396.g1:< A D E >">$ADE$</span> $=$ (pyramid <span data-ref="0396.g1:< M A D E >">$MADE$</span>) $:$ (pyramid <span data-ref="0396.g1:< M A D E >">$MADE$</span>).</p>

Therefore, adding the antecedents <span class="justification">[V. 24]</span>, we have
<p class="assertion">(polygon <span data-ref="0396.g1:< A B C D E >">$ABCDE$</span>) $: \triangle$<span data-ref="0396.g1:< A D E >">$ADE$</span> $=$ (pyramid <span data-ref="0396.g1:< M A B C D E >">$MABCDE$</span>) $:$ (pyramid <span data-ref="0396.g1:< M A D E >">$MADE$</span>).</p>

Again, since the pyramids <span data-ref="0396.g1:< M A D E >">$MADE$</span>, <span data-ref="0396.g1:< N F G H >">$NFGH$</span> are of the same height,
<p class="assertion">$\triangle$<span data-ref="0396.g1:< A D E >">$ADE$</span> $: \triangle$<span data-ref="0396.g1:< F G H >">$FGH$</span> $=$ (pyramid <span data-ref="0396.g1:< M A D E >">$MADE$</span>) $:$ (pyramid <span data-ref="0396.g1:< N F G H >">$NFGH$</span>).</p>

Lastly, using the same argument for the pyramid <span data-ref="0396.g1:< N F G H K L >">$NFGHKL$</span> as for<br />
<span data-ref="0396.g1:< M A B C D E >">$MABCDE$</span>, and inverting, we have
<p class="assertion">$\triangle$<span data-ref="0396.g1:< F G H >">$FGH$</span> $:$ (polygon <span data-ref="0396.g1:< F G H K L >">$FGHKL$</span>) $=$ (pyramid <span data-ref="0396.g1:< N F G H >">$NFGH$</span>) $:$ (pyramid <span data-ref="0396.g1:< N F G H K L >">$NFGHKL$</span>).</p>

Thus from the three proportions, *ex aequali*,
<p class="assertion">(polygon <span data-ref="0396.g1:< A B C D E >">$ABCDE$</span>) $:$ (polygon <span data-ref="0396.g1:< F G H K L >">$FGHKL$</span>)<br />
$=$ (pyramid <span data-ref="0396.g1:< M A B C D E >">$MABCDE$</span>) $:$ (pyramid <span data-ref="0396.g1:< N F G H K L >">$NFGHKL$</span>).</p>
