And, since <span data-ref="0396.g1:< A D E M >">$ADEM$</span>, <span data-ref="0396.g1:< F G H N >">$FGHN$</span> are two pyramids which have triangular bases and equal height,
therefore, as the base <span data-ref="0396.g1:< A D E >">$ADE$</span> is to the base <span data-ref="0396.g1:< F G H >">$FGH$</span>, so is the pyramid <span data-ref="0396.g1:< A D E M >">$ADEM$</span> to the pyramid <span data-ref="0396.g1:< F G H N >">$FGHN$</span>. [XII. 5]

But, as the base <span data-ref="0396.g1:< A D E >">$ADE$</span> is to the base <span data-ref="0396.g1:< A B C D E >">$ABCDE$</span>, so was the pyramid <span data-ref="0396.g1:< A D E M >">$ADEM$</span> to the pyramid <span data-ref="0396.g1:< A B C D E M >">$ABCDEM$</span>.

Therefore also, *ex aequali*, as the base <span data-ref="0396.g1:< A B C D E >">$ABCDE$</span> is to the base <span data-ref="0396.g1:< F G H >">$FGH$</span>, so is the pyramid <span data-ref="0396.g1:< A B C D E M >">$ABCDEM$</span> to the pyramid <span data-ref="0396.g1:< F G H N >">$FGHN$</span>. [V. 22]

But further, as the base <span data-ref="0396.g1:< F G H >">$FGH$</span> is to the base <span data-ref="0396.g1:< F G H K L >">$FGHKL$</span>, so also was the pyramid <span data-ref="0396.g1:< F G H N >">$FGHN$</span> to the pyramid <span data-ref="0396.g1:< F G H K L N >">$FGHKLN$</span>.

Therefore also, *ex aequali*, as the base <span data-ref="0396.g1:< A B C D E >">$ABCDE$</span> is to the base <span data-ref="0396.g1:< F G H K L >">$FGHKL$</span>, so is the pyramid <span data-ref="0396.g1:< A B C D E M >">$ABCDEM$</span> to the pyramid <span data-ref="0396.g1:< F G H K L N >">$FGHKLN$</span>. [V. 22]

**Q. E. D.**

---

It will be seen that, in order to obtain the proportion
(base <span data-ref="0396.g1:< A B C D E >">$ABCDE$</span>) $: \triangle$<span data-ref="0396.g1:< A D E >">$ADE$</span> $=$ (pyramid <span data-ref="0396.g1:< M A B C D E >">$MABCDE$</span>) $:$ (pyramid <span data-ref="0396.g1:< M A D E >">$MADE$</span>),
Euclid employs V. 18 (*componendo*) twice over, with an *ex aequali* step [V. 22] intervening.

We might arrive at it more concisely by using V. 24 extended to any number of antecedents.

Thus
$\triangle$<span data-ref="0396.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> $: \triangle$<span data-ref="0396.g1:< A D E >">$ADE$</span> $=$ (pyramid <span data-ref="0396.g1:< M A B C >">$MABC$</span>) $:$ (pyramid <span data-ref="0396.g1:< M A D E >">$MADE$</span>),
$\triangle$<span data-ref="0396.g1:< A C D >">$ACD$</span> $: \triangle$<span data-ref="0396.g1:< A D E >">$ADE$</span> $=$ (pyramid <span data-ref="0396.g1:< M A C D >">$MACD$</span>) $:$ (pyramid <span data-ref="0396.g1:< M A D E >">$MADE$</span>),
and lastly
$\triangle$<span data-ref="0396.g1:< A D E >">$ADE$</span> $: \triangle$<span data-ref="0396.g1:< A D E >">$ADE$</span> $=$ (pyramid <span data-ref="0396.g1:< M A D E >">$MADE$</span>) $:$ (pyramid <span data-ref="0396.g1:< M A D E >">$MADE$</span>).

Therefore, adding the antecedents [V. 24], we have
(polygon <span data-ref="0396.g1:< A B C D E >">$ABCDE$</span>) $: \triangle$<span data-ref="0396.g1:< A D E >">$ADE$</span> $=$ (pyramid <span data-ref="0396.g1:< M A B C D E >">$MABCDE$</span>) $:$ (pyramid <span data-ref="0396.g1:< M A D E >">$MADE$</span>).

Again, since the pyramids <span data-ref="0396.g1:< M A D E >">$MADE$</span>, <span data-ref="0396.g1:< N F G H >">$NFGH$</span> are of the same height,
$\triangle$<span data-ref="0396.g1:< A D E >">$ADE$</span> $: \triangle$<span data-ref="0396.g1:< F G H >">$FGH$</span> $=$ (pyramid <span data-ref="0396.g1:< M A D E >">$MADE$</span>) $:$ (pyramid <span data-ref="0396.g1:< N F G H >">$NFGH$</span>).

Lastly, using the same argument for the pyramid <span data-ref="0396.g1:< N F G H K L >">$NFGHKL$</span> as for <span data-ref="0396.g1:< M A B C D E >">$MABCDE$</span>, and inverting, we have
$\triangle$<span data-ref="0396.g1:< F G H >">$FGH$</span> $:$ (polygon <span data-ref="0396.g1:< F G H K L >">$FGHKL$</span>) $=$ (pyramid <span data-ref="0396.g1:< N F G H >">$NFGH$</span>) $:$ (pyramid <span data-ref="0396.g1:< N F G H K L >">$NFGHKL$</span>).

Thus from the three proportions, *ex aequali*,
(polygon <span data-ref="0396.g1:< A B C D E >">$ABCDE$</span>) $:$ (polygon <span data-ref="0396.g1:< F G H K L >">$FGHKL$</span>)
$=$ (pyramid <span data-ref="0396.g1:< M A B C D E >">$MABCDE$</span>) $:$ (pyramid <span data-ref="0396.g1:< N F G H K L >">$NFGHKL$</span>).
