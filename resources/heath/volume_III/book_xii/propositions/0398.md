### PROPOSITION 7.

<p class="enunciation">Any prism which has a triangular base is divided into three<br />
pyramids equal to one another which have triangular bases.</p>

Let there be a prism in which the triangle <span data-ref="0398.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> is the<br />
base and <span data-ref="0398.g1:< D E F >">$DEF$</span> its opposite;<br />
I say that the prism <span data-ref="0398.g1:< A B C D E F >">$ABCDEF$</span> is<br />
divided into three pyramids equal to<br />
one another, which have triangular<br />
bases.

![Book XII, Proposition 7](0398.g1.svg)

For let <span data-ref="0398.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>, <span data-ref="0398.g1:/ E C /">$EC$</span>, <span data-ref="0398.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> be joined.

<p class="sub-clause">Since <span data-ref="0398.g1:< A B E D >">$ABED$</span> is a parallelogram,<br />
and <span data-ref="0398.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> is its diameter,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the triangle <span data-ref="0398.g1:< A B D >">$ABD$</span> is equal<br />
to the triangle <span data-ref="0398.g1:< E B D >">$EBD$</span>; <span class="justification">[I. 34]</span><br />
therefore also the pyramid of which the triangle <span data-ref="0398.g1:< A B D >">$ABD$</span> is the<br />
base and the point <span data-ref="0398.g1:C">$C$</span> the vertex is equal to the pyramid of<br />
which the triangle <span data-ref="0398.g1:< D E B >">$DEB$</span> is the base and the point <span data-ref="0398.g1:C">$C$</span> the<br />
vertex. <span class="justification">[XII. 5]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But the pyramid of which the triangle <span data-ref="0398.g1:< D E B >">$DEB$</span> is the base<br />
and the point <span data-ref="0398.g1:C">$C$</span> the vertex is the same with the pyramid of<br />
which the triangle <span data-ref="0398.g1:< E B C >">$EBC$</span> is the base and the point <span data-ref="0398.g1:D">$D$</span> the<br />
vertex;<br />
for they are contained by the same planes.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore the pyramid of which the triangle <span data-ref="0398.g1:< A B D >">$ABD$</span> is the<br />
base and the point <span data-ref="0398.g1:C">$C$</span> the vertex is also equal to the pyramid<br />
of which the triangle <span data-ref="0398.g1:< E B C >">$EBC$</span> is the base and the point <span data-ref="0398.g1:D">$D$</span> the<br />
vertex.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">Again, since <span data-ref="0398.g1:< F C B E >">$FCBE$</span> is a parallelogram,<br />
and <span data-ref="0398.g1:/ C E /">$CE$</span> is its diameter,</p>
<p class="assertion">the triangle <span data-ref="0398.g1:< C E F >">$CEF$</span> is equal to the triangle <span data-ref="0398.g1:< C B E >">$CBE$</span>. <span class="justification">[I. 34]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore also the pyramid of which the triangle <span data-ref="0398.g1:< B C E >">$BCE$</span> is<br />
the base and the point <span data-ref="0398.g1:D">$D$</span> the vertex is equal to the pyramid<br />
of which the triangle <span data-ref="0398.g1:< E C F >">$ECF$</span> is the base and the point <span data-ref="0398.g1:D">$D$</span> the<br />
vertex. <span class="justification">[XII. 5]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But the pyramid of which the triangle <span data-ref="0398.g1:< B C E >">$BCE$</span> is the base<br />
and the point <span data-ref="0398.g1:D">$D$</span> the vertex was proved equal to the pyramid<br />
of which the triangle <span data-ref="0398.g1:< A B D >">$ABD$</span> is the base and the point <span data-ref="0398.g1:C">$C$</span> the<br />
vertex;</p>
