### PROPOSITION 7.

*Any prism which has a triangular base is divided into three pyramids equal to one another which have triangular bases.*

Let there be a prism in which the triangle <span data-ref="0398.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> is the base and <span data-ref="0398.g1:< D E F >">$DEF$</span> its opposite;
I say that the prism <span data-ref="0398.g1:< A B C D E F >">$ABCDEF$</span> is divided into three pyramids equal to one another, which have triangular bases.

![Book XII, Proposition 7](0398.g1.svg)

For let <span data-ref="0398.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>, <span data-ref="0398.g1:/ E C /">$EC$</span>, <span data-ref="0398.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> be joined.

Since <span data-ref="0398.g1:< A B E D >">$ABED$</span> is a parallelogram, and <span data-ref="0398.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> is its diameter, therefore the triangle <span data-ref="0398.g1:< A B D >">$ABD$</span> is equal to the triangle <span data-ref="0398.g1:< E B D >">$EBD$</span>; [I. 34]
therefore also the pyramid of which the triangle <span data-ref="0398.g1:< A B D >">$ABD$</span> is the base and the point <span data-ref="0398.g1:C">$C$</span> the vertex is equal to the pyramid of which the triangle <span data-ref="0398.g1:< D E B >">$DEB$</span> is the base and the point <span data-ref="0398.g1:C">$C$</span> the vertex. [XII. 5]

But the pyramid of which the triangle <span data-ref="0398.g1:< D E B >">$DEB$</span> is the base and the point <span data-ref="0398.g1:C">$C$</span> the vertex is the same with the pyramid of which the triangle <span data-ref="0398.g1:< E B C >">$EBC$</span> is the base and the point <span data-ref="0398.g1:D">$D$</span> the vertex;
for they are contained by the same planes.

Therefore the pyramid of which the triangle <span data-ref="0398.g1:< A B D >">$ABD$</span> is the base and the point <span data-ref="0398.g1:C">$C$</span> the vertex is also equal to the pyramid of which the triangle <span data-ref="0398.g1:< E B C >">$EBC$</span> is the base and the point <span data-ref="0398.g1:D">$D$</span> the vertex.

Again, since <span data-ref="0398.g1:< F C B E >">$FCBE$</span> is a parallelogram, and <span data-ref="0398.g1:/ C E /">$CE$</span> is its diameter, the triangle <span data-ref="0398.g1:< C E F >">$CEF$</span> is equal to the triangle <span data-ref="0398.g1:< C B E >">$CBE$</span>. [I. 34]

Therefore also the pyramid of which the triangle <span data-ref="0398.g1:< B C E >">$BCE$</span> is the base and the point <span data-ref="0398.g1:D">$D$</span> the vertex is equal to the pyramid of which the triangle <span data-ref="0398.g1:< E C F >">$ECF$</span> is the base and the point <span data-ref="0398.g1:D">$D$</span> the vertex. [XII. 5]

But the pyramid of which the triangle <span data-ref="0398.g1:< B C E >">$BCE$</span> is the base and the point <span data-ref="0398.g1:D">$D$</span> the vertex was proved equal to the pyramid of which the triangle <span data-ref="0398.g1:< A B D >">$ABD$</span> is the base and the point <span data-ref="0398.g1:C">$C$</span> the vertex;
