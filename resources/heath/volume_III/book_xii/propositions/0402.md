### PROPOSITION 9.

<p class="enunciation">In equal pyramids which have triangular bases the bases<br />
are reciprocally proportional to the heights; and those pyramids<br />
in which the bases are reciprocally proportional to the heights<br />
are equal.</p>

For let there be equal pyramids which have the triangular<br />
bases <span data-ref="0402.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span>, <span data-ref="0402.g1:< D E F >">$DEF$</span> and vertices the points <span data-ref="0402.g1:G">$G$</span>, <span data-ref="0402.g1:H">$H$</span>;<br />
I say that in the pyramids <span data-ref="0402.g1:< A B C G >">$ABCG$</span>, <span data-ref="0402.g1:< D E F H >">$DEFH$</span> the bases are<br />
reciprocally proportional to the heights, that is, as the base<br />
<span data-ref="0402.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> is to the base <span data-ref="0402.g1:< D E F >">$DEF$</span>, so is the height of the pyramid<br />
<span data-ref="0402.g1:< D E F H >">$DEFH$</span> to the height of the pyramid <span data-ref="0402.g1:< A B C G >">$ABCG$</span>.

![Book XII, Proposition 9](0402.g1.svg)

For let the parallelepipedal solids <span data-ref="0402.g1:< B G M L >">$BGML$</span>, <span data-ref="0402.g1:< E H Q P >">$EHQP$</span> be<br />
completed.

<p class="sub-clause">Now, since the pyramid <span data-ref="0402.g1:< A B C G >">$ABCG$</span> is equal to the pyramid<br />
<span data-ref="0402.g1:< D E F H >">$DEFH$</span>,<br />
and the solid <span data-ref="0402.g1:< B G M L >">$BGML$</span> is six times the pyramid <span data-ref="0402.g1:< A B C G >">$ABCG$</span>,<br />
and the solid <span data-ref="0402.g1:< E H Q P >">$EHQP$</span> six times the pyramid <span data-ref="0402.g1:< D E F H >">$DEFH$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the solid <span data-ref="0402.g1:< B G M L >">$BGML$</span> is equal to the solid <span data-ref="0402.g1:< E H Q P >">$EHQP$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">But in equal parallelepipedal solids the bases are recipro-<br />
cally proportional to the heights; <span class="justification">[XI. 34]</span></p>
<p class="assertion">therefore, as the base <span data-ref="0402.g1:< B C M A >">$BM$</span> is to the base <span data-ref="0402.g1:< E F Q D >">$EQ$</span>, so is the height<br />
of the solid <span data-ref="0402.g1:< E H Q P >">$EHQP$</span> to the height of the solid <span data-ref="0402.g1:< B G M L >">$BGML$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">But, as the base <span data-ref="0402.g1:< B C M A >">$BM$</span> is to <span data-ref="0402.g1:< E F Q D >">$EQ$</span>, so is the triangle <span data-ref="0402.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> to<br />
the triangle <span data-ref="0402.g1:< D E F >">$DEF$</span>. <span class="justification">[I. 34]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore also, as the triangle <span data-ref="0402.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> is to the triangle<br />
<span data-ref="0402.g1:< D E F >">$DEF$</span>, so is the height of the solid <span data-ref="0402.g1:< E H Q P >">$EHQP$</span> to the height of<br />
the solid <span data-ref="0402.g1:< B G M L >">$BGML$</span>. <span class="justification">[V. 11]</span></p>
