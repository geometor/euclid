### PROPOSITION 9.

*In equal pyramids which have triangular bases the bases are reciprocally proportional to the heights; and those pyramids in which the bases are reciprocally proportional to the heights are equal.*

For let there be equal pyramids which have the triangular bases <span data-ref="0402.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span>, <span data-ref="0402.g1:< D E F >">$DEF$</span> and vertices the points <span data-ref="0402.g1:G">$G$</span>, <span data-ref="0402.g1:H">$H$</span>;
I say that in the pyramids <span data-ref="0402.g1:< A B C G >">$ABCG$</span>, <span data-ref="0402.g1:< D E F H >">$DEFH$</span> the bases are reciprocally proportional to the heights, that is, as the base <span data-ref="0402.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> is to the base <span data-ref="0402.g1:< D E F >">$DEF$</span>, so is the height of the pyramid <span data-ref="0402.g1:< D E F H >">$DEFH$</span> to the height of the pyramid <span data-ref="0402.g1:< A B C G >">$ABCG$</span>.

![Book XII, Proposition 9](0402.g1.svg)

For let the parallelepipedal solids <span data-ref="0402.g1:< B G M L >">$BGML$</span>, <span data-ref="0402.g1:< E H Q P >">$EHQP$</span> be completed.

Now, since the pyramid <span data-ref="0402.g1:< A B C G >">$ABCG$</span> is equal to the pyramid <span data-ref="0402.g1:< D E F H >">$DEFH$</span>, and the solid <span data-ref="0402.g1:< B G M L >">$BGML$</span> is six times the pyramid <span data-ref="0402.g1:< A B C G >">$ABCG$</span>, and the solid <span data-ref="0402.g1:< E H Q P >">$EHQP$</span> six times the pyramid <span data-ref="0402.g1:< D E F H >">$DEFH$</span>, therefore the solid <span data-ref="0402.g1:< B G M L >">$BGML$</span> is equal to the solid <span data-ref="0402.g1:< E H Q P >">$EHQP$</span>.

But in equal parallelepipedal solids the bases are reciprocally proportional to the heights; [XI. 34]
therefore, as the base <span data-ref="0402.g1:< B C M A >">$BM$</span> is to the base <span data-ref="0402.g1:< E F Q D >">$EQ$</span>, so is the height of the solid <span data-ref="0402.g1:< E H Q P >">$EHQP$</span> to the height of the solid <span data-ref="0402.g1:< B G M L >">$BGML$</span>.

But, as the base <span data-ref="0402.g1:< B C M A >">$BM$</span> is to <span data-ref="0402.g1:< E F Q D >">$EQ$</span>, so is the triangle <span data-ref="0402.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> to the triangle <span data-ref="0402.g1:< D E F >">$DEF$</span>. [I. 34]

Therefore also, as the triangle <span data-ref="0402.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> is to the triangle <span data-ref="0402.g1:< D E F >">$DEF$</span>, so is the height of the solid <span data-ref="0402.g1:< E H Q P >">$EHQP$</span> to the height of the solid <span data-ref="0402.g1:< B G M L >">$BGML$</span>. [V. 11]
