### PROPOSITION 11.

*Cones and cylinders which are of the same height are to one another as their bases.*

Let there be cones and cylinders of the same height,  
let the circles <span data-ref="0410.g1:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span>, <span data-ref="0410.g1:( E F G H )">$EFGH$</span> be their bases, <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ K L /">$KL$</span>, <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ M N /">$MN$</span> their axes and <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ E G /">$EG$</span> the diameters of their bases;  
I say that, as the circle <span data-ref="0410.g1:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span> is to the circle <span data-ref="0410.g1:( E F G H )">$EFGH$</span>, so is the cone <span data-ref="0410.g1:< A L >">$AL$</span> to the cone <span data-ref="0410.g1:< E N >">$EN$</span>.

![Book XII, Proposition 11 Diagram](0410.g1.svg)

For, if not, then, as the circle <span data-ref="0410.g1:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span> is to the circle <span data-ref="0410.g1:( E F G H )">$EFGH$</span>, so will the cone <span data-ref="0410.g1:< A L >">$AL$</span> be either to some solid less than the cone <span data-ref="0410.g1:< E N >">$EN$</span> or to a greater.

First, let it be in that ratio to a less solid <span data-ref="0410.g1:O">$O$</span>, and let the solid <span data-ref="0410.g1:X">$X$</span> be equal to that by which the solid <span data-ref="0410.g1:O">$O$</span> is less than the cone <span data-ref="0410.g1:< E N >">$EN$</span>;  
therefore the cone <span data-ref="0410.g1:< E N >">$EN$</span> is equal to the solids <span data-ref="0410.g1:O">$O$</span>, <span data-ref="0410.g1:X">$X$</span>.

Let the square <span data-ref="0410.g1:< E F G H >">$EFGH$</span> be inscribed in the circle <span data-ref="0410.g1:( E F G H )">$EFGH$</span>;  
therefore the square is greater than the half of the circle.

Let there be set up from the square <span data-ref="0410.g1:< E F G H >">$EFGH$</span> a pyramid of equal height with the cone;  
therefore the pyramid so set up is greater than the half of the cone,  
inasmuch as, if we circumscribe a square about the circle, and set up from it a pyramid of equal height with the cone, the inscribed pyramid is half of the circumscribed pyramid,  
for they are to one another as their bases, [XII. 6]  
while the cone is less than the circumscribed pyramid.
