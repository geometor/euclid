### PROPOSITION 11.

<p class="enunciation">*Cones and cylinders which are of the same height are to*<br />
*one another as their bases.*</p>

Let there be cones and cylinders of the same height,<br />
let the circles <span data-ref="0410.g1:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span>, <span data-ref="0410.g1:( E F G H )">$EFGH$</span> be their bases, <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ K L /">$KL$</span>, <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ M N /">$MN$</span> their<br />
axes and <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0410.g1:/ E G /">$EG$</span> the diameters of their bases;<br />
I say that, as the circle <span data-ref="0410.g1:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span> is to the circle <span data-ref="0410.g1:( E F G H )">$EFGH$</span>, so is<br />
the cone <span data-ref="0410.g1:< A L >">$AL$</span> to the cone <span data-ref="0410.g1:< E N >">$EN$</span>.

![Book XII, Proposition 11 Diagram](0410.g1.svg)

<p class="sub-clause">For, if not, then, as the circle <span data-ref="0410.g1:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span> is to the circle<br />
<span data-ref="0410.g1:( E F G H )">$EFGH$</span>, so will the cone <span data-ref="0410.g1:< A L >">$AL$</span> be either to some solid less<br />
than the cone <span data-ref="0410.g1:< E N >">$EN$</span> or to a greater.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">First, let it be in that ratio to a less solid <span data-ref="0410.g1:O">$O$</span>, and let the<br />
solid <span data-ref="0410.g1:X">$X$</span> be equal to that by which the solid <span data-ref="0410.g1:O">$O$</span> is less than the<br />
cone <span data-ref="0410.g1:< E N >">$EN$</span>;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the cone <span data-ref="0410.g1:< E N >">$EN$</span> is equal to the solids <span data-ref="0410.g1:O">$O$</span>, <span data-ref="0410.g1:X">$X$</span>.</p>

Let the square <span data-ref="0410.g1:< E F G H >">$EFGH$</span> be inscribed in the circle <span data-ref="0410.g1:( E F G H )">$EFGH$</span>;
<p class="assertion">therefore the square is greater than the half of the circle.</p>

Let there be set up from the square <span data-ref="0410.g1:< E F G H >">$EFGH$</span> a pyramid of<br />
equal height with the cone;
<p class="assertion">therefore the pyramid so set up is greater than the half of the<br />
cone,<br />
inasmuch as, if we circumscribe a square about the circle, and<br />
set up from it a pyramid of equal height with the cone, the<br />
inscribed pyramid is half of the circumscribed pyramid,<br />
for they are to one another as their bases, <span class="justification">[XII. 6]</span><br />
while the cone is less than the circumscribed pyramid.</p>
