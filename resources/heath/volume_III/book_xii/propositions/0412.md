Therefore also, as the cone <span data-ref="0410.g1:< A L >">$AL$</span> is to the solid <span data-ref="0410.g1:O">$O$</span>, so is the pyramid of which the polygon <span data-ref="0410.g1:< D T A U B V C W >">$DTAUBVCW$</span> is the base and the point <span data-ref="0410.g1:L">$L$</span> the vertex to the pyramid of which the polygon <span data-ref="0410.g1:< H P E Q F R G S >">$HPEQFRGS$</span> is the base and the point <span data-ref="0410.g1:N">$N$</span> the vertex; [V. 11]  
therefore, alternately, as the cone <span data-ref="0410.g1:< A L >">$AL$</span> is to the pyramid in it, so is the solid <span data-ref="0410.g1:O">$O$</span> to the pyramid in the cone <span data-ref="0410.g1:< E N >">$EN$</span>. [V. 16]

But the cone <span data-ref="0410.g1:< A L >">$AL$</span> is greater than the pyramid in it;  
therefore the solid <span data-ref="0410.g1:O">$O$</span> is also greater than the pyramid in the cone <span data-ref="0410.g1:< E N >">$EN$</span>.

But it is also less:  
which is absurd.

Therefore the cone <span data-ref="0410.g1:< A L >">$AL$</span> is not to any solid less than the cone <span data-ref="0410.g1:< E N >">$EN$</span> as the circle <span data-ref="0410.g1:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span> is to the circle <span data-ref="0410.g1:( E F G H )">$EFGH$</span>.

Similarly we can prove that neither is the cone <span data-ref="0410.g1:< E N >">$EN$</span> to any solid less than the cone <span data-ref="0410.g1:< A L >">$AL$</span> as the circle <span data-ref="0410.g1:( E F G H )">$EFGH$</span> is to the circle <span data-ref="0410.g1:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span>.

I say next that neither is the cone <span data-ref="0410.g1:< A L >">$AL$</span> to any solid greater than the cone <span data-ref="0410.g1:< E N >">$EN$</span> as the circle <span data-ref="0410.g1:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span> is to the circle <span data-ref="0410.g1:( E F G H )">$EFGH$</span>.

For, if possible, let it be in that ratio to a greater solid <span data-ref="0410.g1:O">$O$</span>;  
therefore, inversely, as the circle <span data-ref="0410.g1:( E F G H )">$EFGH$</span> is to the circle <span data-ref="0410.g1:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span>, so is the solid <span data-ref="0410.g1:O">$O$</span> to the cone <span data-ref="0410.g1:< A L >">$AL$</span>.

But, as the solid <span data-ref="0410.g1:O">$O$</span> is to the cone <span data-ref="0410.g1:< A L >">$AL$</span>, so is the cone <span data-ref="0410.g1:< E N >">$EN$</span> to some solid less than the cone <span data-ref="0410.g1:< A L >">$AL$</span>;  
therefore also, as the circle <span data-ref="0410.g1:( E F G H )">$EFGH$</span> is to the circle <span data-ref="0410.g1:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span>, so is the cone <span data-ref="0410.g1:< E N >">$EN$</span> to some solid less than the cone <span data-ref="0410.g1:< A L >">$AL$</span>:  
which was proved impossible.

Therefore the cone <span data-ref="0410.g1:< A L >">$AL$</span> is not to any solid greater than the cone <span data-ref="0410.g1:< E N >">$EN$</span> as the circle <span data-ref="0410.g1:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span> is to the circle <span data-ref="0410.g1:( E F G H )">$EFGH$</span>.

But it was proved that neither is it in this ratio to a less solid;  
therefore, as the circle <span data-ref="0410.g1:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span> is to the circle <span data-ref="0410.g1:( E F G H )">$EFGH$</span>, so is the cone <span data-ref="0410.g1:< A L >">$AL$</span> to the cone <span data-ref="0410.g1:< E N >">$EN$</span>.

But, as the cone is to the cone, so is the cylinder to the cylinder,  
for each is triple of each; [XII. 10]
