### PROPOSITION 12.

<p class="enunciation">Similar cones and cylinders are to one another in the<br />
triplicate ratio of the diameters in their bases.</p>

Let there be similar cones and cylinders,<br />
let the circles <span data-ref="0414.g1:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span>, <span data-ref="0414.g1:( E F G H )">$EFGH$</span> be their bases, <span data-ref="0414.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>, <span data-ref="0414.g1:/ F H /">$FH$</span> the<br />
diameters of the bases, and <span data-ref="0414.g1:/ K L /">$KL$</span>, <span data-ref="0414.g1:/ M N /">$MN$</span> the axes of the cones<br />
and cylinders;<br />
I say that the cone of which the circle <span data-ref="0414.g1:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span> is the base and<br />
the point <span data-ref="0414.g1:L">$L$</span> the vertex has to the cone of which the circle<br />
<span data-ref="0414.g1:( E F G H )">$EFGH$</span> is the base and the point <span data-ref="0414.g1:N">$N$</span> the vertex the ratio<br />
triplicate of that which <span data-ref="0414.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> has to <span data-ref="0414.g1:/ F H /">$FH$</span>.

![Proposition 12](0414.g1.svg)

<p class="sub-clause">For, if the cone <span data-ref="0414.g1:< A B C D L >">$ABCDL$</span> has not to the cone <span data-ref="0414.g1:< E F G H N >">$EFGHN$</span><br />
the ratio triplicate of that which <span data-ref="0414.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> has to <span data-ref="0414.g1:/ F H /">$FH$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">the cone <span data-ref="0414.g1:< A B C D L >">$ABCDL$</span> will have that triplicate ratio either to<br />
some solid less than the cone <span data-ref="0414.g1:< E F G H N >">$EFGHN$</span> or to a greater.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">First, let it have that triplicate ratio to a less solid <span data-ref="0414.g1:O">$O$</span>.</p>

Let the square <span data-ref="0414.g1:< E F G H >">$EFGH$</span> be inscribed in the circle <span data-ref="0414.g1:( E F G H )">$EFGH$</span>;<br />
<span class="justification">[IV. 6]</span>
<p class="assertion">therefore the square <span data-ref="0414.g1:< E F G H >">$EFGH$</span> is greater than the half of the<br />
circle <span data-ref="0414.g1:( E F G H )">$EFGH$</span>.</p>

Now let there be set up on the square <span data-ref="0414.g1:< E F G H >">$EFGH$</span> a pyramid<br />
having the same vertex with the cone;
<p class="assertion">therefore the pyramid so set up is greater than the half part<br />
of the cone.</p>
