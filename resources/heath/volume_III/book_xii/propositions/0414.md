### PROPOSITION 12.

*Similar cones and cylinders are to one another in the triplicate ratio of the diameters in their bases.*

Let there be similar cones and cylinders,
let the circles <span data-ref="0414.g1:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span>, <span data-ref="0414.g1:( E F G H )">$EFGH$</span> be their bases, <span data-ref="0414.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>, <span data-ref="0414.g1:/ F H /">$FH$</span> the diameters of the bases, and <span data-ref="0414.g1:/ K L /">$KL$</span>, <span data-ref="0414.g1:/ M N /">$MN$</span> the axes of the cones and cylinders;
I say that the cone of which the circle <span data-ref="0414.g1:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span> is the base and the point <span data-ref="0414.g1:L">$L$</span> the vertex has to the cone of which the circle <span data-ref="0414.g1:( E F G H )">$EFGH$</span> is the base and the point <span data-ref="0414.g1:N">$N$</span> the vertex the ratio triplicate of that which <span data-ref="0414.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> has to <span data-ref="0414.g1:/ F H /">$FH$</span>.

![Proposition 12](0414.g1.svg)

For, if the cone <span data-ref="0414.g1:< A B C D L >">$ABCDL$</span> has not to the cone <span data-ref="0414.g1:< E F G H N >">$EFGHN$</span> the ratio triplicate of that which <span data-ref="0414.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> has to <span data-ref="0414.g1:/ F H /">$FH$</span>, the cone <span data-ref="0414.g1:< A B C D L >">$ABCDL$</span> will have that triplicate ratio either to some solid less than the cone <span data-ref="0414.g1:< E F G H N >">$EFGHN$</span> or to a greater.

First, let it have that triplicate ratio to a less solid <span data-ref="0414.g1:O">$O$</span>.

Let the square <span data-ref="0414.g1:< E F G H >">$EFGH$</span> be inscribed in the circle <span data-ref="0414.g1:( E F G H )">$EFGH$</span>; [^410_1]
therefore the square <span data-ref="0414.g1:< E F G H >">$EFGH$</span> is greater than the half of the circle <span data-ref="0414.g1:( E F G H )">$EFGH$</span>.

Now let there be set up on the square <span data-ref="0414.g1:< E F G H >">$EFGH$</span> a pyramid having the same vertex with the cone;
therefore the pyramid so set up is greater than the half part of the cone.
