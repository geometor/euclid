<p class="assertion">Therefore the cone <span data-ref="0419.g1:< A B C D L >">$ABCDL$</span> has not to any solid greater<br />
than the cone <span data-ref="0419.g1:< E F G H N >">$EFGHN$</span> the ratio triplicate of that which <span data-ref="0419.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span><br />
has to <span data-ref="0419.g1:/ F H /">$FH$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">But it was proved that neither has it this ratio to a less<br />
solid than the cone <span data-ref="0419.g1:< E F G H N >">$EFGHN$</span>.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore the cone <span data-ref="0419.g1:< A B C D L >">$ABCDL$</span> has to the cone <span data-ref="0419.g1:< E F G H N >">$EFGHN$</span><br />
the ratio triplicate of that which <span data-ref="0419.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> has to <span data-ref="0419.g1:/ F H /">$FH$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">But, as the cone is to the cone, so is the cylinder to the<br />
cylinder,<br />
for the cylinder which is on the same base as the cone and<br />
of equal height with it is triple of the cone; <span class="justification">[XII. 10]</span></p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the cylinder also has to the cylinder the ratio<br />
triplicate of that which <span data-ref="0419.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> has to <span data-ref="0419.g1:/ F H /">$FH$</span>.</p>

<p class="qed">Therefore etc.<span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

The method of proof is precisely that of the previous proposition. The<br />
only addition is caused by the necessity of proving that, if similar equilateral<br />
polygons be inscribed in the bases of two similar cones, and pyramids be<br />
erected on them with the same vertices as those of the cones, the pyramids<br />
(are similar and) are to one another in the triplicate ratio of corresponding<br />
edges.

Let <span data-ref="0419.g1:/ K L /">$KL$</span>, <span data-ref="0419.g1:/ M N /">$MN$</span> be the axes of the cones, <span data-ref="0419.g1:L">$L$</span>, <span data-ref="0419.g1:N">$N$</span> the vertices, and let <span data-ref="0419.g1:/ B T /">$BT$</span>, <span data-ref="0419.g1:/ F P /">$FP$</span><br />
be sides of similar polygons inscribed in the bases. Join <span data-ref="0419.g1:/ B K /">$BK$</span>, <span data-ref="0419.g1:/ T K /">$TK$</span>, <span data-ref="0419.g1:/ B L /">$BL$</span>, <span data-ref="0419.g1:/ T L /">$TL$</span>,<br />
<span data-ref="0419.g1:/ P M /">$PM$</span>, <span data-ref="0419.g1:/ F M /">$FM$</span>, <span data-ref="0419.g1:/ P N /">$PN$</span>, <span data-ref="0419.g1:/ F N /">$FN$</span>.

![Proposition 12 Commentary](0419.g1.svg)

Now <span data-ref="0419.g1:< B K L >">$BKL$</span>, <span data-ref="0419.g1:< F M N >">$FMN$</span> are right-angled triangles, and, since the cones are<br />
similar,<br />
<span data-ref="0419.g1:/ B K /">$BK$</span> : <span data-ref="0419.g1:/ K L /">$KL$</span> = <span data-ref="0419.g1:/ F M /">$FM$</span> : <span data-ref="0419.g1:/ M N /">$MN$</span>. <span class="justification">[XI. Def. 24]</span>

Therefore (1) $\Delta$s <span data-ref="0419.g1:< B K L >">$BKL$</span>, <span data-ref="0419.g1:< F M N >">$FMN$</span> are similar. <span class="justification">[VI. 6]</span><br />
Similarly (2) $\Delta$s <span data-ref="0419.g1:< T K L >">$TKL$</span>, <span data-ref="0419.g1:< P M N >">$PMN$</span> are similar.

Next, in $\Delta$s <span data-ref="0419.g1:< B K T >">$BKT$</span>, <span data-ref="0419.g1:< F M P >">$FMP$</span>, the angles <span data-ref="0419.g1:< B K T )">$BKT$</span>, <span data-ref="0419.g1:< F M P )">$FMP$</span> are equal, since each is<br />
the same fraction of four right angles; and the sides about the equal angles are<br />
proportional;<br />
therefore (3) $\Delta$s <span data-ref="0419.g1:< B K T >">$BKT$</span>, <span data-ref="0419.g1:< F M P >">$FMP$</span> are similar.
