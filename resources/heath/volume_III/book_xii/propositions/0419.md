Therefore the cone <span data-ref="0419.g1:< A B C D L >">$ABCDL$</span> has not to any solid greater than the cone <span data-ref="0419.g1:< E F G H N >">$EFGHN$</span> the ratio triplicate of that which <span data-ref="0419.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> has to <span data-ref="0419.g1:/ F H /">$FH$</span>.

But it was proved that neither has it this ratio to a less solid than the cone <span data-ref="0419.g1:< E F G H N >">$EFGHN$</span>.

Therefore the cone <span data-ref="0419.g1:< A B C D L >">$ABCDL$</span> has to the cone <span data-ref="0419.g1:< E F G H N >">$EFGHN$</span> the ratio triplicate of that which <span data-ref="0419.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> has to <span data-ref="0419.g1:/ F H /">$FH$</span>.

But, as the cone is to the cone, so is the cylinder to the cylinder,
for the cylinder which is on the same base as the cone and of equal height with it is triple of the cone; [^415_1]
therefore the cylinder also has to the cylinder the ratio triplicate of that which <span data-ref="0419.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> has to <span data-ref="0419.g1:/ F H /">$FH$</span>.

Therefore etc.

Q. E. D.

The method of proof is precisely that of the previous proposition. The only addition is caused by the necessity of proving that, if similar equilateral polygons be inscribed in the bases of two similar cones, and pyramids be erected on them with the same vertices as those of the cones, the pyramids (are similar and) are to one another in the triplicate ratio of corresponding edges.

Let <span data-ref="0419.g1:/ K L /">$KL$</span>, <span data-ref="0419.g1:/ M N /">$MN$</span> be the axes of the cones, <span data-ref="0419.g1:L">$L$</span>, <span data-ref="0419.g1:N">$N$</span> the vertices, and let <span data-ref="0419.g1:/ B T /">$BT$</span>, <span data-ref="0419.g1:/ F P /">$FP$</span> be sides of similar polygons inscribed in the bases. Join <span data-ref="0419.g1:/ B K /">$BK$</span>, <span data-ref="0419.g1:/ T K /">$TK$</span>, <span data-ref="0419.g1:/ B L /">$BL$</span>, <span data-ref="0419.g1:/ T L /">$TL$</span>, <span data-ref="0419.g1:/ P M /">$PM$</span>, <span data-ref="0419.g1:/ F M /">$FM$</span>, <span data-ref="0419.g1:/ P N /">$PN$</span>, <span data-ref="0419.g1:/ F N /">$FN$</span>.

![Proposition 12 Commentary](0419.g1.svg)

Now <span data-ref="0419.g1:< B K L >">$BKL$</span>, <span data-ref="0419.g1:< F M N >">$FMN$</span> are right-angled triangles, and, since the cones are similar,
<span data-ref="0419.g1:/ B K /">$BK$</span> : <span data-ref="0419.g1:/ K L /">$KL$</span> = <span data-ref="0419.g1:/ F M /">$FM$</span> : <span data-ref="0419.g1:/ M N /">$MN$</span>. [XI. Def. 24]

Therefore (1) $\triangle$s <span data-ref="0419.g1:< B K L >">$BKL$</span>, <span data-ref="0419.g1:< F M N >">$FMN$</span> are similar. [^415_2]
Similarly (2) $\triangle$s <span data-ref="0419.g1:< T K L >">$TKL$</span>, <span data-ref="0419.g1:< P M N >">$PMN$</span> are similar.

Next, in $\triangle$s <span data-ref="0419.g1:< B K T >">$BKT$</span>, <span data-ref="0419.g1:< F M P >">$FMP$</span>, the angles <span data-ref="0419.g1:< B K T )">$BKT$</span>, <span data-ref="0419.g1:< F M P )">$FMP$</span> are equal, since each is the same fraction of four right angles; and the sides about the equal angles are proportional;
therefore (3) $\triangle$s <span data-ref="0419.g1:< B K T >">$BKT$</span>, <span data-ref="0419.g1:< F M P >">$FMP$</span> are similar.
