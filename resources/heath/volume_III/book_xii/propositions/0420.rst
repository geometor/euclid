| Again, since from the similar $\triangle\text{s}$ :g:`$BKL$ <0419.g1:< B K L >>`, :g:`$FMN$ <0419.g1:< F M N >>`, and the similar $\triangle\text{s}$ :g:`$BKT$ <0419.g1:< B K T >>`,
| :g:`$FMP$ <0419.g1:< F M P >>` respectively,
| :g:`$LB$ <0419.g1:/ L B />` : :g:`$BK$ <0419.g1:/ B K />` = :g:`$NF$ <0419.g1:/ N F />` : :g:`$FM$ <0419.g1:/ F M />`,
| :g:`$BK$ <0419.g1:/ B K />` : :g:`$BT$ <0419.g1:/ B T />` = :g:`$MF$ <0419.g1:/ M F />` : :g:`$FP$ <0419.g1:/ F P />`,
| :term:`*ex aequali* <ex-aequali>`, :g:`$LB$ <0419.g1:/ L B />` : :g:`$BT$ <0419.g1:/ B T />` = :g:`$NF$ <0419.g1:/ N F />` : :g:`$FP$ <0419.g1:/ F P />`.
| Similarly :g:`$LT$ <0419.g1:/ L T />` : :g:`$TB$ <0419.g1:/ T B />` = :g:`$NP$ <0419.g1:/ N P />` : :g:`$PF$ <0419.g1:/ P F />`.
| Inverting the latter ratio and compounding it with the preceding one, we
| have, :term:`*ex aequali* <ex-aequali>`,
| :g:`$LB$ <0419.g1:/ L B />` : :g:`$LT$ <0419.g1:/ L T />` = :g:`$NF$ <0419.g1:/ N F />` : :g:`$NP$ <0419.g1:/ N P />`.

| Thus in $\triangle\text{s}$ :g:`$LTB$ <0419.g1:< L T B >>`, :g:`$NPF$ <0419.g1:< N P F >>` the sides are proportional in pairs ;
| therefore (4) $\triangle\text{s}$ :g:`$LTB$ <0419.g1:< L T B >>`, :g:`$NPF$ <0419.g1:< N P F >>` are similar.

| Thus the partial pyramids :g:`$L\text{-}BKT$ <0419.g1:< L B K T >>`, :g:`$N\text{-}FMP$ <0419.g1:< N F M P >>` are similar.
| In exactly the same way it is proved that all the other partial pyramids are
| similar.

| Now
| (pyramid :g:`$L\text{-}BKT$ <0419.g1:< L B K T >>`) : (pyramid :g:`$N\text{-}FMP$ <0419.g1:< N F M P >>`) = ratio triplicate of (:g:`$BK$ <0419.g1:/ B K />` : :g:`$FM$ <0419.g1:/ F M />`).
| The other partial pyramids are to one another in the same triplicate ratio.
| The sum of the antecedents is therefore to the sum of the consequents in
| the same triplicate ratio,
| i.e. (pyramid :g:`$L\text{-}ATBU\dots$ <0419.g1:< L A T B U >>`) : (pyramid :g:`$N\text{-}EPFQ\dots$ <0419.g1:< N E P F Q >>`)
| = ratio triplicate of ratio (:g:`$BK$ <0419.g1:/ B K />` : :g:`$FM$ <0419.g1:/ F M />`)
| = ratio triplicate of ratio (:g:`$BD$ <0419.g1:/ B D />` : :g:`$FH$ <0419.g1:/ F H />`).

| [The fact that Euclid makes this transition from the partial pyramids to
| the whole pyramids in the body of this proposition seems to me to suggest
| grave doubts as to the genuineness of the Porism to XII. 8, which contains a
| similar but rather more general extension from the case of triangular pyramids
| to pyramids with polygonal bases. Were that Porism genuine, Euclid would
| have been more likely to refer to it than to repeat here the same arguments
| which it contains.]

| Now we are in a position to apply the method of exhaustion.
| If $X$, $X'$ be the volumes of the cones, $d$, $d'$ the diameters of their bases, and if

$$(\text{ratio triplicate of } d : d') \ne X : X',$$

 
then must 

$$(\text{ratio triplicate of } d : d') = X : O,$$

 
where $O$ is either less or greater than $X'$.

| I. Suppose that $O$ is *less* than $X'$.
| Construct in the way described a pyramid ($\Pi'$) in $X'$ leaving over portions
| of $X'$ together less than $(X' - O)$, so that $X' > \Pi' > O$,
| and construct in $X$ a pyramid ($\Pi$), with the same vertex as $X$ has, on a
| polygon inscribed in its base similar to the base of $\Pi'$.

Then, by what has just been proved, 

$$\begin{aligned}
\Pi : \Pi' &= (\text{ratio triplicate of } d : d') \\
&= X : O\text{, by hypothesis,}
\end{aligned}$$

 
and, alternately, 

$$\Pi : X = \Pi' : O.$$

| But $X$ includes, and is therefore greater than, $\Pi$;
| therefore

$$O > \Pi'.$$

 
But, by construction, 

$$O < \Pi' :$$

 
which is impossible.

Therefore 

$$O\text{ cannot be less than } X'.$$
