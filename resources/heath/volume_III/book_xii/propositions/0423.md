### PROPOSITION 14.

*Cones and cylinders which are on equal bases are to one another as their heights.*

    For let <span data-ref="0423.g1:< E B >">$EB$</span>, <span data-ref="0423.g1:< F D >">$FD$</span> be cylinders on equal bases, the circles <span data-ref="0423.g1:( A B )">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0423.g1:( C D )">$CD$</span>;
I say that, as the cylinder <span data-ref="0423.g1:< E B >">$EB$</span> is to the cylinder <span data-ref="0423.g1:< F D >">$FD$</span>, so is the axis <span data-ref="0423.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span> to the axis <span data-ref="0423.g1:/ K L /">$KL$</span>.
    For let the axis <span data-ref="0423.g1:/ K L /">$KL$</span> be produced to the point <span data-ref="0423.g1:N">$N$</span>,
let <span data-ref="0423.g1:/ L N /">$LN$</span> be made equal to the axis <span data-ref="0423.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span>,
and let the cylinder <span data-ref="0423.g1:< C M >">$CM$</span> be conceived about <span data-ref="0423.g1:/ L N /">$LN$</span> as axis.

![Proposition 14 Diagram](0423.g1.svg)

    Since then the cylinders <span data-ref="0423.g1:< E B >">$EB$</span>, <span data-ref="0423.g1:< C M >">$CM$</span> are of the same height, they are to one another as their bases. [XII. 11]
    But the bases are equal to one another:
therefore the cylinders <span data-ref="0423.g1:< E B >">$EB$</span>, <span data-ref="0423.g1:< C M >">$CM$</span> are also equal.
    And, since the cylinder <span data-ref="0423.g1:< F M >">$FM$</span> has been cut by the plane <span data-ref="0423.g1:( C D )">$CD$</span> which is parallel to its opposite planes,
therefore, as the cylinder <span data-ref="0423.g1:< C M >">$CM$</span> is to the cylinder <span data-ref="0423.g1:< F D >">$FD$</span>, so is the axis <span data-ref="0423.g1:/ L N /">$LN$</span> to the axis <span data-ref="0423.g1:/ K L /">$KL$</span>. [XII. 13]
    But the cylinder <span data-ref="0423.g1:< C M >">$CM$</span> is equal to the cylinder <span data-ref="0423.g1:< E B >">$EB$</span>,
and the axis <span data-ref="0423.g1:/ L N /">$LN$</span> to the axis <span data-ref="0423.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span>;
therefore, as the cylinder <span data-ref="0423.g1:< E B >">$EB$</span> is to the cylinder <span data-ref="0423.g1:< F D >">$FD$</span>, so is the axis <span data-ref="0423.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span> to the axis <span data-ref="0423.g1:/ K L /">$KL$</span>.
    But, as the cylinder <span data-ref="0423.g1:< E B >">$EB$</span> is to the cylinder <span data-ref="0423.g1:< F D >">$FD$</span>, so is the cone <span data-ref="0423.g1:< A B G >">$ABG$</span> to the cone <span data-ref="0423.g1:< C D K >">$CDK$</span>. [XII. 10]
    Therefore also, as the axis <span data-ref="0423.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span> is to the axis <span data-ref="0423.g1:/ K L /">$KL$</span>, so is the cone <span data-ref="0423.g1:< A B G >">$ABG$</span> to the cone <span data-ref="0423.g1:< C D K >">$CDK$</span> and the cylinder <span data-ref="0423.g1:< E B >">$EB$</span> to the cylinder <span data-ref="0423.g1:< F D >">$FD$</span>.

<div align="right">Q. E. D.</div>
