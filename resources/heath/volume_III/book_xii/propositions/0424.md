### PROPOSITION 15.

*In equal cones and cylinders the bases are reciprocally proportional to the heights; and those cones and cylinders in which the bases are reciprocally proportional to the heights are equal.*

    Let there be equal cones and cylinders of which the circles <span data-ref="0424.g1:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span>, <span data-ref="0424.g1:( E F G H )">$EFGH$</span> are the bases;
let <span data-ref="0424.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0424.g1:/ E G /">$EG$</span> be the diameters of the bases,
and <span data-ref="0424.g1:/ K L /">$KL$</span>, <span data-ref="0424.g1:/ M N /">$MN$</span> the axes, which are also the heights of the cones or cylinders;
let the cylinders <span data-ref="0424.g1:< A O >">$AO$</span>, <span data-ref="0424.g1:< E P >">$EP$</span> be completed.
    I say that in the cylinders <span data-ref="0424.g1:< A O >">$AO$</span>, <span data-ref="0424.g1:< E P >">$EP$</span> the bases are reciprocally proportional to the heights,
that is, as the base <span data-ref="0424.g1:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span> is to the base <span data-ref="0424.g1:( E F G H )">$EFGH$</span>, so is the height <span data-ref="0424.g1:/ M N /">$MN$</span> to the height <span data-ref="0424.g1:/ K L /">$KL$</span>.

    For the height <span data-ref="0424.g1:/ L K /">$LK$</span> is either equal to the height <span data-ref="0424.g1:/ M N /">$MN$</span> or not equal.
    First, let it be equal.
    Now the cylinder <span data-ref="0424.g1:< A O >">$AO$</span> is also equal to the cylinder <span data-ref="0424.g1:< E P >">$EP$</span>.
    But cones and cylinders which are of the same height are to one another as their bases; [XII. 11]
therefore the base <span data-ref="0424.g1:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span> is also equal to the base <span data-ref="0424.g1:( E F G H )">$EFGH$</span>.
