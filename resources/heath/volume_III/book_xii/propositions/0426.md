<p class="assertion">Therefore the cylinder <span data-ref="0424.g1:< A O >">$AO$</span> is equal to the cylinder <span data-ref="0424.g1:< E P >">$EP$</span>. <span class="justification">[v. 9]</span><br />
And the same is true for the cones also.</p>

<p class="qed"><span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

---

**I.** If the heights of the two cylinders are equal, and their volumes are<br />
equal, the bases are equal, since the latter are proportional to the volumes.<br />
<span class="justification">[XII. 11]</span><br />
If the heights are *not* equal, cut off from the higher cylinder a cylinder of<br />
the same height as the lower.<br />
Then, if <span data-ref="0424.g1:/ L K /">$LK$</span>, <span data-ref="0424.g1:/ Q N /">$QN$</span> be the equal heights,<br />
we have, by XII. 11,<br />
(base <span data-ref="0424.g1:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span>) : (base <span data-ref="0424.g1:( E F G H )">$EFGH$</span>) = (cylinder <span data-ref="0424.g1:< A O >">$AO$</span>) : (cylinder <span data-ref="0424.g1:< E S >">$ES$</span>)<br />
= (cylinder <span data-ref="0424.g1:< E P >">$EP$</span>) : (cylinder <span data-ref="0424.g1:< E S >">$ES$</span>),<br />
by hypothesis,<br />
= <span data-ref="0424.g1:/ M N /">$MN$</span> : <span data-ref="0424.g1:/ Q N /">$QN$</span> <span class="justification">[XII. 13]</span><br />
= <span data-ref="0424.g1:/ M N /">$MN$</span> : <span data-ref="0424.g1:/ K L /">$KL$</span>.

**II.** In the converse part of the proposition, Euclid omits the case where<br />
the cylinders have equal heights. In this case of course the reciprocal ratios<br />
are both ratios of equality; the bases are therefore equal, and consequently the<br />
cylinders.<br />
If the heights are *not* equal, we have, with the same construction as before,<br />
(base <span data-ref="0424.g1:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span>) : (base <span data-ref="0424.g1:( E F G H )">$EFGH$</span>) = <span data-ref="0424.g1:/ M N /">$MN$</span> : <span data-ref="0424.g1:/ K L /">$KL$</span>.<br />
But <span class="justification">[XII. 11]</span><br />
(base <span data-ref="0424.g1:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span>) : (base <span data-ref="0424.g1:( E F G H )">$EFGH$</span>) = (cylinder <span data-ref="0424.g1:< A O >">$AO$</span>) : (cylinder <span data-ref="0424.g1:< E S >">$ES$</span>),<br />
and<br />
<span data-ref="0424.g1:/ M N /">$MN$</span> : <span data-ref="0424.g1:/ K L /">$KL$</span> = <span data-ref="0424.g1:/ M N /">$MN$</span> : <span data-ref="0424.g1:/ Q N /">$QN$</span><br />
= (cylinder <span data-ref="0424.g1:< E P >">$EP$</span>) : (cylinder <span data-ref="0424.g1:< E S >">$ES$</span>). <span class="justification">[XII. 13]</span><br />
Therefore<br />
(cylinder <span data-ref="0424.g1:< A O >">$AO$</span>) : (cylinder <span data-ref="0424.g1:< E S >">$ES$</span>) = (cylinder <span data-ref="0424.g1:< E P >">$EP$</span>) : (cylinder <span data-ref="0424.g1:< E S >">$ES$</span>),<br />
and consequently<br />
(cylinder <span data-ref="0424.g1:< A O >">$AO$</span>) = (cylinder <span data-ref="0424.g1:< E P >">$EP$</span>).

Similarly for the cones, which are equal to one-third of the cylinders<br />
respectively.

Legendre deduces these propositions about cones and cylinders from two<br />
others which he establishes by a method similar<br />
to that adopted by him for the theorem of XII. 2<br />
(see note on that proposition).<br />
The first (for the cylinder) is as follows.<br />
*The volume of a cylinder is equal to the<br />
product of its base by its height.*<br />
Suppose <span data-ref="0426.g1:/ C A /">$CA$</span> to be the radius of the base of<br />
the given cylinder, $h$ its height.<br />
For brevity let us denote by (surf. <span data-ref="0426.g1:( C A )">$CA$</span>) the<br />
area of the circle of which <span data-ref="0426.g1:/ C A /">$CA$</span> is the radius.<br />
If (surf. <span data-ref="0426.g1:( C A )">$CA$</span>) $\times h$ is not the measure of the<br />
given cylinder, it will be the measure of a<br />
cylinder greater or less than it.

**I.** First let it be the measure of a less<br />
cylinder, that, for example, of which the circle with radius <span data-ref="0426.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> is the base, and<br />
$h$ is the height.
