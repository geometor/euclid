    Therefore the cylinder <span data-ref="0424.g1:< A O >">$AO$</span> is equal to the cylinder <span data-ref="0424.g1:< E P >">$EP$</span>. [v. 9]
    And the same is true for the cones also.

<div align="right">Q. E. D.</div>

---

**I.** If the heights of the two cylinders are equal, and their volumes are equal, the bases are equal, since the latter are proportional to the volumes. [XII. 11]
    If the heights are *not* equal, cut off from the higher cylinder a cylinder of the same height as the lower.
    Then, if <span data-ref="0424.g1:/ L K /">$LK$</span>, <span data-ref="0424.g1:/ Q N /">$QN$</span> be the equal heights,
we have, by **XII. 11**,

(base <span data-ref="0424.g1:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span>) : (base <span data-ref="0424.g1:( E F G H )">$EFGH$</span>) = (cylinder <span data-ref="0424.g1:< A O >">$AO$</span>) : (cylinder <span data-ref="0424.g1:< E S >">$ES$</span>)
= (cylinder <span data-ref="0424.g1:< E P >">$EP$</span>) : (cylinder <span data-ref="0424.g1:< E S >">$ES$</span>), by hypothesis,
= <span data-ref="0424.g1:/ M N /">$MN$</span> : <span data-ref="0424.g1:/ Q N /">$QN$</span> [XII. 13]
= <span data-ref="0424.g1:/ M N /">$MN$</span> : <span data-ref="0424.g1:/ K L /">$KL$</span>.

**II.** In the converse part of the proposition, Euclid omits the case where the cylinders have equal heights. In this case of course the reciprocal ratios are both ratios of equality; the bases are therefore equal, and consequently the cylinders.
    If the heights are *not* equal, we have, with the same construction as before,

(base <span data-ref="0424.g1:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span>) : (base <span data-ref="0424.g1:( E F G H )">$EFGH$</span>) = <span data-ref="0424.g1:/ M N /">$MN$</span> : <span data-ref="0424.g1:/ K L /">$KL$</span>.

    But [**XII. 11**]

(base <span data-ref="0424.g1:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span>) : (base <span data-ref="0424.g1:( E F G H )">$EFGH$</span>) = (cylinder <span data-ref="0424.g1:< A O >">$AO$</span>) : (cylinder <span data-ref="0424.g1:< E S >">$ES$</span>),

and

<span data-ref="0424.g1:/ M N /">$MN$</span> : <span data-ref="0424.g1:/ K L /">$KL$</span> = <span data-ref="0424.g1:/ M N /">$MN$</span> : <span data-ref="0424.g1:/ Q N /">$QN$</span>
= (cylinder <span data-ref="0424.g1:< E P >">$EP$</span>) : (cylinder <span data-ref="0424.g1:< E S >">$ES$</span>). [XII. 13]

    Therefore

(cylinder <span data-ref="0424.g1:< A O >">$AO$</span>) : (cylinder <span data-ref="0424.g1:< E S >">$ES$</span>) = (cylinder <span data-ref="0424.g1:< E P >">$EP$</span>) : (cylinder <span data-ref="0424.g1:< E S >">$ES$</span>),

and consequently

(cylinder <span data-ref="0424.g1:< A O >">$AO$</span>) = (cylinder <span data-ref="0424.g1:< E P >">$EP$</span>).

    Similarly for the cones, which are equal to one-third of the cylinders respectively.
    Legendre deduces these propositions about cones and cylinders from two others which he establishes by a method similar to that adopted by him for the theorem of **XII. 2** (see note on that proposition).
    The first (for the cylinder) is as follows.
    *The volume of a cylinder is equal to the product of its base by its height.*
    Suppose <span data-ref="0426.g1:/ C A /">$CA$</span> to be the radius of the base of the given cylinder, $h$ its height.
    For brevity let us denote by (surf. <span data-ref="0426.g1:( C A )">$CA$</span>) the area of the circle of which <span data-ref="0426.g1:/ C A /">$CA$</span> is the radius.
    If (surf. <span data-ref="0426.g1:( C A )">$CA$</span>) $\times h$ is not the measure of the given cylinder, it will be the measure of a cylinder greater or less than it.

    **I.** First let it be the measure of a less cylinder, that, for example, of which the circle with radius <span data-ref="0426.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> is the base, and $h$ is the height.
