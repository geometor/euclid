:title: Proposition 15 (concluded and commentary)

.. assertion::

   | Therefore the cylinder :g:`$AO$ <0424.g1:< A O >>` is equal to the cylinder :g:`$EP$ <0424.g1:< E P >>`. :just:`[v. 9]`
   | And the same is true for the cones also.

.. qed::

   :just:`Q. E. D.`

----

| **I.** If the heights of the two cylinders are equal, and their volumes are
| equal, the bases are equal, since the latter are proportional to the volumes.
| :just:`[XII. 11] <XII.11>`
| If the heights are *not* equal, cut off from the higher cylinder a cylinder of
| the same height as the lower.
| Then, if :g:`$LK$ <0424.g1:/ L K />`, :g:`$QN$ <0424.g1:/ Q N />` be the equal heights,
| we have, by XII. 11,
| (base :g:`$ABCD$ <0424.g1:( A B C D )>`) : (base :g:`$EFGH$ <0424.g1:( E F G H )>`) = (cylinder :g:`$AO$ <0424.g1:< A O >>`) : (cylinder :g:`$ES$ <0424.g1:< E S >>`)
| = (cylinder :g:`$EP$ <0424.g1:< E P >>`) : (cylinder :g:`$ES$ <0424.g1:< E S >>`),
| by hypothesis,
| = :g:`$MN$ <0424.g1:/ M N />` : :g:`$QN$ <0424.g1:/ Q N />` :just:`[XII. 13] <XII.13>`
| = :g:`$MN$ <0424.g1:/ M N />` : :g:`$KL$ <0424.g1:/ K L />`.

| **II.** In the converse part of the proposition, Euclid omits the case where
| the cylinders have equal heights. In this case of course the reciprocal ratios
| are both ratios of equality; the bases are therefore equal, and consequently the
| cylinders.
| If the heights are *not* equal, we have, with the same construction as before,
| (base :g:`$ABCD$ <0424.g1:( A B C D )>`) : (base :g:`$EFGH$ <0424.g1:( E F G H )>`) = :g:`$MN$ <0424.g1:/ M N />` : :g:`$KL$ <0424.g1:/ K L />`.
| But :just:`[XII. 11] <XII.11>`
| (base :g:`$ABCD$ <0424.g1:( A B C D )>`) : (base :g:`$EFGH$ <0424.g1:( E F G H )>`) = (cylinder :g:`$AO$ <0424.g1:< A O >>`) : (cylinder :g:`$ES$ <0424.g1:< E S >>`),
| and
| :g:`$MN$ <0424.g1:/ M N />` : :g:`$KL$ <0424.g1:/ K L />` = :g:`$MN$ <0424.g1:/ M N />` : :g:`$QN$ <0424.g1:/ Q N />`
| = (cylinder :g:`$EP$ <0424.g1:< E P >>`) : (cylinder :g:`$ES$ <0424.g1:< E S >>`). :just:`[XII. 13] <XII.13>`
| Therefore
| (cylinder :g:`$AO$ <0424.g1:< A O >>`) : (cylinder :g:`$ES$ <0424.g1:< E S >>`) = (cylinder :g:`$EP$ <0424.g1:< E P >>`) : (cylinder :g:`$ES$ <0424.g1:< E S >>`),
| and consequently
| (cylinder :g:`$AO$ <0424.g1:< A O >>`) = (cylinder :g:`$EP$ <0424.g1:< E P >>`).

| Similarly for the cones, which are equal to one-third of the cylinders
| respectively.

| Legendre deduces these propositions about cones and cylinders from two
| others which he establishes by a method similar
| to that adopted by him for the theorem of XII. 2
| (see note on that proposition).
| The first (for the cylinder) is as follows.
| *The volume of a cylinder is equal to the
| product of its base by its height.*
| Suppose :g:`$CA$ <0426.g1:/ C A />` to be the radius of the base of
| the given cylinder, $h$ its height.
| For brevity let us denote by (surf. :g:`$CA$ <0426.g1:( C A )>`) the
| area of the circle of which :g:`$CA$ <0426.g1:/ C A />` is the radius.
| If (surf. :g:`$CA$ <0426.g1:( C A )>`) $\times h$ is not the measure of the
| given cylinder, it will be the measure of a
| cylinder greater or less than it.
