Circumscribe about the circle with radius <span data-ref="0426.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> a regular polygon <span data-ref="0426.g1:< G H I >">$GHI\dots$</span><br />
such that its sides do not anywhere meet the circle with radius <span data-ref="0426.g1:/ C A /">$CA$</span>. [See note<br />
on XII. 2, p. 393 above, for Legendre's lemma relating to this construction.]<br />
Imagine a prism erected on the polygon as base and with height $h$.<br />
Then (volume of prism) = (polygon <span data-ref="0426.g1:< G H I >">$GHI\dots$</span>) $\times h$.<br />
[Legendre has previously proved this proposition, first for a parallelepiped<br />
(by transforming it into a rectangular one), then for a triangular prism (half of<br />
a parallelepiped of the same height), and lastly for a prism with a polygonal<br />
base.]<br />
But (polygon <span data-ref="0426.g1:< G H I >">$GHI\dots$</span>) $<$ (surf. <span data-ref="0426.g1:( C A )">$CA$</span>).<br />
Therefore (volume of prism) $<$ (surf. <span data-ref="0426.g1:( C A )">$CA$</span>) $\times h$<br />
$<$ (cylinder on circle of rad. <span data-ref="0426.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>),<br />
by hypothesis.<br />
But the prism is *greater* than the latter cylinder, since it includes it:<br />
which is impossible.

**II.** In order not to multiply figures let us, in this second case, suppose<br />
that <span data-ref="0426.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> is the radius of the base of the given cylinder, and that (surf. <span data-ref="0426.g1:( C D )">$CD$</span>) $\times h$<br />
is the measure of a cylinder greater than it, e.g. a cylinder on the circle with<br />
radius <span data-ref="0426.g1:/ C A /">$CA$</span> as base and of height $h$.<br />
Then, with the same construction,<br />
(volume of prism) = (polygon <span data-ref="0426.g1:< G H I >">$GHI\dots$</span>) $\times h$.<br />
And (polygon <span data-ref="0426.g1:< G H I >">$GHI\dots$</span>) $>$ (surf. <span data-ref="0426.g1:( C D )">$CD$</span>).<br />
Therefore (volume of prism) $>$ (surf. <span data-ref="0426.g1:( C D )">$CD$</span>) $\times h$<br />
$>$ (cylinder on surf. <span data-ref="0426.g1:( C A )">$CA$</span>), by hypothesis.<br />
But the volume of the prism is also *less* than that cylinder, being included<br />
by it:<br />
which is impossible.

Therefore (volume of cylinder) = (its base) $\times$ (its height).

It follows as a corollary that<br />
*Cylinders of the same height are to one another as their bases* [XII. 13], *and*<br />
*cylinders on the same base are to one another as their heights* [XII. 14].

Also<br />
*Similar cylinders are as the cubes of their heights, or as the cubes of the*<br />
*diameters of their bases* [Eucl. XII. 12].

For the bases are as the squares on their diameters; and, since the<br />
cylinders are similar, the diameters of the bases are as their heights.<br />
Therefore the bases are as the squares on the heights, and the bases<br />
multiplied by the heights, or the cylinders themselves, are as the cubes of the<br />
heights.

I need not reproduce Legendre's proofs of the corresponding propositions<br />
for the cone.
