Circumscribe about the circle with radius <span data-ref="0426.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> a regular polygon <span data-ref="0426.g1:< G H I >">$GHI\dots$</span> such that its sides do not anywhere meet the circle with radius <span data-ref="0426.g1:/ C A /">$CA$</span>. [See note on **XII. 2**, p. 393 above, for Legendre's lemma relating to this construction.]
    Imagine a prism erected on the polygon as base and with height $h$.
    Then
(volume of prism) = (polygon <span data-ref="0426.g1:< G H I >">$GHI\dots$</span>) $\times h$.
    [Legendre has previously proved this proposition, first for a parallelepiped (by transforming it into a rectangular one), then for a triangular prism (half of a parallelepiped of the same height), and lastly for a prism with a polygonal base.]
    But
(polygon <span data-ref="0426.g1:< G H I >">$GHI\dots$</span>) $<$ (surf. <span data-ref="0426.g1:( C A )">$CA$</span>).
    Therefore
(volume of prism) $<$ (surf. <span data-ref="0426.g1:( C A )">$CA$</span>) $\times h$
$<$ (cylinder on circle of rad. <span data-ref="0426.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>), by hypothesis.
    But the prism is *greater* than the latter cylinder, since it includes it: which is impossible.
    **II.** In order not to multiply figures let us, in this second case, suppose that <span data-ref="0426.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> is the radius of the base of the given cylinder, and that (surf. <span data-ref="0426.g1:( C D )">$CD$</span>) $\times h$ is the measure of a cylinder greater than it, e.g. a cylinder on the circle with radius <span data-ref="0426.g1:/ C A /">$CA$</span> as base and of height $h$.
    Then, with the same construction,
(volume of prism) = (polygon <span data-ref="0426.g1:< G H I >">$GHI\dots$</span>) $\times h$.
    And
(polygon <span data-ref="0426.g1:< G H I >">$GHI\dots$</span>) $>$ (surf. <span data-ref="0426.g1:( C D )">$CD$</span>).
    Therefore
(volume of prism) $>$ (surf. <span data-ref="0426.g1:( C D )">$CD$</span>) $\times h$
$>$ (cylinder on surf. <span data-ref="0426.g1:( C A )">$CA$</span>), by hypothesis.
    But the volume of the prism is also *less* than that cylinder, being included by it:
which is impossible.
    Therefore
$$(\text{volume of cylinder}) = (\text{its base}) \times (\text{its height}).$$
    It follows as a corollary that
    *Cylinders of the same height are to one another as their bases* [**XII. 13**], *and cylinders on the same base are to one another as their heights* [**XII. 14**].
    *Also*
    *Similar cylinders are as the cubes of their heights, or as the cubes of the diameters of their bases* [Eucl. **XII. 12**].
    For the bases are as the squares on their diameters; and, since the cylinders are similar, the diameters of the bases are as their heights.
    Therefore the bases are as the squares on the heights, and the bases multiplied by the heights, or the cylinders themselves, are as the cubes of the heights.
    I need not reproduce Legendre's proofs of the corresponding propositions for the cone.
