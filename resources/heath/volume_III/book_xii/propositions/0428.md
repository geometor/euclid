    Let <span data-ref="0428.g1:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span>, <span data-ref="0428.g1:( E F G H )">$EFGH$</span> be the two given circles about the same centre <span data-ref="0428.g1:K">$K$</span>;
thus it is required to inscribe in the greater circle <span data-ref="0428.g1:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span> an equilateral polygon with an even number of sides which does not touch the circle <span data-ref="0428.g1:( E F G H )">$EFGH$</span>.
    For let the straight line <span data-ref="0428.g1:/ B K D /">$BKD$</span> be drawn through the centre <span data-ref="0428.g1:K">$K$</span>,
and from the point <span data-ref="0428.g1:G">$G$</span> let <span data-ref="0428.g1:/ G A /">$GA$</span> be drawn at right angles to the straight line <span data-ref="0428.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> and carried through to <span data-ref="0428.g1:C">$C$</span>;
therefore <span data-ref="0428.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> touches the circle <span data-ref="0428.g1:( E F G H )">$EFGH$</span>. [III. 16, Por.]
    Then, bisecting the circumference <span data-ref="0428.g1:( B A D )">$BAD$</span>, bisecting the half of it, and doing this continually, we shall leave a circumference less than <span data-ref="0428.g1:( A D )">$AD$</span>. [x. 1]
    Let such be left, and let it be <span data-ref="0428.g1:( L D )">$LD$</span>;
from <span data-ref="0428.g1:L">$L$</span> let <span data-ref="0428.g1:/ L M /">$LM$</span> be drawn perpendicular to <span data-ref="0428.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> and carried through to <span data-ref="0428.g1:N">$N$</span>,
and let <span data-ref="0428.g1:/ L D /">$LD$</span>, <span data-ref="0428.g1:/ D N /">$DN$</span> be joined;
therefore <span data-ref="0428.g1:/ L D /">$LD$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0428.g1:/ D N /">$DN$</span>. [III. 3, I. 4]

![Proposition 16 Diagram](0428.g1.svg)

    Now, since <span data-ref="0428.g1:/ L N /">$LN$</span> is parallel to <span data-ref="0428.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>,
and <span data-ref="0428.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> touches the circle <span data-ref="0428.g1:( E F G H )">$EFGH$</span>,
therefore <span data-ref="0428.g1:/ L N /">$LN$</span> does not touch the circle <span data-ref="0428.g1:( E F G H )">$EFGH$</span>;
therefore <span data-ref="0428.g1:/ L D /">$LD$</span>, <span data-ref="0428.g1:/ D N /">$DN$</span> are far from touching the circle <span data-ref="0428.g1:( E F G H )">$EFGH$</span>.
    If then we fit into the circle <span data-ref="0428.g1:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span> straight lines equal to the straight line <span data-ref="0428.g1:/ L D /">$LD$</span> and placed continuously, there will be inscribed in the circle <span data-ref="0428.g1:( A B C D )">$ABCD$</span> an equilateral polygon with an even number of sides which does not touch the lesser circle <span data-ref="0428.g1:( E F G H )">$EFGH$</span>.

<div align="right">Q. E. F.</div>

---

It must be carefully observed that the polygon inscribed in the outer circle in this proposition is such that not only do its own sides not touch the inner circle, but also *the chords, as <span data-ref="0428.g1:/ L N /">$LN$</span>, joining angular points next but one to each other do not touch the inner circle either*. In other words, the polygon is the second in order, not the first, which satisfies the condition of the enunciation. This is important, because such a polygon is wanted in the next proposition; hence in that proposition the *exact* construction here given must be followed.
