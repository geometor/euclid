And, if we make the same construction in the case of each of the sides <span data-ref="0429.g1:/ K L /">$KL$</span>, <span data-ref="0429.g1:/ L M /">$LM$</span>, <span data-ref="0429.g1:/ M E /">$ME$</span> as in the case of <span data-ref="0429.g1:/ B K /">$BK$</span>, and further in the case of the remaining three quadrants, there will be constructed a certain polyhedral figure inscribed in the sphere and contained by pyramids, of which the said quadrilaterals and the triangle <span data-ref="0429.g1:< U R O >">$URO$</span>, and the others corresponding to them, are the bases and the point <span data-ref="0429.g1:A">$A$</span> the vertex.

I say that the said polyhedron will not touch the lesser sphere at the surface on which the circle <span data-ref="0429.g1:( F G H )">$FGH$</span> is.
Let <span data-ref="0429.g1:/ A X /">$AX$</span> be drawn from the point <span data-ref="0429.g1:A">$A$</span> perpendicular to the plane of the quadrilateral <span data-ref="0429.g1:< K B P S >">$KBPS$</span>, and let it meet the plane at the point <span data-ref="0429.g1:X">$X$</span>; [XI. 11]
let <span data-ref="0429.g1:/ X B /">$XB$</span>, <span data-ref="0429.g1:/ X K /">$XK$</span> be joined.
Then, since <span data-ref="0429.g1:/ A X /">$AX$</span> is at right angles to the plane of the quadrilateral <span data-ref="0429.g1:< K B P S >">$KBPS$</span>,
therefore it is also at right angles to all the straight lines which meet it and are in the plane of the quadrilateral. [XI. Def. 3]
Therefore <span data-ref="0429.g1:/ A X /">$AX$</span> is at right angles to each of the straight lines <span data-ref="0429.g1:/ B X /">$BX$</span>, <span data-ref="0429.g1:/ X K /">$XK$</span>.
And, since <span data-ref="0429.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0429.g1:/ A K /">$AK$</span>,
the square on <span data-ref="0429.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is also equal to the square on <span data-ref="0429.g1:/ A K /">$AK$</span>.
And the squares on <span data-ref="0429.g1:/ A X /">$AX$</span>, <span data-ref="0429.g1:/ X B /">$XB$</span> are equal to the square on <span data-ref="0429.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>,
for the angle at <span data-ref="0429.g1:X">$X$</span> is right; [I. 47]
and the squares on <span data-ref="0429.g1:/ A X /">$AX$</span>, <span data-ref="0429.g1:/ X K /">$XK$</span> are equal to the square on <span data-ref="0429.g1:/ A K /">$AK$</span>. [*id.*]
Therefore the squares on <span data-ref="0429.g1:/ A X /">$AX$</span>, <span data-ref="0429.g1:/ X B /">$XB$</span> are equal to the squares on <span data-ref="0429.g1:/ A X /">$AX$</span>, <span data-ref="0429.g1:/ X K /">$XK$</span>.
Let the square on <span data-ref="0429.g1:/ A X /">$AX$</span> be subtracted from each;
therefore the remainder, the square on <span data-ref="0429.g1:/ B X /">$BX$</span>, is equal to the remainder, the square on <span data-ref="0429.g1:/ X K /">$XK$</span>;
therefore <span data-ref="0429.g1:/ B X /">$BX$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0429.g1:/ X K /">$XK$</span>.
Similarly we can prove that the straight lines joined from <span data-ref="0429.g1:X">$X$</span> to <span data-ref="0429.g1:P">$P$</span>, <span data-ref="0429.g1:S">$S$</span> are equal to each of the straight lines <span data-ref="0429.g1:/ B X /">$BX$</span>, <span data-ref="0429.g1:/ X K /">$XK$</span>.
