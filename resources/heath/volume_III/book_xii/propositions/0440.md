It is the method of this proposition which Legendre adopted for his proof<br />
of XII. 2 (see note on that proposition).

The argument can be put very shortly. We will suppose $S, S'$ to be the<br />
volumes of the spheres, and $d, d'$ to be their diameters; and we will for brevity<br />
express the triplicate ratio of $d$ to $d'$ by $d^3 : d'^3$.

If
$$d^3 : d'^3 \ne S : S',$$
then
$$d^3 : d'^3 = S : T,$$
where $T$ is the volume of some sphere either greater or less than $S'$.

I. Suppose, if possible, that $T < S'$.

Let $T$ be supposed concentric with $S'$.

As in XII. 17, inscribe a polyhedron in $S'$ such that its faces do not any-<br />
where touch $T$;<br />
and inscribe in $S$ a polyhedron similar to that in $S'$.
