# Transcription Notes: Folio 0442 (Page 438)

## Summary & Folio Continuity
- **Page Context**: Page 438 marks the beginning of Book XIII of Euclid's *Elements* in Heath's edition.
- **Section**: Historical Note introducing Book XIII and the theory of the five regular polyhedra (Platonic solids).
- **Continuity**: Sets the historical framework for the constructions in Book XIII, detailing the earlier discoveries attributed to the Pythagoreans, Theaetetus, and Aristaeus the elder, leading directly to page 439.

## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Attributions**:
  - Cube, tetrahedron (pyramid), octahedron known empirically to Egyptians; bronze dodecahedra predating Pythagoras (references to Moritz Cantor, *Geschichte der Mathematik* I<sub>3</sub>, pp. 175–176).
  - Scholium No. 1 to Book XIII (likely Geminus): cube, pyramid, dodecahedron attributed to Pythagoreans; octahedron and icosahedron to Theaetetus.
  - Suidas entry on Theaetetus (s. v. Θεαίτητος): first systematic treatise on the five solids.
  - Connection noted between Theaetetus' work on irrationals (Book X) and regular solids (Book XIII).
  - Aristaeus the elder (c. 320 B.C.), author of *Solid Loci* and *Comparison of the five regular solids*.
  - Hypsicles (2nd cent. B.C., Book XIV of the *Elements*): theorem that the same circle circumscribes both the pentagon of the dodecahedron and the triangle of the icosahedron inscribed in the same sphere.
