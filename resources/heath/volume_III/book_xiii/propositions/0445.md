The first five propositions are in the nature of lemmas, which are required for later propositions but are not in themselves of much importance.

It will be observed that, while the method of the propositions is that of Book II., being strictly geometrical and not algebraical, none of the results of that Book are made use of (except indeed in the Lemma to XIII. 2, which is probably not genuine). It would therefore appear as though these propositions were taken from an earlier treatise without being revised or rewritten in the light of Book II. It will be remembered that, according to Proclus (p. 67, 6), Eudoxus "greatly added to the number of the theorems which originated with Plato regarding *the section*" (i.e. presumably the "*golden section*"); and it is therefore probable that the five theorems are due to Eudoxus.

That, if <span data-ref="0444.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is divided at <span data-ref="0444.g1:C">$C$</span> in extreme and mean ratio, the rectangle <span data-ref="0444.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0444.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is equal to the square on <span data-ref="0444.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is inferred from VI. 17.

<span data-ref="0444.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> is made equal to half <span data-ref="0444.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, and we have to prove that
<p align="center">(sq. on <span data-ref="0444.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>) = 5 (sq. on <span data-ref="0444.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>).</p>

The figure shows at once that
<p align="center">□ <span data-ref="0444.g1:< C H >">$CH$</span> = □ <span data-ref="0444.g1:< H L >">$HL$</span>,</p>
so that
<p align="center">□ <span data-ref="0444.g1:< C H >">$CH$</span> + □ <span data-ref="0444.g1:< H L >">$HL$</span> = 2 (□ <span data-ref="0444.g1:< C H >">$CH$</span>)<br>
= □ <span data-ref="0444.g1:< A G >">$AG$</span>.</p>

Also
<p align="center">sq. <span data-ref="0444.g1:< H F >">$HF$</span> = (sq. on <span data-ref="0444.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>)<br>
= rect. <span data-ref="0444.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0444.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span><br>
= <span data-ref="0444.g1:< C E >">$CE$</span>.</p>

By addition,
<p align="center">(gnomon <span data-ref="0444.g1:< M N O >">$MNO$</span>) = sq. on <span data-ref="0444.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span><br>
= 4 (sq. on <span data-ref="0444.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>);</p>
whence, adding the sq. on <span data-ref="0444.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> to each, we have
<p align="center">(sq. on <span data-ref="0444.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>) = 5 (sq. on <span data-ref="0444.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>).</p>

The result here, and in the next propositions, is really seen more readily by means of the figure of II. 11.
