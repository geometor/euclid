<p class="sub-clause">But <span data-ref="0444.g1:< A E >">$AE$</span> is equal to the gnomon <span data-ref="0444.g1:< M N O >">$MNO$</span>;</p>

The first five propositions are in the nature of lemmas, which are required<br />
for later propositions but are not in themselves of much importance.

It will be observed that, while the method of the propositions is that of<br />
Book II., being strictly geometrical and not algebraical, none of the results of<br />
that Book are made use of (except indeed in the Lemma to XIII. 2, which is<br />
probably not genuine). It would therefore appear as though these propositions<br />
were taken from an earlier treatise without being revised or rewritten in the<br />
light of Book II. It will be remembered that, according to Proclus (p. 67, 6),<br />
Eudoxus "greatly added to the number of the theorems which originated with<br />
Plato regarding *the section*" (i.e. presumably the "*golden section*"); and it is<br />
therefore probable that the five theorems are due to Eudoxus.

That, if <span data-ref="0444.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is divided at <span data-ref="0444.g1:C">$C$</span> in extreme and mean ratio, the rectangle<br />
<span data-ref="0444.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0444.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is equal to the square on <span data-ref="0444.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is inferred from VI. 17.

<span data-ref="0444.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> is made equal to half <span data-ref="0444.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, and we have to prove that<br />
(sq. on <span data-ref="0444.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>) = 5 (sq. on <span data-ref="0444.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>).

The figure shows at once that<br />
□ <span data-ref="0444.g1:< C H >">$CH$</span> = □ <span data-ref="0444.g1:< H L >">$HL$</span>,<br />
so that □ <span data-ref="0444.g1:< C H >">$CH$</span> + □ <span data-ref="0444.g1:< H L >">$HL$</span> = 2 (□ <span data-ref="0444.g1:< C H >">$CH$</span>)<br />
= □ <span data-ref="0444.g1:< A G >">$AG$</span>.<br />
Also sq. <span data-ref="0444.g1:< H F >">$HF$</span> = (sq. on <span data-ref="0444.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>)<br />
= rect. <span data-ref="0444.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0444.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span><br />
= <span data-ref="0444.g1:< C E >">$CE$</span>.<br />
By addition,<br />
(gnomon <span data-ref="0444.g1:< M N O >">$MNO$</span>) = sq. on <span data-ref="0444.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span><br />
= 4 (sq. on <span data-ref="0444.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>);<br />
whence, adding the sq. on <span data-ref="0444.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> to each, we have<br />
(sq. on <span data-ref="0444.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>) = 5 (sq. on <span data-ref="0444.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>).

The result here, and in the next propositions,<br />
is really seen more readily by means of the figure<br />
of II. 11.
