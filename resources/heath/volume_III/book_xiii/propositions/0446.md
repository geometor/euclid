The MSS. contain a curious addition to XIII. 1—5 in the shape of analyses<br />
and syntheses for each proposition prefaced by the heading:<br />
"**What is analysis and what is synthesis.**<br />
"Analysis is the assumption of that which is sought as if it were admitted<br />
&lt;and the arrival&gt; by means of its consequences at something admitted to<br />
be true.<br />
"Synthesis is an assumption of that which is admitted &lt;and the arrival&gt;<br />
by means of its consequences at something admitted to be true."<br />
There must apparently be some corruption in the text; it does not, in the<br />
case of synthesis, give what is wanted. B and V have, instead of "something<br />
admitted to be true," the words "the end or attainment of what is sought."

The whole of this addition is evidently interpolated. To begin with, the<br />
analyses and syntheses of the five propositions are placed all together in four<br />
MSS.; in P, q they come after an alternative proof of XIII. 5 (which alternative<br />
proof P gives after XIII. 6, while q gives it instead of XIII. 6), in B (which has<br />
not the alternative proof of XIII. 5) after XIII. 6, and in b (in which XIII. 6 is<br />
wanting, and the alternative proof of XIII. 5 is in the margin, in the first hand)<br />
after XIII. 5, while V has the analyses of 1—3 in the text after XIII. 6 and<br />
those of 4—5 in the same place in the margin, by the second hand. Further,<br />
the addition is altogether alien from the plan and manner of the *Elements*.<br />
The interpolation took place before Theon’s time, and the probability is that<br />
it was originally in the margin, whence it crept into the text of P after XIII. 5.<br />
Heiberg (after Bretschneider) suggested in his edition (Vol. v. p. lxxxiv.) that<br />
it might be a relic of analytical investigations by Theaetetus or Eudoxus, and<br />
he cited the remark of Pappus (v. p. 410) at the beginning of his<br />
"comparisons of the five [regular solid] figures which have an equal surface,"<br />
to the effect that he will not use "the so-called analytical investigation by<br />
means of which some of the ancients effected their demonstrations." More<br />
recently (*Paralipomena zu Euklid* in *Hermes* XXXVIII., 1903) Heiberg con-<br />
jectures that the author is Heron, on the ground that the sort of analysis and<br />
synthesis recalls Heron’s remarks on analysis and synthesis in his commentary<br />
on the beginning of Book II. (quoted by an-Nairīzī, ed. Curtze, p. 89) and his<br />
quasi-algebraical alternative proofs of propositions in that Book.

To show the character of the interpolated matter I need only give the<br />
analysis and synthesis of one proposition. In the case of XIII. 1 it is in<br />
substance as follows. The figure is a mere<br />
straight line.

I say that (sq. on <span data-ref="0446.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>) = 5 (sq. on <span data-ref="0446.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>).<br />
(Analysis.)<br />
