For let any straight line <span data-ref="0450.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> be cut in extreme and mean ratio at the point <span data-ref="0450.g1:C">$C$</span>, let <span data-ref="0450.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> be the greater segment, and let <span data-ref="0450.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> be bisected at <span data-ref="0450.g1:D">$D$</span>;
I say that the square on <span data-ref="0450.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> is five times the square on <span data-ref="0450.g1:/ D C /">$DC$</span>.

For let the square <span data-ref="0450.g1:< A E >">$AE$</span> be described on <span data-ref="0450.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, and let the figure be drawn double.

![Book XIII, Proposition 3](0450.g1.svg)

Since <span data-ref="0450.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is double of <span data-ref="0450.g1:/ D C /">$DC$</span>, therefore the square on <span data-ref="0450.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is quadruple of the square on <span data-ref="0450.g1:/ D C /">$DC$</span>, that is, <span data-ref="0450.g1:< R S >">$RS$</span> is quadruple of <span data-ref="0450.g1:< F G >">$FG$</span>.

And, since the rectangle <span data-ref="0450.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0450.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is equal to the square on <span data-ref="0450.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, and <span data-ref="0450.g1:< C E >">$CE$</span> is the rectangle <span data-ref="0450.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0450.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, therefore <span data-ref="0450.g1:< C E >">$CE$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0450.g1:< R S >">$RS$</span>.

But <span data-ref="0450.g1:< R S >">$RS$</span> is quadruple of <span data-ref="0450.g1:< F G >">$FG$</span>; therefore <span data-ref="0450.g1:< C E >">$CE$</span> is also quadruple of <span data-ref="0450.g1:< F G >">$FG$</span>.

Again, since <span data-ref="0450.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0450.g1:/ D C /">$DC$</span>, <span data-ref="0450.g1:/ H K /">$HK$</span> is also equal to <span data-ref="0450.g1:/ K F /">$KF$</span>.

Hence the square <span data-ref="0450.g1:< G F >">$GF$</span> is also equal to the square <span data-ref="0450.g1:< H L >">$HL$</span>.

Therefore <span data-ref="0450.g1:/ G K /">$GK$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0450.g1:/ K L /">$KL$</span>, that is, <span data-ref="0450.g1:/ M N /">$MN$</span> to <span data-ref="0450.g1:/ N E /">$NE$</span>; hence <span data-ref="0450.g1:< M F >">$MF$</span> is also equal to <span data-ref="0450.g1:< F E >">$FE$</span>.

But <span data-ref="0450.g1:< M F >">$MF$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0450.g1:< C G >">$CG$</span>; therefore <span data-ref="0450.g1:< C G >">$CG$</span> is also equal to <span data-ref="0450.g1:< F E >">$FE$</span>.

Let <span data-ref="0450.g1:< C N >">$CN$</span> be added to each; therefore the gnomon <span data-ref="0450.g1:< O P Q >">$OPQ$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0450.g1:< C E >">$CE$</span>.

But <span data-ref="0450.g1:< C E >">$CE$</span> was proved quadruple of <span data-ref="0450.g1:< G F >">$GF$</span>; therefore the gnomon <span data-ref="0450.g1:< O P Q >">$OPQ$</span> is also quadruple of the square <span data-ref="0450.g1:< F G >">$FG$</span>.

Therefore the gnomon <span data-ref="0450.g1:< O P Q >">$OPQ$</span> and the square <span data-ref="0450.g1:< F G >">$FG$</span> are five times <span data-ref="0450.g1:< F G >">$FG$</span>.

But the gnomon <span data-ref="0450.g1:< O P Q >">$OPQ$</span> and the square <span data-ref="0450.g1:< F G >">$FG$</span> are the square <span data-ref="0450.g1:< D N >">$DN$</span>.

And <span data-ref="0450.g1:< D N >">$DN$</span> is the square on <span data-ref="0450.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span>, and <span data-ref="0450.g1:< G F >">$GF$</span> the square on <span data-ref="0450.g1:/ D C /">$DC$</span>.

Therefore the square on <span data-ref="0450.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> is five times the square on <span data-ref="0450.g1:/ D C /">$DC$</span>.

**Q. E. D.**
