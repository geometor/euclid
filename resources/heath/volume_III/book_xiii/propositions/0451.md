### PROPOSITION 4.

*If a straight line be cut in extreme and mean ratio, the square on the whole and the square on the lesser segment together are triple of the square on the greater segment.*

Let <span data-ref="0451.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span> be a straight line, let it be cut in extreme and mean ratio at <span data-ref="0451.g2:C">$C$</span>, and let <span data-ref="0451.g2:/ A C /">$AC$</span> be the greater segment;
I say that the squares on <span data-ref="0451.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0451.g2:/ B C /">$BC$</span> are triple of the square on <span data-ref="0451.g2:/ C A /">$CA$</span>.

![Book XIII, Proposition 4](0451.g2.svg)

For let the square <span data-ref="0451.g2:< A D E B >">$ADEB$</span> be described on <span data-ref="0451.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span>,
and let the figure be drawn.

Since then <span data-ref="0451.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span> has been cut in extreme and mean ratio at <span data-ref="0451.g2:C">$C$</span>,
and <span data-ref="0451.g2:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is the greater segment,
therefore the rectangle <span data-ref="0451.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0451.g2:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is equal to the square on <span data-ref="0451.g2:/ A C /">$AC$</span>. [VI. Def. 3, VI. 17]

And <span data-ref="0451.g2:< A K >">$AK$</span> is the rectangle <span data-ref="0451.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0451.g2:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, and <span data-ref="0451.g2:< H G >">$HG$</span> the square on <span data-ref="0451.g2:/ A C /">$AC$</span>;
therefore <span data-ref="0451.g2:< A K >">$AK$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0451.g2:< H G >">$HG$</span>.
