### PROPOSITION 4.

<p class="enunciation">
If a straight line be cut in extreme and mean ratio, the<br />
square on the whole and the square on the lesser segment together<br />
are triple of the square on the greater segment.
</p>

Let <span data-ref="0451.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span> be a straight line,<br />
let it be cut in extreme and mean ratio at <span data-ref="0451.g2:C">$C$</span>,<br />
and let <span data-ref="0451.g2:/ A C /">$AC$</span> be the greater segment;<br />
I say that the squares on <span data-ref="0451.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0451.g2:/ B C /">$BC$</span> are<br />
triple of the square on <span data-ref="0451.g2:/ C A /">$CA$</span>.

![Book XIII, Proposition 4](0451.g2.svg)

For let the square <span data-ref="0451.g2:< A D E B >">$ADEB$</span> be described<br />
on <span data-ref="0451.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span>,<br />
and let the figure be drawn.

<p class="sub-clause">
Since then <span data-ref="0451.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span> has been cut in extreme<br />
and mean ratio at <span data-ref="0451.g2:C">$C$</span>,<br />
and <span data-ref="0451.g2:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is the greater segment,
</p>
<p class="assertion">
therefore the rectangle <span data-ref="0451.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0451.g2:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is equal to the square on <span data-ref="0451.g2:/ A C /">$AC$</span>.<br />
<span class="justification">[VI. Def. 3, VI. 17]</span>
</p>

<p class="assertion">
And <span data-ref="0451.g2:< A K >">$AK$</span> is the rectangle <span data-ref="0451.g2:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0451.g2:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, and <span data-ref="0451.g2:< H G >">$HG$</span> the square<br />
on <span data-ref="0451.g2:/ A C /">$AC$</span>;<br />
therefore <span data-ref="0451.g2:< A K >">$AK$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0451.g2:< H G >">$HG$</span>.
</p>
