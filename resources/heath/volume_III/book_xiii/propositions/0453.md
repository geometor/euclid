Since $AB$ has been cut in extreme and mean ratio at $C$,
therefore the rectangle $AB$, $BC$ is equal to the square on $AC$. [VI. Def. 3, VI. 17]

And $CE$ is the rectangle $AB$, $BC$, and $CH$ the square on $AC$;
therefore $CE$ is equal to $HC$.

But $HE$ is equal to $CE$,
and $DH$ is equal to $HC$;
therefore $DH$ is also equal to $HE$.

Therefore the whole $DK$ is equal to the whole $AE$.

And $DK$ is the rectangle $BD$, $DA$,
for $AD$ is equal to $DL$;
and $AE$ is the square on $AB$;
therefore the rectangle $BD$, $DA$ is equal to the square on $AB$.

Therefore, as $DB$ is to $BA$, so is $BA$ to $AD$. [VI. 17]
And $DB$ is greater than $BA$;
therefore $BA$ is also greater than $AD$. [V. 14]

Therefore $DB$ has been cut in extreme and mean ratio at $A$, and $AB$ is the greater segment.

Q. E. D.

---

We have
$$\begin{align*}
(\text{sq. } DH) &= (\text{sq. } HC) \\
&= (\text{rect. } CE)\text{, by hypothesis,} \\
&= (\text{rect. } HE)\text{.}
\end{align*}$$
Add to each side the rectangle $AK$, and
$$\begin{align*}
(\text{rect. } DK) &= (\text{sq. } AE)\text{,} \\
\text{or }\quad (\text{rect. } BD\text{, } DA) &= (\text{sq. on } AB)\text{.}
\end{align*}$$
The result is of course obvious from II. 11.

There is an alternative proof given in P after XIII. 6, which depends on Book V.

By hypothesis,
$$BA : AC = AC : CB\text{,}$$
or, inversely,
$$AC : AB = CB : AC\text{.}$$
*Componendo*,
$$(AB + AC) : AB = AB : AC\text{,}$$
or
$$DB : BA = BA : AD\text{.}$$

### PROPOSITION 6.

*If a rational straight line be cut in extreme and mean ratio, each of the segments is the irrational straight line called apotome.*
