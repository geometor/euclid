.. assertion::

   And :g:`$CA$ <0454.g1:/ C A />` was also proved to be an apotome.

.. qed::

   Therefore etc. :just:`Q. E. D.`

----

| It seems certain that this proposition is an interpolation. P has it, but the
| copyist (or rather the copyist of its archetype) says that "this theorem is not
| found in most copies of the new recension, but is found in those of the old."
| In the first place, there is a scholium to XIII. 17 in P itself which proves the
| same thing as XIII. 6, and which would therefore have been useless if XIII. 6
| had preceded. Hence, when the scholium was written, this proposition had
| not yet been interpolated. Secondly, P has it before the alternative proof of
| XIII. 5; this proof is considered, on general grounds, to be interpolated, and
| it would appear that it must have been a *later* interpolation (XIII. 6) which
| divorced it from the proposition to which it belonged. Thirdly, there is cause
| for suspicion in the proposition itself, for, while the enunciation states that
| each segment of the straight line is an *apotome*, the proposition adds that the
| lesser segment is a :term:`*first apotome* <first-apotome>`. The scholium in P referred to has not this
| blot. What is actually wanted in XIII. 17 is the fact that the *greater* segment
| is an apotome. It is probable that Euclid assumed this fact as evident enough
| from XIII. 1 without further proof, and that he neither wrote XIII. 6 nor the
| quotation of its enunciation in XIII. 17.

----

PROPOSITION 7.
--------------

.. enunciation::

   If three angles of an equilateral pentagon, taken either in order or not in order, be equal, the pentagon will be equiangular.

| For in the equilateral pentagon :g:`$ABCDE$ <0455.g1:< A B C D E >>` let, first, three
| angles taken in order, those at :g:`$A$ <0455.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0455.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0455.g1:C>`,
| be equal to one another;
| I say that the pentagon :g:`$ABCDE$ <0455.g1:< A B C D E >>` is
| equiangular.

.. picture:: 0455.g1.svg
   :alt: Book XIII, Proposition 7

For let :g:`$AC$ <0455.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`$BE$ <0455.g1:/ B E />`, :g:`$FD$ <0455.g1:/ F D />` be joined.

.. subclause::

   | Now, since the two sides :g:`$CB$ <0455.g1:/ C B />`, :g:`$BA$ <0455.g1:/ B A />`
   | are equal to the two sides :g:`$BA$ <0455.g1:/ B A />`, :g:`$AE$ <0455.g1:/ A E />`
   | respectively,
   | and the angle :g:`$CBA$ <0455.g1:< C B A )>` is equal to the
   | angle :g:`$BAE$ <0455.g1:< B A E )>`,

.. assertion::

   | therefore the base :g:`$AC$ <0455.g1:/ A C />` is equal to the base :g:`$BE$ <0455.g1:/ B E />`,
   | the triangle :g:`$ABC$ <0455.g1:< A B C >>` is equal to the triangle :g:`$ABE$ <0455.g1:< A B E >>`,
   | and the remaining angles will be equal to the remaining angles,
   | namely those which the equal sides subtend, :just:`[I. 4] <I.4>`
   | that is, the angle :g:`$BCA$ <0455.g1:< B C A )>` to the angle :g:`$BEA$ <0455.g1:< B E A )>`, and the angle
   | :g:`$ABE$ <0455.g1:< A B E )>` to the angle :g:`$CAB$ <0455.g1:< C A B )>`;

.. assertion:: [I. 6] <I.6>

   hence the side :g:`$AF$ <0455.g1:/ A F />` is also equal to the side :g:`$BF$ <0455.g1:/ B F />`.
