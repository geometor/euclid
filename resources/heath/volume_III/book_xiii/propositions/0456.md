But the whole <span data-ref="0455.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> was also proved equal to the whole <span data-ref="0455.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span>;
therefore the remainder <span data-ref="0455.g1:/ F C /">$FC$</span> is also equal to the remainder <span data-ref="0455.g1:/ F E /">$FE$</span>.

But <span data-ref="0455.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> is also equal to <span data-ref="0455.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>.
Therefore the two sides <span data-ref="0455.g1:/ F C /">$FC$</span>, <span data-ref="0455.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> are equal to the two sides <span data-ref="0455.g1:/ F E /">$FE$</span>, <span data-ref="0455.g1:/ E D /">$ED$</span>;
and the base <span data-ref="0455.g1:/ F D /">$FD$</span> is common to them;
therefore the angle <span data-ref="0455.g1:< F C D )">$FCD$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0455.g1:< F E D )">$FED$</span>. [I. 8]

But the angle <span data-ref="0455.g1:< B C A )">$BCA$</span> was also proved equal to the angle <span data-ref="0455.g1:< A E B )">$AEB$</span>;
therefore the whole angle <span data-ref="0455.g1:< B C D )">$BCD$</span> is also equal to the whole angle <span data-ref="0455.g1:< A E D )">$AED$</span>.

But, by hypothesis, the angle <span data-ref="0455.g1:< B C D )">$BCD$</span> is equal to the angles at <span data-ref="0455.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0455.g1:B">$B$</span>;
therefore the angle <span data-ref="0455.g1:< A E D )">$AED$</span> is also equal to the angles at <span data-ref="0455.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0455.g1:B">$B$</span>.

Similarly we can prove that the angle <span data-ref="0455.g1:< C D E )">$CDE$</span> is also equal to the angles at <span data-ref="0455.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0455.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0455.g1:C">$C$</span>;
therefore the pentagon <span data-ref="0455.g1:< A B C D E >">$ABCDE$</span> is equiangular.

Next, let the given equal angles not be angles taken in order, but let the angles at the points <span data-ref="0455.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0455.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0455.g1:D">$D$</span> be equal;
I say that in this case too the pentagon <span data-ref="0455.g1:< A B C D E >">$ABCDE$</span> is equiangular.

For let <span data-ref="0455.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> be joined.

Then, since the two sides <span data-ref="0455.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span>, <span data-ref="0455.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> are equal to the two sides <span data-ref="0455.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, <span data-ref="0455.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>,
and they contain equal angles,
therefore the base <span data-ref="0455.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span> is equal to the base <span data-ref="0455.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>,
the triangle <span data-ref="0455.g1:< A B E >">$ABE$</span> is equal to the triangle <span data-ref="0455.g1:< B C D >">$BCD$</span>,
and the remaining angles will be equal to the remaining angles, namely those which the equal sides subtend; [I. 4]
therefore the angle <span data-ref="0455.g1:< A E B )">$AEB$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0455.g1:< C D B )">$CDB$</span>.

But the angle <span data-ref="0455.g1:< B E D )">$BED$</span> is also equal to the angle <span data-ref="0455.g1:< B D E )">$BDE$</span>,
since the side <span data-ref="0455.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span> is also equal to the side <span data-ref="0455.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>. [I. 5]

Therefore the whole angle <span data-ref="0455.g1:< A E D )">$AED$</span> is equal to the whole angle <span data-ref="0455.g1:< C D E )">$CDE$</span>.

But the angle <span data-ref="0455.g1:< C D E )">$CDE$</span> is, by hypothesis, equal to the angles at <span data-ref="0455.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0455.g1:C">$C$</span>;
therefore the angle <span data-ref="0455.g1:< A E D )">$AED$</span> is also equal to the angles at <span data-ref="0455.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0455.g1:C">$C$</span>.
