:title: Proposition 7

.. subclause::

   But the whole :g:`$AC$ <0455.g1:/ A C />` was also proved equal to the whole :g:`$BE$ <0455.g1:/ B E />`;

.. assertion::

   therefore the remainder :g:`$FC$ <0455.g1:/ F C />` is also equal to the remainder :g:`$FE$ <0455.g1:/ F E />`.

.. subclause::

   | But :g:`$CD$ <0455.g1:/ C D />` is also equal to :g:`$DE$ <0455.g1:/ D E />`.
   | Therefore the two sides :g:`$FC$ <0455.g1:/ F C />`, :g:`$CD$ <0455.g1:/ C D />` are equal to the two
   | sides :g:`$FE$ <0455.g1:/ F E />`, :g:`$ED$ <0455.g1:/ E D />`;
   | and the base :g:`$FD$ <0455.g1:/ F D />` is common to them;

.. assertion:: [I. 8] <I.8>

   therefore the angle :g:`$FCD$ <0455.g1:< F C D )>` is equal to the angle :g:`$FED$ <0455.g1:< F E D )>`.

.. subclause::

   | But the angle :g:`$BCA$ <0455.g1:< B C A )>` was also proved equal to the angle
   | :g:`$AEB$ <0455.g1:< A E B )>`;

.. assertion::

   | therefore the whole angle :g:`$BCD$ <0455.g1:< B C D )>` is also equal to the whole
   | angle :g:`$AED$ <0455.g1:< A E D )>`.

.. subclause::

   | But, by hypothesis, the angle :g:`$BCD$ <0455.g1:< B C D )>` is equal to the angles
   | at :g:`$A$ <0455.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0455.g1:B>`;

.. assertion::

   therefore the angle :g:`$AED$ <0455.g1:< A E D )>` is also equal to the angles at :g:`$A$ <0455.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0455.g1:B>`.

.. assertion::

   | Similarly we can prove that the angle :g:`$CDE$ <0455.g1:< C D E )>` is also equal
   | to the angles at :g:`$A$ <0455.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0455.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0455.g1:C>`;
   | therefore the pentagon :g:`$ABCDE$ <0455.g1:< A B C D E >>` is equiangular.

| Next, let the given equal angles not be angles taken in
| order, but let the angles at the points :g:`$A$ <0455.g1:A>`, :g:`$C$ <0455.g1:C>`, :g:`$D$ <0455.g1:D>` be equal;
| I say that in this case too the pentagon :g:`$ABCDE$ <0455.g1:< A B C D E >>` is equiangular.

For let :g:`$BD$ <0455.g1:/ B D />` be joined.

.. subclause::

   | Then, since the two sides :g:`$BA$ <0455.g1:/ B A />`, :g:`$AE$ <0455.g1:/ A E />` are equal to the two
   | sides :g:`$BC$ <0455.g1:/ B C />`, :g:`$CD$ <0455.g1:/ C D />`,
   | and they contain equal angles,

.. assertion::

   | therefore the base :g:`$BE$ <0455.g1:/ B E />` is equal to the base :g:`$BD$ <0455.g1:/ B D />`,
   | the triangle :g:`$ABE$ <0455.g1:< A B E >>` is equal to the triangle :g:`$BCD$ <0455.g1:< B C D >>`,
   | and the remaining angles will be equal to the remaining angles,
   | namely those which the equal sides subtend; :just:`[I. 4] <I.4>`
   | therefore the angle :g:`$AEB$ <0455.g1:< A E B )>` is equal to the angle :g:`$CDB$ <0455.g1:< C D B )>`.

.. subclause::

   | But the angle :g:`$BED$ <0455.g1:< B E D )>` is also equal to the angle :g:`$BDE$ <0455.g1:< B D E )>`,
   | since the side :g:`$BE$ <0455.g1:/ B E />` is also equal to the side :g:`$BD$ <0455.g1:/ B D />`. :just:`[I. 5] <I.5>`

.. assertion::

   | Therefore the whole angle :g:`$AED$ <0455.g1:< A E D )>` is equal to the whole
   | angle :g:`$CDE$ <0455.g1:< C D E )>`.

.. subclause::

   | But the angle :g:`$CDE$ <0455.g1:< C D E )>` is, by hypothesis, equal to the angles
   | at :g:`$A$ <0455.g1:A>`, :g:`$C$ <0455.g1:C>`;

.. assertion::

   therefore the angle :g:`$AED$ <0455.g1:< A E D )>` is also equal to the angles at :g:`$A$ <0455.g1:A>`, :g:`$C$ <0455.g1:C>`.
