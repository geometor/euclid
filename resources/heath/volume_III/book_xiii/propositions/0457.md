For the same reason
the angle <span data-ref="0455.g1:< A B C )">$ABC$</span> is also equal to the angles at <span data-ref="0455.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0455.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0455.g1:D">$D$</span>.

---

This proposition is required in XIII. 17.
The steps of the proof may be shown thus.

I. Suppose that the angles at <span data-ref="0455.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0455.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0455.g1:C">$C$</span> are all equal.
Then the isosceles triangles <span data-ref="0455.g1:< B A E >">$BAE$</span>, <span data-ref="0455.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> are equal in all respects;
thus
<span data-ref="0455.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span> $= $ <span data-ref="0455.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, $\angle$<span data-ref="0455.g1:< B C A )">$BCA$</span> $= \angle$<span data-ref="0455.g1:< B E A )">$BEA$</span>, $\angle$<span data-ref="0455.g1:< C A B )">$CAB$</span> $= \angle$<span data-ref="0455.g1:< E B A )">$EBA$</span>.
By the last equality,
<span data-ref="0455.g1:/ F A /">$FA$</span> $= $ <span data-ref="0455.g1:/ F B /">$FB$</span>,
so that, since <span data-ref="0455.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span> $= $ <span data-ref="0455.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>,
<span data-ref="0455.g1:/ F C /">$FC$</span> $= $ <span data-ref="0455.g1:/ F E /">$FE$</span>.
The $\Delta\text{s}$ <span data-ref="0455.g1:< F E D >">$FED$</span>, <span data-ref="0455.g1:< F C D >">$FCD$</span> are now equal in all respects, [I. 8, 4]
and
$\angle$<span data-ref="0455.g1:< F C D )">$FCD$</span> $= \angle$<span data-ref="0455.g1:< F E D )">$FED$</span>.
But
$\angle$<span data-ref="0455.g1:< A C B )">$ACB$</span> $= \angle$<span data-ref="0455.g1:< A E B )">$AEB$</span>, from above,
whence, by addition,
$\angle$<span data-ref="0455.g1:< B C D )">$BCD$</span> $= \angle$<span data-ref="0455.g1:< A E D )">$AED$</span>.
Similarly it may be proved that $\angle$<span data-ref="0455.g1:< C D E )">$CDE$</span> is also equal to any one of the angles at <span data-ref="0455.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0455.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0455.g1:C">$C$</span>.

II. Suppose the angles at <span data-ref="0455.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0455.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0455.g1:D">$D$</span> to be equal.
Then the isosceles triangles <span data-ref="0455.g1:< A B E >">$ABE$</span>, <span data-ref="0455.g1:< C B D >">$CBD$</span> are equal in all respects, and hence <span data-ref="0455.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span> $= $ <span data-ref="0455.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> (so that $\angle$<span data-ref="0455.g1:< B D E )">$BDE$</span> $= \angle$<span data-ref="0455.g1:< B E D )">$BED$</span>),
and
$\angle$<span data-ref="0455.g1:< C D B )">$CDB$</span> $= \angle$<span data-ref="0455.g1:< A E B )">$AEB$</span>.
By addition of the equal angles,
$\angle$<span data-ref="0455.g1:< C D E )">$CDE$</span> $= \angle$<span data-ref="0455.g1:< D E A )">$DEA$</span>.
Similarly it may be proved that $\angle$<span data-ref="0455.g1:< A B C )">$ABC$</span> is also equal to each of the angles at <span data-ref="0455.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0455.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0455.g1:D">$D$</span>.

### PROPOSITION 8.

*If in an equilateral and equiangular pentagon straight lines subtend two angles taken in order, they cut one another in extreme and mean ratio, and their greater segments are equal to the side of the pentagon.*

For in the equilateral and equiangular pentagon <span data-ref="0457.g1:< A B C D E >">$ABCDE$</span> let the straight lines <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span>, cutting one another at the point <span data-ref="0457.g1:H">$H$</span>, subtend two angles taken in order, the angles at <span data-ref="0457.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0457.g1:B">$B$</span>;

![Book XIII, Proposition 8](0457.g1.svg)

I say that each of them has been cut in extreme and mean ratio at the point <span data-ref="0457.g1:H">$H$</span>, and their greater segments are equal to the side of the pentagon.

For let the circle <span data-ref="0457.g1:( A B C D E )">$ABCDE$</span> be circumscribed about the pentagon <span data-ref="0457.g1:< A B C D E >">$ABCDE$</span>. [IV. 14]
