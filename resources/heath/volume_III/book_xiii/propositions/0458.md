Then, since the two straight lines <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ E A /">$EA$</span>, <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> are equal to the two <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>,
and they contain equal angles,
therefore the base <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span> is equal to the base <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, [I. 4]
the triangle <span data-ref="0457.g1:< A B E >">$ABE$</span> is equal to the triangle <span data-ref="0457.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span>,
and the remaining angles will be equal to the remaining angles respectively, namely those which the equal sides subtend. [I. 4]

Therefore the angle <span data-ref="0457.g1:< B A C )">$BAC$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0457.g1:< A B E )">$ABE$</span>;
therefore the angle <span data-ref="0457.g1:< A H E )">$AHE$</span> is double of the angle <span data-ref="0457.g1:< B A H )">$BAH$</span>. [I. 32]

But the angle <span data-ref="0457.g1:< E A C )">$EAC$</span> is also double of the angle <span data-ref="0457.g1:< B A C )">$BAC$</span>,
inasmuch as the circumference <span data-ref="0457.g1:( E D C )">$EDC$</span> is also double of the circumference <span data-ref="0457.g1:( C B )">$CB$</span>; [III. 28, VI. 33]
therefore the angle <span data-ref="0457.g1:< H A E )">$HAE$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0457.g1:< A H E )">$AHE$</span>;
hence the straight line <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ H E /">$HE$</span> is also equal to <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ E A /">$EA$</span>, that is, to <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>. [I. 6]

And, since the straight line <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span>,
the angle <span data-ref="0457.g1:< A B E )">$ABE$</span> is also equal to the angle <span data-ref="0457.g1:< A E B )">$AEB$</span>. [I. 5]

But the angle <span data-ref="0457.g1:< A B E )">$ABE$</span> was proved equal to the angle <span data-ref="0457.g1:< B A H )">$BAH$</span>;
therefore the angle <span data-ref="0457.g1:< B E A )">$BEA$</span> is also equal to the angle <span data-ref="0457.g1:< B A H )">$BAH$</span>.

And the angle <span data-ref="0457.g1:< A B E )">$ABE$</span> is common to the two triangles <span data-ref="0457.g1:< A B E >">$ABE$</span> and <span data-ref="0457.g1:< A B H >">$ABH$</span>;
therefore the remaining angle <span data-ref="0457.g1:< B A E )">$BAE$</span> is equal to the remaining angle <span data-ref="0457.g1:< A H B )">$AHB$</span>; [I. 32]
therefore the triangle <span data-ref="0457.g1:< A B E >">$ABE$</span> is equiangular with the triangle <span data-ref="0457.g1:< A B H >">$ABH$</span>;
therefore, proportionally, as <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> to <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ B H /">$BH$</span>. [VI. 4]

But <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span>;
therefore, as <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span> is to <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span> to <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ H B /">$HB$</span>.

And <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span> is greater than <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span>;
therefore <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span> is also greater than <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ H B /">$HB$</span>. [V. 14]

Therefore <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span> has been cut in extreme and mean ratio at <span data-ref="0457.g1:H">$H$</span>, and the greater segment <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ H E /">$HE$</span> is equal to the side of the pentagon.

Similarly we can prove that <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> has also been cut in extreme and mean ratio at <span data-ref="0457.g1:H">$H$</span>, and its greater segment <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ C H /">$CH$</span> is equal to the side of the pentagon.

Q. E. D.
