<p class="sub-clause">Then, since the two straight lines <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ E A /">$EA$</span>, <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> are equal to<br />
the two <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>,<br />
and they contain equal angles,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the base <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span> is equal to the base <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>,<br />
the triangle <span data-ref="0457.g1:< A B E >">$ABE$</span> is equal to the triangle <span data-ref="0457.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span>,<br />
and the remaining angles will be equal to the remaining angles<br />
respectively, namely those which the equal sides subtend. <span class="justification">[I. 4]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore the angle <span data-ref="0457.g1:< B A C )">$BAC$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0457.g1:< A B E )">$ABE$</span>;<br />
therefore the angle <span data-ref="0457.g1:< A H E )">$AHE$</span> is double of the angle <span data-ref="0457.g1:< B A H )">$BAH$</span>. <span class="justification">[I. 32]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But the angle <span data-ref="0457.g1:< E A C )">$EAC$</span> is also double of the angle <span data-ref="0457.g1:< B A C )">$BAC$</span>,<br />
inasmuch as the circumference <span data-ref="0457.g1:( E D C )">$EDC$</span> is also double of the<br />
circumference <span data-ref="0457.g1:( C B )">$CB$</span>; <span class="justification">[III. 28, VI. 33]</span></p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the angle <span data-ref="0457.g1:< H A E )">$HAE$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0457.g1:< A H E )">$AHE$</span>;<br />
hence the straight line <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ H E /">$HE$</span> is also equal to <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ E A /">$EA$</span>, that is, to <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>.<br />
<span class="justification">[I. 6]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">And, since the straight line <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span>,<br />
the angle <span data-ref="0457.g1:< A B E )">$ABE$</span> is also equal to the angle <span data-ref="0457.g1:< A E B )">$AEB$</span>. <span class="justification">[I. 5]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But the angle <span data-ref="0457.g1:< A B E )">$ABE$</span> was proved equal to the angle <span data-ref="0457.g1:< B A H )">$BAH$</span>;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the angle <span data-ref="0457.g1:< B E A )">$BEA$</span> is also equal to the angle <span data-ref="0457.g1:< B A H )">$BAH$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">And the angle <span data-ref="0457.g1:< A B E )">$ABE$</span> is common to the two triangles <span data-ref="0457.g1:< A B E >">$ABE$</span><br />
and <span data-ref="0457.g1:< A B H >">$ABH$</span>;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the remaining angle <span data-ref="0457.g1:< B A E )">$BAE$</span> is equal to the remaining<br />
angle <span data-ref="0457.g1:< A H B )">$AHB$</span>; <span class="justification">[I. 32]</span><br />
therefore the triangle <span data-ref="0457.g1:< A B E >">$ABE$</span> is equiangular with the triangle<br />
<span data-ref="0457.g1:< A B H >">$ABH$</span>;<br />
therefore, proportionally, as <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> to <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ B H /">$BH$</span>.<br />
<span class="justification">[VI. 4]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span>;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore, as <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span> is to <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span> to <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ H B /">$HB$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">And <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span> is greater than <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span>;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span> is also greater than <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ H B /">$HB$</span>. <span class="justification">[V. 14]</span></p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span> has been cut in extreme and mean ratio at<br />
<span data-ref="0457.g1:H">$H$</span>, and the greater segment <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ H E /">$HE$</span> is equal to the side of the<br />
pentagon.</p>

<p class="assertion">Similarly we can prove that <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> has also been cut in<br />
extreme and mean ratio at <span data-ref="0457.g1:H">$H$</span>, and its greater segment <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ C H /">$CH$</span><br />
is equal to the side of the pentagon.</p>

<p class="qed"><span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>
