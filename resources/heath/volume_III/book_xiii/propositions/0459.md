In order to prove this theorem we have to show (1) that the $\Delta\text{s}$ <span data-ref="0457.g1:< A E B >">$AEB$</span>, <span data-ref="0457.g1:< H A B >">$HAB$</span> are similar, and (2) that <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span> $= $ <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ E A /">$EA$</span> ($= $ <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>).

To prove (2) we have
$\Delta\text{s}$ <span data-ref="0457.g1:< A E B >">$AEB$</span>, <span data-ref="0457.g1:< B A C >">$BAC$</span> equal in all respects,
whence
<span data-ref="0457.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span> $= $ <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>,
and
$\angle$<span data-ref="0457.g1:< B A C )">$BAC$</span> $= \angle$<span data-ref="0457.g1:< A B E )">$ABE$</span>.

Therefore
$\angle$<span data-ref="0457.g1:< A H E )">$AHE$</span> $= 2\angle$<span data-ref="0457.g1:< B A C )">$BAC$</span> $= \angle$<span data-ref="0457.g1:< E A C )">$EAC$</span>,
so that
<span data-ref="0457.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span> $= $ <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ E A /">$EA$</span> $= $ <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>.

To prove (1) we have, in the $\Delta\text{s}$ <span data-ref="0457.g1:< A E B >">$AEB$</span>, <span data-ref="0457.g1:< H A B >">$HAB$</span>,
$\angle$<span data-ref="0457.g1:< B A H )">$BAH$</span> $= \angle$<span data-ref="0457.g1:< E B A )">$EBA$</span> $= \angle$<span data-ref="0457.g1:< A E B )">$AEB$</span>,
and
$\angle$<span data-ref="0457.g1:< A B E )">$ABE$</span> is common,
therefore the third $\angle\text{s}$ <span data-ref="0457.g1:< A H B )">$AHB$</span>, <span data-ref="0457.g1:< E A B )">$EAB$</span> are equal,
and
$\Delta\text{s}$ <span data-ref="0457.g1:< A E B >">$AEB$</span>, <span data-ref="0457.g1:< H A B >">$HAB$</span> are similar.

Now, since these triangles are similar,
<span data-ref="0457.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span> $:$ <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span> $= $ <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span> $:$ <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ B H /">$BH$</span>,
or
$(\text{rect. }$ <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span>, <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ B H /">$BH$</span>$) = (\text{sq. on }$ <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span>$) = (\text{sq. on }$ <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span>$)$,
so that <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span> is divided in extreme and mean ratio at <span data-ref="0457.g1:H">$H$</span>.

Similarly its equal, <span data-ref="0457.g1:/ C A /">$CA$</span>, is divided in extreme and mean ratio at <span data-ref="0457.g1:H">$H$</span>.

### PROPOSITION 9.

*If the side of the hexagon and that of the decagon inscribed in the same circle be added together, the whole straight line has been cut in extreme and mean ratio, and its greater segment is the side of the hexagon.*

Let <span data-ref="0459.g1:( A B C )">$ABC$</span> be a circle;
of the figures inscribed in the circle <span data-ref="0459.g1:( A B C )">$ABC$</span> let <span data-ref="0459.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> be the side of a decagon, <span data-ref="0459.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> that of a hexagon,
and let them be in a straight line;
I say that the whole straight line <span data-ref="0459.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span> has been cut in extreme and mean ratio, and <span data-ref="0459.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> is its greater segment.

and let <span data-ref="0459.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span> be carried through to <span data-ref="0459.g1:A">$A$</span>.

Since <span data-ref="0459.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is the side of an equilateral decagon,
