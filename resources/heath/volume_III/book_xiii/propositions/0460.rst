:title: Proposition 9 (cont.)

.. assertion::

   | therefore the circumference :g:`$ACB$ <0459.g1:( A C B )>` is five times the circum-
   | ference :g:`$BC$ <0459.g1:( B C )>`;
   | therefore the circumference :g:`$AC$ <0459.g1:( A C )>` is quadruple of :g:`$CB$ <0459.g1:( C B )>`.

.. subclause::

   | But, as the circumference :g:`$AC$ <0459.g1:( A C )>` is to :g:`$CB$ <0459.g1:( C B )>`, so is the angle
   | :g:`$AEC$ <0459.g1:< A E C )>` to the angle :g:`$CEB$ <0459.g1:< C E B )>`; :just:`[VI. 33] <VI.33>`

.. assertion::

   therefore the angle :g:`$AEC$ <0459.g1:< A E C )>` is quadruple of the angle :g:`$CEB$ <0459.g1:< C E B )>`.

.. subclause:: [I. 5] <I.5>

   And, since the angle :g:`$EBC$ <0459.g1:< E B C )>` is equal to the angle :g:`$ECB$ <0459.g1:< E C B )>`,

.. assertion:: [I. 32] <I.32>

   therefore the angle :g:`$AEC$ <0459.g1:< A E C )>` is double of the angle :g:`$ECB$ <0459.g1:< E C B )>`.

.. subclause::

   | And, since the straight line :g:`$EC$ <0459.g1:/ E C />` is equal to :g:`$CD$ <0459.g1:/ C D />`,
   | for each of them is equal to the side of the hexagon inscribed
   | in the circle :g:`$ABC$ <0459.g1:( A B C )>`, :just:`[IV. 15, Por.]`
   | the angle :g:`$CED$ <0459.g1:< C E D )>` is also equal to the angle :g:`$CDE$ <0459.g1:< C D E )>`; :just:`[I. 5] <I.5>`

.. assertion:: [I. 32] <I.32>

   therefore the angle :g:`$ECB$ <0459.g1:< E C B )>` is double of the angle :g:`$EDC$ <0459.g1:< E D C )>`.

.. subclause::

   But the angle :g:`$AEC$ <0459.g1:< A E C )>` was proved double of the angle :g:`$ECB$ <0459.g1:< E C B )>`;

.. assertion::

   therefore the angle :g:`$AEC$ <0459.g1:< A E C )>` is quadruple of the angle :g:`$EDC$ <0459.g1:< E D C )>`.

.. subclause::

   | But the angle :g:`$AEC$ <0459.g1:< A E C )>` was also proved quadruple of the
   | angle :g:`$BEC$ <0459.g1:< B E C )>`;

.. assertion::

   therefore the angle :g:`$EDC$ <0459.g1:< E D C )>` is equal to the angle :g:`$BEC$ <0459.g1:< B E C )>`.

.. subclause::

   | But the angle :g:`$EBD$ <0459.g1:< E B D )>` is common to the two triangles :g:`$BEC$ <0459.g1:< B E C >>`
   | and :g:`$BED$ <0459.g1:< B E D >>`;

.. assertion::

   | therefore the remaining angle :g:`$BED$ <0459.g1:< B E D )>` is also equal to the
   | remaining angle :g:`$ECB$ <0459.g1:< E C B )>`; :just:`[I. 32] <I.32>`

.. assertion::

   | therefore the triangle :g:`$EBD$ <0459.g1:< E B D >>` is equiangular with the triangle
   | :g:`$EBC$ <0459.g1:< E B C >>`.

.. assertion::

   | Therefore, proportionally, as :g:`$DB$ <0459.g1:/ D B />` is to :g:`$BE$ <0459.g1:/ B E />`, so is :g:`$EB$ <0459.g1:/ E B />` to :g:`$BC$ <0459.g1:/ B C />`.
   | :just:`[VI. 4] <VI.4>`

.. subclause::

   But :g:`$EB$ <0459.g1:/ E B />` is equal to :g:`$CD$ <0459.g1:/ C D />`.

.. assertion::

   Therefore, as :g:`$BD$ <0459.g1:/ B D />` is to :g:`$DC$ <0459.g1:/ D C />`, so is :g:`$DC$ <0459.g1:/ D C />` to :g:`$CB$ <0459.g1:/ C B />`.

.. subclause::

   And :g:`$BD$ <0459.g1:/ B D />` is greater than :g:`$DC$ <0459.g1:/ D C />`;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$DC$ <0459.g1:/ D C />` is also greater than :g:`$CB$ <0459.g1:/ C B />`.

.. assertion::

   | Therefore the straight line :g:`$BD$ <0459.g1:/ B D />` has been cut in extreme
   | and mean ratio, and :g:`$DC$ <0459.g1:/ D C />` is its greater segment.

.. qed::

   Q. E. D.

----

| :g:`$BC$ <0459.g1:/ B C />` is the side of a regular decagon inscribed in the circle; :g:`$CD$ <0459.g1:/ C D />` is the
| side of the inscribed regular hexagon, and is therefore equal to the radius :g:`$BE$ <0459.g1:/ B E />`
| or :g:`$EC$ <0459.g1:/ E C />`.

| Therefore, in order to prove our theorem, we have only to show that the
| triangles :g:`$EBC$ <0459.g1:< E B C >>`, :g:`$DBE$ <0459.g1:< D B E >>` are similar.
