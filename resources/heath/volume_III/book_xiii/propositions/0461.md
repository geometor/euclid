Since <span data-ref="0459.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is the side of a regular decagon,
<p class="assertion">$(\text{arc }$ <span data-ref="0459.g1:( B C A )">$BCA$</span>$) = 5\,(\text{arc }$ <span data-ref="0459.g1:( B C )">$BC$</span>$)$,<br />
so that $(\text{arc }$ <span data-ref="0459.g1:( C F A )">$CFA$</span>$) = 4\,(\text{arc }$ <span data-ref="0459.g1:( B C )">$BC$</span>$)$,<br />
whence $\angle$<span data-ref="0459.g1:< C E A )">$CEA$</span> $= 4\,\angle$<span data-ref="0459.g1:< B E C )">$BEC$</span>.</p>
<p class="sub-clause">But $\angle$<span data-ref="0459.g1:< C E A )">$CEA$</span> $= 2\,\angle$<span data-ref="0459.g1:< E C B )">$ECB$</span>.</p>
<p class="assertion">Therefore $\angle$<span data-ref="0459.g1:< E C B )">$ECB$</span> $= 2\,\angle$<span data-ref="0459.g1:< B E C )">$BEC$</span> $\ldots\ldots\ldots\ldots\ldots\ldots\ldots\ldots\ldots\ldots\text{(1)}$.</p>
<p class="sub-clause">But, since <span data-ref="0459.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> $= $ <span data-ref="0459.g1:/ C E /">$CE$</span>,<br />
$\angle$<span data-ref="0459.g1:< C D E )">$CDE$</span> $= \angle$<span data-ref="0459.g1:< C E D )">$CED$</span>,<br />
so that $\angle$<span data-ref="0459.g1:< E C B )">$ECB$</span> $= 2\,\angle$<span data-ref="0459.g1:< C D E )">$CDE$</span>.</p>
<p class="assertion">It follows from (1) that $\angle$<span data-ref="0459.g1:< B E C )">$BEC$</span> $= \angle$<span data-ref="0459.g1:< C D E )">$CDE$</span>.</p>

Now, in the $\Delta\text{s}$ <span data-ref="0459.g1:< E B C >">$EBC$</span>, <span data-ref="0459.g1:< D B E >">$DBE$</span>,
<p class="assertion">$\angle$<span data-ref="0459.g1:< B E C )">$BEC$</span> $= \angle$<span data-ref="0459.g1:< B D E )">$BDE$</span>,</p>
<p class="sub-clause">and $\angle$<span data-ref="0459.g1:< E B C )">$EBC$</span> is common,</p>
so that $\angle$<span data-ref="0459.g1:< E C B )">$ECB$</span> $= \angle$<span data-ref="0459.g1:< D E B )">$DEB$</span>,
and
<p class="assertion">$\Delta\text{s}$ <span data-ref="0459.g1:< E B C >">$EBC$</span>, <span data-ref="0459.g1:< D B E >">$DBE$</span> are similar.</p>
Hence
<p class="assertion"><span data-ref="0459.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> $:$ <span data-ref="0459.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span> $= $ <span data-ref="0459.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span> $:$ <span data-ref="0459.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>,</p>
or
<p class="assertion">$(\text{rect. }$ <span data-ref="0459.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span>, <span data-ref="0459.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>$) = (\text{sq. on }$ <span data-ref="0459.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span>$)$<br />
$= (\text{sq. on }$ <span data-ref="0459.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>$)$,</p>
and <span data-ref="0459.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> is divided at <span data-ref="0459.g1:C">$C$</span> in extreme and mean ratio.

To find the side of the decagon algebraically in terms of the radius we<br />
have, if $x$ be the side required,
$$(r + x)x = r^2,$$
whence
$$x = \frac{r}{2}(\sqrt{5} - 1).$$

square on the side of the pentagon is equal to the squares on<br />
the side of the hexagon and on that of the decagon inscribed in<br />
the same circle.</p>
