Since $BC$ is the side of a regular decagon,
$$\begin{aligned}
(\text{arc } BCA) &= 5\,(\text{arc } BC), \\
\text{so that}\qquad (\text{arc } CFA) &= 4\,(\text{arc } BC), \\
\text{whence}\qquad \angle CEA &= 4\,\angle BEC. \\
\text{But}\qquad \angle CEA &= 2\,\angle ECB. \\
\text{Therefore}\qquad \angle ECB &= 2\,\angle BEC \quad \ldots\ldots\ldots\ldots\ldots\ldots\ldots\ldots\ldots\ldots\text{(1)}. \\
\text{But, since } CD = CE, & \\
\angle CDE &= \angle CED, \\
\text{so that}\qquad \angle ECB &= 2\,\angle CDE.
\end{aligned}$$
It follows from (1) that $\angle BEC = \angle CDE$.

Now, in the $\Delta\text{s}$ $EBC$, $DBE$,
$$\begin{aligned}
\angle BEC &= \angle BDE, \\
\text{and}\qquad \angle EBC &\text{ is common,} \\
\text{so that}\qquad \angle ECB &= \angle DEB, \\
\text{and}\qquad \Delta\text{s } EBC, DBE &\text{ are similar.} \\
\text{Hence}\qquad DB : BE &= EB : BC, \\
\text{or}\qquad (\text{rect. } DB, BC) &= (\text{sq. on } EB) \\
&= (\text{sq. on } CD),
\end{aligned}$$
and $DB$ is divided at $C$ in extreme and mean ratio.

To find the side of the decagon algebraically in terms of the radius we have, if $x$ be the side required,
$$(r + x)x = r^2,$$
whence
$$x = \frac{r}{2}(\sqrt{5} - 1).$$
