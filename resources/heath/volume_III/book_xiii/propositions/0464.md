<span data-ref="0462.g1:< A B C D E >">$ABCDE$</span> being a regular pentagon inscribed in a circle, and <span data-ref="0462.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span> the diameter through <span data-ref="0462.g1:A">$A$</span>, it follows that
$(\text{arc }$ <span data-ref="0462.g1:( C G )">$CG$</span>$) = (\text{arc }$ <span data-ref="0462.g1:( G D )">$GD$</span>$)$,
and <span data-ref="0462.g1:( C G )">$CG$</span>, <span data-ref="0462.g1:( G D )">$GD$</span> are sides of an inscribed regular decagon.

<span data-ref="0462.g1:/ F H K /">$FHK$</span> being drawn perpendicular to <span data-ref="0462.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, it follows, by I. 26, that $\angle\text{s}$ <span data-ref="0462.g1:< A F K )">$AFK$</span>, <span data-ref="0462.g1:< B F K )">$BFK$</span> are equal, and <span data-ref="0462.g1:/ B K /">$BK$</span>, <span data-ref="0462.g1:/ K A /">$KA$</span> are sides of the regular decagon.

Similarly it may be proved that, <span data-ref="0462.g1:/ F L M /">$FLM$</span> being perpendicular to <span data-ref="0462.g1:/ A K /">$AK$</span>,
<span data-ref="0462.g1:/ A L /">$AL$</span> $= $ <span data-ref="0462.g1:/ L K /">$LK$</span>,
and
$(\text{arc }$ <span data-ref="0462.g1:( A M )">$AM$</span>$) = (\text{arc }$ <span data-ref="0462.g1:( M K )">$MK$</span>$)$.

The main facts to prove are that
(1) the triangles <span data-ref="0462.g1:< A B F >">$ABF$</span>, <span data-ref="0462.g1:< F B N >">$FBN$</span> are similar, and (2) the triangles <span data-ref="0462.g1:< A B K >">$ABK$</span>, <span data-ref="0462.g1:< A K N >">$AKN$</span> are similar.

(1)
$2\,(\text{arc }$ <span data-ref="0462.g1:( C G )">$CG$</span>$) = (\text{arc }$ <span data-ref="0462.g1:( C D )">$CD$</span>$) = (\text{arc }$ <span data-ref="0462.g1:( A B )">$AB$</span>$) = 2\,(\text{arc }$ <span data-ref="0462.g1:( B K )">$BK$</span>$)$,
or $(\text{arc }$ <span data-ref="0462.g1:( C G )">$CG$</span>$) = (\text{arc }$ <span data-ref="0462.g1:( B K )">$BK$</span>$) = (\text{arc }$ <span data-ref="0462.g1:( A K )">$AK$</span>$) = 2\,(\text{arc }$ <span data-ref="0462.g1:( K M )">$KM$</span>$)$.
And $(\text{arc }$ <span data-ref="0462.g1:( C B )">$CB$</span>$) = 2\,(\text{arc }$ <span data-ref="0462.g1:( B K )">$BK$</span>$)$.
Therefore, by addition,
$(\text{arc }$ <span data-ref="0462.g1:( B C G )">$BCG$</span>$) = 2\,(\text{arc }$ <span data-ref="0462.g1:( B K M )">$BKM$</span>$)$.
Therefore
$\angle$<span data-ref="0462.g1:< B F G )">$BFG$</span> $= 2\,\angle$<span data-ref="0462.g1:< B F N )">$BFN$</span>.
But
$\angle$<span data-ref="0462.g1:< B F G )">$BFG$</span> $= 2\,\angle$<span data-ref="0462.g1:< F A B )">$FAB$</span>,
so that
$\angle$<span data-ref="0462.g1:< F A B )">$FAB$</span> $= \angle$<span data-ref="0462.g1:< B F N )">$BFN$</span>.
Hence, in the $\Delta\text{s}$ <span data-ref="0462.g1:< A B F >">$ABF$</span>, <span data-ref="0462.g1:< F B N >">$FBN$</span>,
$\angle$<span data-ref="0462.g1:< F A B )">$FAB$</span> $= \angle$<span data-ref="0462.g1:< B F N )">$BFN$</span>,
and $\angle$<span data-ref="0462.g1:< A B F )">$ABF$</span> is common;
therefore
$\angle$<span data-ref="0462.g1:< A F B )">$AFB$</span> $= \angle$<span data-ref="0462.g1:< B N F )">$BNF$</span>,
and $\Delta\text{s}$ <span data-ref="0462.g1:< A B F >">$ABF$</span>, <span data-ref="0462.g1:< F B N >">$FBN$</span> are similar.

(2) Since <span data-ref="0462.g1:/ A L /">$AL$</span> $= $ <span data-ref="0462.g1:/ L K /">$LK$</span>, and the angles at <span data-ref="0462.g1:L">$L$</span> are right,
<span data-ref="0462.g1:/ A N /">$AN$</span> $= $ <span data-ref="0462.g1:/ N K /">$NK$</span>,
and
$\angle$<span data-ref="0462.g1:< N K A )">$NKA$</span> $= \angle$<span data-ref="0462.g1:< N A K )">$NAK$</span> $= \angle$<span data-ref="0462.g1:< K B A )">$KBA$</span>.
Hence, in the $\Delta\text{s}$ <span data-ref="0462.g1:< A B K >">$ABK$</span>, <span data-ref="0462.g1:< A K N >">$AKN$</span>,
$\angle$<span data-ref="0462.g1:< A B K )">$ABK$</span> $= \angle$<span data-ref="0462.g1:< A K N )">$AKN$</span>,
and $\angle$<span data-ref="0462.g1:< K A N )">$KAN$</span> is common;
whence the third angles are equal;
therefore the triangles <span data-ref="0462.g1:< A B K >">$ABK$</span>, <span data-ref="0462.g1:< A K N >">$AKN$</span> are similar.
