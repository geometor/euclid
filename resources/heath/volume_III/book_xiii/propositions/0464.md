<p class="assertion">therefore the rectangle <span data-ref="0462.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0462.g1:/ B N /">$BN$</span> together with the rectangle<br />
<span data-ref="0462.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span>, <span data-ref="0462.g1:/ A N /">$AN$</span>, that is, the square on <span data-ref="0462.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span> <span class="justification">[II. 2]</span>, is equal to the<br />
square on <span data-ref="0462.g1:/ B F /">$BF$</span> together with the square on <span data-ref="0462.g1:/ A K /">$AK$</span>.</p>

<p class="assertion">And <span data-ref="0462.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span> is a side of the pentagon, <span data-ref="0462.g1:/ B F /">$BF$</span> of the hexagon<br />
<span class="justification">[IV. 15, Por.]</span>, and <span data-ref="0462.g1:/ A K /">$AK$</span> of the decagon.</p>

<p class="qed">Therefore etc.<span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

---

<span data-ref="0462.g1:< A B C D E >">$ABCDE$</span> being a regular pentagon inscribed in a circle, and <span data-ref="0462.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span> the<br />
diameter through <span data-ref="0462.g1:A">$A$</span>, it follows that<br />
$(\text{arc }$ <span data-ref="0462.g1:( C G )">$CG$</span>$) = (\text{arc }$ <span data-ref="0462.g1:( G D )">$GD$</span>$),<br />
and <span data-ref="0462.g1:( C G )">$CG$</span>, <span data-ref="0462.g1:( G D )">$GD$</span> are sides of an inscribed regular decagon.

<span data-ref="0462.g1:/ F H K /">$FHK$</span> being drawn perpendicular to <span data-ref="0462.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, it follows, by I. 26, that<br />
$\angle\text{s}$ <span data-ref="0462.g1:< A F K )">$AFK$</span>, <span data-ref="0462.g1:< B F K )">$BFK$</span> are equal, and <span data-ref="0462.g1:/ B K /">$BK$</span>, <span data-ref="0462.g1:/ K A /">$KA$</span> are sides of the regular decagon.

Similarly it may be proved that, <span data-ref="0462.g1:/ F L M /">$FLM$</span> being perpendicular to <span data-ref="0462.g1:/ A K /">$AK$</span>,<br />
<span data-ref="0462.g1:/ A L /">$AL$</span> $= $ <span data-ref="0462.g1:/ L K /">$LK$</span>,<br />
and<br />
$(\text{arc }$ <span data-ref="0462.g1:( A M )">$AM$</span>$) = (\text{arc }$ <span data-ref="0462.g1:( M K )">$MK$</span>$).

The main facts to prove are that<br />
(1) the triangles <span data-ref="0462.g1:< A B F >">$ABF$</span>, <span data-ref="0462.g1:< F B N >">$FBN$</span> are similar, and (2) the triangles <span data-ref="0462.g1:< A B K >">$ABK$</span>, <span data-ref="0462.g1:< A K N >">$AKN$</span><br />
are similar.

(1)<br />
$2\,(\text{arc }$ <span data-ref="0462.g1:( C G )">$CG$</span>$) = (\text{arc }$ <span data-ref="0462.g1:( C D )">$CD$</span>$)$<br />
$= (\text{arc }$ <span data-ref="0462.g1:( A B )">$AB$</span>$)$<br />
$= 2\,(\text{arc }$ <span data-ref="0462.g1:( B K )">$BK$</span>$),<br />
or $(\text{arc }$ <span data-ref="0462.g1:( C G )">$CG$</span>$) = (\text{arc }$ <span data-ref="0462.g1:( B K )">$BK$</span>$) = (\text{arc }$ <span data-ref="0462.g1:( A K )">$AK$</span>$)$<br />
$= 2\,(\text{arc }$ <span data-ref="0462.g1:( K M )">$KM$</span>$).<br />
And $(\text{arc }$ <span data-ref="0462.g1:( C B )">$CB$</span>$) = 2\,(\text{arc }$ <span data-ref="0462.g1:( B K )">$BK$</span>$).<br />
Therefore, by addition,<br />
$(\text{arc }$ <span data-ref="0462.g1:( B C G )">$BCG$</span>$) = 2\,(\text{arc }$ <span data-ref="0462.g1:( B K M )">$BKM$</span>$).<br />
Therefore<br />
$\angle$<span data-ref="0462.g1:< B F G )">$BFG$</span> $= 2\,\angle$<span data-ref="0462.g1:< B F N )">$BFN$</span>.<br />
But<br />
$\angle$<span data-ref="0462.g1:< B F G )">$BFG$</span> $= 2\,\angle$<span data-ref="0462.g1:< F A B )">$FAB$</span>,<br />
so that<br />
$\angle$<span data-ref="0462.g1:< F A B )">$FAB$</span> $= \angle$<span data-ref="0462.g1:< B F N )">$BFN$</span>.<br />
Hence, in the $\Delta\text{s}$ <span data-ref="0462.g1:< A B F >">$ABF$</span>, <span data-ref="0462.g1:< F B N >">$FBN$</span>,<br />
$\angle$<span data-ref="0462.g1:< F A B )">$FAB$</span> $= \angle$<span data-ref="0462.g1:< B F N )">$BFN$</span>,<br />
and $\angle$<span data-ref="0462.g1:< A B F )">$ABF$</span> is common;<br />
therefore<br />
$\angle$<span data-ref="0462.g1:< A F B )">$AFB$</span> $= \angle$<span data-ref="0462.g1:< B N F )">$BNF$</span>,<br />
and $\Delta\text{s}$ <span data-ref="0462.g1:< A B F >">$ABF$</span>, <span data-ref="0462.g1:< F B N >">$FBN$</span> are similar.

(2) Since <span data-ref="0462.g1:/ A L /">$AL$</span> $= $ <span data-ref="0462.g1:/ L K /">$LK$</span>, and the angles at <span data-ref="0462.g1:L">$L$</span> are right,<br />
<span data-ref="0462.g1:/ A N /">$AN$</span> $= $ <span data-ref="0462.g1:/ N K /">$NK$</span>,<br />
and<br />
$\angle$<span data-ref="0462.g1:< N K A )">$NKA$</span> $= \angle$<span data-ref="0462.g1:< N A K )">$NAK$</span><br />
$= \angle$<span data-ref="0462.g1:< K B A )">$KBA$</span>.<br />
Hence, in the $\Delta\text{s}$ <span data-ref="0462.g1:< A B K >">$ABK$</span>, <span data-ref="0462.g1:< A K N >">$AKN$</span>,<br />
$\angle$<span data-ref="0462.g1:< A B K )">$ABK$</span> $= \angle$<span data-ref="0462.g1:< A K N )">$AKN$</span>,<br />
and $\angle$<span data-ref="0462.g1:< K A N )">$KAN$</span> is common;<br />
whence the third angles are equal;<br />
therefore the triangles <span data-ref="0462.g1:< A B K >">$ABK$</span>, <span data-ref="0462.g1:< A K N >">$AKN$</span> are similar.
