   But, if from a rational straight line there be subtracted a
rational straight line which is commensurable with the whole
in square only, the remainder is irrational, namely an apotome;
therefore <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ M B /">$MB$</span> is an apotome and <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ M K /">$MK$</span> the annex to it. [x. 73]
   I say next that <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ M B /">$MB$</span> is also a fourth apotome.
   Let the square on <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ N /">$N$</span> be equal to that by which the square
on <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ B K /">$BK$</span> is greater than the square on <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ K M /">$KM$</span>;
therefore the square on <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ B K /">$BK$</span> is greater than the square on <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ K M /">$KM$</span>
by the square on <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ N /">$N$</span>.
   And, since <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ K F /">$KF$</span> is commensurable with <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ F B /">$FB$</span>,
*componendo* also, <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ K B /">$KB$</span> is commensurable with <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ F B /">$FB$</span>. [x. 15]
   But <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ B F /">$BF$</span> is commensurable with <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ B H /">$BH$</span>;
therefore <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ B K /">$BK$</span> is also commensurable with <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ B H /">$BH$</span>. [x. 12]
   And, since the square on <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ B K /">$BK$</span> is five times the square
on <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ K M /">$KM$</span>,
therefore the square on <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ B K /">$BK$</span> has to the square on <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ K M /">$KM$</span> the
ratio which $5$ has to $1$.
   Therefore, *convertendo*, the square on <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ B K /">$BK$</span> has to the square
on <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ N /">$N$</span> the ratio which $5$ has to $4$ [v. 19, Por.], and this is not the
ratio which a square number has to a square number;
therefore <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ B K /">$BK$</span> is incommensurable with <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ N /">$N$</span>; [x. 9]
therefore the square on <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ B K /">$BK$</span> is greater than the square on <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ K M /">$KM$</span>
by the square on a straight line incommensurable with <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ B K /">$BK$</span>.
   Since then the square on the whole <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ B K /">$BK$</span> is greater than
the square on the annex <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ K M /">$KM$</span> by the square on a straight line
incommensurable with <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ B K /">$BK$</span>,
and the whole <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ B K /">$BK$</span> is commensurable with the rational straight
line, <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ B H /">$BH$</span>, set out,
therefore <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ M B /">$MB$</span> is a fourth apotome. [x. Deff. III. 4]
   But the rectangle contained by a rational straight line and
a fourth apotome is irrational,
and its square root is irrational, and is called minor. [x. 94]
   But the square on <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is equal to the rectangle <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ H B /">$HB$</span>, <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ B M /">$BM$</span>,
because, when <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ A H /">$AH$</span> is joined, the triangle <span data-ref="0466.g1:< A B H >">$ABH$</span> is equiangular
with the triangle <span data-ref="0466.g1:< A B M >">$ABM$</span>, and, as <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ H B /">$HB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>
to <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ B M /">$BM$</span>.
