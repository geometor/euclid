<p class="assertion">Therefore the side <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> of the pentagon is the irrational<br />
straight line called minor.</p>

<p class="qed"><span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

---

Here we require certain definitions and propositions of Book X.<br />
First we require the definition of an *apotome* <span class="justification">[see X. 73]</span>, which is a straight<br />
line of the form $(\rho \sim \sqrt{k} \cdot \rho)$, where $\rho$ is a "rational" straight line and $k$ is any<br />
integer or numerical fraction, the square root of which is not *integral* or<br />
expressible in integers. The lesser of the straight lines $\rho$, $\sqrt{k} \cdot \rho$ is the *annex*.

Next we require the definition of the *fourth apotome* <span class="justification">[X. Deff. III. (after<br />
X. 84)]</span>, which is a straight line of the form $(x - y)$, where $x$, $y$ are (being both<br />
rational and commensurable in square only) are also such that $\sqrt{x^2 - y^2}$ is<br />
incommensurable with $x$, while $x$ is commensurable with a given rational<br />
straight line $\rho$. As shown on X. 88 (note), the *fourth apotome* is of the form
$$(k\rho - \frac{k\rho}{\sqrt{1 + \lambda}}).$$

Lastly the *minor* (straight line) is the irrational straight line defined in<br />
X. 76. It is of the form $(x - y)$, where $x$, $y$ are incommensurable in square,<br />
and $(x^2 + y^2)$ is 'rational,' while $xy$ is 'medial.' As shown in the note on<br />
X. 76, the *minor irrational straight line* is of the form
$$\frac{\rho}{\sqrt{2}} \sqrt{1 + \frac{k}{\sqrt{1 + k^2}}} - \frac{\rho}{\sqrt{2}} \sqrt{1 - \frac{k}{\sqrt{1 + k^2}}}.$$

The proposition may be put as follows. <span data-ref="0466.g1:< A B C D E >">$ABCDE$</span> being a regular<br />
pentagon inscribed in a circle, <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ B H /">$BH$</span> the diameters through <span data-ref="0466.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0466.g1:B">$B$</span> meeting<br />
<span data-ref="0466.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> in <span data-ref="0466.g1:L">$L$</span> and <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> in <span data-ref="0466.g1:M">$M$</span> respectively, <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ F K /">$FK$</span> is made equal to $\frac{1}{4}$<span data-ref="0466.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span>.<br />
Now, the radius <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span> ($r$) being rational, so are <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ F K /">$FK$</span>, <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ B K /">$BK$</span>.<br />
The arcs <span data-ref="0466.g1:( C G )">$CG$</span>, <span data-ref="0466.g1:( G D )">$GD$</span> are equal;<br />
hence $\angle\text{s}$ at <span data-ref="0466.g1:L">$L$</span> are right, and <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> $= 2$<span data-ref="0466.g1:/ C L /">$CL$</span>.<br />
Similarly $\angle\text{s}$ at <span data-ref="0466.g1:M">$M$</span> are right, and <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> $= 2$<span data-ref="0466.g1:/ C M /">$CM$</span>.

We have to prove<br />
(1) that <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ B M /">$BM$</span> is an apotome,<br />
(2) that <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ B M /">$BM$</span> is a fourth apotome,<br />
(3) that <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span> is a minor irrational straight line.

Remembering that, if <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ C A /">$CA$</span> is divided in extreme and mean ratio, the<br />
greater segment is equal to the side of the pentagon <span class="justification">[XIII. 8]</span>, and that accord-<br />
ingly <span class="justification">[XIII. 1]</span> $($<span data-ref="0466.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> $+ \frac{1}{2}$<span data-ref="0466.g1:/ C A /">$CA$</span>$)^2 = 5(\frac{1}{2}$<span data-ref="0466.g1:/ C A /">$CA$</span>$)^2$, we work towards a proportion con-<br />
taining the ratio $($<span data-ref="0466.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> $+ $ <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ C M /">$CM$</span>$)^2 : $ <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ C M /">$CM$</span>$^2$, thus.

The $\Delta\text{s}$ <span data-ref="0466.g1:< A C L >">$ACL$</span>, <span data-ref="0466.g1:< A F M >">$AFM$</span> are equiangular and therefore similar.<br />
Therefore <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ L C /">$LC$</span> $:$ <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ C A /">$CA$</span> $= $ <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ M F /">$MF$</span> $:$ <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ F A /">$FA$</span>,<br />
and accordingly $2$<span data-ref="0466.g1:/ L C /">$LC$</span> $:$ <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ C A /">$CA$</span> $= $ <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ M F /">$MF$</span> $: \frac{1}{2}$<span data-ref="0466.g1:/ F A /">$FA$</span>;<br />
thus $2$<span data-ref="0466.g1:/ L C /">$LC$</span> $: \frac{1}{2}$<span data-ref="0466.g1:/ C A /">$CA$</span> $= $ <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ M F /">$MF$</span> $: \frac{1}{4}$<span data-ref="0466.g1:/ F A /">$FA$</span>,<br />
or <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ D C /">$DC$</span> $:$ <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ C M /">$CM$</span> $= $ <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ M F /">$MF$</span> $:$ <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ F K /">$FK$</span>;<br />
whence, *componendo*, and squaring,<br />
$($<span data-ref="0466.g1:/ D C /">$DC$</span> $+ $ <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ C M /">$CM$</span>$)^2 : $ <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ C M /">$CM$</span>$^2 = $ <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ M K /">$MK$</span>$^2 : $ <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ K F /">$KF$</span>$^2$.<br />
But $($<span data-ref="0466.g1:/ D C /">$DC$</span> $+ $ <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ C M /">$CM$</span>$)^2 = 5$<span data-ref="0466.g1:/ C M /">$CM$</span>$^2$;<br />
therefore <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ M K /">$MK$</span>$^2 = 5$<span data-ref="0466.g1:/ K F /">$KF$</span>$^2$.
