title: "Proposition 11 (conclusion and commentary)"

Therefore the side <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> of the pentagon is the irrational
straight line called minor.
<div align="right">Q. E. D.</div>

---

   Here we require certain definitions and propositions of Book X.
   First we require the definition of an *apotome* [see x. 73], which is a straight
line of the form $(\rho \sim \sqrt{k} \cdot \rho)$, where $\rho$ is a "rational" straight line and $k$ is any
integer or numerical fraction, the square root of which is not *integral* or
expressible in integers. The lesser of the straight lines $\rho$, $\sqrt{k} \cdot \rho$ is the *annex*.
   Next we require the definition of the *fourth apotome* [x. Deff. III. (after
x. 84)], which is a straight line of the form $(x - y)$, where $x$, $y$ are (being both
rational and commensurable in square only) are also such that $\sqrt{x^2 - y^2}$ is
incommensurable with $x$, while $x$ is commensurable with a given rational
straight line $\rho$. As shown on x. 88 (note), the fourth apotome is of the form
$$(k\rho - \frac{k\rho}{\sqrt{1 + \lambda}}).$$
   Lastly the *minor* (straight line) is the irrational straight line defined in
x. 76. It is of the form $(x - y)$, where $x$, $y$ are incommensurable in square,
and $(x^2 + y^2)$ is 'rational,' while $xy$ is 'medial.' As shown in the note on
x. 76, the *minor irrational straight line* is of the form
$$\frac{\rho}{\sqrt{2}} \sqrt{1 + \frac{k}{\sqrt{1 + k^2}}} - \frac{\rho}{\sqrt{2}} \sqrt{1 - \frac{k}{\sqrt{1 + k^2}}}.$$
   The proposition may be put as follows. <span data-ref="0466.g1:< A B C D E >">$ABCDE$</span> being a regular
pentagon inscribed in a circle, <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span>, <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ B H /">$BH$</span> the diameters through <span data-ref="0466.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0466.g1:B">$B$</span> meeting
<span data-ref="0466.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> in <span data-ref="0466.g1:L">$L$</span> and <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> in <span data-ref="0466.g1:M">$M$</span> respectively, <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ F K /">$FK$</span> is made equal to $\frac{1}{4}$<span data-ref="0466.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span>.
   Now, the radius <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span> ($r$) being rational, so are <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ F K /">$FK$</span>, <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ B K /">$BK$</span>.
   The arcs <span data-ref="0466.g1:( C G )">$CG$</span>, <span data-ref="0466.g1:( G D )">$GD$</span> are equal;
hence $\angle\text{s}$ at <span data-ref="0466.g1:L">$L$</span> are right, and <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> $= 2$<span data-ref="0466.g1:/ C L /">$CL$</span>.
   Similarly $\angle\text{s}$ at <span data-ref="0466.g1:M">$M$</span> are right, and <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> $= 2$<span data-ref="0466.g1:/ C M /">$CM$</span>.
   We have to prove
   (1) that <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ B M /">$BM$</span> is an apotome,
   (2) that <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ B M /">$BM$</span> is a fourth apotome,
   (3) that <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span> is a minor irrational straight line.
   Remembering that, if <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ C A /">$CA$</span> is divided in extreme and mean ratio, the
greater segment is equal to the side of the pentagon [xiii. 8], and that accord-
ingly [xiii. 1] $($<span data-ref="0466.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> $+ \frac{1}{2}$<span data-ref="0466.g1:/ C A /">$CA$</span>$)^2 = 5(\frac{1}{2}$<span data-ref="0466.g1:/ C A /">$CA$</span>$)^2$, we work towards a proportion con-
taining the ratio $($<span data-ref="0466.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> $+ $ <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ C M /">$CM$</span>$)^2 : $ <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ C M /">$CM$</span>$^2$, thus.
   The $\Delta\text{s}$ <span data-ref="0466.g1:< A C L >">$ACL$</span>, <span data-ref="0466.g1:< A F M >">$AFM$</span> are equiangular and therefore similar.
   Therefore <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ L C /">$LC$</span> $:$ <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ C A /">$CA$</span> $= $ <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ M F /">$MF$</span> $:$ <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ F A /">$FA$</span>,
and accordingly $2$<span data-ref="0466.g1:/ L C /">$LC$</span> $:$ <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ C A /">$CA$</span> $= $ <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ M F /">$MF$</span> $: \frac{1}{2}$<span data-ref="0466.g1:/ F A /">$FA$</span>;
thus $2$<span data-ref="0466.g1:/ L C /">$LC$</span> $: \frac{1}{2}$<span data-ref="0466.g1:/ C A /">$CA$</span> $= $ <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ M F /">$MF$</span> $: \frac{1}{4}$<span data-ref="0466.g1:/ F A /">$FA$</span>,
or <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ D C /">$DC$</span> $:$ <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ C M /">$CM$</span> $= $ <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ M F /">$MF$</span> $:$ <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ F K /">$FK$</span>;
whence, *componendo*, and squaring,
$($<span data-ref="0466.g1:/ D C /">$DC$</span> $+ $ <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ C M /">$CM$</span>$)^2 : $ <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ C M /">$CM$</span>$^2 = $ <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ M K /">$MK$</span>$^2 : $ <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ K F /">$KF$</span>$^2$.
   But $($<span data-ref="0466.g1:/ D C /">$DC$</span> $+ $ <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ C M /">$CM$</span>$)^2 = 5$<span data-ref="0466.g1:/ C M /">$CM$</span>$^2$;
therefore <span data-ref="0466.g1:/ M K /">$MK$</span>$^2 = 5$<span data-ref="0466.g1:/ K F /">$KF$</span>$^2$.
