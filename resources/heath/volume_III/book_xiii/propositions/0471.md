   Let <span data-ref="0471.g1:( A B C )">$ABC$</span> be a circle,
and let the equilateral triangle <span data-ref="0471.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> be inscribed in it;
I say that the square on one side of
the triangle <span data-ref="0471.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> is triple of the square
on the radius of the circle.
   For let the centre <span data-ref="0471.g1:D">$D$</span> of the circle
<span data-ref="0471.g1:( A B C )">$ABC$</span> be taken,
let <span data-ref="0471.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> be joined and carried through
to <span data-ref="0471.g1:E">$E$</span>,
and let <span data-ref="0471.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span> be joined.
   Then, since the triangle <span data-ref="0471.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> is
equilateral,
therefore the circumference <span data-ref="0471.g1:( B E C )">$BEC$</span> is a third part of the circum-
ference of the circle <span data-ref="0471.g1:( A B C )">$ABC$</span>.
   Therefore the circumference <span data-ref="0471.g1:( B E )">$BE$</span> is a sixth part of the
circumference of the circle;
therefore the straight line <span data-ref="0471.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span> belongs to a hexagon;
therefore it is equal to the radius <span data-ref="0471.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>. [iv. 15, Por.]
   And, since <span data-ref="0471.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> is double of <span data-ref="0471.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>,
the square on <span data-ref="0471.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> is quadruple of the square on <span data-ref="0471.g1:/ E D /">$ED$</span>, that is,
of the square on <span data-ref="0471.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span>.
   But the square on <span data-ref="0471.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> is equal to the squares on <span data-ref="0471.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0471.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span>; [iii. 31, i. 47]
therefore the squares on <span data-ref="0471.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0471.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span> are quadruple of the square
on <span data-ref="0471.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span>.
   Therefore, *separando*, the square on <span data-ref="0471.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is triple of the
square on <span data-ref="0471.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span>.
   But <span data-ref="0471.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0471.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>;
therefore the square on <span data-ref="0471.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is triple of the square on <span data-ref="0471.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>.
   Therefore the square on the side of the triangle is triple
of the square on the radius.
<div align="right">Q. E. D.</div>
