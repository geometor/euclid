Let <span data-ref="0471.g1:( A B C )">$ABC$</span> be a circle,<br />
and let the equilateral triangle <span data-ref="0471.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> be inscribed in it;<br />
I say that the square on one side of<br />
the triangle <span data-ref="0471.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> is triple of the square<br />
on the radius of the circle.

![Proposition 12 Diagram](0471.g1.svg)

For let the centre <span data-ref="0471.g1:D">$D$</span> of the circle<br />
<span data-ref="0471.g1:( A B C )">$ABC$</span> be taken,<br />
let <span data-ref="0471.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> be joined and carried through<br />
to <span data-ref="0471.g1:E">$E$</span>,<br />
and let <span data-ref="0471.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span> be joined.

<p class="sub-clause">Then, since the triangle <span data-ref="0471.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> is<br />
equilateral,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the circumference <span data-ref="0471.g1:( B E C )">$BEC$</span> is a third part of the circum-<br />
ference of the circle <span data-ref="0471.g1:( A B C )">$ABC$</span>.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore the circumference <span data-ref="0471.g1:( B E )">$BE$</span> is a sixth part of the<br />
circumference of the circle;<br />
therefore the straight line <span data-ref="0471.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span> belongs to a hexagon;<br />
therefore it is equal to the radius <span data-ref="0471.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>. <span class="justification">[IV. 15, Por.]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">And, since <span data-ref="0471.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> is double of <span data-ref="0471.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">the square on <span data-ref="0471.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> is quadruple of the square on <span data-ref="0471.g1:/ E D /">$ED$</span>, that is,<br />
of the square on <span data-ref="0471.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">But the square on <span data-ref="0471.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> is equal to the squares on <span data-ref="0471.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0471.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span>;<br />
<span class="justification">[III. 31, I. 47]</span></p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the squares on <span data-ref="0471.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0471.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span> are quadruple of the square<br />
on <span data-ref="0471.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span>.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore, *separando*, the square on <span data-ref="0471.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is triple of the<br />
square on <span data-ref="0471.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">But <span data-ref="0471.g1:/ B E /">$BE$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0471.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the square on <span data-ref="0471.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is triple of the square on <span data-ref="0471.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore the square on the side of the triangle is triple<br />
of the square on the radius.</p>

<p class="qed"><span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

### PROPOSITION 13.

<p class="enunciation">*To construct a pyramid, to comprehend it in a given sphere,<br />
and to prove that the square on the diameter of the sphere is<br />
one and a half times the square on the side of the pyramid.*</p>
