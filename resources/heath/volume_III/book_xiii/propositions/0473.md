<p class="assertion">For the same reason<br />
each of the straight lines <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ K F /">$KF$</span>, <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ K G /">$KG$</span> is also equal to <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ D A /">$DA$</span>;<br />
therefore the three straight lines <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ K E /">$KE$</span>, <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ K F /">$KF$</span>, <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ K G /">$KG$</span> are equal to<br />
one another.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">And, since <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is double of <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is triple of <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">But, as <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, so is the square on <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> to the square<br />
on <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ D C /">$DC$</span>, as will be proved afterwards.</p>
<p class="assertion">Therefore the square on <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> is triple of the square on <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ D C /">$DC$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">But the square on <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ F E /">$FE$</span> is also triple of the square on <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span>,<br />
<span class="justification">[XIII. 12]</span><br />
and <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ D C /">$DC$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span>;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ D A /">$DA$</span> is also equal to <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">But <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ D A /">$DA$</span> was proved equal to each of the straight lines<br />
<span data-ref="0472.g1:/ K E /">$KE$</span>, <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ K F /">$KF$</span>, <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ K G /">$KG$</span>;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore each of the straight lines <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span>, <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ G E /">$GE$</span> is also equal<br />
to each of the straight lines <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ K E /">$KE$</span>, <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ K F /">$KF$</span>, <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ K G /">$KG$</span>;<br />
therefore the four triangles <span data-ref="0472.g1:< E F G >">$EFG$</span>, <span data-ref="0472.g1:< K E F >">$KEF$</span>, <span data-ref="0472.g1:< K F G >">$KFG$</span>, <span data-ref="0472.g1:< K E G >">$KEG$</span> are<br />
equilateral.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore a pyramid has been constructed out of four<br />
equilateral triangles, the triangle <span data-ref="0472.g1:< E F G >">$EFG$</span> being its base and the<br />
point <span data-ref="0472.g1:K">$K$</span> its vertex.</p>

It is next required to comprehend it in the given sphere<br />
and to prove that the square on the diameter of the sphere<br />
is one and a half times the square on the side of the pyramid.

For let the straight line <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ H L /">$HL$</span> be produced in a straight<br />
line with <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ K H /">$KH$</span>,<br />
and let <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ H L /">$HL$</span> be made equal to <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span>.

<p class="sub-clause">Now, since, as <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is to <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> to <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span>, <span class="justification">[VI. 8, Por.]</span><br />
while <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ K H /">$KH$</span>, <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> to <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ H E /">$HE$</span>, and <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span> to <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ H L /">$HL$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore, as <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ K H /">$KH$</span> is to <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ H E /">$HE$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span> to <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ H L /">$HL$</span>;<br />
therefore the rectangle <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ K H /">$KH$</span>, <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ H L /">$HL$</span> is equal to the square on<br />
<span data-ref="0472.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span>. <span class="justification">[VI. 17]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">And each of the angles <span data-ref="0472.g1:< K H E )">$KHE$</span>, <span data-ref="0472.g1:< E H L )">$EHL$</span> is right;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the semicircle described on <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ K L /">$KL$</span> will pass through<br />
<span data-ref="0472.g1:E">$E$</span> also. <span class="justification">[cf. VI. 8, III. 31]</span></p>
