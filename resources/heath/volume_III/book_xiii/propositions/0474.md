I say next that the square on the diameter of the sphere<br />
is one and a half times the square on the side of the<br />
pyramid.

<p class="sub-clause">For, since <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is double of <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is triple of <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>;<br />
and, *convertendo*, <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span> is one and a half times <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">But, as <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span> is to <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, so is the square on <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span> to the square<br />
on <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore the square on <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span> is also one and a half times<br />
the square on <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">And <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ B A /">$BA$</span> is the diameter of the given sphere, and <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> is<br />
equal to the side of the pyramid.</p>

<p class="qed">Therefore the square on the diameter of the sphere is<br />
one and a half times the square on the side of the pyramid.<span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>
