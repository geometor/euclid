The Lemma is with reason suspected. Euclid commonly takes more<br />
difficult theorems for granted in the stereometrical Books. It is also clumsy<br />
in itself, while, from a gloss in the proposition rejected as an interpolation, it<br />
is clear that the interpolator of the gloss had not the Lemma. With the<br />
Lemma should disappear the words “as will be proved afterwards” (p. 469).

In the figure of the proposition, the semicircle really represents half of<br />
a section of the sphere through its centre and one edge of the inscribed<br />
tetrahedron (<span data-ref="0472.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> being the length of that edge).

The proof is in three parts, the object of which is to prove<br />
(1) that <span data-ref="0472.g1:< K E F G >">$KEFG$</span> is a tetrahedron with all its edges equal to <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>,<br />
(2) that it is inscribable in a sphere of diameter equal to <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>,<br />
(3) that<br />
<span data-ref="0472.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>$^2 = \frac{3}{2}$<span data-ref="0472.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>$^2$.

To prove (1) we have to show<br />
(a) that <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ K E /">$KE$</span> $= $ <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ K F /">$KF$</span> $= $ <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ K G /">$KG$</span> $= $ <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>,<br />
(b) that <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> $= $ <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>.

(a) Since <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ H E /">$HE$</span> $= $ <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ H F /">$HF$</span> $= $ <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ H G /">$HG$</span> $= $ <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span>,<br />
<span data-ref="0472.g1:/ K H /">$KH$</span> $= $ <span data-ref="0472.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>,<br />
and $\angle\text{s}$ <span data-ref="0472.g1:< A C D )">$ACD$</span>, <span data-ref="0472.g1:< K H E )">$KHE$</span>, <span data-ref="0472.g1:< K H F )">$KHF$</span>, <span data-ref="0472.g1:< K H G )">$KHG$</span> are right,<br />
