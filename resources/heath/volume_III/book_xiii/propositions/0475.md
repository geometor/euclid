The Lemma is with reason suspected. Euclid commonly takes more difficult theorems for granted in the stereometrical Books. It is also clumsy in itself, while, from a gloss in the proposition rejected as an interpolation, it is clear that the interpolator of the gloss had not the Lemma. With the Lemma should disappear the words "as will be proved afterwards" (p. 469).

In the figure of the proposition, the semicircle really represents half of a section of the sphere through its centre and one edge of the inscribed tetrahedron (*AD* being the length of that edge).

The proof is in three parts, the object of which is to prove

(1) that *KEFG* is a tetrahedron with all its edges equal to *AD*,

(2) that it is inscribable in a sphere of diameter equal to *AB*,

(3) that
$$\text{\it AB}^2 = \frac{3}{2}\text{\it AD}^2.$$

To prove (1) we have to show

(a) that
$$\text{\it KE} = \text{\it KF} = \text{\it KG} = \text{\it AD},$$

(b) that
$$\text{\it AD} = \text{\it EF}.$$

(a) Since
$$\text{\it HE} = \text{\it HF} = \text{\it HG} = \text{\it CD},$$
$$\text{\it KH} = \text{\it AC},$$
and $\angle\text{s } ACD, KHE, KHF, KHG$ are right,

$\Delta\text{s } ACD, KHE, KHF, KHG$ are equal in all respects;
therefore
$$\text{\it KE} = \text{\it KF} = \text{\it KG} = \text{\it AD}.$$

(b) Since
$$\text{\it AB} = 3\text{\it BC},$$
and
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{\it AB} : \text{\it BC} &= \text{\it AB} \cdot \text{\it AC} : \text{\it AC} \cdot \text{\it CB} \\
&= \text{\it AD}^2 : \text{\it CD}^2,
\end{aligned}$$
it follows that
$$\text{\it AD}^2 = 3\text{\it CD}^2.$$
