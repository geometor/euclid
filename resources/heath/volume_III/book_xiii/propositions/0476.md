It will be observed that, although in these cases Euclid’s construction is<br />
equivalent to inscribing the particular regular solid in a given sphere, he does<br />
not actually construct the solid in the sphere but constructs a solid which a<br />
sphere *equal* to the given sphere will circumscribe. Pappus, on the other<br />
hand, in dealing with the same problems, actually constructs the respective<br />
solids in the given spheres. His method is to find circular sections in the<br />
given spheres containing a certain number of the angular points of the given<br />
solids. His solutions are interesting, although they require a knowledge of<br />
some properties of a sphere which are of course not found in the *Elements*<br />
but belonged to treatises such as the *Sphaerica* of Theodosius.
