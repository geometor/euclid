## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Use of XI. Def. 14**: Heath highlights how Euclid's rotational definition of a sphere (XI. Def. 14) is applied directly to establish that the vertices lie on the sphere.
- **Metric Formulation**: Heath supplies the modern trigonometric/algebraic length of the edge in terms of the circumradius $r$:
  $$\text{edge} = \frac{2\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{3}}r = \frac{2}{3}\sqrt{6}r.$$
- **Methodological Distinction**: Heath contrasts Euclid's approach (constructing a solid and showing an equal sphere circumscribes it) with Pappus' direct inscription in a given sphere using parallel circular sections (referencing Pappus *Collection* III, pp. 142—144 and Theodosius' *Sphaerica*).
