### PROPOSITION 14.

*To construct an octahedron and comprehend it in a sphere, as in the preceding case; and to prove that the square on the diameter of the sphere is double of the square on the side of the octahedron.*

Let the diameter <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> of the given sphere be set out, and let it be bisected at <span data-ref="0478.g1:C">$C$</span>;
let the semicircle <span data-ref="0478.g1:( A D B )">$ADB$</span> be described on <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>,
let <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> be drawn from <span data-ref="0478.g1:C">$C$</span> at right angles to <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>,
let <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> be joined;
let the square <span data-ref="0478.g1:< E F G H >">$EFGH$</span>, having each of its sides equal to <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span>, be set out,
let <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ H F /">$HF$</span>, <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ E G /">$EG$</span> be joined,
from the point <span data-ref="0478.g1:K">$K$</span> let the straight line <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ K L /">$KL$</span> be set up at right angles to the plane of the square <span data-ref="0478.g1:< E F G H >">$EFGH$</span> [XI. 12], and let it be carried through to the other side of the plane, as <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ K M /">$KM$</span>;
from the straight lines <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ K L /">$KL$</span>, <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ K M /">$KM$</span> let <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ K L /">$KL$</span>, <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ K M /">$KM$</span> be respectively cut off equal to one of the straight lines <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ E K /">$EK$</span>, <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ F K /">$FK$</span>, <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ G K /">$GK$</span>, <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ H K /">$HK$</span>,
and let <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ L E /">$LE$</span>, <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ L F /">$LF$</span>, <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ L G /">$LG$</span>, <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ L H /">$LH$</span>, <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ M E /">$ME$</span>, <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ M F /">$MF$</span>, <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ M G /">$MG$</span>, <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ M H /">$MH$</span> be joined.

![Proposition 14 Diagram](0478.g1.svg)

Then, since <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ K E /">$KE$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ K H /">$KH$</span>,
and the angle <span data-ref="0478.g1:< E K H )">$EKH$</span> is right,
therefore the square on <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ H E /">$HE$</span> is double of the square on <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ E K /">$EK$</span>.
<span style="float: right;">[I. 47]</span>

Again, since <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ L K /">$LK$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ K E /">$KE$</span>,
and the angle <span data-ref="0478.g1:< L K E )">$LKE$</span> is right,
therefore the square on <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ E L /">$EL$</span> is double of the square on <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ E K /">$EK$</span>.
