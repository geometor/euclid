But the square on <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ H E /">$HE$</span> was also proved double of the square on <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ E K /">$EK$</span>;
therefore the square on <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ L E /">$LE$</span> is equal to the square on <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span>;
therefore <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ L E /">$LE$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ E H /">$EH$</span>.

For the same reason
<span data-ref="0478.g1:/ L H /">$LH$</span> is also equal to <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ H E /">$HE$</span>;
therefore the triangle <span data-ref="0478.g1:< L E H >">$LEH$</span> is equilateral.

Similarly we can prove that each of the remaining triangles of which the sides of the square <span data-ref="0478.g1:< E F G H >">$EFGH$</span> are the bases, and the points <span data-ref="0478.g1:L">$L$</span>, <span data-ref="0478.g1:M">$M$</span> the vertices, is equilateral;
therefore an octahedron has been constructed which is contained by eight equilateral triangles.

It is next required to comprehend it in the given sphere, and to prove that the square on the diameter of the sphere is double of the square on the side of the octahedron.

For, since the three straight lines <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ L K /">$LK$</span>, <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ K M /">$KM$</span>, <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ K E /">$KE$</span> are equal to one another,
therefore the semicircle described on <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ L M /">$LM$</span> will also pass through <span data-ref="0478.g1:E">$E$</span>.

And for the same reason,
if, <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ L M /">$LM$</span> remaining fixed, the semicircle be carried round and restored to the same position from which it began to be moved,
it will also pass through the points <span data-ref="0478.g1:F">$F$</span>, <span data-ref="0478.g1:G">$G$</span>, <span data-ref="0478.g1:H">$H$</span>,
and the octahedron will have been comprehended in a sphere.

I say next that it is also comprehended in the given sphere.

For, since <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ L K /">$LK$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ K M /">$KM$</span>,
while <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ K E /">$KE$</span> is common,
and they contain right angles,
therefore the base <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ L E /">$LE$</span> is equal to the base <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ E M /">$EM$</span>.
<span style="float: right;">[I. 4]</span>

And, since the angle <span data-ref="0478.g1:< L E M )">$LEM$</span> is right, for it is in a semicircle,
<span style="float: right;">[III. 31]</span>
therefore the square on <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ L M /">$LM$</span> is double of the square on <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ L E /">$LE$</span>.
<span style="float: right;">[I. 47]</span>

Again, since <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span>,
<span data-ref="0478.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is double of <span data-ref="0478.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>.
