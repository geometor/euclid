(4)
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{\it AB}^2 &= 2\text{\it BD}^2 \\
&= 2\text{\it EF}^2.
\end{aligned}$$

If $r$ be the radius of the circumscribed sphere,
$$\text{(edge of octahedron)} = \sqrt{2}\cdot r.$$

#### Pappus' method.

Pappus (III. pp. 148—150) finds the two equal and parallel sections of the<br />
sphere which circumscribe two opposite faces of the octahedron thus.

*Analysis.*

Suppose the octahedron inscribed, <span data-ref="0481.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0481.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0481.g1:C">$C$</span>; <span data-ref="0481.g1:D">$D$</span>, <span data-ref="0481.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0481.g1:F">$F$</span> being the vertices.

Through <span data-ref="0481.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span>, <span data-ref="0481.g1:< D E F >">$DEF$</span> describe planes<br />
making the circular sections <span data-ref="0481.g1:( A B C )">$ABC$</span>, <span data-ref="0481.g1:( D E F )">$DEF$</span>.

Since the straight lines <span data-ref="0481.g1:/ D A /">$DA$</span>, <span data-ref="0481.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span>, <span data-ref="0481.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span>, <span data-ref="0481.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span><br />
are equal, the points <span data-ref="0481.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0481.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0481.g1:F">$F$</span>, <span data-ref="0481.g1:B">$B$</span> lie on a circle<br />
of which <span data-ref="0481.g1:D">$D$</span> is the pole.

![Pappus' construction of regular octahedron in a sphere](0481.g1.svg)

Again, since <span data-ref="0481.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0481.g1:/ B F /">$BF$</span>, <span data-ref="0481.g1:/ F E /">$FE$</span>, <span data-ref="0481.g1:/ E A /">$EA$</span> are equal,<br />
<span data-ref="0481.g1:< A B F E >">$ABFE$</span> is a square inscribed in the said circle,<br />
and <span data-ref="0481.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0481.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> are parallel.

Similarly <span data-ref="0481.g1:/ D E /">$DE$</span> is parallel to <span data-ref="0481.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, and <span data-ref="0481.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span><br />
to <span data-ref="0481.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>.

Therefore the circles through <span data-ref="0481.g1:D">$D$</span>, <span data-ref="0481.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0481.g1:F">$F$</span> and<br />
<span data-ref="0481.g1:A">$A$</span>, <span data-ref="0481.g1:B">$B$</span>, <span data-ref="0481.g1:C">$C$</span> are parallel; and they are also equal<br />
because the equilateral triangles inscribed in<br />
them are equal.

Now, <span data-ref="0481.g1:( A B C )">$ABC$</span>, <span data-ref="0481.g1:( D E F )">$DEF$</span> being equal and parallel circular sections, and <span data-ref="0481.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0481.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span><br />
equal and parallel chords "not on the same side of the centres,"<br />
<span data-ref="0481.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span> is a diameter of the sphere.<br />
[Pappus has a lemma for this, pp. 136—138].

And <span data-ref="0481.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> $=$ <span data-ref="0481.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, so that $\text{\it AF}^2 = 2\text{\it FE}^2$.

But, if $d'$ be the diameter of the circle <span data-ref="0481.g1:( D E F )">$DEF$</span>,
$$d'^2 = \frac{4}{3}\text{\it EF}^2. \qquad \text{[cf. XIII. 12]}$$

Therefore, if $d$ be the diameter of the sphere,
$$d^2 : d'^2 = 3 : 2.$$

Now $d$ is given, and therefore $d'$ is given; hence the circles <span data-ref="0481.g1:( D E F )">$DEF$</span>, <span data-ref="0481.g1:( A B C )">$ABC$</span><br />
are given.

*Synthesis.*

Draw two equal and parallel circular sections with diameter $d'$, such that
$$d^2 = \frac{3}{2}d'^2,$$
where $d$ is the diameter of the sphere.

Inscribe an equilateral triangle <span data-ref="0481.g1:< A B C >">$ABC$</span> in either circle (<span data-ref="0481.g1:( A B C )">$ABC$</span>).

In the other circle draw <span data-ref="0481.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> equal and parallel to <span data-ref="0481.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> but on the opposite<br />
side of the centre, and complete the inscribed equilateral triangle <span data-ref="0481.g1:< D E F >">$DEF$</span>.

<span data-ref="0481.g1:< A B C D E F >">$ABCDEF$</span> is the octahedron required.

It will be observed that, whereas in the problem of XIII. 13 Euclid first<br />
finds the circle circumscribing a face and Pappus first finds an edge, in this<br />
problem Euclid finds the edge first and Pappus the circle circumscribing<br />
a face.
