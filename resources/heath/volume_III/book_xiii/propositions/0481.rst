:title: Proposition 14 Commentary (Pappus' Method)

(4)

$$\begin{aligned}
\text{\it AB}^2 &= 2\text{\it BD}^2 \\
&= 2\text{\it EF}^2.
\end{aligned}$$

If $r$ be the radius of the circumscribed sphere,

$$\text{(edge of octahedron)} = \sqrt{2}\cdot r.$$

Pappus' method.
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

| Pappus (III. pp. 148—150) finds the two equal and parallel sections of the
| sphere which circumscribe two opposite faces of the octahedron thus.

*Analysis.*

Suppose the octahedron inscribed, :g:`$A$ <0481.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0481.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0481.g1:C>`; :g:`$D$ <0481.g1:D>`, :g:`$E$ <0481.g1:E>`, :g:`$F$ <0481.g1:F>` being the vertices.

| Through :g:`$ABC$ <0481.g1:< A B C >>`, :g:`$DEF$ <0481.g1:< D E F >>` describe planes
| making the circular sections :g:`$ABC$ <0481.g1:( A B C )>`, :g:`$DEF$ <0481.g1:( D E F )>`.

| Since the straight lines :g:`$DA$ <0481.g1:/ D A />`, :g:`$DB$ <0481.g1:/ D B />`, :g:`$DE$ <0481.g1:/ D E />`, :g:`$DF$ <0481.g1:/ D F />`
| are equal, the points :g:`$A$ <0481.g1:A>`, :g:`$E$ <0481.g1:E>`, :g:`$F$ <0481.g1:F>`, :g:`$B$ <0481.g1:B>` lie on a circle
| of which :g:`$D$ <0481.g1:D>` is the pole.

.. picture:: 0481.g1.svg
   :alt: Pappus' construction of regular octahedron in a sphere

| Again, since :g:`$AB$ <0481.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$BF$ <0481.g1:/ B F />`, :g:`$FE$ <0481.g1:/ F E />`, :g:`$EA$ <0481.g1:/ E A />` are equal,
| :g:`$ABFE$ <0481.g1:< A B F E >>` is a square inscribed in the said circle,
| and :g:`$AB$ <0481.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$EF$ <0481.g1:/ E F />` are parallel.

| Similarly :g:`$DE$ <0481.g1:/ D E />` is parallel to :g:`$BC$ <0481.g1:/ B C />`, and :g:`$DF$ <0481.g1:/ D F />`
| to :g:`$AC$ <0481.g1:/ A C />`.

| Therefore the circles through :g:`$D$ <0481.g1:D>`, :g:`$E$ <0481.g1:E>`, :g:`$F$ <0481.g1:F>` and
| :g:`$A$ <0481.g1:A>`, :g:`$B$ <0481.g1:B>`, :g:`$C$ <0481.g1:C>` are parallel; and they are also equal
| because the equilateral triangles inscribed in
| them are equal.

| Now, :g:`$ABC$ <0481.g1:( A B C )>`, :g:`$DEF$ <0481.g1:( D E F )>` being equal and parallel circular sections, and :g:`$AB$ <0481.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`$EF$ <0481.g1:/ E F />`
| equal and parallel chords "not on the same side of the centres,"
| :g:`$AF$ <0481.g1:/ A F />` is a diameter of the sphere.
| [Pappus has a lemma for this, pp. 136—138].

And :g:`$AE$ <0481.g1:/ A E />` $=$ :g:`$EF$ <0481.g1:/ E F />`, so that $\text{\it AF}^2 = 2\text{\it FE}^2$.

But, if $d'$ be the diameter of the circle :g:`$DEF$ <0481.g1:( D E F )>`,

$$d'^2 = \frac{4}{3}\text{\it EF}^2. \qquad \text{[cf. XIII. 12]}$$

Therefore, if $d$ be the diameter of the sphere,

$$d^2 : d'^2 = 3 : 2.$$

| Now $d$ is given, and therefore $d'$ is given; hence the circles :g:`$DEF$ <0481.g1:( D E F )>`, :g:`$ABC$ <0481.g1:( A B C )>`
| are given.

*Synthesis.*

Draw two equal and parallel circular sections with diameter $d'$, such that

$$d^2 = \frac{3}{2}d'^2,$$

where $d$ is the diameter of the sphere.

Inscribe an equilateral triangle :g:`$ABC$ <0481.g1:< A B C >>` in either circle (:g:`$ABC$ <0481.g1:( A B C )>`).

| In the other circle draw :g:`$EF$ <0481.g1:/ E F />` equal and parallel to :g:`$AB$ <0481.g1:/ A B />` but on the opposite
| side of the centre, and complete the inscribed equilateral triangle :g:`$DEF$ <0481.g1:< D E F >>`.

:g:`$ABCDEF$ <0481.g1:< A B C D E F >>` is the octahedron required.

| It will be observed that, whereas in the problem of XIII. 13 Euclid first
| finds the circle circumscribing a face and Pappus first finds an edge, in this
| problem Euclid finds the edge first and Pappus the circle circumscribing
| a face.
