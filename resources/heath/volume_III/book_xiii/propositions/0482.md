### PROPOSITION 15.

<p class="enunciation">
To construct a cube and comprehend it in a sphere, like the<br />
pyramid; and to prove that the square on the diameter of the<br />
sphere is triple of the square on the side of the cube.
</p>

Let the diameter <span data-ref="0482.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> of the given sphere be set out,<br />
and let it be cut at <span data-ref="0482.g1:C">$C$</span> so that <span data-ref="0482.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is double of <span data-ref="0482.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span>;<br />
let the semicircle <span data-ref="0482.g1:( A D B )">$ADB$</span> be described on <span data-ref="0482.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>,<br />
let <span data-ref="0482.g1:/ C D /">$CD$</span> be drawn from <span data-ref="0482.g1:C">$C$</span> at right angles to <span data-ref="0482.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>,<br />
and let <span data-ref="0482.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> be joined;<br />
let the square <span data-ref="0482.g1:< E F G H >">$EFGH$</span> having its side equal to <span data-ref="0482.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> be set out,<br />
from <span data-ref="0482.g1:E">$E$</span>, <span data-ref="0482.g1:F">$F$</span>, <span data-ref="0482.g1:G">$G$</span>, <span data-ref="0482.g1:H">$H$</span> let <span data-ref="0482.g1:/ E K /">$EK$</span>, <span data-ref="0482.g1:/ F L /">$FL$</span>, <span data-ref="0482.g1:/ G M /">$GM$</span>, <span data-ref="0482.g1:/ H N /">$HN$</span> be drawn at right<br />
angles to the plane of the square <span data-ref="0482.g1:< E F G H >">$EFGH$</span>,<br />
from <span data-ref="0482.g1:/ E K /">$EK$</span>, <span data-ref="0482.g1:/ F L /">$FL$</span>, <span data-ref="0482.g1:/ G M /">$GM$</span>, <span data-ref="0482.g1:/ H N /">$HN$</span> let <span data-ref="0482.g1:/ E K /">$EK$</span>, <span data-ref="0482.g1:/ F L /">$FL$</span>, <span data-ref="0482.g1:/ G M /">$GM$</span>, <span data-ref="0482.g1:/ H N /">$HN$</span> respectively<br />
be cut off equal to one of the straight lines <span data-ref="0482.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, <span data-ref="0482.g1:/ F G /">$FG$</span>,<br />
<span data-ref="0482.g1:/ G H /">$GH$</span>, <span data-ref="0482.g1:/ H E /">$HE$</span>,<br />
and let <span data-ref="0482.g1:/ K L /">$KL$</span>, <span data-ref="0482.g1:/ L M /">$LM$</span>, <span data-ref="0482.g1:/ M N /">$MN$</span>, <span data-ref="0482.g1:/ N K /">$NK$</span> be joined;<br />
therefore the cube <span data-ref="0482.g1:< F N >">$FN$</span> has been constructed which is contained<br />
by six equal squares.

<p class="sub-clause">
It is then required to comprehend it in the given sphere,<br />
and to prove that the square on the diameter of the sphere is<br />
triple of the square on the side of the cube.
</p>

For let <span data-ref="0482.g1:/ K G /">$KG$</span>, <span data-ref="0482.g1:/ E G /">$EG$</span> be joined.

Then, since the angle <span data-ref="0482.g1:< K E G )">$KEG$</span> is right, because <span data-ref="0482.g1:/ K E /">$KE$</span> is also<br />
at right angles to the plane <span data-ref="0482.g1:< E F G H >">$EG$</span> and of course to the straight<br />
line <span data-ref="0482.g1:/ E G /">$EG$</span> also, <span class="justification">[XI. Def. 3]</span><br />
therefore the semicircle described on <span data-ref="0482.g1:/ K G /">$KG$</span> will also pass through<br />
the point <span data-ref="0482.g1:E">$E$</span>.

Again, since <span data-ref="0482.g1:/ G F /">$GF$</span> is at right angles to each of the straight<br />
lines <span data-ref="0482.g1:/ F L /">$FL$</span>, <span data-ref="0482.g1:/ F E /">$FE$</span>,<br />
<span data-ref="0482.g1:/ G F /">$GF$</span> is also at right angles to the plane <span data-ref="0482.g1:< E F L K >">$FK$</span>;<br />
hence also, if we join <span data-ref="0482.g1:/ F K /">$FK$</span>, <span data-ref="0482.g1:/ G F /">$GF$</span> will be at right angles to <span data-ref="0482.g1:/ F K /">$FK$</span>;
