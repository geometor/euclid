And, since each of the straight lines <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ Q L /">$QL$</span>, <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ Q P /">$QP$</span> was proved to belong to a pentagon,
and <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ L P /">$LP$</span> also belongs to a pentagon,
therefore the triangle <span data-ref="0485.g1:< Q L P >">$QLP$</span> is equilateral.

For the same reason
each of the triangles <span data-ref="0485.g1:< L R M >">$LRM$</span>, <span data-ref="0485.g1:< M S N >">$MSN$</span>, <span data-ref="0485.g1:< N T O >">$NTO$</span>, <span data-ref="0485.g1:< O U P >">$OUP$</span> is also equilateral.

Let the centre of the circle <span data-ref="0485.g1:( E F G H K )">$EFGHK$</span>, the point <span data-ref="0485.g1:V">$V$</span>, be taken;
from <span data-ref="0485.g1:V">$V$</span> let <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ V Z /">$VZ$</span> be set up at right angles to the plane of the circle,
let it be produced in the other direction, as <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ V X /">$VX$</span>,
let there be cut off <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ V W /">$VW$</span>, the side of a hexagon, and each of the straight lines <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ V X /">$VX$</span>, <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ W Z /">$WZ$</span>, being sides of a decagon,
and let <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ Q Z /">$QZ$</span>, <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ Q W /">$QW$</span>, <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ U Z /">$UZ$</span>, <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ E V /">$EV$</span>, <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ L V /">$LV$</span>, <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ L X /">$LX$</span>, <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ X M /">$XM$</span> be joined.

Now, since each of the straight lines <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ V W /">$VW$</span>, <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ Q E /">$QE$</span> is at right angles to the plane of the circle,
therefore <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ V W /">$VW$</span> is parallel to <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ Q E /">$QE$</span>. [XI. 6]

But they are also equal;
therefore <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ E V /">$EV$</span>, <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ Q W /">$QW$</span> are also equal and parallel. [I. 33]

But <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ E V /">$EV$</span> belongs to a hexagon;
therefore <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ Q W /">$QW$</span> also belongs to a hexagon.

And, since <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ Q W /">$QW$</span> belongs to a hexagon,
and <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ W Z /">$WZ$</span> to a decagon,
and the angle <span data-ref="0485.g1:< Q W Z )">$QWZ$</span> is right,
therefore <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ Q Z /">$QZ$</span> belongs to a pentagon. [XIII. 10]

For the same reason
<span data-ref="0485.g1:/ U Z /">$UZ$</span> also belongs to a pentagon,
inasmuch as, if we join <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ V K /">$VK$</span>, <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ W U /">$WU$</span>, they will be equal and opposite, and <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ V K /">$VK$</span>, being a radius, belongs to a hexagon; [IV. 15, Por.]
therefore <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ W U /">$WU$</span> also belongs to a hexagon.

But <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ W Z /">$WZ$</span> belongs to a decagon,
and the angle <span data-ref="0485.g1:< U W Z )">$UWZ$</span> is right;
therefore <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ U Z /">$UZ$</span> belongs to a pentagon. [XIII. 10]

But <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ Q U /">$QU$</span> also belongs to a pentagon;
therefore the triangle <span data-ref="0485.g1:< Q U Z >">$QUZ$</span> is equilateral.
