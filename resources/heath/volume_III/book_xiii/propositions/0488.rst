.. subclause::

   | For the same reason
   | each of the remaining triangles of which the straight lines
   | :g:`$QR$ <0485.g1:/ Q R />`, :g:`$RS$ <0485.g1:/ R S />`, :g:`$ST$ <0485.g1:/ S T />`, :g:`$TU$ <0485.g1:/ T U />` are the bases, and the point :g:`$Z$ <0485.g1:Z>` the vertex,
   | is also equilateral.

| Again, since :g:`$VL$ <0485.g1:/ V L />` belongs to a hexagon,
| and :g:`$VX$ <0485.g1:/ V X />` to a decagon,
| and the angle :g:`$LVX$ <0485.g1:< L V X )>` is right,

.. assertion:: [XIII. 10] <XIII.10>

   therefore :g:`$LX$ <0485.g1:/ L X />` belongs to a pentagon.

.. subclause::

   | For the same reason,
   | if we join :g:`$MV$ <0485.g1:/ M V />`, which belongs to a hexagon,
   | :g:`$MX$ <0485.g1:/ M X />` is also inferred to belong to a pentagon.

.. subclause::

   But :g:`$LM$ <0485.g1:/ L M />` also belongs to a pentagon;

.. assertion::

   therefore the triangle :g:`$LMX$ <0485.g1:< L M X >>` is equilateral.

.. assertion::

   | Similarly it can be proved that each of the remaining
   | triangles of which :g:`$MN$ <0485.g1:/ M N />`, :g:`$NO$ <0485.g1:/ N O />`, :g:`$OP$ <0485.g1:/ O P />`, :g:`$PL$ <0485.g1:/ P L />` are the bases, and the
   | point :g:`$X$ <0485.g1:X>` the vertex, is also equilateral.

.. assertion::

   | Therefore an icosahedron has been constructed which is
   | contained by twenty equilateral triangles.

| It is next required to comprehend it in the given sphere,
| and to prove that the side of the icosahedron is the irrational
| straight line called minor.

| For, since :g:`$VW$ <0485.g1:/ V W />` belongs to a hexagon,
| and :g:`$WZ$ <0485.g1:/ W Z />` to a decagon,
| therefore :g:`$VZ$ <0485.g1:/ V Z />` has been cut in extreme and mean ratio at :g:`$W$ <0485.g1:W>`,
| and :g:`$VW$ <0485.g1:/ V W />` is its greater segment; :just:`[XIII. 9] <XIII.9>`

.. assertion::

   therefore, as :g:`$ZV$ <0485.g1:/ Z V />` is to :g:`$VW$ <0485.g1:/ V W />`, so is :g:`$VW$ <0485.g1:/ V W />` to :g:`$WZ$ <0485.g1:/ W Z />`.

.. subclause::

   But :g:`$VW$ <0485.g1:/ V W />` is equal to :g:`$VE$ <0485.g1:/ V E />`, and :g:`$WZ$ <0485.g1:/ W Z />` to :g:`$VX$ <0485.g1:/ V X />`;

.. assertion::

   therefore, as :g:`$ZV$ <0485.g1:/ Z V />` is to :g:`$VE$ <0485.g1:/ V E />`, so is :g:`$EV$ <0485.g1:/ E V />` to :g:`$VX$ <0485.g1:/ V X />`.

| And the angles :g:`$ZVE$ <0485.g1:< Z V E )>`, :g:`$EVX$ <0485.g1:< E V X )>` are right;
| therefore, if we join the straight line :g:`$EZ$ <0485.g1:/ E Z />`, the angle :g:`$XEZ$ <0485.g1:< X E Z )>`
| will be right because of the similarity of the triangles :g:`$XEZ$ <0485.g1:< X E Z >>`,
| :g:`$VEZ$ <0485.g1:< V E Z >>`.

.. subclause::

   | For the same reason,
   | since, as :g:`$ZV$ <0485.g1:/ Z V />` is to :g:`$VW$ <0485.g1:/ V W />`, so is :g:`$VW$ <0485.g1:/ V W />` to :g:`$WZ$ <0485.g1:/ W Z />`,
   | and :g:`$ZV$ <0485.g1:/ Z V />` is equal to :g:`$XW$ <0485.g1:/ X W />`, and :g:`$VW$ <0485.g1:/ V W />` to :g:`$WQ$ <0485.g1:/ W Q />`,

.. assertion::

   therefore, as :g:`$XW$ <0485.g1:/ X W />` is to :g:`$WQ$ <0485.g1:/ W Q />`, so is :g:`$QW$ <0485.g1:/ Q W />` to :g:`$WZ$ <0485.g1:/ W Z />`.
