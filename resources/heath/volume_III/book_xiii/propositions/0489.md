And for this reason again,  
if we join <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ Q X /">$QX$</span>, the angle at <span data-ref="0485.g1:Q">$Q$</span> will be right; [VI. 8]  
therefore the semicircle described on <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ X Z /">$XZ$</span> will also pass  
through <span data-ref="0485.g1:Q">$Q$</span>. [III. 31]  
And if, <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ X Z /">$XZ$</span> remaining fixed, the semicircle be carried  
round and restored to the same position from which it began  
to be moved, it will also pass through <span data-ref="0485.g1:Q">$Q$</span> and the remaining  
angular points of the icosahedron,  
and the icosahedron will have been comprehended in a  
sphere.

I say next that it is also comprehended in the given sphere.  
For let <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ V W /">$VW$</span> be bisected at <span data-ref="0485.g1:A'">$A'$</span>.  
Then, since the straight line <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ V Z /">$VZ$</span> has been cut in extreme  
and mean ratio at <span data-ref="0485.g1:W">$W$</span>,  
and <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ Z W /">$ZW$</span> is its lesser segment,  
therefore the square on <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ Z W /">$ZW$</span> added to the half of the greater  
segment, that is <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ W A' /">$WA'$</span>, is five times the square on the half  
of the greater segment; [XIII. 3]  
therefore the square on <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ Z A' /">$ZA'$</span> is five times the square on  
<span data-ref="0485.g1:/ A' W /">$A'W$</span>.

And <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ Z X /">$ZX$</span> is double of <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ Z A' /">$ZA'$</span>, and <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ V W /">$VW$</span> double of <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ A' W /">$A'W$</span>;  
therefore the square on <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ Z X /">$ZX$</span> is five times the square on  
<span data-ref="0485.g1:/ W V /">$WV$</span>.

And, since <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is quadruple of <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span>,  
therefore <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is five times <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>.

But, as <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, so is the square on <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> to the square  
on <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>; [VI. 8, V. Def. 9]  
therefore the square on <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is five times the square on <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ B D /">$BD$</span>.

But the square on <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ Z X /">$ZX$</span> was also proved to be five times  
the square on <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ V W /">$VW$</span>.

And <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ V W /">$VW$</span>,  
for each of them is equal to the radius of the circle <span data-ref="0485.g1:( E F G H K )">$EFGHK$</span>;  
therefore <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is also equal to <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ X Z /">$XZ$</span>.

And <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is the diameter of the given sphere;  
therefore <span data-ref="0485.g1:/ X Z /">$XZ$</span> is also equal to the diameter of the given sphere.

Therefore the icosahedron has been comprehended in the  
given sphere
