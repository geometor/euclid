<p class="assertion">I say next that the side of the icosahedron is the irrational<br />
straight line called minor.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">For, since the diameter of the sphere is rational,<br />
and the square on it is five times the square on the radius<br />
of the circle <span data-ref="0485.g1:( E F G H K )">$EFGHK$</span>,</p>

<p class="assertion">therefore the radius of the circle <span data-ref="0485.g1:( E F G H K )">$EFGHK$</span> is also rational;<br />
hence its diameter is also rational.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">But, if an equilateral pentagon be inscribed in a circle<br />
which has its diameter rational, the side of the pentagon is<br />
the irrational straight line called minor. <span class="justification">[XIII. 11]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">And the side of the pentagon <span data-ref="0485.g1:< E F G H K >">$EFGHK$</span> is the side of the<br />
icosahedron.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore the side of the icosahedron is the irrational<br />
straight line called minor.</p>

**Porism.** From this it is manifest that the square on the<br />
diameter of the sphere is five times the square on the radius<br />
of the circle from which the icosahedron has been described,<br />
and that the diameter of the sphere is composed of the side<br />
of the hexagon and two of the sides of the decagon inscribed<br />
in the same circle.

<p class="qed">Q. E. D.</p>

---

Euclid's method is<br />
(1) to find the pentagons in the two parallel circular sections of the sphere,<br />
the sides of which form ten (five in each circle) of the edges of the icosahedron,<br />
(2) to find the two points which are the poles of the two circular sections,<br />
(3) to prove that the triangles formed by joining the angular points of the<br />
pentagons which are nearest to one another two and two are equilateral,<br />
(4) to prove that the triangles of which the poles are the vertices and the<br />
sides of the pentagons the bases are also equilateral,<br />
(5) that all the angular points other than the poles lie on a sphere the<br />
diameter of which is the straight line joining the poles,<br />
(6) that this sphere is of the same size as the given sphere,<br />
(7) that, if the diameter of the sphere is rational, the edge of the icosahedron<br />
is the *minor* irrational straight line.
