(3) Taking two adjacent perpendiculars, <span data-ref="0491.g1:/ E Q /">$EQ$</span>, <span data-ref="0491.g1:/ K U /">$KU$</span>, to the plane of the circle<br />
<span data-ref="0491.g1:( E F G H K )">$EFGHK$</span>, we see that they are parallel as well as equal;<br />
therefore <span data-ref="0491.g1:/ Q U /">$QU$</span>, <span data-ref="0491.g1:/ E K /">$EK$</span> are equal and parallel.

Similarly for <span data-ref="0491.g1:/ Q R /">$QR$</span>, <span data-ref="0491.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> etc.

Thus the pentagons have their sides equal.

To prove that the triangles <span data-ref="0491.g1:< Q P L >">$QPL$</span> etc., are equilateral, we have, e.g.<br />
<span data-ref="0491.g1:/ Q L /">$QL$</span>$^2 = $ <span data-ref="0491.g1:/ L E /">$LE$</span>$^2 + $ <span data-ref="0491.g1:/ E Q /">$EQ$</span>$^2$<br />
$$= (\text{side of decagon})^2 + (\text{side of hexagon})^2$$
$$= (\text{side of pentagon})^2, \qquad \text{[XIII. 10]}$$
i.e.<br />
<span data-ref="0491.g1:/ Q L /">$QL$</span> $= (\text{side of pentagon in circle})$<br />
$= $ <span data-ref="0491.g1:/ L P /">$LP$</span>.<br />
Similarly<br />
<span data-ref="0491.g1:/ Q P /">$QP$</span> $= $ <span data-ref="0491.g1:/ L P /">$LP$</span>,<br />
and $\triangle$ <span data-ref="0491.g1:< Q P L >">$QPL$</span> is equilateral.

So for the other triangles between the two pentagons.

(4) Since <span data-ref="0491.g1:/ V W /">$VW$</span>, <span data-ref="0491.g1:/ E Q /">$EQ$</span> are equal and parallel,<br />
<span data-ref="0491.g1:/ V E /">$VE$</span>, <span data-ref="0491.g1:/ W Q /">$WQ$</span> are equal and parallel.<br />
Thus <span data-ref="0491.g1:/ W Q /">$WQ$</span> is equal to the side of a regular hexagon in the circles.

Now the angle <span data-ref="0491.g1:< Z W Q )">$ZWQ$</span> is right;<br />
therefore<br />
<span data-ref="0491.g1:/ Z Q /">$ZQ$</span>$^2 = $ <span data-ref="0491.g1:/ Z W /">$ZW$</span>$^2 + $ <span data-ref="0491.g1:/ W Q /">$WQ$</span>$^2$<br />
$$= (\text{side of decagon})^2 + (\text{side of hexagon})^2$$
$$= (\text{side of pentagon})^2. \qquad \text{[XIII. 10]}$$

Thus <span data-ref="0491.g1:/ Z Q /">$ZQ$</span>, <span data-ref="0491.g1:/ Z R /">$ZR$</span>, <span data-ref="0491.g1:/ Z S /">$ZS$</span>, <span data-ref="0491.g1:/ Z T /">$ZT$</span>, <span data-ref="0491.g1:/ Z U /">$ZU$</span> are all equal to <span data-ref="0491.g1:/ Q R /">$QR$</span>, <span data-ref="0491.g1:/ R S /">$RS$</span> etc. ; and the<br />
triangles with <span data-ref="0491.g1:Z">$Z$</span> as vertex and bases <span data-ref="0491.g1:/ Q R /">$QR$</span>, <span data-ref="0491.g1:/ R S /">$RS$</span> etc. are equilateral.

Similarly for the triangles with <span data-ref="0491.g1:X">$X$</span> as vertex and <span data-ref="0491.g1:/ L M /">$LM$</span>, <span data-ref="0491.g1:/ M N /">$MN$</span> etc. as bases.

Hence the figure is an icosahedron, being contained by twenty equal<br />
equilateral triangles.

(5) *To prove that all the vertices of the icosahedron lie on the sphere<br />
which has* <span data-ref="0491.g1:/ X Z /">$XZ$</span> *for diameter.*

<span data-ref="0491.g1:/ V W /">$VW$</span> being equal to the side of a regular hexagon, and <span data-ref="0491.g1:/ W Z /">$WZ$</span> to the side of<br />
a regular decagon inscribed in the same circle,<br />
<span data-ref="0491.g1:/ V Z /">$VZ$</span> is divided at <span data-ref="0491.g1:W">$W$</span> in extreme and mean ratio. <span class="justification">[XIII. 9]</span>

Therefore<br />
<span data-ref="0491.g1:/ Z V /">$ZV$</span> $:$ <span data-ref="0491.g1:/ V W /">$VW$</span> $= $ <span data-ref="0491.g1:/ V W /">$VW$</span> $:$ <span data-ref="0491.g1:/ W Z /">$WZ$</span>,<br />
or, since <span data-ref="0491.g1:/ V W /">$VW$</span> $= $ <span data-ref="0491.g1:/ V E /">$VE$</span>, <span data-ref="0491.g1:/ W Z /">$WZ$</span> $= $ <span data-ref="0491.g1:/ V X /">$VX$</span>,<br />
<span data-ref="0491.g1:/ Z V /">$ZV$</span> $:$ <span data-ref="0491.g1:/ V E /">$VE$</span> $= $ <span data-ref="0491.g1:/ V E /">$VE$</span> $:$ <span data-ref="0491.g1:/ V X /">$VX$</span>.

Thus <span data-ref="0491.g1:E">$E$</span> lies on the semicircle on <span data-ref="0491.g1:/ Z X /">$ZX$</span> as diameter. <span class="justification">[VI. 8]</span>

Similarly for all the other vertices of the icosahedron.

Hence the sphere with diameter <span data-ref="0491.g1:/ X Z /">$XZ$</span> circumscribes it.
