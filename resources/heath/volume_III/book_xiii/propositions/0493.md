**Pappus' solution.**<br />
This solution (Pappus, III. pp. 150—6) differs considerably from that of<br />
Euclid. Whereas Euclid uses *two* circular sections of the sphere (those<br />
circumscribing the pentagons of his construction), Pappus finds *four* parallel<br />
circular sections each passing through three of the vertices of the icosahedron;<br />
two of the circles are small circles circumscribing two opposite triangular<br />
faces respectively, and the other two circles are between these two circles,<br />
parallel to them and equal to one another.
