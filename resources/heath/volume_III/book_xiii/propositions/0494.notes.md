## Textual, Lexical, and Mathematical Observations
- **Pappus' Lemmata**: Heath cites Pappus' preparatory lemmas from Book III (pp. 136–138 of Hultsch's edition):
  - When two equal and parallel chords lie on opposite sides of the centers of two equal and parallel circles of a sphere, the line joining opposite endpoints ($FK$) is a diameter of the sphere.
  - Consequently, $\triangle FEK$ is inscribed in a great circle semicircle, so $\angle FEK = 90^\circ$, giving $EF^2 + EK^2 = FK^2 = d^2$.
- **Golden Ratio Linkage**: In regular pentagon $GEACF$, diagonal $EF$ and side $AC$ satisfy the golden ratio property (XIII. 8): the greater segment of $EF$ is $AC$. By XIII. 9, $EF$ and $AC$ are as the sides of the hexagon and decagon in a circle whose pentagon side is $\sqrt{EF^2 + AC^2} = d = FK$ (by XIII. 10).
- **Radii of Sections**: Since equilateral triangles of sides $EF$ and $AC$ are inscribed in circles of radii $r$ and $r'$, we have $EF = r\sqrt{3}$ and $AC = r'\sqrt{3}$, hence $r^2 = \frac{1}{3}EF^2$ and $r'^2 = \frac{1}{3}AC^2$.
