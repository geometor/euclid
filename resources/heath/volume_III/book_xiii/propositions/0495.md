*Synthesis.*  
If $d$ be the diameter of the sphere, set out two straight lines <span data-ref="0494.g1:/ x /">$x$</span>, <span data-ref="0494.g1:/ y /">$y$</span>, such that $d$, <span data-ref="0494.g1:/ x /">$x$</span>, <span data-ref="0494.g1:/ y /">$y$</span> are in the ratio of the sides of the pentagon, hexagon and decagon respectively inscribed in one and the same circle.  
Draw (1) two equal and parallel circular sections in the sphere, with radii equal to $r$, where $r^2 = \frac{1}{3}$<span data-ref="0494.g1:/ x /">$x$</span>$^2$, as <span data-ref="0494.g1:( D E F )">$DEF$</span>, <span data-ref="0494.g1:( K G H )">$KGH$</span>,  
and (2) two equal and parallel circular sections as <span data-ref="0494.g1:( A B C )">$ABC$</span>, <span data-ref="0494.g1:( L M N )">$LMN$</span>, with radius $r'$ such that $r'^2 = \frac{1}{3}$<span data-ref="0494.g1:/ y /">$y$</span>$^2$.  
In the circles (1) draw <span data-ref="0494.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, <span data-ref="0494.g1:/ K H /">$KH$</span> as sides of inscribed equilateral triangles, parallel to one another, and on opposite sides of the centres;  
and in the circles (2) draw <span data-ref="0494.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0494.g1:/ L M /">$LM$</span> as sides of inscribed equilateral triangles parallel to one another and to <span data-ref="0494.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span>, <span data-ref="0494.g1:/ K H /">$KH$</span>, and so that <span data-ref="0494.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, <span data-ref="0494.g1:/ E F /">$EF$</span> are on opposite sides of the centres, and likewise <span data-ref="0494.g1:/ K H /">$KH$</span>, <span data-ref="0494.g1:/ L M /">$LM$</span>.  
Complete the figure.  
The correctness of the construction is proved as in the analysis.  
It follows also (says Pappus) that
$(\text{diam. of sphere})^2 = 3 \, (\text{side of pentagon in }$ <span data-ref="0494.g1:( D E F )">$DEF$</span>$)^2$.
For, by construction,
<span data-ref="0494.g1:/ K F /">$KF$</span> $:$ <span data-ref="0494.g1:/ F E /">$FE$</span> $= p : h$,
where $p, h$ are the sides of the pentagon and hexagon inscribed in the same circle <span data-ref="0494.g1:( D E F )">$DEF$</span>.  
And <span data-ref="0494.g1:/ F E /">$FE$</span> $: h =$ the ratio of the side of an equilateral triangle to that of a hexagon inscribed in the same circle;  
that is,
<span data-ref="0494.g1:/ F E /">$FE$</span> $: h = \sqrt{3} : 1$,
whence
<span data-ref="0494.g1:/ K F /">$KF$</span> $: p = \sqrt{3} : 1$,
or
<span data-ref="0494.g1:/ K F /">$KF$</span>$^2 = 3p^2$.

**Another construction.**  
Mr H. M. Taylor has a neat construction for an icosahedron of edge $a$.  
Let $l$ be the length of the diagonal of a regular pentagon with side equal to $a$.  
Then (figure of XIII. 8), by Ptolemy's theorem,
$$l^2 = la + a^2.$$
Construct a cube with edge equal to $l$.  
Let <span data-ref="0496.g1:O">$O$</span> be the centre of the cube.  
From <span data-ref="0496.g1:O">$O$</span> draw <span data-ref="0496.g1:/ O L /">$OL$</span>, <span data-ref="0496.g1:/ O M /">$OM$</span>, <span data-ref="0496.g1:/ O N /">$ON$</span> perpendicular to three adjacent faces, and in these draw <span data-ref="0496.g1:/ P P' /">$PP'$</span>, <span data-ref="0496.g1:/ Q Q' /">$QQ'$</span>, <span data-ref="0496.g1:/ R R' /">$RR'$</span> parallel to <span data-ref="0496.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0496.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0496.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> respectively.  
Make <span data-ref="0496.g1:/ L P /">$LP$</span>, <span data-ref="0496.g1:/ L P' /">$LP'$</span>, <span data-ref="0496.g1:/ M Q /">$MQ$</span>, <span data-ref="0496.g1:/ M Q' /">$MQ'$</span>, <span data-ref="0496.g1:/ N R /">$NR$</span>, <span data-ref="0496.g1:/ N R' /">$NR'$</span> all equal to $\frac{1}{2}a$.  
Let <span data-ref="0496.g1:p">$\bar{p}$</span>, <span data-ref="0496.g1:p'">$\bar{p}'$</span>, <span data-ref="0496.g1:q">$\bar{q}$</span>, <span data-ref="0496.g1:q'">$\bar{q}'$</span>, <span data-ref="0496.g1:r">$\bar{r}$</span>, <span data-ref="0496.g1:r'">$\bar{r}'$</span> be the reflexes of <span data-ref="0496.g1:P">$P$</span>, <span data-ref="0496.g1:P'">$P'$</span>, <span data-ref="0496.g1:Q">$Q$</span>, <span data-ref="0496.g1:Q'">$Q'$</span>, <span data-ref="0496.g1:R">$R$</span>, <span data-ref="0496.g1:R'">$R'$</span> respectively.  
Then will <span data-ref="0496.g1:P">$P$</span>, <span data-ref="0496.g1:P'">$P'$</span>, <span data-ref="0496.g1:Q">$Q$</span>, <span data-ref="0496.g1:Q'">$Q'$</span>, <span data-ref="0496.g1:R">$R$</span>, <span data-ref="0496.g1:R'">$R'$</span>, <span data-ref="0496.g1:p">$\bar{p}$</span>, <span data-ref="0496.g1:p'">$\bar{p}'$</span>, <span data-ref="0496.g1:q">$\bar{q}$</span>, <span data-ref="0496.g1:q'">$\bar{q}'$</span>, <span data-ref="0496.g1:r">$\bar{r}$</span>, <span data-ref="0496.g1:r'">$\bar{r}'$</span> be the vertices of a regular icosahedron.  
The projections of <span data-ref="0496.g1:/ P Q /">$PQ$</span> on <span data-ref="0496.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, <span data-ref="0496.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, <span data-ref="0496.g1:/ A E /">$AE$</span> are equal to $\frac{1}{2}(l - a), \frac{1}{2}a, \frac{1}{2}l$ respectively.  
Therefore
<span data-ref="0496.g1:/ P Q /">$PQ$</span>$^2 = \frac{1}{4}(l - a)^2 + \frac{1}{4}a^2 + \frac{1}{4}l^2$
$$= \frac{1}{2}(l^2 - al + a^2)$$
$$= a^2.$$
