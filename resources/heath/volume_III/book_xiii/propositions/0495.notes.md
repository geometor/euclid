## Textual, Lexical, and Mathematical Observations
- **Pappus' Relation**: Pappus shows $(\text{diam. of sphere})^2 = 3 p^2$, where $p$ is the side of the pentagon inscribed in circle $DEF$. Since $FE = h\sqrt{3}$, the ratio $KF : p = \sqrt{3} : 1$, leading directly to $KF^2 = 3p^2$.
- **H. M. Taylor's Construction**:
  - H. M. Taylor (author of the Cambridge *Euclid's Elements*) provided this famous Cartesian coordinate / golden rectangle construction of the icosahedron.
  - In a cube of edge $l$, with $l^2 = la + a^2$ (i.e., $l = a \phi$, the golden ratio), three mutually perpendicular golden rectangles of dimensions $l \times a$ are centered at $O$ parallel to the cube's faces.
  - The 12 vertices are given by $P, P', Q, Q', R, R'$ and their central reflexes $\bar{p}, \bar{p}', \bar{q}, \bar{q}', \bar{r}, \bar{r}'$ (distinguished in the text with macrons/bars).
  - The distance between adjacent vertices is computed via 3D coordinate projections:
    $$\Delta x = \frac{1}{2}(l - a), \quad \Delta y = \frac{1}{2}a, \quad \Delta z = \frac{1}{2}l$$
    $$PQ^2 = \frac{1}{4}[(l - a)^2 + a^2 + l^2] = \frac{1}{2}(l^2 - al + a^2) = a^2 \quad (\text{since } l^2 - al = a^2).$$
