But if two triangles, as <span data-ref="0497.g1:< X P H >">$XPH$</span>, <span data-ref="0497.g1:< H T W >">$HTW$</span>, which have two sides proportional to two sides be placed together at one angle so that their corresponding sides are also parallel, the remaining straight lines will be in a straight line; [VI. 32]
therefore <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ X H /">$XH$</span> is in a straight line with <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ H W /">$HW$</span>.

But every straight line is in one plane; [XI. 1]
therefore the pentagon <span data-ref="0497.g1:< U B W C V >">$UBWCV$</span> is in one plane.

I say next that it is also equiangular.

For, since the straight line <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ N P /">$NP$</span> has been cut in extreme and mean ratio at <span data-ref="0497.g1:R">$R$</span>, and <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ P R /">$PR$</span> is the greater segment,
while <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ P R /">$PR$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ P S /">$PS$</span>,
therefore <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ N S /">$NS$</span> has also been cut in extreme and mean ratio at <span data-ref="0497.g1:P">$P$</span>,
and <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ N P /">$NP$</span> is the greater segment; [XIII. 5]
therefore the squares on <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ N S /">$NS$</span>, <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ S P /">$SP$</span> are triple of the square on <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ N P /">$NP$</span>. [XIII. 4]

But <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ N P /">$NP$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ N B /">$NB$</span>, and <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ P S /">$PS$</span> to <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ S V /">$SV$</span>;
therefore the squares on <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ N S /">$NS$</span>, <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ S V /">$SV$</span> are triple of the square on <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ N B /">$NB$</span>;
hence the squares on <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ V S /">$VS$</span>, <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ S N /">$SN$</span>, <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ N B /">$NB$</span> are quadruple of the square on <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ N B /">$NB$</span>.

But the square on <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ S B /">$SB$</span> is equal to the squares on <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ S N /">$SN$</span>, <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ N B /">$NB$</span>;
therefore the squares on <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ B S /">$BS$</span>, <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ S V /">$SV$</span>, that is, the square on <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ B V /">$BV$</span>
---for the angle <span data-ref="0497.g1:< V S B )">$VSB$</span> is right---is quadruple of the square on <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ N B /">$NB$</span>;
therefore <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ V B /">$VB$</span> is double of <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ B N /">$BN$</span>.

But <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> is also double of <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ B N /">$BN$</span>;
therefore <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ B V /">$BV$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>.

And, since the two sides <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ B U /">$BU$</span>, <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ U V /">$UV$</span> are equal to the two sides <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ B W /">$BW$</span>, <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ W C /">$WC$</span>,
and the base <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ B V /">$BV$</span> is equal to the base <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>,
therefore the angle <span data-ref="0497.g1:< B U V )">$BUV$</span> is equal to the angle <span data-ref="0497.g1:< B W C )">$BWC$</span>. [I. 8]

Similarly we can prove that the angle <span data-ref="0497.g1:< U V C )">$UVC$</span> is also equal to the angle <span data-ref="0497.g1:< B W C )">$BWC$</span>;
therefore the three angles <span data-ref="0497.g1:< B W C )">$BWC$</span>, <span data-ref="0497.g1:< B U V )">$BUV$</span>, <span data-ref="0497.g1:< U V C )">$UVC$</span> are equal to one another.
