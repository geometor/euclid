<p class="sub-clause">But if in an equilateral pentagon three angles are equal to<br />
one another, the pentagon will be equiangular, <span class="justification">[XIII. 7]</span></p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the pentagon <span data-ref="0497.g1:< B U V C W >">$BUVCW$</span> is equiangular.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">And it was also proved equilateral ;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the pentagon <span data-ref="0497.g1:< B U V C W >">$BUVCW$</span> is equilateral and equi-<br />
angular, and it is on one side <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span> of the cube.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">Therefore, if we make the same construction in the case<br />
of each of the twelve sides of the cube,</p>
<p class="assertion">a solid figure will have been constructed which is contained<br />
by twelve equilateral and equiangular pentagons, and which is<br />
called a dodecahedron.</p>

It is then required to comprehend it in the given sphere,<br />
and to prove that the side of the dodecahedron is the irrational<br />
straight line called apotome.

For let <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ X P /">$XP$</span> be produced, and let the produced straight<br />
line be <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ X Z /">$XZ$</span> ;
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ P Z /">$PZ$</span> meets the diameter of the cube, and they bisect<br />
one another,<br />
for this has been proved in the last theorem but one of the<br />
eleventh book. <span class="justification">[XI. 38]</span></p>

Let them cut at <span data-ref="0497.g1:Z">$Z$</span> ;
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0497.g1:Z">$Z$</span> is the centre of the sphere which comprehends<br />
the cube,<br />
and <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ Z P /">$ZP$</span> is half of the side of the cube.</p>

Let <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ U Z /">$UZ$</span> be joined.

<p class="sub-clause">Now, since the straight line <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ N S /">$NS$</span> has been cut in extreme<br />
and mean ratio at <span data-ref="0497.g1:P">$P$</span>,<br />
and <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ N P /">$NP$</span> is its greater segment,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the squares on <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ N S /">$NS$</span>, <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ S P /">$SP$</span> are triple of the square<br />
on <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ N P /">$NP$</span>. <span class="justification">[XIII. 4]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ N S /">$NS$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ X Z /">$XZ$</span>,<br />
inasmuch as <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ N P /">$NP$</span> is also equal to <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ P Z /">$PZ$</span>, and <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ X P /">$XP$</span> to <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ P S /">$PS$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">But further <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ P S /">$PS$</span> is also equal to <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ X U /">$XU$</span>,<br />
since it is also equal to <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ R P /">$RP$</span> ;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the squares on <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ Z X /">$ZX$</span>, <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ X U /">$XU$</span> are triple of the square<br />
on <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ N P /">$NP$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">But the square on <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ U Z /">$UZ$</span> is equal to the squares on <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ Z X /">$ZX$</span>, <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ X U /">$XU$</span> ;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the square on <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ U Z /">$UZ$</span> is triple of the square on <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ N P /">$NP$</span>.</p>
