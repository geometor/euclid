.. subclause::

   But if in an equilateral pentagon three angles are equal to one another, the pentagon will be equiangular, :just:`[XIII. 7] <XIII.7>`

.. assertion::

   therefore the pentagon :g:`$BUVCW$ <0497.g1:< B U V C W >>` is equiangular.

.. subclause::

   And it was also proved :eq:`equilateral <0497.g1:/ U B / = 0497.g1:/ V U / = 0497.g1:/ C V / = 0497.g1:/ W C / = 0497.g1:/ B W />` ;

.. assertion::

   therefore the pentagon :g:`$BUVCW$ <0497.g1:< B U V C W >>` is :eq:`equilateral <0497.g1:/ U B / = 0497.g1:/ V U / = 0497.g1:/ C V / = 0497.g1:/ W C / = 0497.g1:/ B W />` and equiangular, and it is on one side :g:`$BC$ <0497.g1:/ B C />` of the cube.

.. subclause::

   Therefore, if we make the same construction in the case of each of the twelve sides of the cube,

.. assertion::

   a solid figure will have been constructed which is contained by twelve equilateral and equiangular pentagons, and which is called a dodecahedron.

It is then required to comprehend it in the given sphere, and to prove that the side of the dodecahedron is the irrational straight line called apotome.

For let :g:`$XP$ <0497.g1:/ X P />` be produced, and let the produced straight line be :g:`$XZ$ <0497.g1:/ X Z />` ;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$PZ$ <0497.g1:/ P Z />` meets the diameter of the cube, and they bisect one another, for this has been proved in the last theorem but one of the eleventh book. :just:`[XI. 38] <XI.38>`

Let them cut at :g:`$Z$ <0497.g1:Z>` ;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`$Z$ <0497.g1:Z>` is the centre of the sphere which comprehends the cube, and :g:`$ZP$ <0497.g1:/ Z P />` is half of the side of the cube.

Let :g:`$UZ$ <0497.g1:/ U Z />` be joined.

.. subclause::

   Now, since the straight line :g:`$NS$ <0497.g1:/ N S />` has been cut in extreme and mean ratio at :g:`$P$ <0497.g1:P>`, and :g:`$NP$ <0497.g1:/ N P />` is its greater segment,

.. assertion::

   therefore the squares on :g:`$NS$ <0497.g1:/ N S />`, :g:`$SP$ <0497.g1:/ S P />` are triple of the square on :g:`$NP$ <0497.g1:/ N P />`. :just:`[XIII. 4] <XIII.4>`

.. subclause::

   But :g:`$NS$ <0497.g1:/ N S />` is :eq:`equal <0497.g1:/ N S / = 0497.g1:/ X Z />` to :g:`$XZ$ <0497.g1:/ X Z />`, inasmuch as :g:`$NP$ <0497.g1:/ N P />` is also :eq:`equal <0497.g1:/ N P / = 0497.g1:/ P Z /; 0497.g1:/ P X / = 0497.g1:/ P S />` to :g:`$PZ$ <0497.g1:/ P Z />`, and :g:`$XP$ <0497.g1:/ X P />` to :g:`$PS$ <0497.g1:/ P S />`.

.. subclause::

   But further :g:`$PS$ <0497.g1:/ P S />` is also :eq:`equal <0497.g1:/ P S / = 0497.g1:/ U X />` to :g:`$XU$ <0497.g1:/ X U />`, since it is also :eq:`equal <0497.g1:/ P S / = 0497.g1:/ R P />` to :g:`$RP$ <0497.g1:/ R P />` ;

.. assertion::

   therefore the squares on :g:`$ZX$ <0497.g1:/ Z X />`, :g:`$XU$ <0497.g1:/ X U />` are triple of the square on :g:`$NP$ <0497.g1:/ N P />`.

.. subclause::

   But the square on :g:`$UZ$ <0497.g1:/ U Z />` is equal to the squares on :g:`$ZX$ <0497.g1:/ Z X />`, :g:`$XU$ <0497.g1:/ X U />` ;

.. assertion::

   therefore the square on :g:`$UZ$ <0497.g1:/ U Z />` is triple of the square on :g:`$NP$ <0497.g1:/ N P />`.
