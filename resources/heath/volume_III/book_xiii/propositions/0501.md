<p class="sub-clause">But the square on the radius of the sphere which compre-<br />
hends the cube is also triple of the square on the half of the<br />
side of the cube,<br />
for it has previously been shown how to construct a cube and<br />
comprehend it in a sphere, and to prove that the square on<br />
the diameter of the sphere is triple of the square on the side<br />
of the cube. <span class="justification">[XIII. 15]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But, if whole is so related to whole, so is half to half also ;<br />
and <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ N P /">$NP$</span> is half of the side of the cube ;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ U Z /">$UZ$</span> is equal to the radius of the sphere which com-<br />
prehends the cube.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">And <span data-ref="0497.g1:Z">$Z$</span> is the centre of the sphere which comprehends the<br />
cube ;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the point <span data-ref="0497.g1:U">$U$</span> is on the surface of the sphere.</p>

<p class="assertion">Similarly we can prove that each of the remaining angles<br />
of the dodecahedron is also on the surface of the sphere ;<br />
therefore the dodecahedron has been comprehended in the<br />
given sphere.</p>

I say next that the side of the dodecahedron is the irrational<br />
straight line called apotome.

<p class="sub-clause">For since, when <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ N P /">$NP$</span> has been cut in extreme and mean<br />
ratio, <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ R P /">$RP$</span> is the greater segment,<br />
and, when <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ P O /">$PO$</span> has been cut in extreme and mean ratio, <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ P S /">$PS$</span><br />
is the greater segment,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore, when the whole <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ N O /">$NO$</span> is cut in extreme and mean<br />
ratio, <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ R S /">$RS$</span> is the greater segment.</p>

[Thus, since, as <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ N P /">$NP$</span> is to <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ P R /">$PR$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ P R /">$PR$</span> to <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ R N /">$RN$</span>,<br />
the same is true of the doubles also,<br />
for parts have the same ratio as their equimultiples ; <span class="justification">[v. 15]</span><br />
therefore as <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ N O /">$NO$</span> is to <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ R S /">$RS$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ R S /">$RS$</span> to the sum of <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ N R /">$NR$</span>, <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ S O /">$SO$</span>.

But <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ N O /">$NO$</span> is greater than <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ R S /">$RS$</span> ;<br />
therefore <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ R S /">$RS$</span> is also greater than the sum of <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ N R /">$NR$</span>, <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ S O /">$SO$</span> ;<br />
therefore <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ N O /">$NO$</span> has been cut in extreme and mean ratio,<br />
and <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ R S /">$RS$</span> is its greater segment.]

<p class="sub-clause">But <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ R S /">$RS$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ U V /">$UV$</span> ;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore, when <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ N O /">$NO$</span> is cut in extreme and mean ratio, <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ U V /">$UV$</span> is<br />
the greater segment.</p>
