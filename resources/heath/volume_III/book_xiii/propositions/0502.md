And, since the diameter of the sphere is rational,
and the square on it is triple of the square on the side of the cube,
therefore <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ N O /">$NO$</span>, being a side of the cube, is rational.

[But if a rational line be cut in extreme and mean ratio, each of the segments is an irrational apotome.]
Therefore <span data-ref="0497.g1:/ U V /">$UV$</span>, being a side of the dodecahedron, is an irrational apotome. [XIII. 6]

PORISM. From this it is manifest that, when the side of the cube is cut in extreme and mean ratio, the greater segment is the side of the dodecahedron.

Q. E. D.

---

In this proposition we find Euclid using two propositions which precede but are used nowhere else, notably VI. 32, which some authors, in consequence of their having overlooked its use here, have been hard put to it to explain.

Euclid's construction in this case is really identical with that given by Mr H. M. Taylor, and also referred to by Henrici and Treutlein under "crystal-formation."

Euclid starts from the cube inscribed in a sphere, as in XIII. 15, and then finds the side of the regular pentagon in which the side of the cube is a diagonal.

Mr Taylor takes $l$ to be the diagonal of a regular pentagon of side $a$, so that, by Ptolemy's theorem,
$$l^2 = al + a^2,$$
constructs a cube of which $l$ is the edge, and gets the side of the pentagon by drawing <span data-ref="0503.g1:/ Z X /">$ZX$</span> from <span data-ref="0503.g1:Z">$Z$</span>, the centre of the cube, perpendicular to the face <span data-ref="0503.g1:< B F >">$BF$</span> and equal to $\frac{1}{2}(l + a)$, then drawing <span data-ref="0503.g1:/ U V /">$UV$</span> through <span data-ref="0503.g1:X">$X$</span> parallel to <span data-ref="0503.g1:/ B C /">$BC$</span>, and making <span data-ref="0503.g1:/ U X /">$UX$</span>, <span data-ref="0503.g1:/ X V /">$XV$</span> both equal to $\frac{1}{2}a$.

Euclid finds <span data-ref="0503.g1:/ U V /">$UV$</span> thus.

Draw <span data-ref="0503.g1:/ N O /">$NO$</span>, <span data-ref="0503.g1:/ M H /">$MH$</span> bisecting pairs of opposite sides in the square <span data-ref="0503.g1:< B F >">$BF$</span> and meeting in <span data-ref="0503.g1:P">$P$</span>.

Draw <span data-ref="0503.g1:/ G K /">$GK$</span>, <span data-ref="0503.g1:/ H L /">$HL$</span> bisecting pairs of opposite sides in the square <span data-ref="0503.g1:< B D >">$BD$</span> and meeting in <span data-ref="0503.g1:Q">$Q$</span>.

Divide <span data-ref="0503.g1:/ P N /">$PN$</span>, <span data-ref="0503.g1:/ P O /">$PO$</span>, <span data-ref="0503.g1:/ Q H /">$QH$</span> respectively in extreme and mean ratio at <span data-ref="0503.g1:R">$R$</span>, <span data-ref="0503.g1:S">$S$</span>, <span data-ref="0503.g1:T">$T$</span> (<span data-ref="0503.g1:/ P R /">$PR$</span>, <span data-ref="0503.g1:/ P S /">$PS$</span>, <span data-ref="0503.g1:/ T Q /">$TQ$</span> being the greater segments); draw <span data-ref="0503.g1:/ R U /">$RU$</span>, <span data-ref="0503.g1:/ S V /">$SV$</span>, <span data-ref="0503.g1:/ T W /">$TW$</span> outwards perpendicular to the respective faces of the cube, and all equal in length to <span data-ref="0503.g1:/ P R /">$PR$</span>, <span data-ref="0503.g1:/ P S /">$PS$</span>, <span data-ref="0503.g1:/ T Q /">$TQ$</span>.

Join <span data-ref="0503.g1:/ B U /">$BU$</span>, <span data-ref="0503.g1:/ U V /">$UV$</span>, <span data-ref="0503.g1:/ V C /">$VC$</span>, <span data-ref="0503.g1:/ C W /">$CW$</span>, <span data-ref="0503.g1:/ W B /">$WB$</span>.

Then <span data-ref="0503.g1:< B U V C W >">$BUVCW$</span> is one of the pentagonal faces of the dodecahedron; and the others can be constructed in the same way.
