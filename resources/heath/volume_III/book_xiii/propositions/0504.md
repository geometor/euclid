(3) To prove that the pentagon $BUVCW$ is equiangular.
We have
$$\begin{aligned}
BV^2 &= BS^2 + SV^2 \\
&= (BN^2 + NS^2) + SP^2 \\
&= PN^2 + (NS^2 + SP^2) \\
&= PN^2 + 3PN^2,
\end{aligned}$$
since $NS$ is divided in extreme and mean ratio at $P$ [XIII. 5], so that
$$NS^2 + SP^2 = 3PN^2. \qquad\qquad\text{[XIII. 4]}$$
Consequently
$$\begin{aligned}
BV^2 &= 4PN^2 \\
&= BC^2,
\end{aligned}$$
or
$$BV = BC.$$
The $\Delta\text{s } UBV, WBC$ are therefore equal in all respects, and
$$\angle BUV = \angle BWC.$$
Similarly
$$\angle CVU = \angle BWC.$$
Therefore the pentagon is equiangular. [XIII. 7]

(4) To prove that the sphere which circumscribes the cube also circumscribes the dodecahedron we have only to prove that, if $Z$ be the centre of the sphere, $ZU = ZB$, for example.
Now, by **XI. 38**, $XP$ produced meets the diagonal of the cube, and the portion of $XP$ produced which is within the cube and the diagonal bisect one another.
And
$$\begin{aligned}
ZU^2 &= ZX^2 + XU^2 \\
&= NS^2 + PS^2 \\
&= 3PN^2,
\end{aligned}$$
as before.
Also (cf. XIII. 15)
$$\begin{aligned}
ZB^2 &= ZP^2 + PB^2 \\
&= ZP^2 + PN^2 + NB^2 \\
&= 3PN^2.
\end{aligned}$$
Hence
$$ZU = ZB.$$
