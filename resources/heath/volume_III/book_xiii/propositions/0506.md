And the angle <span data-ref="0506.g1:< O L F )">$OLF$</span> is right; for <span data-ref="0506.g1:/ P O /">$PO$</span>, <span data-ref="0506.g1:/ L F /">$LF$</span> are equal and parallel chords in two equal and parallel circular sections of a sphere [Pappus' lemma, p. 138, 20–26].
Therefore
<span data-ref="0506.g1:/ O F /">$OF$</span>$^2 = $ <span data-ref="0506.g1:/ O L /">$OL$</span>$^2 + $ <span data-ref="0506.g1:/ F L /">$FL$</span>$^2 = 3$<span data-ref="0506.g1:/ F L /">$FL$</span>$^2$. [from above]
And <span data-ref="0506.g1:/ O F /">$OF$</span> is a diameter of the sphere; for <span data-ref="0506.g1:/ P O /">$PO$</span>, <span data-ref="0506.g1:/ L F /">$LF$</span> are on opposite sides of the centres of the circles in which they are [Pappus' lemma, pp. 136–8].

Now suppose $p$, $t$, $h$ to be the sides of an equilateral pentagon, triangle and hexagon in the circle <span data-ref="0506.g1:( F G H K L )">$FGHKL$</span>, $d$ the diameter of the sphere.
Then
$d : $ <span data-ref="0506.g1:/ F L /">$FL$</span> $= \sqrt{3} : 1$ [from above]
$$= t : h; \qquad \text{[Eucl. XIII. 12]}$$
and it follows *alternando* (since <span data-ref="0506.g1:/ F L /">$FL$</span> $= p$) that
$$d : t = p : h.$$
Now let $d'$, $p'$, $h'$ be the sides of a regular decagon, pentagon and hexagon respectively inscribed in *any one* circle.
Since, if <span data-ref="0506.g1:/ F L /">$FL$</span> be divided in extreme and mean ratio, the greater segment is equal to <span data-ref="0506.g1:/ E D /">$ED$</span>, [XIII. 8]
<span data-ref="0506.g1:/ F L /">$FL$</span> $:$ <span data-ref="0506.g1:/ E D /">$ED$</span> $= h' : d'$. [VI. Def. 3, XIII. 9]
And <span data-ref="0506.g1:/ F L /">$FL$</span> $:$ <span data-ref="0506.g1:/ E D /">$ED$</span> is the ratio of the sides of the regular pentagons inscribed in the circles <span data-ref="0506.g1:( F G H K L )">$FGHKL$</span>, <span data-ref="0506.g1:( A B C D E )">$ABCDE$</span>, and is therefore equal to the ratio of the sides of the equilateral triangles inscribed in the same circles.
Therefore
$t : (\text{side of }\triangle\text{ in }$ <span data-ref="0506.g1:( A B C D E )">$ABCDE$</span>$) = h' : d'$.
But
$$\begin{aligned}
d : t &= p : h \\
&= p' : h';
\end{aligned}$$
therefore, *ex aequali*, $d : (\text{side of }\triangle\text{ in }$ <span data-ref="0506.g1:( A B C D E )">$ABCDE$</span>$) = p' : d'$.
Now $d$ is given;
therefore the sides of the equilateral triangles inscribed in the circles <span data-ref="0506.g1:( A B C D E )">$ABCDE$</span>, <span data-ref="0506.g1:( F G H K L )">$FGHKL$</span> respectively are given, whence the radii of those circles are also given.
