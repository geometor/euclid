Thus the two circles are given, and so accordingly are the equal and parallel circular sections.

*Synthesis.*
Set out two straight lines <span data-ref="0506.g1:/ x /">$x$</span>, <span data-ref="0506.g1:/ y /">$y$</span> such that $d$, <span data-ref="0506.g1:/ x /">$x$</span>, <span data-ref="0506.g1:/ y /">$y$</span> are in the ratio of the sides of a regular pentagon, hexagon and decagon respectively inscribed in one and the same circle.
Find two circular sections of the sphere with radii $r, r'$, where
$r^2 = \tfrac{1}{3}$ <span data-ref="0506.g1:/ x /">$x$</span>$^2,\quad {r'}^2 = \tfrac{1}{3}$ <span data-ref="0506.g1:/ y /">$y$</span>$^2.$
Let these be the circles <span data-ref="0506.g1:( F G H K L )">$FGHKL$</span>, <span data-ref="0506.g1:( A B C D E )">$ABCDE$</span> respectively, and draw the equal and parallel circles on the other side of the centre, namely <span data-ref="0506.g1:( M N O P Q )">$MNOPQ$</span>, <span data-ref="0506.g1:( R S T U V )">$RSTUV$</span>.
In the first two circles inscribe regular pentagons with their sides respectively parallel, <span data-ref="0506.g1:/ E D /">$ED$</span> being parallel to <span data-ref="0506.g1:/ F L /">$FL$</span>.
Draw equal and parallel chords (on the other sides of the centres) in the other two circles, namely <span data-ref="0506.g1:/ S T /">$ST$</span> equal and parallel to <span data-ref="0506.g1:/ E D /">$ED$</span>, and <span data-ref="0506.g1:/ P O /">$PO$</span> equal and parallel to <span data-ref="0506.g1:/ F L /">$FL$</span>; and complete the regular pentagons on <span data-ref="0506.g1:/ S T /">$ST$</span>, <span data-ref="0506.g1:/ P O /">$PO$</span> inscribed in the circles.
Thus all the vertices of the dodecahedron are determined.
The proof of the correctness of the construction is clear from the analysis.
Pappus adds that the construction shows that the circles containing five vertices of the dodecahedron are the same respectively as those containing three vertices of the icosahedron, and that the same circle circumscribes the triangle of the icosahedron and the pentagonal face of the dodecahedron in the same sphere.

### PROPOSITION 18.

*To set out the sides of the five figures and to compare them with one another.*

Let <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, the diameter of the given sphere, be set out, and let it be cut at <span data-ref="0507.g1:C">$C$</span> so that <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span>, and at <span data-ref="0507.g1:D">$D$</span> so that <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> is double of <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span>;
let the semicircle <span data-ref="0507.g1:( A E B )">$AEB$</span> be described on <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>,
from <span data-ref="0507.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0507.g1:D">$D$</span> let <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ C E /">$CE$</span>, <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span> be drawn at right angles to <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>,
and let <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span>, <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ F B /">$FB$</span>, <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span> be joined.
Then, since <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> is double of <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span>,
therefore <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is triple of <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ D B /">$BD$</span>.
*Convertendo*, therefore, <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A B /">$BA$</span> is one and a half times <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>.
