Find two circular sections of the sphere with radii $r, r'$, where<br />
&nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp; $r^2 = \tfrac{1}{3}$ <span data-ref="0506.g1:/ x /">$x$</span>$^2,\quad {r'}^2 = \tfrac{1}{3}$ <span data-ref="0506.g1:/ y /">$y$</span>$^2.$<br />
Let these be the circles <span data-ref="0506.g1:( F G H K L )">$FGHKL$</span>, <span data-ref="0506.g1:( A B C D E )">$ABCDE$</span> respectively, and draw the<br />

In the first two circles inscribe regular pentagons with their sides respec-<br />
tively parallel, <span data-ref="0506.g1:/ E D /">$ED$</span> being parallel to <span data-ref="0506.g1:/ F L /">$FL$</span>.<br />
Draw equal and parallel chords (on the other sides of the centres) in the<br />
other two circles, namely <span data-ref="0506.g1:/ S T /">$ST$</span> equal and parallel to <span data-ref="0506.g1:/ E D /">$ED$</span>, and <span data-ref="0506.g1:/ P O /">$PO$</span> equal and<br />

the circles.<br />
Thus all the vertices of the dodecahedron are determined.<br />
The proof of the correctness of the construction is clear from the analysis.<br />
Pappus adds that the construction shows that the circles containing five<br />
vertices of the dodecahedron are the same respectively as those containing<br />
three vertices of the icosahedron, and that the same circle circumscribes the<br />
triangle of the icosahedron and the pentagonal face of the dodecahedron in<br />
the same sphere.

### PROPOSITION 18.

<p class="enunciation">
To set out the sides of the five figures and to compare them<br />
with one another.
</p>

Let <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>, the diameter of the given sphere, be set out,<br />
and let it be cut at <span data-ref="0507.g1:C">$C$</span> so that<br />
<span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span>, and at <span data-ref="0507.g1:D">$D$</span><br />
so that <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> is double of <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span>;<br />
let the semicircle <span data-ref="0507.g1:( A E B )">$AEB$</span> be de-<br />
scribed on <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>,<br />
from <span data-ref="0507.g1:C">$C$</span>, <span data-ref="0507.g1:D">$D$</span> let <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ C E /">$CE$</span>, <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ D F /">$DF$</span> be drawn<br />
at right angles to <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>,<br />
and let <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span>, <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ F B /">$FB$</span>, <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ E B /">$EB$</span> be joined.

<p class="assertion">
Then, since <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> is double<br />
of <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span>,<br />
therefore <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is triple of <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ D B /">$BD$</span>.
</p>

<p class="assertion">
*Convertendo*, therefore, <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A B /">$BA$</span> is one and a half times <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>.
</p>
