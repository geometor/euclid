But, as <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A B /">$BA$</span> is to <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>, so is the square on <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A B /">$BA$</span> to the square on <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span>, [v. Def. 9, VI. 8]
for the triangle <span data-ref="0507.g1:< A F B >">$AFB$</span> is equiangular with the triangle <span data-ref="0507.g1:< A F D >">$AFD$</span>;
therefore the square on <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A B /">$BA$</span> is one and a half times the square on <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span>.
But the square on the diameter of the sphere is also one and a half times the square on the side of the pyramid. [XIII. 13]
And <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is the diameter of the sphere;
therefore <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span> is equal to the side of the pyramid.

Again, since <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> is double of <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span>,
therefore <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is triple of <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ D B /">$BD$</span>.
But, as <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ D B /">$BD$</span>, so is the square on <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> to the square on <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ F B /">$BF$</span>; [VI. 8, v. Def. 9]
therefore the square on <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is triple of the square on <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ F B /">$BF$</span>.
But the square on the diameter of the sphere is also triple of the square on the side of the cube. [XIII. 15]
And <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is the diameter of the sphere;
therefore <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ F B /">$BF$</span> is the side of the cube.

And, since <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span>,
therefore <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is double of <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ C B /">$BC$</span>.
But, as <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ C B /">$BC$</span>, so is the square on <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> to the square on <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ E B /">$BE$</span>;
therefore the square on <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is double of the square on <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ E B /">$BE$</span>.
But the square on the diameter of the sphere is also double of the square on the side of the octahedron. [XIII. 14]
And <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is the diameter of the given sphere;
therefore <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ E B /">$BE$</span> is the side of the octahedron.

Next, let <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span> be drawn from the point <span data-ref="0507.g1:A">$A$</span> at right angles to the straight line <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>,
let <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span> be made equal to <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>,
let <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ G C /">$GC$</span> be joined,
and from <span data-ref="0507.g1:H">$H$</span> let <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ H K /">$HK$</span> be drawn perpendicular to <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>.
Then, since <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A G /">$GA$</span> is double of <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>,
for <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A G /">$GA$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>,
and, as <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A G /">$GA$</span> is to <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ H K /">$HK$</span> to <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ C K /">$KC$</span>,
therefore <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ H K /">$HK$</span> is also double of <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ C K /">$KC$</span>.
