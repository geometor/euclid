<p class="sub-clause">And, since <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ L B /">$LB$</span> belongs to a decagon, and <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ L M /">$ML$</span> to a<br />
hexagon,<br />
for <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ L M /">$ML$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ K L /">$KL$</span>, since it is also equal to <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ H K /">$HK$</span>, being<br />
the same distance from the centre, and each of the straight<br />
lines <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ H K /">$HK$</span>, <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ K L /">$KL$</span> is double of <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ C K /">$KC$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ M B /">$MB$</span> belongs to a pentagon. <span class="justification">[XIII. 10]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">But the side of the pentagon is the side of the icosa-<br />
hedron ; <span class="justification">[XIII. 16]</span></p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ M B /">$MB$</span> belongs to the icosahedron.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">Now, since <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ F B /">$FB$</span> is a side of the cube,<br />
let it be cut in extreme and mean ratio at <span data-ref="0507.g1:N">$N$</span>,<br />
and let <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ N B /">$NB$</span> be the greater segment ;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ N B /">$NB$</span> is a side of the dodecahedron. <span class="justification">[XIII. 17, Por.]</span></p>

<p class="sub-clause">And, since the square on the diameter of the sphere was<br />
proved to be one and a half times the square on the side <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span><br />
of the pyramid, double of the square on the side <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ E B /">$BE$</span> of the<br />
octahedron and triple of the side <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ F B /">$FB$</span> of the cube,</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore, of parts of which the square on the diameter of the<br />
sphere contains six, the square on the side of the pyramid<br />
contains four, the square on the side of the octahedron three,<br />
and the square on the side of the cube two.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore the square on the side of the pyramid is four-<br />
thirds of the square on the side of the octahedron, and double<br />
of the square on the side of the cube ;<br />
and the square on the side of the octahedron is one and a half<br />
times the square on the side of the cube.</p>

<p class="assertion">The said sides, therefore, of the three figures, I mean the<br />
pyramid, the octahedron and the cube, are to one another in<br />
rational ratios.</p>

<p class="assertion">But the remaining two, I mean the side of the icosa-<br />
hedron and the side of the dodecahedron, are not in rational<br />
ratios either to one another or to the aforesaid sides ;<br />
for they are irrational, the one being minor <span class="justification">[XIII. 16]</span> and the<br />
other an apotome <span class="justification">[XIII. 17]</span>.</p>

<p class="assertion">That the side <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ M B /">$MB$</span> of the icosahedron is greater than the<br />
side <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ N B /">$NB$</span> of the dodecahedron we can prove thus.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">For, since the triangle <span data-ref="0507.g1:< F D B >">$FDB$</span> is equiangular with the<br />
triangle <span data-ref="0507.g1:< A F B >">$FAB$</span>, <span class="justification">[VI. 8]</span></p>
<p class="assertion">proportionally, as <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ F B /">$BF$</span>, so is <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ F B /">$BF$</span> to <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A B /">$BA$</span>. <span class="justification">[VI. 4]</span></p>
