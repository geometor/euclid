And, since the three straight lines are proportional,
as the first is to the third, so is the square on the first to the square on the second; [v. Def. 9, VI. 20, Por.]
therefore, as <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A B /">$BA$</span>, so is the square on <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> to the square on <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ F B /">$BF$</span>;
therefore, inversely, as <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ D B /">$BD$</span>, so is the square on <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ F B /">$FB$</span> to the square on <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ D B /">$BD$</span>.
But <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is triple of <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ D B /">$BD$</span>;
therefore the square on <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ F B /">$FB$</span> is triple of the square on <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ D B /">$BD$</span>.
But the square on <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> is also quadruple of the square on <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span>,
for <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> is double of <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span>;
therefore the square on <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> is greater than the square on <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ F B /">$FB$</span>;
therefore <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> is greater than <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ F B /">$FB$</span>;
therefore <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A L /">$AL$</span> is by far greater than <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ F B /">$FB$</span>.
And, when <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A L /">$AL$</span> is cut in extreme and mean ratio,
<span data-ref="0507.g1:/ K L /">$KL$</span> is the greater segment,
inasmuch as <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ K L /">$LK$</span> belongs to a hexagon, and <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A K /">$KA$</span> to a decagon; [XIII. 9]
and, when <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ F B /">$FB$</span> is cut in extreme and mean ratio, <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ N B /">$NB$</span> is the greater segment;
therefore <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ K L /">$KL$</span> is greater than <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ N B /">$NB$</span>.
But <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ K L /">$KL$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ L M /">$LM$</span>;
therefore <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ L M /">$LM$</span> is greater than <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ N B /">$NB$</span>.
Therefore <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ M B /">$MB$</span>, which is a side of the icosahedron, is by far greater than <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ N B /">$NB$</span> which is a side of the dodecahedron.

Q. E. D.

I say next that *no other figure, besides the said five figures, can be constructed which is contained by equilateral and equiangular figures equal to one another.*
For a solid angle cannot be constructed with two triangles, or indeed planes.
With three triangles the angle of the pyramid is constructed, with four the angle of the octahedron, and with five the angle of the icosahedron;
