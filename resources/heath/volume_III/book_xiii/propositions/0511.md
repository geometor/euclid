<p class="sub-clause">And, since the three straight lines are proportional,<br />
as the first is to the third, so is the square on the first to the<br />
square on the second ; <span class="justification">[v. Def. 9, VI. 20, Por.]</span></p>
<p class="assertion">therefore, as <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A B /">$BA$</span>, so is the square on <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> to the<br />
square on <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ F B /">$BF$</span>;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore, inversely, as <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is to <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ D B /">$BD$</span>, so is the square on <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ F B /">$FB$</span><br />
to the square on <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ D B /">$BD$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">But <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> is triple of <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ D B /">$BD$</span> ;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the square on <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ F B /">$FB$</span> is triple of the square on <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ D B /">$BD$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">But the square on <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> is also quadruple of the square<br />
on <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span>,<br />
for <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> is double of <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> ;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore the square on <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> is greater than the square on <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ F B /">$FB$</span> ;<br />
therefore <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> is greater than <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ F B /">$FB$</span> ;<br />
therefore <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A L /">$AL$</span> is by far greater than <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ F B /">$FB$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">And, when <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A L /">$AL$</span> is cut in extreme and mean ratio,<br />
<span data-ref="0507.g1:/ K L /">$KL$</span> is the greater segment,<br />
inasmuch as <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ K L /">$LK$</span> belongs to a hexagon, and <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A K /">$KA$</span> to a decagon ;<br />
<span class="justification">[XIII. 9]</span><br />
and, when <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ F B /">$FB$</span> is cut in extreme and mean ratio, <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ N B /">$NB$</span> is the<br />
greater segment ;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ K L /">$KL$</span> is greater than <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ N B /">$NB$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">But <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ K L /">$KL$</span> is equal to <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ L M /">$LM$</span> ;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ L M /">$LM$</span> is greater than <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ N B /">$NB$</span>.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ M B /">$MB$</span>, which is a side of the icosahedron, is by<br />
far greater than <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ N B /">$NB$</span> which is a side of the dodecahedron.</p>

<p class="qed"><span class="justification">Q. E. D.</span></p>

can be constructed which is contained by equilateral and equi-<br />
angular figures equal to one another.</em></p>

or indeed planes.</p>
