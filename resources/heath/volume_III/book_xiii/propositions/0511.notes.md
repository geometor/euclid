# Transcription Notes: Folio 0511 (Page 507)

## Summary & Folio Continuity
Folio 0511 (page 507) concludes the proof that the side of the icosahedron exceeds that of the dodecahedron ($MB > NB$), concluding Proposition 18 with **Q. E. D.** Following a horizontal rule, it embarks on Euclid's famous crowning theorem: that no other regular polyhedra can exist other than the five Platonic solids. It systematically analyzes solid angles composed of equilateral triangles (3 for tetrahedron, 4 for octahedron, 5 for icosahedron) and shows 6 cannot form a solid angle.

## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Inequality Chain**: $AD > FB \implies AL > FB$. Since $AL$ and $FB$ are cut in extreme and mean ratio with greater segments $KL$ and $NB$, $AL > FB \implies KL > NB$. Then since $LM = KL$, $LM > NB$, and hypotenuse $MB = \sqrt{LM^2 + LB^2} > LM > NB$.
- **Crowning Theorem Enunciation**: "I say next that *no other figure, besides the said five figures, can be constructed which is contained by equilateral and equiangular figures equal to one another.*"
