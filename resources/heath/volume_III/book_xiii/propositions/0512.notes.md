# Transcription Notes: Folio 0512 (Page 508)

## Summary & Folio Continuity
Folio 0512 (page 508) finishes the proof of the crowning theorem, showing why regular polyhedra can only have 3 squares at a vertex (cube), 3 pentagons (dodecahedron), and cannot have 4 squares, 4 pentagons, or any regular hexagons or higher polygons (by Book XI Prop. 21). It concludes the crowning proof with **Q. E. D.** Then follows a **LEMMA** proving that the interior angle of a regular pentagon is equal to a right angle and a fifth ($108^\circ$).

## Textual, Lexical & Mathematical Observations
- **Angular Sum Criterion**: Relies heavily on Book XI Prop. 21 (the sum of the plane angles forming any solid angle is strictly less than four right angles / $360^\circ$).
- **Angles Evaluated**:
  - Equilateral triangle angle: $\frac{2}{3} \times 90^\circ = 60^\circ$; $6 \times 60^\circ = 360^\circ$ (impossible).
  - Square angle: $90^\circ$; $4 \times 90^\circ = 360^\circ$ (impossible).
  - Regular pentagon angle: $1 \frac{1}{5} \times 90^\circ = 108^\circ$; $4 \times 108^\circ = 432^\circ > 360^\circ$ (impossible).
  - Hexagon angle: $\frac{4}{3} \times 90^\circ = 120^\circ$; $3 \times 120^\circ = 360^\circ$ (impossible a fortiori).
