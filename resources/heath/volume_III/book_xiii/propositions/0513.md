We have seen in the preceding notes that, if $r$ be the radius of the sphere<br />
circumscribing the five solid figures,
$$\begin{aligned}
(\text{edge of tetrahedron}) &= \tfrac{2}{3} \sqrt{6} \cdot r, \\
(\text{edge of octahedron}) &= \sqrt{2} \cdot r, \\
(\text{edge of cube}) &= \tfrac{2}{3} \sqrt{3} \cdot r, \\
(\text{edge of icosahedron}) &= \tfrac{r}{5} \sqrt{10(5-\sqrt{5})}, \\
(\text{edge of dodecahedron}) &= \tfrac{r}{3} (\sqrt{15}-\sqrt{3}).
\end{aligned}$$
Euclid here exhibits the edges of all the five regular solids in one figure.

(1) Make <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> equal to $2$<span data-ref="0507.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span>.<br />
Thus <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A B /">$BA$</span> $= \tfrac{3}{2}$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>,<br />
and <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A B /">$BA$</span> $:$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> $=$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A B /">$BA$</span>$^2 :$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span>$^2$;<br />
therefore <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A B /">$BA$</span>$^2 = \tfrac{3}{2}$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span>$^2$.<br />
Thus <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A F /">$AF$</span> $= \sqrt{\tfrac{2}{3}} \cdot 2r = \tfrac{2}{3} \sqrt{6} \cdot r = (\text{edge of tetrahedron})$.

(2) <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>$^2 :$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ F B /">$BF$</span>$^2 =$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> $:$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ D B /">$BD$</span><br />
$= 3 : 1$.<br />
Therefore <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ F B /">$BF$</span>$^2 = \tfrac{1}{3}$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>$^2$,<br />
or <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ F B /">$BF$</span> $= \frac{2}{\sqrt{3}} \cdot r = \tfrac{2}{3} \sqrt{3} \cdot r = (\text{edge of cube})$.

(3) <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>$^2 = 2$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ E B /">$BE$</span>$^2$.<br />
Therefore <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ E B /">$BE$</span> $= \sqrt{2} \cdot r = (\text{edge of octahedron})$.

(4) Draw <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A G /">$AG$</span> perpendicular and equal to <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>. Join <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ G C /">$GC$</span>, meeting the<br />
semicircle in <span data-ref="0507.g1:H">$H$</span>, and draw <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ H K /">$HK$</span> perpendicular to <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span>.<br />
Then <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A G /">$GA$</span> $= 2$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A C /">$AC$</span>;<br />
therefore, by similar triangles, <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ H K /">$HK$</span> $= 2$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ C K /">$KC$</span>.<br />
Hence <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ H K /">$HK$</span>$^2 = 4$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ C K /">$KC$</span>$^2$,<br />
and therefore $5$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ C K /">$KC$</span>$^2 =$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ H K /">$HK$</span>$^2 +$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ C K /">$KC$</span>$^2$<br />
$= $ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ H C /">$HC$</span>$^2$<br />
$= $ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span>$^2$.

Again, since <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A B /">$AB$</span> $= 2$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ C B /">$CB$</span>, and <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> $= 2$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span>,<br />
by subtraction, <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ D B /">$BD$</span> $= 2$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ C D /">$DC$</span>,<br />
or <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ C B /">$BC$</span> $= 3$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ C D /">$DC$</span>.
