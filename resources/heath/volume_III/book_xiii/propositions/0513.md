We have seen in the preceding notes that, if $r$ be the radius of the sphere circumscribing the five solid figures,
$$\begin{aligned}
(\text{edge of tetrahedron}) &= \tfrac{2}{3} \sqrt{6} \cdot r, \\
(\text{edge of octahedron}) &= \sqrt{2} \cdot r, \\
(\text{edge of cube}) &= \tfrac{2}{3} \sqrt{3} \cdot r, \\
(\text{edge of icosahedron}) &= \tfrac{r}{5} \sqrt{10(5-\sqrt{5})}, \\
(\text{edge of dodecahedron}) &= \tfrac{r}{3} (\sqrt{15}-\sqrt{3}).
\end{aligned}$$
Euclid here exhibits the edges of all the five regular solids in one figure.
(1) Make $AD$ equal to $2DB$.
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Thus} \quad BA &= \tfrac{3}{2} AD, \\
\text{and} \quad BA : AD &= BA^2 : AF^2; \\
\text{therefore} \quad BA^2 &= \tfrac{3}{2} AF^2. \\
\text{Thus} \quad AF = \sqrt{\tfrac{2}{3}} \cdot 2r &= \tfrac{2}{3} \sqrt{6} \cdot r = (\text{edge of tetrahedron}).
\end{aligned}$$
(2)
$$\begin{aligned}
AB^2 : BF^2 &= AB : BD \\
&= 3 : 1. \\
\text{Therefore} \quad BF^2 &= \tfrac{1}{3} AB^2, \\
\text{or} \quad BF = \frac{2}{\sqrt{3}} \cdot r &= \tfrac{2}{3} \sqrt{3} \cdot r = (\text{edge of cube}).
\end{aligned}$$
(3)
$$\begin{aligned}
AB^2 &= 2 BE^2. \\
\text{Therefore} \quad BE &= \sqrt{2} \cdot r = (\text{edge of octahedron}).
\end{aligned}$$
(4) Draw $AG$ perpendicular and equal to $AB$. Join $GC$, meeting the semicircle in $H$, and draw $HK$ perpendicular to $AB$.
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Then} \quad GA &= 2 AC; \\
\text{therefore, by similar triangles,} \quad HK &= 2 KC. \\
\text{Hence} \quad HK^2 &= 4 KC^2, \\
\text{and therefore} \quad 5 KC^2 &= HK^2 + KC^2 \\
&= HC^2 \\
&= CB^2.
\end{aligned}$$
Again, since $AB = 2 CB$, and $AD = 2 DB$,
by subtraction,
$$BD = 2 DC,$$
or
$$BC = 3 DC.$$
