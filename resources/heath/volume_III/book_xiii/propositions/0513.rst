.. assertion::

   therefore one of them, as the angle :g:`AFB <0512.g1:< A F B )>`, is one right angle less a fifth; therefore the remaining angles :g:`FAB <0512.g1:< F A B )>`, :g:`ABF <0512.g1:< A B F )>` consist of one right angle and a fifth.

.. subclause::

   But the angle :g:`FAB <0512.g1:< F A B )>` is :eq:`equal <0512.g1:< F A B ) = 0512.g1:< F B C )>` to the angle :g:`FBC <0512.g1:< F B C )>`;

.. assertion::

   therefore the whole angle :g:`ABC <0512.g1:< A B C )>` of the pentagon consists of one right angle and a fifth.

.. qed::

   :just:`Q. E. D.`

----

We have seen in the preceding notes that, if $r$ be the radius of the sphere circumscribing the five solid figures,

$$\begin{aligned}
(\text{edge of tetrahedron}) &= \tfrac{2}{3} \sqrt{6} \cdot r, \\
(\text{edge of octahedron}) &= \sqrt{2} \cdot r, \\
(\text{edge of cube}) &= \tfrac{2}{3} \sqrt{3} \cdot r, \\
(\text{edge of icosahedron}) &= \tfrac{r}{5} \sqrt{10(5-\sqrt{5})}, \\
(\text{edge of dodecahedron}) &= \tfrac{r}{3} (\sqrt{15}-\sqrt{3}).
\end{aligned}$$

Euclid here exhibits the edges of all the five regular solids in one figure.

(1) Make :g:`AD <0507.g1:/ A D />` equal to $2$ :g:`DB <0507.g1:/ D B />`. Thus :g:`BA <0507.g1:/ A B />` $= \tfrac{3}{2}$ :g:`AD <0507.g1:/ A D />`, and :g:`BA <0507.g1:/ A B />` $:$ :g:`AD <0507.g1:/ A D />` $=$ :g:`BA <0507.g1:/ A B />` $^2 :$ :g:`AF <0507.g1:/ A F />` $^2$; therefore :g:`BA <0507.g1:/ A B />` $^2 = \tfrac{3}{2}$ :g:`AF <0507.g1:/ A F />` $^2$. Thus :g:`AF <0507.g1:/ A F />` $= \sqrt{\tfrac{2}{3}} \cdot 2r = \tfrac{2}{3} \sqrt{6} \cdot r = (\text{edge of tetrahedron})$.

(2) :g:`AB <0507.g1:/ A B />` $^2 :$ :g:`BF <0507.g1:/ F B />` $^2 =$ :g:`AB <0507.g1:/ A B />` $:$ :g:`BD <0507.g1:/ D B />` $= 3 : 1$. Therefore :g:`BF <0507.g1:/ F B />` $^2 = \tfrac{1}{3}$ :g:`AB <0507.g1:/ A B />` $^2$, or :g:`BF <0507.g1:/ F B />` $= \frac{2}{\sqrt{3}} \cdot r = \tfrac{2}{3} \sqrt{3} \cdot r = (\text{edge of cube})$.

(3) :g:`AB <0507.g1:/ A B />` $^2 = 2$ :g:`BE <0507.g1:/ E B />` $^2$. Therefore :g:`BE <0507.g1:/ E B />` $= \sqrt{2} \cdot r = (\text{edge of octahedron})$.

(4) Draw :g:`AG <0507.g1:/ A G />` perpendicular and :eq:`equal <0507.g1:/ A G / = 0507.g1:/ A B />` to :g:`AB <0507.g1:/ A B />`. Join :g:`GC <0507.g1:/ G C />`, meeting the semicircle in :g:`H <0507.g1:H>`, and draw :g:`HK <0507.g1:/ H K />` perpendicular to :g:`AB <0507.g1:/ A B />`. Then :g:`GA <0507.g1:/ A G />` $= 2$ :g:`AC <0507.g1:/ A C />`; therefore, by similar triangles, :g:`HK <0507.g1:/ H K />` $= 2$ :g:`KC <0507.g1:/ C K />`. Hence :g:`HK <0507.g1:/ H K />` $^2 = 4$ :g:`KC <0507.g1:/ C K />` $^2$, and therefore $5$ :g:`KC <0507.g1:/ C K />` $^2 =$ :g:`HK <0507.g1:/ H K />` $^2 +$ :g:`KC <0507.g1:/ C K />` $^2$ $= $ :g:`HC <0507.g1:/ H C />` $^2$ $= $ :g:`CB <0507.g1:/ C B />` $^2$.

Again, since :g:`AB <0507.g1:/ A B />` $= 2$ :g:`CB <0507.g1:/ C B />`, and :g:`AD <0507.g1:/ A D />` $= 2$ :g:`DB <0507.g1:/ D B />`, by subtraction, :g:`BD <0507.g1:/ D B />` $= 2$ :g:`DC <0507.g1:/ C D />`, or :g:`BC <0507.g1:/ C B />` $= 3$ :g:`DC <0507.g1:/ C D />`.
