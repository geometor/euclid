Make $CL$ equal to $KC$, draw $LM$ at right angles to $AB$, and join $AM, MB$.
Since
$$\begin{aligned}
CB^2 &= 5 KC^2, \\

\end{aligned}$$
It follows that $KL$ ($= \sqrt{\tfrac{4}{5}} \cdot r$) is the radius of, or the side of the regular hexagon in, the circle containing the pentagonal sections of the icosahedron. [XIII. 16]
And, since
$$2r = (\text{side of hexagon}) + 2 (\text{side of decagon in same circle})$$ [XIII. 16, Por.]
$$AK = LB = (\text{side of decagon in the said circle}).$$
But
$$LM = HK = KL = (\text{side of hexagon in circle}).$$
Therefore
$$\begin{aligned}
LM^2 + LB^2 (= BM^2) &= (\text{side of pentagon in circle})^2 && \text{[XIII. 10]} \\
&= (\text{edge of icosahedron})^2,
\end{aligned}$$
and
$$BM = (\text{edge of icosahedron}).$$
[More shortly,
$$HK = 2 KC,$$
whence
$$HK^2 = 4 KC^2,$$
and
$$5 KC^2 = HC^2 = r^2.$$
Also
$$AK = r - CK = r \left( 1 - \frac{1}{\sqrt{5}} \right).$$
Thus
$$\begin{aligned}
BM^2 &= HK^2 + AK^2 \\
&= \tfrac{4}{5} r^2 + r^2 \left( 1 - \frac{1}{\sqrt{5}} \right)^2 \\
&= r^2 \left( \frac{10}{5} - \frac{2}{\sqrt{5}} \right) \\
&= \frac{r^2}{5} (10 - 2\sqrt{5}),
\end{aligned}$$
and
$$BM = \frac{r}{5} \sqrt{10(5-\sqrt{5})} = (\text{edge of icosahedron}).]$$
