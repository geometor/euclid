:title: Proposition 18 Commentary (continued)

Therefore $9$ :g:`$DC$ <0507.g1:/ C D />` $^2 =$ :g:`$BC$ <0507.g1:/ C B />` $^2$ $= 5$ :g:`$KC$ <0507.g1:/ C K />` $^2$.

Hence :g:`$KC$ <0507.g1:/ C K />` $>$ :g:`$CD$ <0507.g1:/ C D />`.

Make :g:`$CL$ <0507.g1:/ C L />` equal to :g:`$KC$ <0507.g1:/ C K />`, draw :g:`$LM$ <0507.g1:/ L M />` at right angles to :g:`$AB$ <0507.g1:/ A B />`, and join :g:`$AM$ <0507.g1:/ A M />`, :g:`$MB$ <0507.g1:/ M B />`.

Since :g:`$CB$ <0507.g1:/ C B />` $^2 = 5$ :g:`$KC$ <0507.g1:/ C K />` $^2$, :g:`$AB$ <0507.g1:/ A B />` $^2 = 5$ :g:`$KL$ <0507.g1:/ K L />` $^2$.

It follows that :g:`$KL$ <0507.g1:/ K L />` ($= \sqrt{\tfrac{4}{5}} \cdot r$) is the radius of, or the side of the regular hexagon in, the circle containing the pentagonal sections of the icosahedron. :just:`[XIII. 16] <XIII.16>`

And, since $2r = (\text{side of hexagon}) + 2 (\text{side of decagon in same circle})$ :just:`[XIII. 16, Por.]` :g:`$AK$ <0507.g1:/ A K />` $=$ :g:`$LB$ <0507.g1:/ L B />` $= (\text{side of decagon in the said circle})$.

But :g:`$LM$ <0507.g1:/ L M />` $=$ :g:`$HK$ <0507.g1:/ H K />` $=$ :g:`$KL$ <0507.g1:/ K L />` $= (\text{side of hexagon in circle})$.

Therefore :g:`$LM$ <0507.g1:/ L M />` $^2 +$ :g:`$LB$ <0507.g1:/ L B />` $^2 (=$ :g:`$BM$ <0507.g1:/ M B />` $^2) = (\text{side of pentagon in circle})^2$ :just:`[XIII. 10] <XIII.10>` $= (\text{edge of icosahedron})^2$, and :g:`$BM$ <0507.g1:/ M B />` $= (\text{edge of icosahedron})$.

[More shortly, :g:`$HK$ <0507.g1:/ H K />` $= 2$ :g:`$KC$ <0507.g1:/ C K />`, whence :g:`$HK$ <0507.g1:/ H K />` $^2 = 4$ :g:`$KC$ <0507.g1:/ C K />` $^2$, and $5$ :g:`$KC$ <0507.g1:/ C K />` $^2 =$ :g:`$HC$ <0507.g1:/ H C />` $^2 = r^2$.

Also :g:`$AK$ <0507.g1:/ A K />` $= r -$ :g:`$CK$ <0507.g1:/ C K />` $= r \left( 1 - \frac{1}{\sqrt{5}} \right)$.

Thus :g:`$BM$ <0507.g1:/ M B />` $^2 =$ :g:`$HK$ <0507.g1:/ H K />` $^2 +$ :g:`$AK$ <0507.g1:/ A K />` $^2$ $= \tfrac{4}{5} r^2 + r^2 \left( 1 - \frac{1}{\sqrt{5}} \right)^2$ $= r^2 \left( \frac{10}{5} - \frac{2}{\sqrt{5}} \right)$ $= \frac{r^2}{5} (10 - 2\sqrt{5})$, and :g:`$BM$ <0507.g1:/ M B />` $= \frac{r}{5} \sqrt{10(5-\sqrt{5})} = (\text{edge of icosahedron})$.]

(5) Cut :g:`$BF$ <0507.g1:/ F B />` (the edge of the cube) in extreme and mean ratio at :g:`$N$ <0507.g1:N>`. Then, if :g:`$BN$ <0507.g1:/ N B />` be the greater segment, :g:`$BN$ <0507.g1:/ N B />` $= (\text{edge of dodecahedron})$. :just:`[XIII. 17] <XIII.17>`

[Solving, we obtain :g:`$BN$ <0507.g1:/ N B />` $= \frac{\sqrt{5}-1}{2} \cdot$ :g:`$BF$ <0507.g1:/ F B />` $= \frac{\sqrt{5}-1}{2} \cdot \frac{2}{\sqrt{3}} \cdot r$ $= \frac{r}{3} (\sqrt{15}-\sqrt{3})$ $= (\text{edge of dodecahedron})$.]
