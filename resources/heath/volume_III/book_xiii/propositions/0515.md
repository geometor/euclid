(6) If $t, o, c$ are the edges of the tetrahedron, octahedron and cube<br />
respectively,
$$4r^2 = \tfrac{3}{2} t^2 = 2o^2 = 3c^2.$$
If each of these equals is put equal to $X$,
$$\begin{aligned}
4r^2 &= X, \\
t^2 &= \tfrac{2}{3} \cdot X, \\
o^2 &= \tfrac{1}{2} \cdot X, \\
c^2 &= \tfrac{1}{3} \cdot X,
\end{aligned}$$
whence
$$4r^2 : t^2 : o^2 : c^2 = 6 : 4 : 3 : 2,$$
and the ratios between $2r, t, o, c$ are all rational (in Euclid's sense).<br />
The ratios between these and the edges of the icosahedron and the<br />
dodecahedron are *irrational*.

(7) To prove that
$$(\text{edge of icosahedron}) > (\text{edge of dodecahedron}),$$
i.e. that <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ M B /">$MB$</span> $>$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ N B /">$NB$</span>.

<p class="sub-clause">By similar $\triangle\text{s}$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:< F D B >">$FDB$</span>, <span data-ref="0507.g1:< A F B >">$AFB$</span>,</p>
<p class="assertion"><span data-ref="0507.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> $:$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ F B /">$BF$</span> $=$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ F B /">$BF$</span> $:$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A B /">$BA$</span>,<br />
or<br />
<span data-ref="0507.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> $:$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A B /">$BA$</span> $=$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span>$^2 :$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ F B /">$BF$</span>$^2$.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">But $3$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> $=$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A B /">$BA$</span>;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ F B /">$BF$</span>$^2 = 3$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span>$^2$.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">By hypothesis, <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>$^2 = 4$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span>$^2$;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> $>$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ F B /">$BF$</span>,<br />
and, *a fortiori*,<br />
<span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A L /">$AL$</span> $>$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ F B /">$FB$</span>.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">Now <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ K L /">$LK$</span> is the side of a hexagon, and <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A K /">$AK$</span> the side of a decagon in the<br />
same circle;</p>
<p class="assertion">therefore, when <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A L /">$AL$</span> is divided in extreme and mean ratio, <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ K L /">$KL$</span> is the greater<br />
segment.</p>

<p class="sub-clause">And, when <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ F B /">$FB$</span> is divided in extreme and mean ratio, <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ N B /">$BN$</span> is the greater<br />
segment.</p>

<p class="assertion">Therefore, since<br />
<span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A L /">$AL$</span> $>$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ F B /">$BF$</span>,<br />
<span data-ref="0507.g1:/ K L /">$KL$</span> $>$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ N B /">$BN$</span>,<br />
or<br />
<span data-ref="0507.g1:/ L M /">$LM$</span> $>$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ N B /">$BN$</span>.</p>

<p class="assertion">And therefore, *a fortiori*,<br />
<span data-ref="0507.g1:/ M B /">$MB$</span> $>$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ N B /">$BN$</span>.</p>
