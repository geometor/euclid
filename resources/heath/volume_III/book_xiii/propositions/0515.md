(6) If $t, o, c$ are the edges of the tetrahedron, octahedron and cube respectively,
$$4r^2 = \tfrac{3}{2} t^2 = 2o^2 = 3c^2.$$
If each of these equals is put equal to $X$,
$$\begin{aligned}
4r^2 &= X, \\
t^2 &= \tfrac{2}{3} \cdot X, \\
o^2 &= \tfrac{1}{2} \cdot X, \\
c^2 &= \tfrac{1}{3} \cdot X,
\end{aligned}$$
whence
$$4r^2 : t^2 : o^2 : c^2 = 6 : 4 : 3 : 2,$$
and the ratios between $2r, t, o, c$ are all rational (in Euclid's sense).
The ratios between these and the edges of the icosahedron and the dodecahedron are *irrational*.

(7) To prove that
$$(\text{edge of icosahedron}) > (\text{edge of dodecahedron}),$$
i.e. that
$$MB > NB.$$
By similar $\triangle\text{s } FDB, AFB$,
$$DB : BF = BF : BA,$$
or
$$DB : BA = DB^2 : BF^2.$$
But
$$3 DB = BA;$$
therefore
$$BF^2 = 3 DB^2.$$
By hypothesis,
$$AD^2 = 4 DB^2;$$
therefore
$$AD > BF,$$
and, *a fortiori*,
$$AL > BF.$$
Now $LK$ is the side of a hexagon, and $AK$ the side of a decagon in the same circle;
therefore, when $AL$ is divided in extreme and mean ratio, $KL$ is the greater segment.
And, when $BF$ is divided in extreme and mean ratio, $BN$ is the greater segment.
Therefore, since
$$AL > BF,$$
$$KL > BN,$$
or
$$LM > BN.$$
And therefore, *a fortiori*,
$$MB > BN.$$
