(6) If $t, o, c$ are the edges of the tetrahedron, octahedron and cube respectively,
$$4r^2 = \tfrac{3}{2} t^2 = 2o^2 = 3c^2.$$
If each of these equals is put equal to $X$,
$$\begin{aligned}
4r^2 &= X, \\
t^2 &= \tfrac{2}{3} \cdot X, \\
o^2 &= \tfrac{1}{2} \cdot X, \\
c^2 &= \tfrac{1}{3} \cdot X,
\end{aligned}$$
whence
$$4r^2 : t^2 : o^2 : c^2 = 6 : 4 : 3 : 2,$$
and the ratios between $2r, t, o, c$ are all rational (in Euclid's sense).
The ratios between these and the edges of the icosahedron and the dodecahedron are *irrational*.

(7) To prove that
$$(\text{edge of icosahedron}) > (\text{edge of dodecahedron}),$$
i.e. that
<span data-ref="0507.g1:/ M B /">$MB$</span> $>$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ N B /">$NB$</span>.
By similar $\triangle\text{s}$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:< F D B >">$FDB$</span>, <span data-ref="0507.g1:< A F B >">$AFB$</span>,
<span data-ref="0507.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> $:$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ F B /">$BF$</span> $=$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ F B /">$BF$</span> $:$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A B /">$BA$</span>,
or
<span data-ref="0507.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> $:$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A B /">$BA$</span> $=$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span>$^2 :$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ F B /">$BF$</span>$^2$.
But
$3$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span> $=$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A B /">$BA$</span>;
therefore
<span data-ref="0507.g1:/ F B /">$BF$</span>$^2 = 3$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span>$^2$.
By hypothesis,
<span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span>$^2 = 4$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ D B /">$DB$</span>$^2$;
therefore
<span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A D /">$AD$</span> $>$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ F B /">$BF$</span>,
and, *a fortiori*,
<span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A L /">$AL$</span> $>$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ F B /">$FB$</span>.
Now <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ K L /">$LK$</span> is the side of a hexagon, and <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A K /">$AK$</span> the side of a decagon in the same circle;
therefore, when <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A L /">$AL$</span> is divided in extreme and mean ratio, <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ K L /">$KL$</span> is the greater segment.
And, when <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ F B /">$FB$</span> is divided in extreme and mean ratio, <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ N B /">$BN$</span> is the greater segment.
Therefore, since
<span data-ref="0507.g1:/ A L /">$AL$</span> $>$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ F B /">$BF$</span>,
<span data-ref="0507.g1:/ K L /">$KL$</span> $>$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ N B /">$BN$</span>,
or
<span data-ref="0507.g1:/ L M /">$LM$</span> $>$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ N B /">$BN$</span>.
And therefore, *a fortiori*,
<span data-ref="0507.g1:/ M B /">$MB$</span> $>$ <span data-ref="0507.g1:/ N B /">$BN$</span>.
