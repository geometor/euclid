Types / book

Content of type 'book'

Book I

Foundations of plane geometry: 23 definitions, 5 postulates, 5 common notions, and 48 propositions culminating in the Pythagorean theorem (I.47–48).

Elements

The thirteen books of plane geometry, proportion, number theory, incommensurable magnitudes, and the five regular polyhedra, translated from Heiberg’s Greek text with commentary by Sir Thomas Little Heath.

Euclid of Alexandria

Book II

Geometric algebra of rectangles and squares: 2 definitions and 14 propositions including gnomons, extreme and mean ratio (II.11), and quadrature of rectilinear figures (II.14).

Data

Composed by Euclid of Alexandria alongside the thirteen books of the Elements, the Data (Δεδομένα, Dedomena) is the foundational grammar of Greek geometric analysis: given certain magnitudes, ratios, angles, or loci in hypothesis, what further geometric entities are thereby uniquely determined (πόριμον)?

English · Greek (Menge 1896)
Euclid of Alexandria

Propositions 1–94 of Euclid’s Data

The ninety-four propositions of Euclid’s Data establish the constructive closure of Greek geometric analysis, progressing systematically across magnitudes, ratios, position loci, triangle species, area application with defect and excess, proportional figures, and circular chords.

Book III

Geometry of the circle: 11 definitions and 37 propositions on chords, tangents, inscribed angles, cyclic quadrilaterals, and intersecting secant-tangent power.

Book IV

Inscribed and circumscribed regular polygons: 7 definitions and 16 propositions constructing triangles, squares, regular pentagons (IV.11), hexagons, and 15-gons.

Book V

Eudoxus’s general theory of ratios and proportions for commensurable and incommensurable magnitudes across 18 definitions and 25 propositions.

Book VI

Similar plane figures and application of areas: 4 definitions and 33 propositions including angle bisectors, mean proportionals, and extreme and mean ratio (VI.30).

Book VII

Foundations of arithmetic and number theory: 22 definitions and 39 propositions starting from the Euclidean GCD algorithm (VII.1–2) and prime divisibility.

Book VIII

Geometric progressions in whole numbers (continued proportions), square and cube numbers, and commensurability of ratios across 27 propositions.

Book IX

Culmination of Euclidean number theory across 36 propositions: Euclid’s theorem on the infinitude of primes (IX.20), geometric series sums, and even perfect numbers (IX.36).

Book X

Classification of incommensurable magnitudes across 115 propositions: commensurability in length vs. square, medial lines, binomials, and apotomes.

Book XI

Foundations of three-dimensional solid geometry: 28 definitions and 39 propositions on planes in space, dihedral and solid angles, and parallelepipeds.

Book XII

The method of exhaustion across 18 propositions: exact area and volume ratios of circles, pyramids, prisms, cones, cylinders, and spheres.

Book XIII

The Golden Ratio and the five Platonic regular solids across 18 propositions: inscribing the tetrahedron, octahedron, cube, icosahedron, and dodecahedron in a sphere.
