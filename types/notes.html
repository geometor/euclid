Unit 1: Magnitudes & Ratios (Propositions 1–24) — Philological & Critical Notes

Unit 1 (D.1–D.24) operates at the universal level of Elements Book V magnitudes (μεγέθη): lines, plane areas, solids, and angles. In his critical notes on D.2 (pp. 456–457), Robert Simson pointed out a subtle foundational boundary that Euclid leaves tacit: in the Elements, constructing a fourth proportional $C : D = A : B$ is proved constructively only for straight lines ([VI.12]), not directly for arbitrary solids or inhomogeneous magnitudes.

Unit 2: Position, Angles & Parallel Loci (Propositions 25–38) — Philological & Critical Notes

The opening propositions of Unit 2 (D.25–D.30) exhibit a striking stylistic feature in the Greek manuscripts: instead of constructing an auxiliary copy, they argue by reductio ad absurdum using the kinematic verb μεταπεσεῖται ("it will shift / fall elsewhere"), appealing directly to [Data Def. 4] ("always occupying the same place").

Unit 5: Proportional Parallelograms, Triangles & Four-Line Relations (Propositions 68–83) — Philological & Critical Notes

Unit 5 (D.68–D.83) lifts the theory of equiangular parallelograms ([Elements VI.14], [VI.23]) to arbitrary given angles. Euclid’s key geometric operator in D.69, D.70, D.73, and D.74 is an area-preserving Cavalieri shear ([I.35]) on a fixed base $BC$: shearing a parallelogram from given angle $\\theta_1$ to given angle $\\theta_2$ introduces a corner triangle $\\triangle ABP$ whose angles are given, so its slant-side ratio $BA : BP = \sin\\theta_2 / \sin\\theta_1$ is given by D.40!
